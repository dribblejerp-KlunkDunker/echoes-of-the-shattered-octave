## CHAPTER TWENTY-FOUR
### Heartglass

> *Writ of Attainder and Grace. Be it known that the persons herein named are declared Dissonant, their goods forfeit, their testimony void, their names struck. Provided always that grace is offered to each, once, and that a man who refuses grace has chosen what follows.*
>
> — Standing form, the Arbiterium

*The clearing by the Mausoleum of Chords. Rootday, dawn.*

---

Four fingers and a half.

He measured it sitting on the keel-block in the grey light with the string and the knots, and then he measured it again because the first answer was wrong, and the second answer was the same.

At midsummer it had been one finger from the point of the shoulder. On Rootday, in his father's hall, two and a half. On Massday, after he drank a Legionnaire's bell in a depot at the quarry, three. Four at the Malting, after a Steam Golem and a boiler's worth of working noise. And now four and a half, because last night he had put his fist through a thing that had eaten five people and two years of sound, and the glass had taken all of it, and he had felt it go in like a man drinking in a doorway.

Four and a half fingers from the point of the shoulder puts the tideline past the collarbone, across the top of the chest, and three inches from the notch of the throat.

The heart is deeper than that and further over. He had worked out the distance in the second winter with a piece of chalk on the hut wall, and had then not looked at the sum for seven years, which was its own kind of arithmetic.

He wrote the number on the deck-beam under the nine names, in charcoal, and sat looking at the column.

*1 · 2½ · 3 · 4 · 4½*

Six weeks, he thought. Perhaps eight, if nothing came at them. Nothing had failed to come at them in nineteen days.

"Engineer."

Sable was at the hatch with the Ledger at her elbow and her arm out of its sling, which Bettany had forbidden.

"There is a man in the clearing," she said, "in white, under a parley flag, and he says he has a writ for Kaelen Vance, and he is going to read it aloud whether or not anyone comes, because the form requires it to be read aloud and witnessed."

---

He was about twenty-five, and he had walked nine miles through the Spectral Woods at night alone to do this, which told you something about the Arbiterium's training or about what they had on him.

He stood in the middle of the clearing in a white herald's tabard with the scales on the breast, holding a staff with a white rag knotted to it, with a nine-foot Office automaton eleven feet away and a skyship crew around him in a loose and extremely unfriendly ring, and he read from a sealed sheet with a black ribbon in a clear carrying voice, and his hands shook the entire time and he did not let it get into his reading, and Kaelen found that he respected him enormously.

"*By the Arbiterium of the Iron Synod, under the hand of the Supreme Arbiter, at the Organum, the fourth day of Leaffall, 419.*

"*Be it known: the persons styled Kaelen Vance, late Chief Resonant Engineer, badge four-oh-eight-A; Lyra Ashgrove, Cantor, of Oakhaven; the carrier styled Vane, of registry OK-114; and the scribe styled Sable, formerly of the Office of the Catalogue — are declared Dissonant. Their goods are forfeit. Their testimony is void in any court of the Synod. The Church of the Pure Note has entered a claim upon the Cantor, which claim the Arbiterium has stayed, for now.*"

Hetty Bram said something short under her breath. The herald did not look up.

"*Be it further known that the Justiciar Aurelian Vaelen, Dread Arbiter, is upon the Iron Tramway with a column of the Carillon Legions and the authority of the Scales, and will be at the Mausoleum of Chords within nine days.*

"*And be it known that grace is offered, once.*"

He turned the sheet.

"*To Kaelen Vance: surrender of his person at the Organum, upon these terms. The glass shall be drawn. The Supreme Arbiter attests, upon his own body, that it can be drawn, and that he has carried eighty years of it and will show the engineer how.*"

Pennick, on his crutch, made a small noise.

"*To the Cantor Lyra Ashgrove: amnesty, full and recorded, and the Church's claim extinguished, and transit to any province she names.*

"*To the persons of Oakhaven aboard the said vessel: relief, transit, and registration waived.*"

"*Waived*," said Sable, very quietly. "He knows what we saw at the Malting."

"*And to the consignment of engram cores presently aboard the said vessel: proper disposition by the Office of the Catalogue, under the Registrar-General's hand.*"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"There it is," said Vane.

---

"I'm taking it," said Kaelen.

They had gone twenty yards into the trees to have it, which fooled nobody, because Mags could read lips at that distance and the rest of them could hear perfectly well.

"Of course you are," said Lyra.

"Listen to me—"

"No, it's wonderful. It's the best one yet." Her voice was still a ruin; it made everything she said sound as though it had been dragged over gravel, which suited her this morning. "Nineteen days ago you were going to go down a shaft on your own and break a bore with your hands. Then you were going to sign a paper so the Hollow wouldn't burn. Now you're going to walk into the Organum and lie down on a table for the man who used you, and every single time, Kaelen, every single time, the plan is *only Kaelen goes into the dark*."

"It's twenty-one people off this ship and out of a war."

"It's four hundred and thirty-two people into a furnace!" She pointed back through the trees. "*Proper disposition.* You heard it. Say what it means."

He said it, because she was right and because she had earned it. "It means they go back into Legionnaires."

"And Annet Sowerby is in the third crate," said Lyra.

---

The others came up, because of course they did.

"I'd like to put one thing in," said Vane. He had his hands in his pockets. "I've been thinking about it since the herald started, which is longer than I usually think about anything. Nine days, he said. That's a column on the Tramway with a Justiciar at the head of it, and they'll be at this clearing on Driftday, and the *Kestrel* can't fly on three tanks."

"I know."

"Which means the choice isn't *Kaelen goes in or nobody goes in*. It's *Kaelen goes in and buys us nine days we'd have had anyway*, because that writ already told us where they are and how fast." He shrugged. "It's a bad trade, that's all I'm saying. I've made a career of bad trades and that one's bad."

"He also told us something else," said Sable.

She had the Ledger open and she was looking at it the way she looked at a page that had something wrong with the ruling.

"Think about the terms. Amnesty for the Cantor, transit for twenty-one farmers, registration waived — the Registrar-General would never waive registration; it is the only thing he has — and the cores returned, which the Catalogue would demand anyway. All of that is small." She looked up. "And the thing he is buying with it is one man with a Dawning-glass arm who can hear the Choristers."

"Sable—"

"He does not want you punished, Engineer. He wants you *at the Organum*." Her voice did not rise. "He has eight Anchors to ask a question of, and a Planetary Engine thirty years in the building, and exactly two people alive who have heard a god answer. He is one of them. He is a hundred and forty years old and eighty percent glass and he will not last another twenty years, and he has just offered you a cure, a desk, and an education.

"That is not a sentence. That is a *succession*."

The wood was very quiet.

"He told me the Cantor was the most important person alive," said Kaelen slowly.

"Then he will come for her next," said Sable, "and he will be polite about that too."

---

In the end it was Lyra who answered the herald, because Kaelen found that he could not.

She walked out into the middle of the clearing in a borrowed coat with her hair unbrushed and the mark of a wire under her jaw, and stood in front of a young man in white with a staff, and said, in that torn and carrying voice:

"Write this down, and get it exactly right, because he'll ask you.

"The Cantor of Oakhaven thanks the Supreme Arbiter for the amnesty and declines it.

"The persons of Oakhaven aboard this ship are not goods and are not in his gift, and they will go where they choose, and if he wants to do something useful for them he can put the Reach back.

"The consignment is not a consignment. It is four hundred and thirty-two people out of Whisper-Grove, and we know all of their numbers and some of their names, and we are taking them home.

"And Engineer Vance declines grace."

The herald's pen had stopped.

"He will ask me why," he said.

Lyra thought about that for a moment.

"Tell him the Cantor says: *we heard the question too.*"

---

"You didn't ask me," said Kaelen afterward, by the bow.

"No."

"Lyra—"

"I'm not doing this because I've forgiven you," said Lyra Ashgrove. "I want to be extremely clear, because I know exactly what you'll make of it if I leave a gap. I have not forgiven you. I'm not sure I'm going to. Last night I killed the last place my father's voice existed, and the day before that a thing in a fog told me what you did, kindly, and let me sit down, and both of those are in me now and they are not coming out."

She turned round and faced him.

"But I have watched you try to pay for seven years," she said, "and the arithmetic is wrong, and I'm the only person here who can say so, because everybody else on this ship wants something from you. You can't pay. There isn't enough of you. Eleven thousand and forty people and a sky — there is no amount of Kaelen Vance that buys that back, and you have been trying to settle it one limb at a time since the year it happened, and the Supreme Arbiter has just offered you the biggest instalment yet and you lit up like a lamp."

He did not deny it. He had felt it light.

"So: no," she said. "You don't get to pay. You get to *help*. You get to get up tomorrow and do the next useful thing and then the one after that, for as long as it takes, with people who know exactly what you are, which is the worst sentence I can think of and the only one that's any use to anybody." Her mouth twisted. "That's not mercy. Don't write it down as mercy."

"What should I write it down as?"

"Work," said Lyra.

And she put her hand in his — the glass one, the left, the cold one with the ringing in it, because the right one was easy and she had never once in her life taken the easy one — and stood beside him looking east, and did not say anything else.

---

They buried nothing and marked nothing, because there was nothing to bury.

Mags went up the envelope at noon with a palm and a needle. Pennick drew the number-three tank down and sealed the clip and pronounced it good for perhaps forty miles, and then said the thing he had been not saying for a day and a half.

"We need a tank, Captain. A whole one. And a yard with a drift-bench and somebody who knows how to tune one, and there is one place on this continent inside forty miles of where we can get to."

Vane stood at the wheel for a while.

"Say it," he said.

"Port Cantabile," said Pennick.

The Rust Coast. Oona Salt, and four hundred and eighty sovereigns, and a debt called in. The Salt Stair, and a dealer who had sold him a bottle with a name on the label. And somewhere on that coast, or off it, a woman with eyes gone violet who had come in off the water in the evening, which nobody does, and asked very politely which captain it was who had cut his tow over the trench.

"Right," said Vane.

He said it in a perfectly ordinary voice, and went below, and stood in the hold among the crates in the dark for a while with his hand flat on the third one.

Then he came up and set a course west-south-west, for the coast, which he had sworn for five years he would never do.

---

That night Kaelen went down into the hold with a lantern and a nub of charcoal and read his own list on the beam, which had got longer.

*Tamsin Reed*
*Aldric Vance*
*Davin Hale*
*Tam — (no surname. ASK.)*
*Orla Hale*
*Hob Wendle*
*Ada Pellow*
*8 men at the pumps — Edders. Ruhl. Caine. Ott. Sennet. Vey. Marrow. Hale, B. (no relation)*
*Halder Wick*
*Coralie — (no surname. There is no one to ask.)*

He had added the eight from the carriage file, which had been exactly where Vaelen said it would be, and he had thought about that for a long time afterward and had still written them down.

He sat with his back against the third crate with the murmur going on around him, four hundred and thirty-two people talking in their sleep, and through the glass — faint, four days and a hundred miles behind them, with a hole in it, still going — he could hear the Chorister of Growth losing the tune of a five-note lullaby and starting again from the beginning.

*Somebody has to hold it.*

He had been pushing the thought away for four days, the way you push a dog off a bed.

He stopped pushing it, and looked at it, and found it looking back.

Then Kaelen Vance picked up the charcoal, and under the last name on the list, in the hold of a stolen skyship flying west toward a coast he had never seen, he wrote two words that were not a name and were not a number, and that he could not have explained to anybody aboard:

*Not yet.*
