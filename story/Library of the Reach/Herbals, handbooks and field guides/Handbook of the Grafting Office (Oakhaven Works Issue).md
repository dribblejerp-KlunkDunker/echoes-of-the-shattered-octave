# Handbook of the Grafting Office (Oakhaven Works Issue)

*The Grafting Office of the Iron Synod; this copy annotated in ink in an unknown hand · 417 SR · the Organum; issued at the Oakhaven Works · manual*

> The Synod's regulations for surgeons who cut the burl-sick, in the Office's flat hand, with a working surgeon's replies in the margin.

HANDBOOK OF THE GRAFTING OFFICE. Issued under the authority of the Office for the use of Surgeon-Grafters and their attendants in provinces subject to law-rot. Oakhaven Works Issue, 417 SR. This handbook is the property of the Office. It is not to be lent, copied or annotated.

Margin, in small neat ink: Annotated.

1\. Of the Grafter. The Surgeon-Grafter is an officer of the Synod and not of the household. He attends the patient on behalf of the street. His first duty is to the silence of the street, his second to the law of the province, and his third to the patient.

Margin: Read it backwards.

2\. Of attendance. Inspections are made every second Rootday. The Grafter is accompanied by two attendants, styled sawboys, by a brazier of banked coals in an iron cage, and by an escort. Harness is to be felted and hooves booted. The Grafter will wear the mask, which renders the breath at the level of the Narrow, at all times in the patient's house.

Margin: Not for the children. They have enough to look at.

3\. Of examination. The affected part is to be examined by lens and by pressure, and the sound of the pressure attended to. Living tissue gives. Tissue gone to wood answers. The chest is to be examined by trumpet below the collarbone for the dry sound, which signifies roots in the lung and places the patient beyond the remedy of the saw.

Margin: It sounds like wind in barley. You will not forget it the first time, so do not try.

4\. Of the mute-drops. The patient must not be permitted to cry out. Administer the mute-drops a quarter hour before the first incision, and remind the family that silence is a mercy extended to the whole street. The drops do not relieve pain, and the Grafter is not to represent to the family that they do.

Margin: Somebody must hold the head. The Office does not say who. In this town the Cantor is usually there before you. Let her.

5\. Of the instruments. The hush-saw, felted at the grip, with a blade thin enough to flex. The sap-iron, heated at the brazier and applied to the cut surface to stop the bleed, which in this province is green and slow to cease. No instrument is to be laid on stone or permitted to fall.

Margin: The sap-iron is the one loud thing in the morning. Warn them before it, not after.

6\. Of the sap-mark. Where the patient is found past remedy by the saw, the Grafter will chalk the outer door in green: a circle, with one line struck through. The mark signifies past cutting. The household so marked is to be visited monthly and kept comfortable, and at the last the long drops are to be administered by the Grafter's own hand.

Margin: That is all it means. Write it on the door small, and tell them what it means before you go, so that they do not have to ask the street.

7\. Of the register. The Grafter keeps a register of marked households, giving the name, the house and the date of marking. The register is the property of the Synod. It is to be surrendered without delay to any officer of the Church or of the Arbiterium presenting a warrant under seal.

Something was written in the margin here and scraped away with a knife, more than once, until the paper is thin enough to see the lamp through.

8\. Of children. Children are to be inspected as adults are. Early signs in the hands are to be recorded, and are not to be left to the judgement of the family.

Margin: Unusual is also a word. So is recheck next month.

9\. Of conduct. The Grafter will not hum, sing, pray aloud or converse with the patient beyond the questions required. The Grafter will not express to the family any opinion as to the justice of the procedure.

Margin: No. But I may go slowly. Nothing here says how fast.

10\. Of this Handbook. The Grafter will keep this Handbook about his person and consult it in all cases of doubt.

Margin: I do. In every case. And then I consult the margin.
