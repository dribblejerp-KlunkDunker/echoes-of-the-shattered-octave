# The Honest Fire: A Sermon Between the Beats

*Furnace-Priest Kell · 418 SR · Cinderhold; printed by subscription of the Deep Guilds · sermon*

> A Cinderhold priest's forge-lighting sermon arguing that heat is the only honest law in the world, because it alone admits what it consumes.

THE HONEST FIRE. Preached by the Furnace-Priest Kell at the forge-lighting of the Hearth Feast, in Stillmonth of 418, on the lower tiers, and taken down by a Guild clerk who set a stroke wherever the Great Beat fell, as those who heard it will remember. The Basilica has declared it unsound. It is printed by subscription of the Deep Guilds, who have declared the Basilica cold.

Still standing? | Good. | Then listen between the strokes, | because I will not shout over them. | Nobody shouts over the Beat. | That is the first thing Cinderhold teaches | and the last thing the Church will learn.

(The clerk set the strokes for the first page only. After that, he says, any Cinderholder will hear them for himself, and nobody else deserves to.)

I am a priest of the Pure Note. I hold it. I have held it at the forge-lighting for thirty years. I am not here tonight to tell you the one note is false. I am here to tell you that of the eight laws of the world, only one has ever told us the truth about itself, and it is ours.

Consider the others, as the Church teaches them. Growth gives: the apple, the wheat, the oak. Light gives the day. Mass gives the floor under you and the weight of your child in your arms. Time gives your years, Velocity your winds, Memory your dead, kept. Gift, gift, gift, says the Church, and the glass in the ground the greatest gift of all, the Light's surplus, given for nothing to be burned for nothing. Eight laws, eight presents, and never once a bill.

I have kept a furnace since I was nine years old. I tell you I have never been given anything for nothing in my life, and neither has the world.

Heat does not pretend. Heat says: give me the coal and I will give you warmth, and the coal is gone. Give me the ore and I will give you iron, and the mountain is less by that much. Fire eats, and says it eats, and leaves you ash for a receipt. That is why I call it holy. Not because it is kind. It is not kind. Every one of you has buried someone it took. It is holy because it is the only law in the world that has never once told a man it loved him.

Now. The Church says the glass is surplus. I have a question for the Church, and I put it at the forge-lighting because this is the one night in the year they cannot stop me. Go down to Tier Eleven, where the Guild men cut the richest seams on the continent, and lay your ear against the face the way they do at the end of a shift, when they think nobody is watching. Then come back up and tell me that surplus whispers.

I do not know what the glass was before it was glass. I am a furnace-priest; I know nothing I have not burned. But I know that everything that burns was something first. The coal was a forest. The tallow was a beast. Nothing on this earth comes to the fire without having been something else, and a man who burns a thing and will not ask what it was is not grateful. He is only comfortable.

And the Cold Burn. Our fires freeze in the grate, and our frost scalds, and the ember-blind walk the lower tiers with nothing left alight in them, and the Church calls it a sickness of the flat world and sends us a hymn. I will tell you what I think it is. I think we have drawn on Heat since my grandfather's day as if it were a gift, and it has begun at last to behave like one. A gift is cold. A gift costs the giver everything and the getter nothing, and it comes with no receipt; and that is the fire we have now, fire that takes nothing and gives nothing and lies in the grate looking like fire. We taught it that. We taught it to lie.

So here is my heresy, and you may carry it out of the gate tonight. Every honest thing consumes, and says so: the forge, the furnace, a worker's back, a mother's sleep. If a man tells you he gives and takes nothing, he is a god or a liar, and I have never yet met a god. If a law of the world tells you the same, ask it what it is eating.

The Basilica writes that I have made an idol of a furnace. The Basilica has never stood at one. I invite it. Let a Flambeau come down to the lower tiers and hold the Pure Note in front of the Great Beat for one shift, and keep his hand on it, and then we will talk about idols.

Now light the forges. On the ingot, light them. And when the first heat comes up off the coal into your faces, do not thank it. It does not want thanks. Pay it. Give it what it asks, and look at what it leaves, and remember the price; because the day you stop remembering what the fire costs is the day you will believe anything anybody tells you is free.

Still standing? | Then keep your hand on it.
