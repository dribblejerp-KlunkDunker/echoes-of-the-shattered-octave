# The Little Catechism of the Pure Note

*the Choir-Church of the Pure Note, for the instruction of children (a child's copy) · 416 SR · printed for the parishes of the Resonant Reach · catechism*

> The questions and answers every child of the Reach learns before confirmation, in a copy that once belonged to a Greenroot girl.

THE LITTLE CATECHISM OF THE PURE NOTE. For the instruction of children before their confirmation in the Light. Set forth under the seal of His Resonance the High Patriarch, to be learned by heart, said in the Narrow, and never sung except in the Church's keeping.

To the teacher. Let the child give each answer in one breath and on one pitch. If the voice rises or falls within an answer, let the child say it again until it does not. This is not unkindness. It is the first lesson.

OF THE LIGHT

Q. Who made the world?  A. The Architects, who came out of the Light and pinned the world to eight Spires, so that it would not fall.

Q. Why does the apple grow, and the fire warm, and the stone fall?  A. Because the Spires are singing, each its own law, and none of them has ever stopped.

Q. Do the Spires grow tired?  A. No. What is holy does not tire.

Q. Who keeps the Spires?  A. The Iron Synod, which the Light appointed. One Note. One Law. One Hand.

Q. What is Aether-Glass?  A. The Light's generous surplus, given so that we may be warm and fed and carried. It would be ungrateful to leave it in the ground.

OF HOLINESS

Q. What is holiness?  A. One note, held without end.

Q. What is sin?  A. The second note.

Q. Is the second note wicked?  A. It is a crack. Through a crack the whole jar empties.

Q. What is the Pure Note?  A. Five hundred and twenty-eight, the Light's own pitch, which the choirs hold for us so that we need not.

Q. Why did the world go flat?  A. Because in the Screaming Year a dissonance was raised at the Dawning, and the Light was wounded, and every note in the world went a little wrong.

Q. Will it be mended?  A. When every voice holds one note, the Light will be whole.

OF THE NARROW

Q. Why must I speak low?  A. Because the hounds come to noise, and most noise is the second note.

Q. Why must I not be silent?  A. Because a silent child is a sullen child, and the hush-lamp is there to remind me.

Q. What is the Hush?  A. A thing we do not speak of in the Church, because the Pure Note is never silent.

Q. May I sing?  A. Not alone, and not in the open, and not in two parts. Only in the Church's keeping, where the Pure Flame goes before the choir.

Q. Why do the hounds not come to the choir?  A. Because the Flame goes before it, and they will not cross the Flame. This shows that they know what is holy.

OF THE KEPT

Q. Who are the Consecrated?  A. Those in whom the Light has set its glass, so that they may be kept.

Q. Who is our Queen?  A. Maelis, whom the Light has kept long past her dying, and whose hand does not shake.

Q. Is she afraid?  A. The kept are never afraid.

Q. What is the highest thing a voice may do?  A. Hold the Pure Note for the Light for as long as it is asked, and not let it waver.

Q. And if it hurts?  A. Then I offer the hurt, and hold.

OF THE PURE FLAME

Q. What shall I do if I hear the second note in my own mouth?  A. Close my mouth, and tell my mother, and tell the priest.

Q. What shall I do if I hear the second note in my mother's mouth?  A. Love her, and tell the priest.

Q. What is done with the second note?  A. Where it has taken root, it is given to the Pure Flame, which is the only song.

Q. Is the Flame cruel?  A. The Flame is mercy. It does not hurt what is pure.

A PRAYER AT THE HUSH-LAMP, to be said low, at bedtime: Light, keep me true. Keep my mouth to one note. Keep my house from the second note, and my street from the hounds, and if I wake in the dark, let the lamp be whistling.

Inside the back board, in pencil, in a child's round hand:

Dilly Teale her book. Greenroot. Lernt to the end.

Below it, in the same hand and the same pencil, a question of her own, set out properly with a Q and an A, the way she had been taught:

Q. Why has the lamp stopped whistling.

A.

She has left the rest of the line for the answer.
