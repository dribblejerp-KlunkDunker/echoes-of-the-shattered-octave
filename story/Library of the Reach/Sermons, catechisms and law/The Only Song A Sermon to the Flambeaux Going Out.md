# The Only Song: A Sermon to the Flambeaux Going Out

*Lucan Ostrel, Flambeau-Preceptor · 418 SR · the Basilica of the Pure Note; printed so that every Flambeau might carry a copy · sermon*

> The sermon preached to new Flambeaux on the eve of their going out to the provinces, on why the Church alone sings aloud, and why it burns.

Preached to the Flambeaux of the year's going-out, on the eve of their dispersal to the provinces, in the Basilica of the Pure Note, and printed so that each of them might carry it. Where the second note has taken root, let the Pure Flame be the only song.

Brothers. Sisters. Tomorrow you go out, and tonight I will tell you what you are going into, because nobody told me.

You are going into the whispering country. You will come to towns where nobody has raised a voice in six years. They have hedges where their walls were and thorn where their gates were, and in every room a lamp with a reed in it that whistles all night so that the house is never quite silent. They will speak to you in what they call the Narrow, the strip of voice they have left between too loud and too still, and they will tell you it is wisdom. They teach it to their children as a skipping rhyme. Listen to the rhyme. Then ask yourself what kind of province teaches its children to skip to their own fear.

I do not despise them. Hear that before anything else. I was born in a valley like those valleys, and my mother hummed me into the world through a clay jar. They are not wicked. They are frightened, and fear has a pitch, and it is never the true one.

Now hear what you are. You are the only people in Aethelgard who walk in the open, singing. The hounds come to every voice in the world but yours. They will pace the tree-line beside your procession. You will see them, a shaking in the air like heat over a summer road, keeping step with you, and they will not come in. Ask yourselves why. It is not because you are brave. I have known Flambeaux who were not brave. It is the Flame. The Light gave us glass-fire, white and gold, out of its own surplus, and nothing hungry will cross it. That is not a weapon. It is a testimony. The Light has drawn a line on the ground around the one note and written: here, nothing comes.

So you will sing aloud, and they will stare at you from behind their hands. Let them stare. You are the thing they have forgotten is possible: a voice raised, and alive afterwards.

You will be asked about the burning. You will be asked by the people in the hedges, and by magistrates who cannot read our warrants, and at last by yourselves, in the third hour of the night, which is the hour the question comes. I will answer it now, so that you have the answer in your mouth when you need it.

The second note is not a wickedness of the heart. I have given women to the Flame who were kinder than I am, and I knew it at the time. The second note is a crack. In a jar a crack is a small thing. You can hold the jar to the window and hardly see it, and the jar empties through it all the same.

Consider a town in the whispering country. Four hundred souls, holding their breath. In one house a woman hums her child to sleep at an open window, in two parts, because her grandmother did and it is a pretty thing. You know what comes to it. And when it comes, it does not come only to her window. A pack that has found one door walks the whole street. One lullaby. A hundred doors.

Consider the burl-sick: the second note taken root in the flesh. A woman grown into her chair. A child with bark at the wrist. The Grafters cut, and the root comes back, and they cut again, and it comes back again, because you cannot cut a note out of a body. The flesh is singing the wrong song and will go on singing it, and every night it sings, the hounds sit a little closer to the hedge.

You will be told that this is cruelty. I tell you it is arithmetic, and the Light did the sum before we were born. The Flame does not hurt what is pure. What it takes, it was always going to take. You choose only whether it takes one house or the street.

Now the hardest thing, and then I will let you sleep. When you stand at the warrant's hour with the stave lifted, do not weep. Weep afterwards. I do. Weep in your cell, into your sleeve, at the foot of the Spire if you are ever let near it. Not at the hour. A flame held in a shaking hand is a flame that wavers, and a wavering flame is a second note, and the hounds know the difference better than you do.

In some of those towns there will be a Cantor. There are fewer every year. She will be the kindest person you meet, and she will look at you as if you were the thing at the tree-line. Be gentle with her. She has spent her life singing second notes to apple trees and calling it a craft, and she does not know what she has been doing. Most of them do not. That is not their sin. It is ours, for leaving them so long without the Light.

Go out singing. Let them hear you coming a mile off, as they have not heard anything coming in six years, and let them hate you for it from behind their thorn. They will be alive to hate you. That is the whole of our ministry, and it is enough.

And when you come to the gate of a whispering town, do not lower your voice. Nothing that holds the one note has ever needed to.
