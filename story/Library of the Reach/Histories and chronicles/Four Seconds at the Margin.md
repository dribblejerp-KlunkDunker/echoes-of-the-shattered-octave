# Four Seconds at the Margin

*Gerda Stahl, of Tier Three, Cinderhold; taken down and printed on Broadsheet Row · 418 SR · printed on Broadsheet Row, Port Cantabile; carried up the river through Stillwater Landing · chronicle*

> A drill-hand of Cinderhold's Tier Three tells, between the hammer-falls, how nine hundred of her tier died in four seconds at the Thermal Margin, and why she is the one left to tell it.

Printed on Broadsheet Row. The printer's note: This was spoken to us by a woman of Cinderhold who came down the coast in the spring with nothing but a gauge-house key on a string. She would only talk between the falls of a hammer, so we found her one at the forge on the Lantern Yard, and wrote down what she said in the spaces. We have kept the spaces.

I am Gerda Stahl, of Tier Three. Still standing.

You want the Margin. Everyone wants the Margin. Let the hammer come round.

The Margin is the ring of drills outside the caldera, where the heat comes up out of the ground and the Consortium puts pipes in it. Tier Three works it. Worked it. Nine hundred and seven on the day shift, though nobody had counted until after. My Odo had the number-six head. I had the gauge-house behind it. That is a stone hut the size of a cart with a basalt wall a yard thick on the drill side, and a slot to read the needle through.

The order came on a Massday in 417, by a runner in grey. Pressure up by a third. The Synod wanted more out of the ground and wanted it before the Weighing. Our foreman read it out between falls. He said it was against the seal-tolerance. The runner said the tolerance had been revised. The foreman said, revised by who. The runner said, by people who are not standing here. That was true.

We put the pressure up. You do what the paper says. Odo kissed me at the gauge-house door. He tasted of sulphur. Everybody did.

For nine days it held. The needles sat high and steady and the Synod's men wrote it down. On the tenth day the fires in the drill-houses went cold.

Not out. Cold. You have not seen it, down here by your sea. A furnace burning white, and frost on the iron round it. Men pulled their hands off the rails with the skin left behind, and it was the frost that burned them. We have had the Cold Burn on the upper tiers since the Screaming Year. We had never had it at the Margin. The Margin was the hottest place in the world.

Let the hammer come round.

Then the whistle. The old women have a name for it. F-sharp. It is the heat remembering what it is for. It goes up out of the ground like a kettle the size of a mountain, and when you hear it you have four seconds, and the only thing in the world that will stand four seconds of it is basalt.

I was in the gauge-house. I did not decide anything. My body got down behind the wall before I knew the sound had a name.

One.

Two. I heard Odo shout my name from the number-six head. He was forty yards off in the open. He was not shouting for help. He was shouting to tell me where he was, so I would not come out to look.

Three. I did not come out. I want that printed. I did not go out. A Cinderholder will call that cold. Let them.

Four.

When I stood up, the frost had gone off everything at once and the air was so hot it hurt the eyes, and the Margin was quiet, and there was nobody on it. Not bodies. Shapes on the ground like the outlines a soot-fall leaves round a cart when you move it. Nine hundred of us, from the drill-heads out to the pipe-road. Seven came out alive. Six were in the gauge-houses, like me. The seventh is a scrapper of ours who dived behind a basalt wall-end out in the open, and he has not stopped talking about it since, loud, in every tavern on the tier, because when he stops he hears it.

The Consortium sent a clerk the next week with a form for the widows. Next of kin, number of the head, hours worked. There was a line for cause, and the cause was printed already. Seal failure. Nobody had looked at a seal.

They say in the deep galleries that the glass down there whispers. I never worked the deep galleries. But I will tell you what the Margin said, in the half-second before the whistle, under it, and I have not told this at home because I know what they would call me. It said a word. It sounded like a word. It sounded like somebody who has been holding something hot for a very long time, saying, enough.

They are talking about a strike in the guild-halls now. They talk about it between the falls, very low. When it comes, I will be there.

Odo kept his hand on it. On the ingot. Let the hammer come round.
