# Tench's Reach Almanack for the Year 419

*Cuthbert Tench, almanack-maker; with notes in the margin by Ezra Dunnock, farmer · 419 SR · printed at Stillwater Landing; annotated in Oakhaven · almanac*

> A cheap printed almanack of feasts, weather and planting for the Reach in 419, argued with all year in the margins by an Oakhaven farmer.

TENCH'S REACH ALMANACK for the Year 419. Feasts, Weather, Planting and Sundry Useful Matters, for the Use of Households in the Reach and along the Flume. Printed at Stillwater Landing. One Chime, or the value.

In the margin: Paid a Chime for it at the gate. A Chime! Ezra Dunnock, his book, north field, Oakhaven.

THE COMPILER TO HIS READERS. The year has three hundred and sixty days, twelve months of thirty and forty-five weeks of eight, and so every date falls on the same day of the week as it did last year and will next. A household that keeps this almanack need never buy another. The compiler hopes it will.

In the margin: He hopes it will not, or he is a poor sort of tradesman.

THE EIGHT DAYS, and their old uses, as the country people keep them. Rootday, for setting and sowing. Hearthday, for baking. Massday, for carting and heavy work. Stasisday, for mending what is broken. Dawnday, for the Chapel. Driftday, for journeys. Memoryday, for letters to the absent. Nullday, for rest.

In the margin: My gran would not fell a tree on a Rootday. Said that was the trees' own day and you let them have it. Nobody rests on Nullday. You keep somebody by you on Nullday and you leave the lamp in.

STILLMONTH. The year begins on the first, which is a Dawnday, as it always is, the morning after the Longest Night. The Hearth Feast, the lighting of the forges, on Hearthday the sixth: chiefly kept in the Caldera. Weather: open and mild, as is usual in the Reach. Mend tools, prune, carry muck.

In the margin: Mild. Mended the long fence. Nobody here has lit a forge for a feast in my life.

THAWMONTH. Ice off the Flume, where there is any. Contract Day, the Feast of the Dial, on Stasisday the second, when every term in the province falls due and is made again. Settle accounts. Turn the soil when it will take the spade.

In the margin: There's no ice on the Flume. There never is. Tench has copied that out of somebody's book from the Peaks. Contract Day: went to the Greenhall to make my ground-rent again. The clerk wrote the same figure. Nobody makes a term again in Oakhaven. They copy it out.

SEEDMONTH. The Root Feast on Rootday the first: the planting feast, the one day of the year kept loud on purpose. Sow barley, oats and beans. This is the loudest month in the Reach and the compiler wishes his readers a noisy one.

In the margin: We were loud. Inside the wool, all of us in the square, the old planting song, the whole of it, top to bottom, the first time since the Hedge. The Hedge took it. Nothing came. Then everyone went home very quick and quiet as if we'd done something wrong. Sowed the north field Rootday. The ground said wait. I didn't.

LAMBMONTH. Lambing. Weather: soft. Sow the late peas. Watch the hedges for nesting.

In the margin: No lambs. The flocks went into the Hedge two years back and into us after. Watched the Hedge anyway. It nests something. Not birds.

REEDMONTH. When the wind-reeds come up, Reed-Hanging: take down last year's reeds over the door and hang new, cut to each child's own note. The Cantor's year properly begins.

In the margin: No wind-reeds since the year the Spire went. Cut the girl's new reed from the beds by the Flume, same as last year, and the Cantor tuned it at the door against her own breastbone, and it hangs there. Flume reed does well enough. It doesn't sing like the old ones. Nothing does.

LONGSUN. Hay. The Empyrean, the dawn service, on Dawnday the third, which all are bound to attend. The Longest Day on the thirtieth, a Nullday, on which there is no feast and nothing is to be done.

In the margin: Stood in the square at dawn as the paper says. Chapel-reader read the service. Nobody sang. Did nothing on the thirtieth. Felt it all day, like weather coming. Hay was in twice. Cut it Massday and it stood again by Dawnday, thinner. The second hay smells of nothing.

DUSTMONTH. Dry. The Feast of the Drift and the Great Race on Driftday the sixth, on the Coast, where the captains keep it. Water stock. Watch for fire.

In the margin: Not dry. Damp all month, and everything growing as if it were being chased. Sapling up through the yard cobbles. Sawed it. It came back from the cut by morning. Sawed it again. That is my Dustmonth, Tench.

FIRSTFRUIT. Early apples, plums. The Eel-Run, the night the eels go down the Flume, late in the month: lanterns, traps, and quiet.

In the margin: No eels. Old men at the crossing say they'll go down late and they've never been wrong about eels. Apples split on the bough from swelling. Pressed what I could.

SHEAFMONTH. Reaping. The Keystone, the Weighing at Fortis, on Massday the seventh, where the soldiers weigh their kit and cut names in the wall. Bind sheaves dry.

In the margin: Harrow had it off a carter that they cut more names this year than last at Fortis. Reaped the long field. Bread from it tastes of the bin. Third cut since the spring. Every cut is less. My girl asked why bread used to taste of something. I said it still does, you're just used to it. She knew I was lying. They always do, at eight.

HARVESTMONTH. The Last Sheaf, which the almanack cannot date, for it falls when the last field is cut, and is known by the sheaf hung on the gate. The whole village cuts the last stand together, one cut to the beat. The sheaf is plaited and hung over the gate till spring, and is the one sheaf the Reach lets stay cut.

In the margin: The last stand is mine this year, in the north field, the corner by the oaks. Had the plait-straw ready and the girl had her ribbon. Then the Church came up the Greenroot road singing their one note with the white fire on poles, and the reader read it out in the square: there is to be a Rite on Hearthday the sixteenth, and the Last Sheaf is to wait until after, so as not to sing two things at once. So it waits. It stands there in the corner with the light on it. I went out at dusk and put my hand on it. Feels like it's waiting for something.

ASHFALL. Lift roots. The Canopy, the remembering of the dead, on Memoryday the seventh. (Not presently kept.)

FROSTMONTH. Mild, as is usual in the Reach. Ringing-Wood Day at the first hard frost, when the year's timber is felled and named. The Longest Night on the thirtieth, a Stasisday. The Reach has known one hard winter in living memory, in the year of the Fracture, and the almanack does not look for another.

In the margin, the last thing written in the book: Sheaf still standing. We'll cut it when we're let.
