# Ledger and Day-Book of a Timber Run, Stillwater to Fortis

*Rowland Ruddock, timber factor, of Stillwater Landing · 416 SR · Stillwater Landing, and the road east · travel*

> A Stillwater timber factor keeps his accounts on a run of ringing iron-bough to the Bastion, and finds one entry he cannot price.

Hearthday, 2 Seedmonth, at Stillwater.
Bought of the Timber Compact cutters, forty logs of iron-bough, felled at the first frost, seasoned, chalked for Fortis. Paid: 20 sovereigns.
Paid the keeper his lock dues, in advance, not refunded. Paid: 1 sovereign.
Paid the keeper for news of the river. Paid: 1 sovereign. He sold the same to the Compact's man before me and the Bores factor after, and told me so, which I suppose is what I paid for.
Hired Huw and Dickon to raft, and Bartle to pole. Wages advanced, 2 sovereigns each. Paid: 6 sovereigns.
Took my nephew Seb, fifteen, because his mother asked me, and because she asked me loudly.

Stasisday, 4 Seedmonth.
Out through the lower lock at first light. The basin carries; we said nothing until the cutting. Every lashing oiled so it will not creak. Every log wrapped at the ends in old fleece, so that if two touch they knock and do not ring. Paid for fleece: 4 pence. The rule on the raft is the rule of the trade: no log touches another, and no man raises his voice, and nobody whistles, and that includes you, Seb.

Driftday, 6 Seedmonth.
At the Drop the raft took the weir crooked and the big log at the head, the best of the forty, came out of its fleece and struck the sill. It rang. I have heard iron-bough ring all my life and I have never heard it ring like that, over water, at dusk, like the Chapel bell at the Landing.
There were hounds on the east bank inside the count of twenty. Huw did not wait for me to tell him. He cut the head log loose with the hatchet and kicked it off down the race, and it went away down the river ringing every time it touched a stone, and the hounds went with it along the bank, and we lay flat on the raft and let the current have us the other way.
Item: one log, the best, given to the river. Value 3 sovereigns. Entered here for the record, but not added, since I paid for it already at Stillwater and do not mean to pay for it twice. It bought something. I am not sure the ledger has a column for what.

Rootday, 9 Seedmonth, the railhead at Ironford.
Thirty-nine logs onto the flatcars of the Iron Tramway. Freight: 8 sovereigns. The yard clerk would not take Chimes, though I had them. Paid him besides, for his consideration, which is what he called it: 1 sovereign.

Dawnday, 13 Seedmonth.
The Tramway goes round the Valley of Velocity by the long loop, and from the cars you can see down into it. It is raining in the Valley. It has been raining in the Valley, the brakeman says, since '98, the same rain, hanging in the air like beads on threads, and the light goes through it and does not come out the same colour. Nobody runs on the cars. The brakeman told Seb so, and then told him again, and then showed him a man's boot nailed to the brake-house wall and did not say whose.
Seb bet Bartle a penny he could throw a stone into the Valley. He threw it. It went in at the edge and it is going still, I suppose, faster all the time. Bartle says the wager is not decided until it lands. Paid Seb's wager anyway, to have peace: 1 penny.

Rootday, 17 Seedmonth.
Into the Peaks. Grey rain, grey rock, and a mist that pulls at anything iron, so that Dickon's knife went out of his belt sideways and over the drop like a bird. At the second halt the axle under the fourth car began to groan, and the brakeman said the tide was on, and we watched the logs on that car sink the springs flat, as if each one weighed a house. Nobody touched them. In an hour the tide went over and the springs came back up and the logs were logs again.
A Peaks woman at the halt asked Huw "You're heavy?" and he said "Middling", and she looked at him the way you would look at a man who had spat on your doorstep. Seb has learned to say "Heavy enough" and says it to everybody, including the mules.
Food and lodging at the halts, the whole road. Paid: 2 sovereigns.

Driftday, 22 Seedmonth.
Seb asks about the Sounders, who walk ahead of the work-gangs on the line tapping the stone with iron rods as tall as themselves. He walked behind one for a mile this morning, at a respectful distance, listening. The Sounder let him.

Nullday, 24 Seedmonth.
No train runs on Nullday in the Peaks. We sat up in the halt-house all night with the hush-lamp and talked, the five of us, about nothing, so as not to be still. Seb asked what the dead note sounds like. Nobody knew. Dickon said if you have heard it you do not come back to say.

Massday, 27 Seedmonth, at Fortis.
Delivered thirty-nine logs to the Compact's quartermaster on the fourth terrace. They weighed them on Fortis scales, checked against the Marshal's own weight, and the logs weighed a fifth again what they weighed at Stillwater, and I said so, honestly, and the quartermaster said the weight is the weight, and paid by it, 2 sovereigns the log.
Received: 78 sovereigns.
I asked her what the Bastion wants with ringing wood, when it has the whole mountain. She said they use Reach iron-bough for props in the deep levels, because a prop of iron-bough rings when the load comes on it, and a Sounder can hear a ringing prop three levels off. Your wood tells us when the mountain moves, she said. It is the only timber in the world that will.
Paid Huw, Dickon and Bartle the balance of their wages, 3 sovereigns each: 9 sovereigns.

Rootday, 3 Lambmonth.
Seb has asked to stay. The Sounders take any boy who can stand still and listen, and the Captain of them tells me he can. I said his mother would have my hide. He said his mother would have it anyway, for the boots. I bought him boots fit for the deep. Paid: 2 sovereigns.
I have written to Joan. I have not posted it.

ACCOUNT OF THE RUN
Paid out: 50 sovereigns and 5 pence.
Received: 78 sovereigns.
Balance in hand: 28 sovereigns, less 5 pence.
Left at Fortis: one nephew, Sebastian Ruddock, fifteen, at his own asking.

I have looked for the column to put him in, and there is not one.

I go home lighter, which up here is an insult, and down there will only be the truth.
