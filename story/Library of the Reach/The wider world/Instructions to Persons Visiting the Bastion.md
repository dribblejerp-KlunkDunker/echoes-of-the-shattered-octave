# Instructions to Persons Visiting the Bastion

*Keld Holm, Clerk of Visitors, for the Martial Compact of Fortis · 416 SR · Bastion Fortis, the Iron Peaks · travel*

> The rules every carter, factor and pilgrim is handed at the gate of Fortis, written by a clerk who means every word and wastes none.

Issued at the Lower Gate to every person not of the Compact. Read it before the second terrace. There is no third copy.

1\. You will be asked: "You're heavy?" Answer: "Heavy enough." Do not answer "Yes". Do not answer "Very". Do not tell the sentry what you ate. He is asking whether you are yourself. Say that you are.

2\. Do not offer your hand to anyone. In the Bastion a hand offered to a stranger is a claim on his weight. If a man of the Compact offers you his, he has decided something about you. Take it.

3\. Do not call any person light. Not in jest. Not of a child. Not of yourself, in the tavern, at the end of a long day. It is the worst thing that can be said here, and it is not said.

4\. Weight wanders in the Peaks. Your pack may weigh twice on the fourth terrace what it weighed at the gate, and nothing at all by noon. Hold it by the strap, not the buckle. If it rises, let it go up a little and bring it down slowly. Do not chase it. The Compact does not refund carriage, and does not pay for goods that have floated.

5\. Iron goes sideways in the mist. On the outer stair, carry your knife inside your coat. A dropped knife does not go down here. It goes over.

6\. Do not run on any terrace. Running is heavy, and heavy goes through. If the ground before you feels soft, or wrong, or like bread, stop. Step backward, slowly, in your own footprints, or in the footprints of the person ahead. Then tell a sentry. He will thank you. He will not thank you twice.

7\. You will hear tapping in the lower levels: the Sounders, testing the floor with their rods. This is a good sound. If the tapping stops, turn round and go back the way you came. Do not ask why. Do not wait to see. They will tell you if they want you to know, and they will not want you to know.

8\. The Weighing Hall, on the third terrace, is open to visitors on every day but the Weighing itself, which falls on the first Massday of Sheafmonth. Uncover your head. When a soldier of the Compact dies his kit is weighed, and the weight is cut in the wall beside his name. Do not touch the wall. There is no room left at the height of a hand. The new names are cut higher now, and the masons use a ladder.

9\. The dead of the Peaks are buried standing, so that the mountain can carry them. You may see this done. Do not remark on it. Do not ask whether it is uncomfortable for them. A visitor asked that in the spring. He was not answered, and he left by the next cart.

10\. Prices are fixed and are not discussed. Every scale in the Bastion is checked against the Marshal's weight, so the weight you are charged is the true weight, whatever your pack did on the stair. Sovereigns are taken. Chimes are not. Promises are not.

11\. Visitors may not go on the Wall. If you find yourself on the Wall, you are no longer a visitor, and these instructions no longer apply to you. Others will.

12\. If the bell on the Wall is struck in threes, go indoors, to the nearest door that opens, and stay there. Any door in the Bastion will open to a stranger when that bell is struck. That is the oldest law in Fortis, and it has never once been broken.

13\. If a member of your party is lost within the Bastion, go to the Clerk of Visitors. The Compact will weigh what he carried, if anything is left, and cut the weight in the wall of the Weighing Hall with his name and the year, in the visitors' row by the west door. He does not have to be of the Compact. He has only to have been heavy. We have done it for carters. We have done it for a pedlar's boy. It costs you nothing. Do not offer to pay.

14\. Persons who say that these instructions are short are reminded that they are the weight they need to be.

15\. On leaving. Your pack will be lighter on the road down. This is the mountain, and not your purse. Count your purse anyway.

Heavy enough.
K. Holm, Clerk of Visitors.
