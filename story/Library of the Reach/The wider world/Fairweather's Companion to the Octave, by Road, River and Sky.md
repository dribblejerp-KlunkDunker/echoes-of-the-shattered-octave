# Fairweather's Companion to the Octave, by Road, River and Sky

*Jasper Fairweather, Gentleman, of No Fixed Abode · 414 SR (the fourth edition) · Port Cantabile, printed on Broadsheet Row · travel*

> A cheerful and not entirely trustworthy guide to every realm of the continent, revised by its author after the Cascade with as little alarm as possible.

TO THE READER OF THIS FOURTH EDITION

The first three editions of this Companion were received with a warmth I have never been able to account for. Since the third edition certain alterations have occurred in the world. I have noted them wherever a reader wrote to complain, and I have left the rest as it was, on the principle that a guide which changes every time the world does will never be finished, and must be paid for.

THE RESONANT REACH

The orchard country of the west, and the kindest country I know. The birdsong is in tune, which the visitor will find charming for very nearly a week. The oaks ring like bronze when the wind moves them, and a man felling one sounds like a bell-founder dropping his stock. Greet a Reach man with "Does it ring?" and he will answer "True enough", and you will have made a friend for the length of a cider.

At Oakhaven, take your cider at the Boar and Dulcimer, whose hearth has not gone out in ninety years because letting it go out would be an admission. Order the moot-curd, a sharp white cheese aged beside a humming stone, which tastes faintly of thunder. At Wethercombe on the sheep-walk the drovers' house will give you the best mutton of your life and the worst bed. At Stillwater Landing, pay the lock dues in advance. They are not refunded. Do not ask about the board.

The Reach murmurs since the Cascade. So should you. A gentleman who has been told to lower his voice in four realms says this with authority.

THE DAWNING HEIGHTS

Sol-Luminary, the city that hangs from the mountain on chains, was for three editions my chief recommendation, and the reader will find my old chapter on its tiers, its rose-gardens and the rising chord of its dawn reprinted unchanged at the back, because I could not bear to cut it.

At present the upper tiers are closed to visitors on account of the light, which since the year before last has been taking more than it gives. The Umbral Tiers on the city's undersides are open, and very welcoming, in a whisper. Windows there are painted black. Do not bring a mirror; it is not merely that they will fine you. Children are taught to walk in the shade of the person ahead, and I recommend the custom to adults, especially if you can find a tall friend. I travelled the whole of the lower city in the shade of a chain-yard foreman named, he said, nothing that concerned me.

CINDERHOLD

Basalt and soot, eleven tiers into a burning caldera, and the Great Beat over all of it like the heart of a sleeping giant. The people speak between the hammer-falls and greet you with "Still standing." The visitor will find himself pausing in his speech in time with the Beat for the rest of his life, which my publisher says explains a great deal about my prose. Do not drink the slag-wine. I say this as a man who drank it.

THE IRON PEAKS

At Fortis you will be asked "You're heavy?" The correct answer is "Heavy enough." Do not answer, as I did, "Very, thank you, it is the mutton." Weight wanders in the mountains since the bleeding began, and a shield may weigh eighty pounds on a wet morning and nothing at noon. Do not offer your hand to a stranger. I have never learned why, and when I asked, nobody would put a weight on the answer.

THE SHIFTING DUNES

Sandglass Bazaar sits under a dome of acoustic glass two miles across, which breaks the singing of the sand into a soft white hiss, and throws rainbows on your coffee. The greeting is "For how long?" and it is a real question; have an answer. Everything has a term in the Dunes. Marriage has a term. Grief has a term. The visitor who grieves past his term is thought indecent.

I had meant to describe the orchard-city of Veridia, on the shore of the Glass Sea, and had written half a page on its apricots, when I learned that since the Cascade it has been living the same twenty-four seconds over and over. I leave the half page in. The apricots were, I am assured, excellent, and are.

THE TEMPEST ARCHIPELAGO

Port Cantabile climbs its cliff from the Long Mole by the Salt Stair, six wet flights with shops on the landings, and every shroud in the harbour carries a chime. Greet a sailor with "What's she singing?" and he will tell you the key of the tide, and from it the hour, and from that whether you may have a drink yet. Do not run on an open deck. Do not buy a bottle with a name on the label as a souvenir, however good the price. The Free Captains read their Articles aloud in the voice of a man reading a tide-table, and enforce them in the same voice from the derrick on the Mole-head, so mind them.

THE SPECTRAL WOODS

Whisper-Grove is the most courteous city in the world. They will call you "little traveller" however large you are, and "my dear one" by the second evening, and they will mean it. Go down to the Weeping Plaza on a festival night, when the lamps are floated on the long basin under the colonnade. Go out along the Cloister road and listen at the gate of the charitable choir, where the children sing very low and very clear. I have heard nothing lovelier in my travels, and I have been about.

THE ORGANUM

There is nothing to see. That is rather the point of it. Registry Square is grey stone and brass plates and pigeons, and there is a coffee stall there that has stood above half a century, whose keeper has served the clerks every morning and has never once asked what they do. I recommend the coffee and his example.

THE CONTINENTAL RIFT

I have not been to Null-Watch, and the reader is advised to go to considerable lengths not to be sent. Since last year the wall has had something to watch. Those who have come back say the garrison never stops talking, which I had thought would suit me, until a man who had been there looked at me for some time in a tavern and said that it would not.

BY SKY

The skyship is the gentleman's road, if the gentleman has money and no particular wish to arrive. Every vessel aloft rides on a Fortis-cut core that takes the weight off her, and hangs from a tank or two of drift-glass, which wants above all things to be somewhere else. Choose a captain who laughs a good deal. You will not regret it until the moment you understand why he does.

A WORD ON COMING HOME

I am asked, by those who have read the previous editions with care, whether I have in fact been to all of these places. I have been to most of them once, and to some of them in the company of men who had been, which is nearly the same thing; and to the Reach I have been more times than I can count, because I was born there, and it is where I go when the roads are unkind.

So here is the whole of my wisdom, and it is cheaper than the book. The best inn on the continent is the one whose landlord remembers your name. Go out while there is still the whole of it to see. Then come home, and lower your voice, and tell them what you saw.

The world is wide, and since the Cascade a quarter-tone flat, and I recommend it without reserve.
