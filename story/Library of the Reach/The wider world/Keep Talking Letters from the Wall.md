# Keep Talking: Letters from the Wall

*Wystan Hobday, penal legionnaire One-Forty-Seven of Null-Watch, to his sister Meggy of Greenroot Hamlet · 415–417 SR · Null-Watch, the Continental Rift · letters*

> A Greenroot man sent to the penal legion at Null-Watch writes home to a sister who cannot read, and does not stop talking.

These were kept in the chapel at Greenroot, folded in the back of the parish book, because Meggy Hobday could not read and they were read to her there, by whoever had the reading that week.

I.
To be read to Meggy Hobday at the chapel at Greenroot, if somebody would be so kind.
Null-Watch. Lambmonth, I am told, of '15. The third hour of the night. They count from the dark here, Meggy, not from the clock.

Here I am then. I am well. They have given me a coat with lead sewn in the hem so I walk like Granda did after the cider, and a number, which is One-Forty-Seven. I may not sign my name. Nobody signs his name. They say names draw the Quiet, and you get yours back when you are discharged. So if this comes signed with a number it is still your brother, the one who took the Reeve's mutton in the hungry winter and would do it again, and you can tell the Reeve so, quietly.

The first rule here is you keep talking. That is the whole of the law, near enough. You talk all day, and in your sleep if you can manage it. About anything. Onions. The days of the week. They give you a partner and you talk at him and he talks at you, and if he stops you shout for the sergeant before you look at him. Before. That is the order. My partner is a Peaks man, number Two-Thirty, who knows more about sheep than any man ought, and I know more about reeds, so we are well matched and we bore each other half to death, which is the idea.

II.
To be read to Meggy at the chapel.
Null-Watch. Firstfruit, '15. The second hour of the night.

Every morning at dawn the whole wall fires. Forty batteries along four miles of black stone, all at once, out over the edge. It is not shot. It is noise. It is the biggest no in the world, Meggy, said every morning, and they call it the Refusal, and I have stood at a gun for it sixty mornings now and my ears ring like the oaks at home.

The General does not speak. She lost her voice on this wall the first night of the Rift. She talks with her knuckles on her breastplate, and there is a man who speaks for her, and when she strikes four flat blows it means the Watch holds the wall and everybody straightens up without knowing why.

There are monks here in lead hoods who stand along the edge and never say a word. And there is one word you must not say. It is the word for what you get in a church when the reed stops. A man said it at supper and the man beside him knocked him off the bench, and the sergeant saw it and ate his soup. That is how serious they are.

Does the little one's reed still sound over your door? Tell me it does.

III.
To be read to Meggy at the chapel.
Null-Watch. Ashfall, '15. I do not know the hour. Late.

I have been trying to write this for a week and I will just say it.

On the lip-walk, at the first hour, Two-Thirty stopped. In the middle of a ewe. He had her halfway through lambing and he stopped. I looked at him, which I should not have done first, and his face was surprised, and then it was not surprised, and then it was the face you have when you put down a heavy load at the end of the day. And he turned and went toward the edge, not fast. Like a man walking home.

I got him by the collar and I talked. I do not know what I said. I think I told him the whole of the Greenroot reed-beds, bed by bed, and who cut which, and he came back in at the end of it like a man coming out of the river, and he was angry with me. He was angry, Meggy, as if I had woken him.

There was no sound. I keep wanting to say what it sounded like. That is the thing. Nothing did.

He went to listen in Frostmonth, on a night I was in the infirmary with my hand. That is what they say here. Gone to listen. Never deserted. I have a new partner now.

IV.
To be read to Meggy at the chapel.
Null-Watch. Seedmonth, '16. The fourth hour.

Some new men up from the west this spring, one of them a Fenwick man, in for debt, who can talk about the price of lamp-oil for an hour together without drawing breath. We call him the Almanac.

We had a Discharge on Driftday. I will tell you how they do it, because you would want to know. They do not bury here and they do not weep out loud. The General lays her hand flat on the man's chest and taps on it, with one finger, his name and his place and his service, and then Discharged, and her voice says it all after her. And then the whole Watch stops talking. All of us. For three breaths. On this wall that is the most dangerous thing a man can do, and we do it on purpose, for him. And then somebody says Rootday, and his voice cracks on it, and we all say Hearthday, Massday, and on round the week, and go on.

That is how you get your name back here. I mean to get mine back the other way, at the gate, with a coat on.

V.
To be read to Meggy at the chapel.
Null-Watch. Lambmonth, '17. The third hour.

Two years. I am learning the General's cant. I can tell holds from goes, and I can tell the blow that means well done, which she has struck once at me, on the gun at the Refusal, when the breech stuck and I cleared it.

The old women at home used to say do your weeping on paper, and there is a book here from the garrison press that says it too. I have done mine. You have had all of it, I think, and so has the clerk at the Organum who reads these on the way through, and hello to him.

This is all talk, Meggy. That is the trick of the place. I will write again at Reedmonth. Kiss the little one. Hum to her. Not too loud, and not too quiet.
One-Forty-Seven, that was and is your brother.

VI.
In another hand, larger, slower.
To the sister of One-Forty-Seven, at Greenroot in the Reach, to be read to her.
Null-Watch. Reedmonth, '17.

He asked me to write if. So I am writing.

The breech of the second gun on Battery Eleven let go at the Refusal four days back. He was on it. It was quick. He was talking. He was telling the boy beside him how you cut a reed so it sounds.

The General laid her hand on him and tapped it out, and her voice said it after her, and that was the first time I ever heard his name, though I had stood next to him two years. We were quiet for him three breaths. Then I said Rootday, because I was nearest.

I am to tell you he was not afraid of the edge. That was true. I am to tell you he was well fed. That was nearly true.

He could never put it at the bottom of these. So I am putting it here, for him, where it belongs.

Wystan Hobday, of Greenroot. Discharged.

Fifty-Two
