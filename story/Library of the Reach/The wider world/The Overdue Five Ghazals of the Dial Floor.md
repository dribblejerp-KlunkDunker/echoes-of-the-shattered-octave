# The Overdue: Five Ghazals of the Dial Floor

*Nadira al-Hassur; rendered into Low Common by a copying-clerk of the Tuners' Collegium · 416 SR · Sandglass Bazaar, the Shifting Dunes · poetry*

> Five linked poems by a widow of the Sandglass Bazaar who would not stop mourning when the contract said she must.

THE COPYIST'S NOTE

A ghazal is a chain of couplets, each ending on the same word or words, and in the last couplet the poet speaks to herself by name. Nadira al-Hassur's husband, Tahir, a caravan-factor, was lost on the Hourglass Erg in the Screaming Year. The term of mourning for a husband in the Bazaar is three hundred days. She kept them, and then she did not stop, and in 415 her House posted her as overdue. These five were passed from hand to hand on the Dial Floor. I have kept her refrains where Low Common would bear them, and let them limp where it would not.

I. FOR HOW LONG

They greet me on the Dial Floor and ask me, as they ask everyone: for how long?
The coffee-seller asks it, pouring. The dome hisses it all day: for how long?
Three hundred days is the term for a husband. I kept them to the hour.
On the three hundred and first I woke, and the pillow said: for how long?
The sand outside the glass was singing on the morning you rode out.
A tritone, my father would have said, and turned the camels round. It did not ask for how long.
All my life I have answered every question with a number.
Ask me again, neighbour. Watch my mouth, and see what it does with: for how long?
Nadira, they have written you overdue. Let them. Let the term run out.
In the sealed rooms under the Bazaar the clocks run backward, and nobody asks them for how long.

II. ON SCHEDULE

My sister came to me with her grief on schedule and went home on schedule.
She wept the proper days, and on the proper morning dried her eyes on schedule.
The House sent a clerk with a paper, a kind man, a little bald,
to read me the clause. He was sorry. He was sorry on schedule.
In the Stasis Vaults your crate holds the hour they carried you in from the Erg.
There is sand in your beard. Nothing in that crate is kept to a schedule.
Each Nullday I go down the forty steps with a lamp.
The warden lets me in and says I am the only visitor he has who is not on schedule.
The hour-merchants on the Floor would buy a year of my life for a fair price.
I could sell them every year I have and still be weeping behind schedule.
Nadira, an overdue woman is a scandal, an open-ended thing, a foreigner.
Good. In the west, they say, the orchard people mourn with no term at all. I will learn their schedule.

III. STILL

They sealed you at the moment you were set down, and you are lying still.
In there it is always that one afternoon, and you are warm, and still.
I lay my palm on the glass of the lid. It is not cold. It is not anything.
The stasis-wright says not to. It upsets the seal. I keep it there still.
Your hand is on your chest where the caravan-master put it.
A grain of sand is falling from your sleeve. It has not landed. It is falling still.
They tell me that a crate opened after its term lets the hour go all at once:
every hour you missed, arriving together. I would rather have you still.
Nadira, you have laid a man down like dial-wine, fifty years in an afternoon.
They say the wine tastes hollow. Everyone buys it. Everyone drinks it still.

IV. UNTIL THE TERM

On Dial Day every contract in the province ends at once, and everyone says: until the term.
The Floor is a riot of paper and the shouting of renewals. Until the term.
I went to the crate-house with the renewal in my hand and the fee in my purse.
I had signed it twice. Three years, and three years more. Until the term.
The clerk held out the pen. The pen waited. The whole dome seemed to wait,
the way the sand waits between one chord and the next. Until the term.
I gave him back the pen. I said: let it run. I said it to him and not to you.
He bowed, as they bow at doors when a guest goes out. Until the term.
Nadira, you who were never late for anything in your life,
you were late for this, and then on time. Go home. Until the term.

V. NONE

At the hour the lid came up, and the afternoon went out of the crate like a bird out of a sleeve.
Three years crossed your face in a breath. Then you were only a man at rest, and the term was none.
The grain of sand landed. I heard it. I am the only one who did.
We put you in the Erg at dusk, the old way, with the dunes holding a fifth. Their price was none.
My sister says: now. Now, surely, the mourning is done with the crate.
I tell her the crate had a term. I signed for it. The grief I never signed for has none.
Let the House post me. Let them write me into the book of the indecent.
I have read that book. It is full of the names of people who loved past the date, and its index is none.
Nadira, they call you open-ended, overdue, a foreigner in your own Bazaar.
Write it on the contract, then. Under how long, write: none.
