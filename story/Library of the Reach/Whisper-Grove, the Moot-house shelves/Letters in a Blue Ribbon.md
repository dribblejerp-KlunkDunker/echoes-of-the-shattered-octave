# Letters in a Blue Ribbon

*Cressida Tilney, copy-clerk to the Moot, and Corbet Ansty, journeyman pipe-voicer; bound by her · 414–416 SR · Whisper-Grove and the Organum · letters*

> Two years of letters between a Moot clerk in Whisper-Grove and a Grove-born pipe-voicer working in the Organum, and a proposal she will only answer aloud.

**Flyleaf:** Cressida Tilney,Corbet Ansty

The Organum, Reedmonth 414. Cressida. I am here and I am alive and I have not been run over by a Justiciar, though it was close on the second day. You will want to know what it is like. It is loud. Not the way the Grove is loud on a Moot night, all of us murmuring at once; loud the way the inside of a bell is loud. The towers are pipes and the streets are ranks, and when the Synod speaks the whole city plays, and you feel it in your back teeth and in the soles of your boots. I am in the east rank under a master who has voiced pipes for forty years and thinks I am an idiot. You said you might write. Might is a word the Moot uses when it means no. Corbet.

Whisper-Grove, Longsun 414. Corbet Ansty. I am writing, which is not the same as might and not the same as yes. The Moot sat nine hours on Massday over the paint on the colonnade, whether white or the old grey, and I copied every word, and at the end they agreed to sit again. I enclose nothing. You may imagine a pressed willow-leaf if it helps. C. T.

The Organum, Firstfruit 414. C. T. Thank you for the willow-leaf. It is the finest I have ever imagined. You asked, or you did not ask but I will tell you, what a voicer does. A pipe is made to speak one note. It does not know which until you tell it, and you tell it by cutting the mouth, a hair at a time, and listening, and cutting again. Every pipe has one note it was made for, and when you have found it you know, because it stops arguing. My master says I cut too fast. He is right. I think it is because I am homesick. I miss the quiet. I miss the pools showing me somebody's breakfast. Will you do a thing for me? On the Lantern Night, set one for my grandfather, Old Ansty of the Cloister road. I cannot get leave, and somebody ought to say him. Corbet.

Whisper-Grove, Ashfall 414. Corbet. I set it. I said his name on the basin, and the people beside me said it after me, and so did the people beside them, a long way down the water, so your grandfather is now a public matter and will be discussed. It was a good lantern. I made it myself, and it went out straight and did not tip. Canopy was quiet. We scored the loaf at home and I lit the candle facing in. I have kept your letters, all four. Do not let that go to your head; I keep the Moot's minutes too. C.

The Organum, Stillmonth 415. Cressida Tilney. I have voiced eleven pipes since I wrote last and every one of them stopped arguing. I have got better at listening. So I am going to say it, as plainly as I can, in ink, because ink is what I have. Marry me. Corbet.

Whisper-Grove, Thawmonth 415. Corbet. No. Read the rest of this before you throw yourself off a tower. A thing written in a letter can be taken back; it is only ink, and ink is cheap, I buy it for the Moot by the jar. In the Grove a thing that matters is said aloud, on the water, in front of other people, so that it is heard and kept. You know this. You were named on a pool like the rest of us. So come home and say it on the basin steps, aloud, and I will answer you there, aloud, and the whole Grove may say it after us if it likes. That is not no, you fool. It is not yet. C.

The Organum, Seedmonth 415. C. My master will not give me leave until my journeyman year is out, which by his count is Sheafmonth of next year, and by mine is a hundred years. Will you wait? I have bought nothing yet, because I do not know what you would like, and because if I buy it I will look at it every night and cut my pipes too fast. Corbet.

Whisper-Grove, Dustmonth 415. Corbet. I will wait, and I will not be gracious about it. News, since you are homesick. The fuller's girl in the Ashlane had twins, and the second would not breathe until the first shouted at it, and the whole lane is telling it, and Grisel Imrie is pretending she is not proud. The lamp-men voted to keep lighting, which they do every year, and Old Lowry made a speech, which he does every year. The pools are high. I copy the Moot and I read your letters on the gallery at noon, where the light is good. Nobody up there can see what I am reading, which is just as well, because it is mostly the same letter. C.

The Organum, Lambmonth 416. Cressida. The city played for three hours yesterday because the Synod was speaking, and I stood in the rank with my hands over my ears like a boy and could not hear a single word of what it said. You never can. That is the thing about this place: everything is said aloud and nobody can make it out. In the Grove you can hear a person drop a spoon across the plaza, and you always know whose spoon it was. I have been away too long. Corbet.

The Organum, Firstfruit 416. C. T. My master has signed my papers. He shook my hand and told me I still cut too fast and that I will be a good voicer in about thirty years. I have bought a ribbon, blue, because you said once that you liked the blue ones on the Moot rolls, and I bought it without looking at it so as not to cut my pipes too fast. I will be home before Canopy. I will come down the Moot-house steps and stand on the basin's edge and say it, aloud, in front of the whole Grove, and you will have to answer aloud, Cressida Tilney, and everybody will hear. Corbet.

On the last leaf, in her hand: Bound by me, Sheafmonth 416, with the ribbon off my good hat, so that I shall have them all in one place when he comes, and can read him back every word if he tries to take any of it back. He is due before Canopy. I have been practising my answer up in the muniment store, where nobody can hear me. It is one word. I have had it ready since Reedmonth of 414.
