# The Little Grove Primer

*set for the Moot schools of Whisper-Grove; written in by two children of the Carters' Yard · 413 SR (new edition, with the Lesson for Quiet); written in until 416 · printed at Whisper-Grove for the Moot schools · children's*

> The alphabet, a counting rhyme and the courtesies every Grove child learned, in a copy passed from an older sister to her little brother, with their pencil in the margins.

**Flyleaf:** Blanche Clough,Wilmot Clough

On the flyleaf, in a careful round hand: This book belongs to Blanche Clough, of the Carters' Yard, Whisper-Grove, the Spectral Woods, the World. Under it, in pencil, much larger: AND NOW TO WILMOT CLOUGH BECAUSE BLANCHE IS TOO BIG FOR IT.

THE LITTLE GROVE PRIMER. Letters, Counting and Courtesies, for the Small Ones of the Moot Schools. New edition, with the Lesson for Quiet.

To the dame, or the mother, or the big sister: go slowly. A letter a day is plenty. Say each one aloud, softly, and let the small one say it after you. That is how the Grove has always learned anything.

THE LETTERS.

A is the Ashlane, steep as a stair; say good morning to all who are there. B is the Basin, long and still, with lanterns upon it, as many as will. C is the Canopy, taller than tall; it keeps the whole Grove, the great and the small.

D is for Dearling, and so are you. Beside it, in Blanche's pencil: NOT WILMOT. Beside that, in a worse pencil: IS.

E is the Evening, the lamps are alight; the lighter goes round, and the Grove says goodnight. F is the Fountain: look, but don't paddle. G is the Grove, and the Grove is home. H is your Hand: give it to whoever is smaller.

I is the Ink, and you will spill it. J is the Jug, and you will spill that too. K is the Kettle; ask before you touch. L is the Lamp-man: if you are lost, wait by his lamp. M is the Moot-house, where the grown people talk, and talk, and talk.

N is your Name. Say it clearly when you are asked, and ask other people theirs.

O is for Others; there are always others. P is the Pool, which remembers. You may look at what it shows you, but do not stare, for it is somebody else's and you would not like them staring at yours. Q is for Quiet, the gentle kind. R is the Rain on the willows.

S is for Singing, as soft as you can. T is for Thank you, said every time. U is for Up the steps, and no running. V is your Voice: keep your small one for indoors. W is the Willow, silver and long.

X is the mark that grown people make when they cannot write their names. You will never need it. Y is for Yesterday, which the Grove keeps for you. Z is the sound of a small one asleep.

COUNTING, for the plaza steps. One step, two steps, down to the plaza; three steps, four steps, hold your sister; five, six, the basin's brim; seven, eight, don't fall in; nine, ten, up again; and eleven lamps in the Bell Yard, lit one by one, and the lamp-man says goodnight when he is done.

COURTESIES. When you meet an old one in the lane, say: good morning, little mother; good morning, little father. When you are given a thing, say: you are kind. When you do not know a person's name, ask it. It is not rude in the Grove to ask a name. It is rude not to.

IF YOU ARE LOST. Do not run. Stand under the nearest lamp, because the lighter will come by. When a grown person asks you, say three things, clearly: your name, your street, and your mother's name. Then you will be home before your supper is cold.

THE LESSON FOR QUIET, new in this edition by order of the Moot. Since the Screaming Year there are things beyond the Woods that come to loud singing. They have not come here. We teach this so that they never need to. Skip it in the yard until you know it: Too loud brings the hounds, too still brings the Hush. So sing in the middle, and sing it soft, and never sing in a rush.

And this, which is harder. If somebody calls your name from the dark or the mist, and you cannot see their face, do not answer. Not even if it sounds like your mam. Go to the nearest lamp, and wait for the lighter, and he will take you home, and your mam will not be cross.

In the margin, in Blanche's pencil: Wilmot answered Mam from the privy and got smacked. It WAS Mam.

NOW WRITE YOUR OWN NAME, as big and as clear as you can, so that everyone will know whose book this is.

BLANCHE CLOUGH, in letters as straight as a fence. Below it, WILMOT CLOUGH, with the C the wrong way round and then crossed out and done again the right way, and the G very large, and a drawing of a lamp on its iron arm.

Well done, dearling. A child who knows her letters, and her name, and her street, can always be brought home.
