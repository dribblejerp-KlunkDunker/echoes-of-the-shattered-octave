# Review: the Moor serial against canon

Reviewed 26 October 2026. Subject: `js/books.js`, commit `9167988`. It contains two pamphlets, *The Things Taken Home* (part 1) and *The Gate Book* (part 2, marked [PROPOSED]). It also adds a bookseller and a donated bundle in the Boar & Dulcimer, and a lending shelf in Lyra's house.

**Checked against:**

- the Series Bible (canon);
- World Bible Vols I–VII, with the later rulings in Vol III, III(b) and VII applied over Vols I–II;
- the four Source Material files;
- the Book One manuscript, Book Two, Book Three and Book Four Part One;
- the game's own data.

**Nothing was edited.** These are findings and suggested fixes for the owner to rule on. Line numbers are approximate.

**Precedence:**

- The Series Bible and the later volumes outrank the Source Material drafts.
- Where a draft disagrees with the game and the Series Bible, it is listed under "overruled" at the end, not as a finding.

**Timing:** the Rite falls on Hearthday 16 Harvestmonth 419 (Vol VII(b) line 28). The pamphlets are therefore on sale in Book One's Oakhaven, in the fortnight before the Rite. Several findings follow from that.

---

## Fix before players see it

### 1. "Silver pieces" are not a coin in this world
- **Serial:** "nineteen silver pieces"; "offered him a silver piece".
- **Canon:**
  - The coins are the Sovereign (Synod-minted, rings at 528 Hz) and the Chime, an old provincial coin (Vol I §4.4, line 369).
  - The books also use pence, "old coin" and Consortium scrip. Oakhaven's works pay in "green and black" scrip (Book One, ch. 4, line 139).
  - No source has silver coinage.
  - The seller in the same file charges sovereigns, so the file contradicts itself.
- **Fix:** Hessa's nineteen coins become **scrip** if her husband worked for the works, which fits best. Otherwise make them **sovereigns** or **pence**, and keep the arithmetic: nineteen, six set aside, one offered.

### 2. "Moor" is already Hennet Moor, with the same story beat
- **Serial:** Jory Moor refuses to sign a statement and is taken away.
- **Canon:** Hennet Moor, a Veil scribe, was "pressed at the Malting in the harvest of 416… for refusing to sign." Sources: Vol V §3, line 151, and Book Four Part One, around lines 1120, 1368 and 1596.
- **Why it matters:** readers who know Book Four will read the serial as Hennet's family, or as a clue.
- **Fix:** rename the family and the serial. Or rule that the echo is deliberate and record it in the ledger.

### 3. "Jory" is the Synod courier Jory Quill, and "dispatch" points straight at him
- **Serial:** Jory, moved under a "dispatch number".
- **Canon:**
  - Jory Quill, the Static Remnant, carries "a sealed Arbiterium dispatch" (Series Bible, line 339).
  - Book One has an Office file reading "*courier Quill, J., in transit*" (around line 5634).
  - The game also already has a Jory Tace and a Jory Fenn (Phase 17 text).
- **Fix:** rename Jory. Check the replacement too; Joss and Tam are taken. Replace "dispatch number" with **docket number**, the Office's own term (Vol VII §8, line 163).

### 4. "Pell" is used by four people
- **Bookseller:** Iven Pell, a travelling pamphlet seller.
- **Canon:**
  - **Rowena Pell** is the Reach's alchemist (Vol II, line 44).
  - **Cosimo Pell** is a Cantabile broadsheet-man who "published the Whisper-Grove story" (Vol II, line 314). A Pell who sells printed tales reads as his brother.
  - **Pell** is a Mole crewwoman in Book Two.
  - **Pellam Quist** is Registrar-General of the Office of the Catalogue (Book Three Part Two, line 471). A man selling a story about record clerks shares a name with the Office's head.
- **Fix:** rename the bookseller. Or make him Cosimo's courier on purpose, which would also solve finding 9.

### 5. The paperwork gives away Book One's twist
- **Serial:** a man is logged as "EQUIPMENT RETURNED FOR ASSESSMENT", moved "one attendant, two escort", with "HOLD FOR REWEIGH".
- **Canon:**
  - In this same fortnight, the Office ships people as cores to the Oakhaven works: "Consignee: Oakhaven Works… Carriers who report hearing the consignment will not be engaged again" (Book One, around line 2564).
  - Residuals travel "under escort" (Book Three Part Two, around line 601).
  - Vol II schedules this reveal for late in Book One (line 584).
- **Why it matters:** sold openly in Oakhaven, the serial reads as an exposé or a spoiler. Players will treat it as a quest clue, despite the frame line "not a current parish case".
- **Fix:** the owner should decide whether this is intended allegory. If it isn't, make the works plainly a mine or foundry and drop "equipment", "reweigh" and the escort counts.

### 6. The door beneath the house reads as the Hush
- **Serial:** "The door beneath the house… We had no cellar." Jory "heard him", and an engineer arrives with questions already written.
- **Canon:**
  - In Oakhaven, a missing relative's voice from an impossible place is the taken calling: "*Answer it once and you'll answer no more*" (Book One, around lines 444–448).
  - Book One ends with the thing coming back through a drain into Lyra's house (around line 6437).
  - Oakhaven's old stair is "a slab with an iron ring" (Vol V, line 205).
- **Hush rules:** none are broken, because the door is only reported, never shown.
- **Fix:** confirm it is meant as a Hush echo. If not, make the door plainly mundane.

## Should fix

### 7. Three sovereigns is six weeks' wages in the books
- **Canon prices:**
  - A deckhand earns "two sovereigns a month and his keep" (Book Three Part One, line 547).
  - Glass shot is "a sovereign a pellet" (Series Bible, line 86).
  - Lamp-oil is "four pence the pint".
- **Clash:** the seller's own "Keep your supper money" doesn't fit a price that size.
- **Game side:** within the game's own economy, 3 is cheap. A tonic costs 5, and Hetty pays 20 for one errand. The game's prices already run higher than the books', so this is partly a game-wide issue.
- **Fix:** if the game's economy stays as it is, keep 3 and drop "supper money". To match the books, charge 1, or sell it for pence or scrip if the game ever adds them.

### 8. "Aldren" sits next to Aldric Vance
- **Serial:** in "Aldren's letter", a father asks his daughter to leave him.
- **Canon:** Aldric Vance is Oakhaven's governor and Kaelen's father. He signs "A." (Book One, around line 636). At the rope, he "looks up and waves them on" (Series Bible, line 505).
- **Why it matters:** sold in Oakhaven, the letter reads as a comment on Aldric. Alder Mossbrook (Vol II, line 42) is close as well.
- **Fix:** choose a name that doesn't start with "Ald".

### 9. "Varek, the adjudicator" sounds like Supreme Arbiter Vaelen
- **Canon:**
  - V-names are already crowded: Vaelen, Vane, Vesper and Vance.
  - Law belongs to the Arbiterium and its Justiciars.
  - Office records carry initials, never names: "the Office did not put names on procedural documents, only an initial" (Book One, around line 4862).
- **Fix:** rename Varek with another initial. Make him a Justiciar or the Consortium's factor, and print him as an initial in the copied record.

### 10. Lyra's lending shelf outlives Lyra's house
- **Canon:**
  - From Memoryday, two Church sisters occupy the house. "The chosen voice is not worn out on ordinary speech" (Book One, line 2836).
  - In the game, once the Rite starts, the house is sealed with a proctor's seal and Lyra is "taken into the Church's protection".
  - After the Greening the house is in an oak crown (Vol V, line 170).
- **Game side:** the shelf is placed on every visit and can always be used.
- **On the word "parish":** the game already uses it. Lyra's own character text says "every child in the parish", and `js/parish.js` exists. That makes it acceptable in game lore.
  - The books use "parish" once, for a Church choir.
  - The old Cantor craft is "practical, agricultural, un-theological" (Vol III A.1, line 22).
  - "A printed tale, not a current parish case" casts Lyra as handling missing-person cases.
- **Fix:** call it **the Cantor's shelf** (or Tobin's). Hide it once the Rite starts, or once the sisters arrive. Change the frame line to "A printed tale, not a true account".

### 11. Who prints this, under the Flambeaux warrant?
- **Canon:**
  - The only free press is Cantabile's Broadsheet Row (Vol I, line 289).
  - Broadsheets reach the Reach smuggled through Stillwater Landing (Vol II, line 36).
  - From Dawnday Day 6, Oakhaven is under the Flambeaux warrant, and the Flambeaux burn "the inconvenient" (Series Bible, lines 241 and 493).
- **Clash:** a tale about a coerced statement and a vanished prisoner is sold openly at a tavern table.
- **Fix:** make it a Cantabile serial that came in through Stillwater. Have the seller keep it under the counter and say so.

### 12. The institutions have no owner in canon
- **Terms with no basis anywhere:** "compensation office", "assessment office", "adjudicator", "infirmary", "dispatch number", "factory yards" and "eastern works".
- **Canon's terms:**
  - The Consortium runs industry, with "a resident factor at the Oakhaven works" (Series Bible, line 375).
  - The Arbiterium runs law.
  - The Office runs records, in dockets, consignments, carriage and the registry.
  - "East" in canon means the Rift.
- **Fix:** borrow canon's words, or set the tale clearly somewhere named. Keep the gatekeeper plainly outside Oakhaven, whose gate is old Harrow's. The works gate has a gatehouse clerk and Legionnaires (Book One, around line 1707).

### 13. Nobody in the serial lives in the Narrow
- **Canon:** "Every scene knows how loud it is." Rooms keep hush-lamps and people murmur (Series Bible, lines 58 and 148).
- **Serial:**
  - A driver "called for the gate", and rooms have plain lamps.
  - Glass and the Cacophony never appear.
  - The axe doesn't ring, though Reach timber "rings like a bronze bell" (Vol II, line 18).
- **Fix:** add one murmur or hush-lamp moment. Or set the tale outside siphoned country and say where.

### 14. Hessa and Hetty in the same tavern
- **Clash:** the serial's heroine is Hessa ("Hess"). The bundle is left by Hetty Bram, in the same room.
- **Fix:** rename Hessa.

## Style and polish

15. **The transcript format isn't the world's.**
    - "MOOR: … / VAREK: …" speaker labels appear in no book.
    - Official records are terse italics with initials: "*Hale, D. Carter. Dissonant: self-inflicted. No further action.*"
    - **Fix:** render Esser's illicit copy that way, or have him say it breaks form.
16. **Hetty writes in capitals.**
    - Her board is in "square white capitals" (Book One, ch. 4, line 80).
    - **Fix:** render her note as "PAID FOR. TAKE ONE OF EACH. LEAVE THE LIBRARY ITS COPIES."
17. **"Hauler" should be "carter".** The books say "carters and quarrymen".
18. **"The Moor serial"** can read as moorland, or as the real-world ethnonym. A series title avoids that.
19. **Part 1 opens mid-story.** It assumes the box, the handcart and "both of them" (the forms). Give it a one-line preface.
20. **The pamphlets reuse the sprite of Tobin's journal page,** a key canon item, so they look like clues on the floor. They should get their own small pamphlet icon.
21. **The bookseller uses the same townsman sprite** as a patron three tiles away at the same table. Give him another body or a hat.

## Inside the serial

22. **Who split the wood.**
    - Part 1 has "someone was splitting firewood". Hessa then looks out at "The axe outside struck wood" and sees only children and an old woman.
    - Part 2 introduces "the woman who had been splitting wood".
    - **Fix:** make her the old woman, or put the splitter out of sight.
23. **The gate logging.** Esser says wagons "are logged when they leave", yet the keeper logs arrivals too. **Fix:** "logged in and out".
24. **The scrap.** Part 2's traveller carries "a separate scrap" with the number, but Part 1 never says who copied it. **Fix:** add one line in Part 1.
25. **Timing.** Jory reaches the infirmary "that morning", is examined, and leaves on "the afternoon hand" the same day. That is tight but possible. **Fix:** move the dispatch to the next day, or accept it.

## Checked and consistent

- **Units and transport:** miles and yards are fine, and so are wagons, mares and coal.
- **Time:** the watches and "lost a morning" fit, and nothing misuses a weekday or a date. Harvestmonth autumn suits the season.
- **Hush rules:** nothing is shown and nobody screams. A lost voice heard through a door fits the mimic's habits.
- **Tone:** the euphemistic paperwork, the forms and the snooping in records match the Office's register, where "banality is the horror". The small kindnesses and Esser's "Because I was afraid" fit the Dread Doctrine.
- **Inheritance rule:** nobody dies, so "no death without an heir" isn't touched.
- **Tavern:** Hetty Bram, the Boar & Dulcimer, its rain, and Hetty writing a note are all canon. Literacy and printing exist in-world.
- **New names:** Hessa, Esser and Varek appear nowhere else (but see findings 9 and 14).
- **Internal arithmetic:** the serial's coin count, escort count, watches, "both papers" and "I heard you, Hess" all agree with themselves.
- **Code:** the books test passes. Reading is free at the shelf, inventory reading doesn't use up the item, and each donated copy can be taken only once.

## Drafts overruled by later canon (not findings)

The Source Material drafts disagree with the game in several places. The Series Bible and the game win, so these are noted only:

- **Lyra as a travelling field healer, and Oakhaven collapsing in Act I.** The game and the books keep her in Oakhaven as its Cantor.
- **No parish structure.** The game uses "parish" for Lyra's charge.
- **No month names.** Vol VII supplies Harvestmonth.

## What this review can't show

- **How the pamphlets read in play.** Whether players treat them as live clues, and whether three sovereigns feels right, needs a real playtest.
- **Coverage.** The novels were searched for each name and term and read around every hit. They were not read end to end.

## Resolution (27 October 2026)

The owner asked for the serial to be brought into canon. Every finding above is resolved in `js/books.js`, `test/books.test.js`, `locales/story/fr.json` and `tools/i18n/p26-books.tsv`. The prose of both installments is kept; only names, terms and a few lines change.

**The ruling that settles most findings.** The serial is now a **Cantabile broadsheet tale of the Caldera**. It was printed on Broadsheet Row and carried up the river through Stillwater, the canon route for broadsheets. The frame line reads: "Printed on Broadsheet Row, Port Cantabile · installment N of 2. A tale of the Caldera; the names are changed."

- A works collapse belongs in Cinderhold's industrial tiers, not in the Reach (Vol I, lines 229–231).
- That keeps the tale out of Oakhaven's institutions, the Narrow and Hush lore.
- It answers who printed it (finding 11) and makes the echoes deliberate. The free press telling true things as fiction fits canon's Row: "all libellous, one occasionally correct."
- The tale names no real Furnace House, so no real House gains an invented collapse. Canon already has Tier Three's dead at the Thermal Margin.
