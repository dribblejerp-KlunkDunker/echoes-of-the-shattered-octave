# Phase 125: who the traveller is, and "in their boots" (design, for ruling)

24 November 2026. Audit item 2. Ruled by the owner on 23 Nov as (a), with "in their boots": the traveller is their own person, a stranger to Oakhaven, with a history that belongs to their calling; at the book's great moments the player steps into the hero whose moment it is and steps back out.

Nothing is built. Two readers went through the books first; their briefs are beside this file (`Phase 125 - lore brief, the four great moments.md`, `Phase 125 - lore brief, the six callings.md`). The lines this design leans on were checked against the manuscript and the Series Bible. **The books change the plan in four places, and two of them are the owner's to rule.**

## 1. What the books change

### 1.1 Five of the six callings are a hero's own title

The game's six callings were written from the Series Bible's cast list, which gives each hero a title:

| The game's calling | The Series Bible | So the calling is |
|---|---|---|
| Dissonant Blade | "Kaelen Vance — The Dissonant Blade" (l. 302) | Kaelen's own name. One man. |
| Cantor | Lyra, "Cantor of the Living Chord" (l. 312) | A real trade; Lyra holds Oakhaven's office. |
| Corsair | Vane, "Corsair of the Rust Coast" (l. 322) | A real trade: the Free Captains' air-crews. |
| Archivist of the Veil | Sable, "Archivist of the Veil" (l. 330); "Sable is the last Veil-sworn scribe, conscripted" (ll. 42, 334) | Sable's own name. The bible says there is no other. |
| Flambeau Penitent | Crane, "Flambeau of the Synod" (l. 340); "the Church's penitents" (l. 292) | The Flambeaux are real and are several chapters; "penitent" is the bible's word for the Church's followers. |
| Quarry-Hand | no hero | A real workforce. |

A Cantor, a Corsair, a follower of the Flambeaux and a quarry hand can each be a stranger. **A second "Dissonant Blade" and a second "Archivist of the Veil" cannot, by the bible's own words.** That is question 1 below.

### 1.2 Vane has one great moment, not two

"The count to ten at the rope" and "the ten heartbeats" are the same scene: chapter 12, Grab the Line. "It happened the way it always happens: slowly, and then in about ten heartbeats." (manuscript l. 3724), then the numerals One to Ten, then "Vane cut the line" (l. 3774). Chapter 21 is *titled* "Ten Heartbeats" and counts nothing; in it Vane says "Don't cut it." (l. 5582). The game's silent count under the cutter (`js/cutter68.js`) is the game's own and is already marked [PROPOSED]. **Decided (the decisions were handed over on 23 Nov): the cutter stays as it is, the traveller's own scene; Vane's boots are the rope.**

### 1.3 Lyra sings the coda herself, and its words are not given

In chapter 23 her voice is back and she sings the close alone. The telling in which the traveller sings it because she has no voice has no footing in the book. The book does not give the coda's words. **Decided, as already recorded on 23 Nov: the traveller-sings telling goes; the player sings the coda in Lyra's boots; no words are invented for it.**

### 1.4 The likenesses and descriptions say things the books contradict

Today's six likenesses carry the six book people's own histories as "you", and several lines contradict the books besides (the reader's list, section 8 and 9 of the callings brief): the hounds "treat you as one of their own" (they would not go near the hut, l. 484); "three shifts", "foremen" and "the ladder" at the quarry (the books have two shifts, a bore-master and a cage; and the men at the pumps are Hollows: "A dozen of them, in grey smocks ... None of them looked at anything", l. 1777); "the old fourth line" of the offices (not in any book). All of it is replaced by the text below, so it goes without a separate fix. **The audit's own table of what each calling could do was written before this reading and is corrected here:** no "men at the pumps by name" for the quarry hand; no "old offices and their fourth lines" for the cantor.

**Good news on the faces.** The six likeness pictures (`img/face_1.jpg` to `face_6.jpg`) are their own paintings and do not look like the six heroes' portraits. They stay. Only their names and histories change, so this phase does not wait on the ruling about Faces of their own.

## 2. Who the traveller is, for each calling [PROPOSED]

Each is a stranger who came up the road before the Church did (the Church arrives on the sixth day). Each avoids every named person of that trade in the books; the lines that make each one safe are in the callings brief. Names and wording are for the build; these are the shapes.

| Calling | The stranger | Safe because | Must not be |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The glassed hand** (now "Dissonant Blade") | A bore-hand from another Consortium works who took raw seam glass in an accident: a hand and forearm at the early stage, not Dawning glass, not "set". Sent to Oakhaven as a replacement for the lost shifts. | Crane says "men like you" in the plural (l. 2341); the Series Bible lets raw glass take flesh anywhere; the works have lost "two shifts" and are hiring. | Of the Dawning; a Foundry survivor; "Consecrated" in the Church's eyes as Kaelen is (l. 2343); able to hear Kaelen's glass as kin (that is for two pieces of the same Spire). |
| **Cantor** | A cantor of another parish, trained by an old orchard-cantor and not at Reedside, who walked in on the Greenroot road. | The orchard-cantors are plural in Book One; no book counts the Reach's cantors. | Cantor of Oakhaven (Lyra's office); a pupil of Ilse Brennan; in on the Cantors' Secret (a later book's). |

## 3. The great moments, as the book gives them

The full beats, with every line, are in the moments brief. In a hero's boots the player can only do what the hero did, well or badly; a slip is shown grey, as the Rite's Echoes already are, and never changes what happens.

| Whose | The moment | Where | The hands on screen | What the boots must never do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaelen** | The needle | ch. 10, ll. 3040-3110 | The glass thumb pinching skin; the wire through skin only. | Put the wire through her voice. Steady his hands by will: they shake. Know yet what Stage 9 is for. |
| **Kaelen** | The eight bolts | ch. 10, ll. 3172-3228 | The glass hand on the lock. | Warn Tobiah beyond "You'll want to hold onto something." |
| **Lyra** | The Hold | ch. 10, ll. 3114-3242 | Her hands, and one note at 174, "not a hair off". | Speak, beyond "I. Am. Fortunate." Break the note before the cue. |
| **Lyra** | The coda | ch. 23, ll. 6008-6154 | Her hands inside the ring of lamps. | Leave the ring past the last lamp. Stop singing. Cover her ears. Forgive Kaelen on screen. |
| **Vane** | One to Ten | ch. 12, ll. 3724-3774 | His hands on the line and the knife; Mags reading his hands and mouth. | Save the five. Hesitate past Ten. Laugh. |
| **Sable** | The three names | ch. 16, ll. 4552-4572 | The Ledger open and unlooked-at. | Read them in any order but Corwin Aye, Mirren Salk, Annet Sowerby. Look down. |

The book's Hold lasts minutes, not hours; the game's scene keeps the book's length. Whether Crane sees the needle is the game's invention (the book: he never does) and comes out.

**It follows the calling** [PROPOSED]: every player steps into all of these; the traveller of the matching trade also gets a line before and after that only they would notice (the glassed hand hears the lock; the cantor hears how true the note is; the corsair counts with Vane; the clerk knows the form Sable is not filling in).

## 4. Questions for the owner

1. **Two calling names belong to one person each.** "The Dissonant Blade" is Kaelen and "Archivist of the Veil" is Sable, and the bible says Sable is the last. Recommended: keep the two callings and what they play like, give them a trade's name, and keep the titles for the two heroes. Proposed names: **Glass-Hand** and **Catalogue Clerk** [PROPOSED]. The other way is to keep the names and say in the game that the traveller is called so loosely, which leaves a second "last Veil-sworn scribe" in the world.
2. **"Flambeau Penitent."** It can stay: the bible uses "penitents" for the Church's followers. Say if you would rather it were plainer.

Everything else here is decided or is [PROPOSED] under the standing acceptance, and can be struck from this record.
