## The wall and the Watch

- B3P1:663 "STANDING ORDER NINE. A trooper whose partner falls silent will sound the alarm at once. He will not first look to see why." Painted over every door, at eye height. Backstory rule, in force in Harvestmonth 419.
- B3:723-737 (land approach, 4th day = ~18 Ashfall, by the Watch's own pitch): grass "stopped"; a pitchless dry cold east wind that blows with no sound in the rigging. Establishes the approach land and the silent wind. Later (Ashfall).
- B3P1:745-755 "a band of something that was not dark and not light and not any colour he had a name for." The not-colour: looking at it makes you forget you were looking; seen as the far edge of the land. Standing condition, so also Harvestmonth 419.
- B3P1:755 "In front of it, on the edge of the land, black against the not-colour, a line of walls." The wall stands on the edge of the land.
- B3P1:767 "Obsidian... blocks the size of wagons... mortar... dull grey, and it was lead." Wall of black glass blocks laid course on course, lead mortar, laced inside and out with grey tungsten cable from iron eyes in slack loops and diagonals "like a net".
- B3P1:767 "Every two hundred yards there was a squat round tower, forty of them along four miles of wall, and on every tower, under a lead hood, a gun." Dampening batteries: brass flared mouths nine feet across. Numbers: 40 towers, 4 miles, 200 yards apart (see Contradictions: 4 vs 40 miles).
- B3P1:771-785 everyone on every wall-walk, tower and stair talks, day and night, in pairs, in lead-plated long coats "like people wading"; paired watchers sit by bunks and talk to sleepers. Talk topics: onions, cable-eyes counted, the days of the week. Standing practice.

- B3P1:993 "The only men on the Watch allowed to be silent. They had taken vows and covered their mouths with lead... When one of them began to walk, very slowly, toward the edge, the ones on either side took hold of his arms." Shifts day and night. They listen for the Watch. Standing custom.

## Contradictions inside this file

1. Wall length: "four miles" (767, 1137, 1143) against "forty miles of wall" on the General's map (1864, 2102) and "forty miles" gash (813). With 40 towers at 200 yards the wall is about 4.5 miles, so 4 is right; "forty miles of wall" is probably an error.
2. Mooring-yard position: below the wall-walk at Five/Six (859, 857) and "on the west side of the Watch... as far from the lip as the Watch goes" (1872, 2028). Reconcile as: the yard is a large one on the landward side.
3. Knots 2,011 (Vane, 927) against QM's "two thousand and fourteen" swings (1215); the QM's count may include the extra trips; Vane notes he missed some nights (927).
4. Shelf depth: "three hundred feet below the lip" (1243, 913) against the QM's "You'll see the Shelf at two hundred" (1219) and "Two hundred... There it is" (1237). The basket rope is therefore at least 300 ft; the QM may be counting something else.
5. Bettany: came down to the Shelf on Stasisday with Sable and Mags (1311, 957) but "had not come down yet" on Dawnday morning (1629).
6. Shelf size: a quarter of a mile (1243); the far end that goes is "a hundred and fifty yards" (1553), leaving the fire (150 yards from the narrow-end hollow, 1387) near the new edge; "the new edge a hundred and fifty yards nearer" (1619). Not exactly inconsistent, but the Shelf is now about 290 yards.
7. Count wording: Orrin "Four hundred and four of us" (1343) excludes the 22; "Forty-seven... Thirty-nine of us. Eight of the twenty-two" (1631); 404 minus 39 equals 365, so consistent. Fine.
8. Dockets "pinned to every coat for three years" (1347) against Hob disposed "Harvestmonth '17" and Ansel "Frostmonth '16" (both fit); and Fen's docket was a scrap "F" (1347).
9. Writ timing: "the third Nullday following receipt" (2072) is not defined in days; received on Driftday 22 Ashfall (2052-2056). Month length not given.
10. The QM is unnamed here (no "Greer"); Sable, Vane call him only "the Quartermaster". (Not a contradiction.)
