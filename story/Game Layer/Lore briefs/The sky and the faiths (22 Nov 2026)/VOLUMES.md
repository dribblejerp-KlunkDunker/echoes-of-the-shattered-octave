## HOOKS (places a star-faith could join what exists without changing it)

1. **The Chord.** The ruled band of star-dust (`R21:121`) joins the doctrine "We left the chord open" (`V1:43`, `V3:182`) and the open Octave with no ninth (`V1:37`). A star-faith can read the band as the unresolved chord and the gap in the week (`V6:92`) without touching the Accord ending. Caution: the name already belongs to the protagonists' movement (Contradiction 5).
2. **The Octave lights and the Rota.** The eight hues are the eight Anchors' notes (`V8:1154`, [PROPOSED]) and the week is the keepers' roster (`V6:43`). Each Anchor already has a day and an order; the Reach already does the god's duty by weekday (`V8:838`). A faith can be the night-attendance of the same roster.
3. **The open slots in the roster.** `V6:78` and `V6:80` leave Heat's and Stasis's keepers "Unattested; open". Stasis (417) owns the Longest Night and the Astrolabe tomb is in its land (`V2:253`), so a time-and-star-reckoning order fits Stasis without displacing the Cantors, Sounders, Reef-Readers, Veil, Sky Choirs or the Watch.
4. **The Longest Night and the stairs.** Keepers went down stairs once a year (`V5:199`); the year turns the morning after (`V7:44`). A faith of the sky can keep the same night from above (stones, lamps, a vigil) and the new year's first dawn on a Dawnday (`V7:54`).
5. **The Light's failing chord.** The sun is losing harmonics (`V1:229`), summers are paler (`SB:133`), and the Church "kept the hour, dropped the chord, and demanded one note" (`V6:81`). A star-faith can be the part of the old dawn service the Church dropped, or the folk answer to a fading day. It must not name a date that overrides the Patriarchy's forbidden 426.
6. **Folk observance that the Synod tolerates.** Festivals "fall when the crop says so", are announced by a sheaf on the Hedge-gate, and are "tolerated as agricultural custom" (`V7b:14`, `V7b:17`); the Reach "dates by what is growing". A star-faith pegged to a rising or a bright night, never to a month, announced by something hung out, fits this exactly and never reaches an Office docket.
7. **The standing-stone ring, the Grey Wethers and the fold-fire hills.** The ring is ruled canon and its purpose undecided (`R21:88`, `V8:195`); the Grey Wethers are "a flock of ringing sarsens" with a five-stone call (`V8:2175`); the three fold-fire hills sit north, east and west of the Downs (`V8:971`); Ewe Barrow is "older than any name for it" (`V8:221`); boundary stones have lost their lettering (`V8:179`). 8. **The Hush and the empty sky.** Hunters say the Hush "keeps closest when the sky is empty" (`V8:728`), and the only star imagery in the books belongs to the Hush's gifts (`V4:71`, `V4:81`). A star-faith could be a ward-faith against dark nights, and also the faith that is most disturbed by Vesper's and Mote's "night sky". The Quiet Faith already holds a vigil at the Grey Wethers (`V8:2190`), so the two can meet at the same stones.
9. **The eight keepers as eight beasts, and the seven birth modes.** The children's fable gives eight creatures for the eight Anchors (`V8:1595`), and the game has seven named birth signs (`V8:842`). Both are [PROPOSED] game material and are the nearest the world has to beasts-in-the-sky and a zodiac; they could be adopted as the figures the constellations are named after.
10. **The Drift.** "The Zodiac Drift is the high sky-current" (`SB:39`) carries a zodiac name no volume explains, and "Drift" is Velocity's word (`SB:163`, `V7:75`). A faith can say why it is called Zodiac, provided skyships still ride it in Book Two and the Reef-Readers still steer by ear.
