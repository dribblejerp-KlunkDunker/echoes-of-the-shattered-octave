## 1. THE SKY

**Nothing at all in the four novels on:** a named star, a named constellation, a zodiac, a named planet or "wanderer", a comet, an eclipse, a solstice or equinox as an astronomical event, the "band of star-dust", an aurora, the "Zodiac Drift". Greps for star/stars/constellation/zodiac/planet/wanderer/comet/eclipse/solstice/aurora/galaxy/cosmos/celestial/astrolog/meteor/firmament returned only the passages below. The only astronomical-flavoured nouns anywhere are "Planetary Engine" (once), "Tomb of the Astrolabe" (once), "sextant" (as a mirror-carrying ship tool), and "reckoning of the stars" (once, as a guess).

**Stars exist and are ordinary, seen at night**
- B1:1715: "black against the stars, rose the mooring mast". Stars visible over Oakhaven's sky-dock. 419.
- B2c:921: "the peaks at the bottom of it pale as teeth under the stars". Stars over the Heights in Ashfall. 419.
- B4P2:471: "frost on the cobbles and the stars out over the gulf". Stars over Sol-Luminary in Frostmonth. 419.
- B3P1:19: "with the stars out over the foothills". Stars at night over the Heights' foothills (Dawnday night). 419.
- B3P1:49: "silver-grey under the stars". Starlight lights the Valley of Velocity. 419.
- B3P1:189: "the stars wheeling over the envelope". Stars as the thing a turning skyship turns against. 419.
- B3P1:685-689: "looked at the stars over the bow". Vane and Kaelen look at the stars, a calm after loss. 419.
- B3P2:481: "She had been looking at the stars over the bow." Sable looking at stars. 419.
- B3P3:349: "where the stars were coming out over the lip of". Stars are visible from the Rift's floor through the strip of sky. 419.
- B1:5848: "Through the willow-tops the stars were out, and the Spire of Decay's end of the sky had that faint wrongness to it that everybody had stopped mentioning years ago." The stars are normal but one end of the sky (toward the Spire of Decay) is wrong, and everyone has stopped talking about it. Seen from the Woods. 419 (wrongness dates from years back, around the Fracture/413).

**Sky with no stars or the wrong colour**
- B3P1:983: "in the pitchless wind, under no stars." At the Watch near the Rift edge, no stars: the Rift is starless. 419.
- B3P2:143 and B3P2:273: "the sky over the Rift, which was black and starless". Same starless black over the Rift. 419.
- B3P3:905: "there was no blue anywhere. Not in the Rift, not in the sky". No blue in the Rift's sky. 419.
- B3P1:1115: "Dawn on the Watch was not a sunrise. The sun did not come up over the edge of the world, because there was no edge". At the world's edge the sun does not rise over a horizon; the light comes up out of the not-colour. 419.

**The sky since the Fracture (pale, grey, wrong)**
- B1:486: "that was why the sky had gone pale." Common rumour that the sky paled when the Dawning Spire broke. Pre-419 (412 onward).
- B1:5392 and B1:5946: "the year the sky went grey". Same event, spoken of as the date the sky changed. Pre-419.
- B1:294: "the sun at noon had once been a thing you could not look at, and now, on a clear day, you could look at it for a long time if you were stubborn." The sun is dimmer (also leaves are rust-pink not red). The Church blames "the Cascade", a quarter-tone flat. Pre-419.
- B1:827 and B1:1211: "go somewhere the sky's still blue" / "go south, where the sky's still—". South has blue sky; the Reach does not. 419.
- B2c:533 and B2c:947: "the peaks, the snow, the sky itself — gone so pale and so bright"; "as if the Spire were a sun that never set". Round the broken Dawning Spire the sky is overexposed white ("the Bleach"), every shadow pointing away from the Spire. 419.
- B2c:945: "There was no moon." The only statement about the moon's presence or absence: on the night of 11/12 Ashfall, at the dead hour, no moon in the sky over Sol-Luminary. 419.
- B2b:1033 and B2b:1063: "Her eyes were purple. Like the sky goes."; "the colour the sky goes on the far side of evening... violet shot through with very small, very cold points of light, as if the woman were looking out through two holes into a night sky". Vesper's eyes (the pale soft thing and the woman) are drawn as a night sky with stars. A strong image the star-faith could claim. 419.
- B2c:106, B3P2:1793, B4P1:824: "lit from inside with a night sky". Vesper's small pale creature is "lit from inside with a night sky". 419.
- B1:5706: "a pale shape, like the moon under water". A moon simile (glass arm). Pre-419/419.

**The moon: only as a shape word**
- B1:1757 and B1:3040: "curved like a new moon". The Rite's silver needle is shaped like a new moon. Pre-419 and 419. (Tobiah's case; the needle that wires a Cantor's throat.)
- B1:4924, B1:5022: "half-moon spectacles". Quist's glasses. Nothing cosmic.
- The books name no moon, give no count of moons, no phases, no tides tied to a moon. Nothing on tides beyond "the tide came into Cantabile harbor singing a high C-flat" (B1:1476).

**Sun**
- B2c:1683 and B2c:2399: "at dawn it takes the first sun and throws it onto the Spire's face." The Eye (a 40-foot bronze mirror on the Altar of Lumina) catches the first sun and throws it into the crack of the Dawning Spire. 419. Pre-419 it did so daily.
- B2c:471: "no glass that shows a face shall be kept... A reflection is a second light, and the Light is jealous." The Sun-Altar bans mirrors and glass that shows a face (sextant mirrors are surrendered, B2c:665-667); the Eye is "the only mirror the Altar permits... the only second light" (B2c:2399). The Light is a jealous god, "second light" is the Church's phrase for any other light. Pre-419 rule, enforced 419.
- B3P1:1115: see above (sun at the edge of the world).
- Sunrise is the Church's counting unit: "the three hundredth sunrise of the Rite" (B1:19, B2c:949); the Rite was counted in mornings. Pre-419.

**Reckoning by the sky**
- B4P1:2077: "Some reckoning of the stars, some Synod calculation, some old Arbiter's sense of occasion. The longest dark of the year." Lyra guesses why Vaelen chose 30 Frostmonth, the Longest Night. It is a guess; the book then gives the real reason (Section 3: the gods listen on that night). 419.
- B2c:665-667: Pennick's sextant has its index-mirror unscrewed and surrendered at Sol-Luminary under the Sun-Altar's mirror ban. 419. Navigation is by compass, chime and sextant; ships do not steer by named stars anywhere in the text.
- B1:674 and B2b: "the Drift". Not a sky-current. The Drift is the Velocity-law zone off the Rust Coast, where "the sea had forgotten which way was up" (B2b:487), a wind that "never stopped" (B3P2:1687: "the endless Velocity wind of the Rust Coast"). "Drift-glass" is tuned lift-glass (B2b:250; B1:1321). Skyships are named Drift-Kestrel, drift-tanks. **No "Zodiac Drift" in any of the four novels**, and no high sky-current.

**Skyships and "sky" as a trade word**: "sky-dock" (B1:4193, 4470), "sky-girl" for a ship (B1:1422), Sky Choir (see section 2). None concern stars.

## 2. FAITHS, CHURCHES AND RITES

**The Church of the Pure Note ("Choir-Church"); its god is the Light**
- B1:47: "The Church taught that the Spire sang one note: the Pure Note, the Law of Light, five hundred and twenty-eight." Core doctrine: one holy note, 528. Narrator: "The Church was wrong." Pre-419.
- B1:29: "five hundred and twenty-eight beats to the second, the holy number, the Light's own pitch." 528 is "the holy number". Pre-419.
- B3P2:929: "Every soul... holds one true note. Sin is the note going flat. Grace is the note held. And the Pure Note, sounded at the Altar every dawn by the Church's choirs, is the note that all souls were made to match: the note of God". The catechism in Seraphina's schooling: sin and grace as pitch. Pre-419 teaching.
- B1:2263: "Held without end, as the catechism said, the breath passed down the line from voice to voice". The Note is held without break; catechism exists. 419.
- B4P2:824: the greeting of the Choir-Church "has no answer. The faithful are taught that the Light gives the answer, and to wait for it". The greeting is the question "Is it you?"; the Church has always waited and never answered. Pre-419 to 419.
- B4P2:715-717: "Somebody coming every eighth day to the god of Light to sing to it... And the Church had kept the day, and kept the hour, and kept the bell, and dropped every note of the song but one... every morning, for four hundred years". The dawn office is the Church's remnant of an older visit to the god; the Church is about four hundred years old in its present form (cf. "four hundred years" on the Basilica school's treble B4P2:1544; Synod "four hundred years" B1:6512). Revealed 419.
- B2c:1055 and B4P2:1206: the Church's Patriarch prays and "has been shown in prayer" (B2c:707); the night office has a sun-disc altar and a black iron lattice screen "worked in the shape of a sun with its rays going out", behind which the Altar sisters sang the response "so that he would hear it come from the place where the Light was". "The Light gives the answer." Pre-419 custom (screens are in "every chapel in the Heights").
- B2b:951: a sun-disc of hammered brass with "the Church's flame" over the Church's counting-house door at Port Cantabile. Symbol: sun-disc and flame. B1:1755: "the Church's gold sunburst", beside "the Arbiterium's scales". Emblems: gold sunburst (Church), scales (Arbiterium). 419.
- B2c:527: "The Solar Empyrean, the priests called it." The Dawning Spire's priestly title. Pre-419.
- B2c:2229, 2385-2407: the Altar of Lumina is "a disc of white stone two hundred feet across" facing the Spire, with the Eye (mirror) behind, a round dais ten feet across, the Vessel's stone; a child (best treble of the Basilica school) sang the first line of the morning hymn alone at every dawn office before the Choir came in. Pre-419 practice; Restoration attempt 13 Ashfall 419.
- B2c:2349, B4P2:1516: the Pilgrim Chain is the road to the Altar, "polished by four hundred years of pilgrims' feet"; pilgrims' chapels cut into the mountainside. Pre-419. B1:1761, B1:2952: "the Pilgrims' Stair at dawn... you could hear [the Sky Choir] all the way down the mountain". The stair/chain pilgrimage is a dawn rite. Pre-419.
- B4P2:114, 118: "with fire, and faith, in shifts"; Dawnday service at the Basilica Altar of the nave, "my children" from the Patriarch. Dawnday is the Church's service day. 419.

**The Sky Choirs, the Rite of Pure Unison, the Flambeaux**
- B1:19, 29, 63: forty girls of the Sky Choirs held the Pure Note for 300 mornings; Inquisitor Ignatius addresses them "Holy daughters. You hold the Light still so that it may be relieved of its excess." The Rite is cast as relief, sanctified surgery. 411-412.
- B2c:1105: Sky Choir robes: "choir-white... girdled at the waist, with the high stiff collars the sopranos wore". Pre-419.
- B1:3036: "Blessed is the single note. Blessed is the throat that holds it. Blessed is the hand that keeps the throat." A liturgical blessing (formula) spoken at the Oakhaven Rite. 419.
- B1:3110, B1:2539, B1:2297: Inquisitor Ignatius blesses with two fingers; swings a censer; the censer-chain creaks. Gestures of the Church. 419.
- B1:2267, B1:3304: "the Pure Flame of the Light itself"; glass-fire (white-gold, smokeless, "burned as long as faith did", B3P2:1769; B4P1:754, 790). The Flambeaux's fire burns as long as the wielder believes. 419; it went out on Lumina when faith broke.
- B1:2828 and B2c:1395: the Rubrics: the chosen voice "shall be told that she is fortunate"; "The Vessel is to be addressed in kindness at all times." Rubric forms: "Rubric of the Rite of Unison", "Rubric of the Sun-Altar for the Keeping of a Vessel, third article". Pre-419/419.
- B1:652: "on Chapel-days, when the chapel-reader's sermons came round to the Consecrated and the blessing of the glass". Provincial chapels have a lay "chapel-reader" who preaches on Chapel-days; Oakhaven has a chapel and a Chapel of the Organum (B1:2345). Pre-419/419. B1:2499: "the Chapel, the Pure Note went on and on and on".
- B1:2345: "A living relic of the Dawning. You would be a sermon in yourself." Glass-armed people are treated as relics by the Church. 419.
- B1:5134: casualty return: the thirty-nine of the Sky Choir are "(CONSECRATED), which meant the Church had decided they were relics rather than corpses". The dead choir are consecrated relics, still singing "seven years thin" in the Basilica pews (B2c:1731, B2c:2387). Pre-419 to 419.
- B2c:1483, 1607: the Patriarch Severin Ashe: "I've prayed for you every night"; Ignatius: "I have prayed every night. And for the first time in thirty years, Cantor, the Light has not answered." Prayer is nightly and expected to be answered. 419.
- B4P2:888: "when she went to him to be shriven before the Rite" – confession/shriving exists. Pre-419.
- B3P2:945: "like a congregation praying under its breath in a very large church": simile only.
- B2c:2765, B1:3312: crowds pray in panic; the Church "ran" when the alarm went (B1:3312). Nothing doctrinal.

**The Iron Synod and its Arbiterium: state religion in law**
- B1:320: Synod decrees carry the seal "Maelis, by the Grace of the Pure Note, Queen of the Reach" (a dead queen's name still used). Pre-419.
- B3P2:511: the Organum's Choir-Hall bell-organ "sounded the hour in a single chord"; "On feast-days it played hymns." The Organum has feast-days. The "Edict of Symmetry" has a day (no date given). 419 recollection.
- B1:6198: "By the Arbiterium of the Iron Synod... the seventeenth day of Leaffall, 419." A decree; "Dissonant" is the legal word for heretics/outlaws ("declared Dissonant. Their goods are forfeit. Their testimony is void"). The word for heresy is Dissonance. 419.
- B1:183: "Purify the Rite!" The Flambeaux's cry. 419. B1:5722: inquisitorial report: unison broken "by a dissonance from the gantry". The Church's concept of sin as dissonance (see B3P2:929).
- B1 Catalogue chapters (c. lines 4194-4560): the Office of the Catalogue (Synod) swears scribes with "the Oath of the Catalogue" (B3P1:1659, B3P1:1966): a fork-copy of the name is taken "while you say the oath". An oath that copies the self. 416 onward.
- Synod soldiers: Carillon Legionnaires with "bell-helms", bells tilted to the sky (B1:628, B1:941, B1:1707, B1:2543). A Legionnaire stands "with its bell lifted to the sky". Ritual posture of the Synod's soldiers. 419.
- B1:410: "the Synod had requisitioned every bell in the Reach". The Synod took the bells (bells attract hounds). Pre-419.

**Folk practice in the Reach and elsewhere (all pre-419 and 419)**
- B1:442, B1:652, B1:484: the ward-sign: "two fingers to the lips and then to the ear. *Mouth shut, ears open.*" Made at the Glass-armed man and at "Spire-blood". Folk sign, not Church.
- B1:254, 332, 412: hush-lamps with a reed whistle in the chimney keep a house from falling silent; lamplighters light them at dusk. Folk practice and civic duty, not a rite, but an evening ritual. B2c:925: the Tiers of Sol-Luminary use them too.
- B1:308: the churchyard graves "heave"; "the sexton had started salting every new grave... binding the jaws of the dead with linen, and setting a flat stone on each tongue, so that no corpse in Oakhaven could ring." Burial custom is new and forced by the Fracture: salt, linen on the jaw, a flat stone on the tongue. Pre-419 (post-412).
- B1:1951: "a stone in the churchyard with a name on it and nothing underneath". A name-stone for the lost. Pre-419/419.
- B2b:134: "a burying-ground on Driftwood Cay, on the high rock above the orphan-house, because the Cay was older than the Coast's customs and the Cay's people had been farmers once." Burial in earth, on a high rock; the Cay is older than the Coast's customs. 419.
- B1:1371-1380: the Boar & Dulcimer's rules: "NO SINGING. NO TOASTS. NO DICE. NO WEEPING ABOVE A HAND." Taboos of the silenced province. 419.
- B1:1382: "the way people drank in Oakhaven, which was the way people prayed elsewhere: quietly, steadily, heads close together." A comparison: elsewhere people pray quietly, close together.
- B1:2135, B1:6046, B4P1:2055: "the calling-song... the old song the Cantors had used for three hundred years to bring lost lambs home off the hills at dusk"; also called "the oldest song in the world", five notes, a lullaby sung to the god of Growth at the First Compact. The shepherd's calling-song is the survival of a lullaby. See Section 3. Folk/herding practice (shepherd mention is a simile, B1:602, but lambs are in the calling-song).
- B4P1:1651, 2057: the Reach's year: "Frostmonth was not cold, in the Reach... You hummed the tending note into the hollow of the oldest tree in the orchard on the Longest Night, and the tree hummed it back". Seasonal rite: Longest Night, the oldest orchard tree, the tending note. 419 recall; custom ended in 417 ("before 417", B4P1:1799: orchard choir "every Longest Night... before 417").
- B4P1:2057: "once a year, on the Longest Night, every Cantor in the Reach went down... into the vault... sat round the Root in the dark and sang it the lullaby, all through." The Cantors' annual rite (ended roughly 300 years ago when the Synod sealed the vault). Pre-419, ended c. 119 SR (or "three hundred years" before 419).
- B1:1937, B1:250: Cantors test themselves "with a clay jar pressed to the hollow of her own throat, the way Cantors had tested themselves for three hundred years". Jar-humming. Pre-419.
- B4P1:1370: "the way the old faith said heaven held the dead." Only mention of "the old faith" and "heaven": a faith (name unknown) said heaven held the dead. The Veil called it "the stacks". Pre-419 recollection.
- B4P1:2081: "as the faiths died and the stairs were sealed." Plural "faiths": the old stair-going faiths of the eight gods are dead. Pre-419.

**Oaths, curses and what people swear by**
- B1:145 "Gods help them all"; B1:1476 "Gods above, Cantor"; B1:3132 "gods, look at it"; B2a:137 "for God's sake"; B2b:1255 "I swear to God"; B2b:1219 "God knew whose"; B2b:1305 "a face like a church candle". People use both "God" and "gods" as oaths, even though the Church says one God/Light. 419.
- B1:1562 and B2b:1187: the pistols are named *Last Rites* and *Second Opinion*. Last Rites exists as a phrase.
- B1:2934, 5260: "no one more than ten feet away could have sworn to it"; "sworn" appears as testimony. Nothing sacred.
- B3P1:1659, 1966: the Oath of the Catalogue (above).
- B3P1:1125, 1513, B3P2:157, 283: the Watch recite "the days of the week" as a chant, "like a congregation praying in a language nobody remembered the meaning of". The week as a litany to keep the mind present; the eight day-names are the nearest thing to a liturgy the Watch have. 419.

**Heresies and what is forbidden**
- Dissonant (B1:6198): the Synod's legal category.
- B2c:471: mirrors and face-showing glass forbidden within the chains of Sol-Luminary.
- B1:1371-1380 singing, toasts, dice, weeping in Oakhaven (taboos of fear, not of faith).
- B1:5256 and B4P1: the Church "will" claim the Cantor and the Engineer; the Arbiterium stays the Church's claim (B1:6200). The Church and the Arbiterium are rivals: the Synod's Supreme Arbiter Vaelen is the one who designed the Rite. 419.
- Hymn 9 of the Cloister of the Lost Choir (B1:4690, 4786; B2c:2299, B4P1:1522): a three-part children's hymn that "brought it to its knees"; also sung by the Choir-That-Eats (B4P1:1596). A hymn from a defunct school, not a rite.

**The Silent Veil and Whisper-Grove (a dead institution)**
- B4P1:1238: "The Silent Veil had kept its house in the deep Woods for eleven hundred years before the Synod absorbed it." An order (the scribes of the Veil) older than the Synod by eleven centuries, whose house is the Mausoleum of Chords; B4P1:1370 "The Veil had called it the stacks, and catalogued it." Pre-419 (the absorption is c. 416).
- B2b:901, B3P1:1966: the Veil's scriptorium, the Cloister of the Lost Choir (B1:4688-4810). Archival monastic life.

## 3. COSMOLOGY

- **The Octave / the Eight (laws, Anchors, Spires, gods).** B1:5252: "They are in pain... All eight. They have been in pain for longer than there has been a word for it, and they were promised relief at the end of ten thousand years". B1:6264: "He has eight Anchors to ask a question of, and a Planetary Engine thirty years in the building". B2c:1063: "He'll be doing it with one god instead of eight." B4P1:2081: "eight of them, under eight cities". B1:3350: "Growth is the kindest of the eight laws and the least merciful." B1:5188: Kaelen learned "the eight laws by rote" at eleven. The world rests on eight laws, each held by a living god (Chorister/Anchor) under an Anchor-spire. The title phrase "eight notes" appears once, of eight bells: B2b:218 "eight notes laid over each other" (bells of Wendmere). Pre-419.
- **The eight named so far (the books only name some).** Light (Dawning Spire / Solar Empyrean / Spire of Light, at Sol-Luminary, 528 Hz; B1:47, B2c:527); Growth (the Root under Oakhaven, Chorister of Growth, 174 Hz, five-note lullaby; B1:250, B1:6361, B4P1:2055); Memory (Weeping Canopy, Whisper-Grove, 741 Hz; B1:4341, B4P1:1230); Velocity/Motion (the "drowned Anchor" under Port Cantabile... note: B1:4341 says the 741 note was "the same note the drowned Anchor under this city had held", and B2b:232 gives 639 to "the Velocity Anchor"; Valley of Velocity B3P1:41: "the law of motion came loose in it like a tooth"; 639 Hz); Mass (Fortis, the Keystone, the Lodestone Vaults, Titan's Spine; B4P1:198, 380); Decay (the "Gate of Decay", "The eighth spire, the one at the very edge of the world, the rest between notes", B3P1:813; the Rift/Maw; B3P1:729 "the Unrotting. The first stage of the eighth law going"). The remaining two are not named in the four novels. Note the Memory/Velocity attribution of "the drowned Anchor" in B1:4341 conflicts with B2b:232 (see Contradictions).
- **Words: Anchor, Chorister, Spire, Gate.** B1:5256 "the Anchor of Memory"; B2c:949 "the Chorister of Light"; B4P1:1230 "the Chorister of Memory"; B3P1:813 "the Gate of Decay... the eighth spire". "Spire-root" (B3P1:475) is the drilled foot. A Spire is the tower; the Anchor the god-body; the Chorister the god as singer; a Gate seems to be the Decay Spire's name. Pre-419.
- **Ten thousand years; the First Compact; the First Cycle.** B1:145: "for the first time in ten thousand years". B4P1:2055: "Ten thousand years ago, at the First Compact, when the god of Growth was bound under the Reach". B4P1:198: "Living quartz, grown out of the mountain in the First Compact, ten thousand years ago, when the stone agreed to be the floor." B4P1:436: "The Light had been holding for ten thousand years on a promise." B2c:1687 and 1857: the Blade was "given to House Aubade by the Light in the First Cycle"; "the way it had been holding it alone since the First Cycle". B2c:2705: the second note on the Altar "had not been heard... since the First Cycle". Two named epochs: the First Compact (the binding, 10,000 years ago) and the First Cycle (a Church-side name for the first age of Light's worship). Pre-419.
- **The gods are held, not worshipped; they are tired and asked for relief.** B1:47 "It sounds tired."; B1:5252 above; B4P1:514 "The god was tired. It had been holding the weight of the world for ten thousand years. It had been promised that someone would come and take a turn."; B4P1:514: "The Ninth, Sable called it; the old books in the Tomb of the Astrolabe; *the ninth will be born*." The only astrolabe: a tomb or library holding prophetic books of "the ninth". Pre-419, then 419.
- **The Reckoning.** B1:6046: the calling-song "went back past the Synod and past the Reckoning and probably past the Compact itself". "the Reckoning" is an epoch between the Compact and the Synod; unexplained. Pre-419.
- **The gods listen on the Longest Night; the stairs.** B4P1:2057, 2081: the Root "listened for the Cantors coming down the stair", "eight of them, under eight cities, that had been listening every Longest Night for ten thousand years". The vault stair was sealed by the Synod c. 300 years ago. The Longest Night, 30 Frostmonth, is the night the gods wait for a visitor. 419 reveal.
- **The Fracture, Cascade, Silencing, Screaming Year.** B1:294 "the Cascade, that all things had gone a quarter-tone flat since the Spire of Dawning broke"; B2b:154 "the Screaming Year" (the year Wendmere slid into the sea; hounds come in from the sea; B1:1566); B3P1:813 "In the spring of 413 the Synod had drilled the Gate of Decay"; B3P1:41 "398, twenty-one years ago, when the Synod drilled the first spire-root". Eras post-398: drilled spires, quiets, Fracture 412, Screaming Year (around 413), Silencing/Unstrung 416. The sky's changes (pale, grey) date from the 412 Fracture; the Rift/starlessness from the 413 drill.
- **Planetary Engine.** B1:6264 (once, Vaelen's machine, "thirty years in the building"). The only planet word. 419.

## 4. THE CALENDAR

- **The year-count: SR.** B1:19 "412 SR"; B1:242 "Autumn, 419 SR. Seven years after the Fracture."; B1:566 "409 SR". SR is never expanded in the four novels (nothing says what SR stands for or what year 1 is). The Fracture is the central year (412). "Reckoning" (B1:6046) is an older era. Pre-419.
- **The eight-day week (confirmed order).** From dated headers: Rootday, Hearthday, Massday, Stasisday, Dawnday, Driftday, Memoryday, Nullday, then Rootday again. Evidence: B2c:18 "Rootday the ninth of Ashfall, to Hearthday the tenth"; B2c:475 "Hearthday the tenth... to Massday the eleventh"; B2c:1799 "Stasisday the twelfth of Ashfall"; B2c:2337 "Dawnday the thirteenth of Ashfall"; B4P2:12 "Stasisday the twenty-second of Frostmonth, to Dawnday the twenty-third"; B4P2:826 "Dawnday the twenty-third of Frostmonth, to Driftday the twenty-fourth"; B4P2:1635 "Driftday the twenty-fourth... to Memoryday the twenty-fifth"; B4P2:1376 "Hearthday, the twentieth of Frostmonth" and B4P2:1939-1949 listing Nullday, Rootday, Hearthday, Stasisday, Dawnday, Driftday. (Nullday = 26th, Rootday = 27th by the pattern; not stated.) Dawnday is the Church's service day ("every eighth day", B4P2:715: "coming every eighth day to the god of Light"). The day-names are known; **their meanings are never explained in the novels**. They echo the laws: Dawn (Light), Root (Growth), Memory, Mass, Drift (Velocity), Stasis, Null, Hearth. Eight days, eight laws, but the books do not draw the link.
- **Months.** Named in the text: Greenleaf (B1:4199, 5140: "the ninth of Greenleaf, 411"), Leafturn (B2b:939), Harvestmonth (B2c:703, B4P1:52), Leaffall (B1:4163, 6198: "the seventeenth day of Leaffall, 419"), Ashfall (B2a:19 "The fourth of Ashfall, 419 SR"), Frostmonth (B2c:703, B4P1:12, 46: "The thirtieth of Frostmonth", so Frostmonth has at least 30 days), Candlefast (B1:849: "By Candlefast we were boiling harness leather", a deep-winter/late-winter name). Month numbers also used: "the ninth month of 417", "ninth month of 412" (B1:5270, 5722). Ashfall has at least 15 days (B3P1:543). Meanings are never given. **Order is not stated beyond harvest, leaf-fall, ash, frost.** Note Leaffall (Oct/Nov) comes before Ashfall, with Harvestmonth earlier; "last week of Harvestmonth" (B2c:707) is when Lyra was taken.
- **Hours.** "the dead hour" (the small hours, "three hours after midnight", B2c:945), "the ninth hour" (B1:4556, B3P2:1743), "the sixth hour" (dawn on the Watch, B3P1:1115), "the fourth hour" (procession at dawn, B2c:2227), "3rd bell". The Organum chimes every hour (B3P2:511). Pre-419 and 419.
- **Feasts.** Only "feast-days" at the Organum (B3P2:511) and the unnamed Chapel-days (B1:652). The Longest Night, 30 Frostmonth, is the only fixed festival in the books (Reach folk-rite, B4P1:1651). The Restoration of the Light was to be on "the first day of Frostmonth" (B2c:703), moved to Dawnday 13 Ashfall (B2c:1641). Hearthday was the day set for the Oakhaven Rite (B1:2311).
- **Seasons and harvest signs.** B4P1:1651: Frostmonth in the Reach is not cold ("the iron-bark oaks dropped their mast and the second reed-crop came up green in the water-meadows"; "the cold stopped at the edge of the Reach... The Root kept it out"). B1:294: iron-oak leaves once turned "a red like a shout" in autumn. B1:592, 1007: "At midsummer it had been one finger" (the glass arm's growth measured against midsummer). "Midsummer", "midwinter" are used only as time-markers. Nothing on solstice or equinox as an event; the Longest Night is the only astronomically fixed date, and its fixing is described as a guess at reckoning (B4P1:2077).
- **The Rite's calendar:** 300 mornings, Stage Nine (B1:3994); the cover-sheet is dated 9 Greenleaf 411, "eight months before the Fracture" (B1:5140); the Inquisitor's report in "the ninth month of 412" (B1:5722). See Contradictions.

## 5. OLD STONES AND OLD PEOPLES

## CONTRADICTIONS (within the four novels; manuscripts rule)

1. **Which Anchor is "drowned".** B1:4341 says the 741 Hz tone is "the same note the drowned Anchor under this city [Whisper-Grove] had held for ten thousand years" (741 is the Memory note elsewhere, B3P1:1419), while B2b:232 and B4P1:980 give the drowned bell and the drowned note as 639, the Velocity note. B2b/B4P1 call 639 the drowned bell of Wendmere (Velocity); B1:4341's use is a loose phrase (Whisper-Grove sits over the Memory Anchor, whose note is 741 per B3P1:1419; the "drowned" label there is unexplained). Treat 741 = Memory, 639 = Velocity.
2. **The Church's age.** The Church is "the Church" with a dawn office kept "for four hundred years" (B4P2:715-717, 1544, 1807) but the Blade was given "in the First Cycle" (B2c:1687, B4P2:30), and Oakhaven's chapel and the Pilgrim Chain are "four hundred years" old. Consistent only if the "First Cycle" is the Light's own age and the Church's form began about four hundred years ago. The Synod too: "for the first time in four hundred years, to turn on itself" (B1:6512). But the Synod's grandfathers sealed the Root-vault "three hundred years ago" (B4P1:2057) and Greenhall is "three hundred years" old; so the Synod is at least 300-400 years old.
3. **When the Fracture was.** B1:5140: cover-sheet 9 Greenleaf 411, "eight months before the Fracture" (so about Leaffall/Ashfall 411 to Greenleaf 412?). B1:19: "412 SR" is the three hundredth sunrise. B1:849: "the winter the Spire broke". B1:5722: report "in the ninth month of 412". The dates fit loosely (Rite begins mid-411/412 and ends 412), but the season of the Fracture is stated as winter, while counting 300 mornings from a spring start would end in winter. No hard contradiction; flag for the calendar volume.
4. (Not a contradiction, a consistency note.) B1:827/1211 blue sky only in the south; B3P3:905 no blue in the Rift; B1:5848 wrongness at the Decay end of the sky; B3P1:983 starless at the Rift. All fit the Decay Spire at the world's edge.
5. **The Church's single god vs the people's "gods".** The Church teaches one God/Light (B3P2:929 "the note of God"; B2c:1055), yet B1, B2, B3 characters swear by "gods" (see section 2) and Vaelen says "eight". The book makes the Church's monotheism a local claim over one of eight gods; no contradiction in the books, but the volumes must keep "God" as the Church's word for the Light only.
6. **"The Church... the Light" doctrine of the note.** B1:47 narrator: "The Church was wrong" (the Spire sang a chord, not one note); B4P2:715-717: the Church "dropped every note of the song but one". These agree.
7. Vol I/II/III bibles were not in my files; no bible-vs-manuscript conflicts could be checked here.

## HOOKS (where a faith of the stars could join without changing anything)

1. **The old stair-faiths.** B4P1:2081 "as the faiths died and the stairs were sealed" and B4P1:1370 "the old faith said heaven held the dead": a forgotten, plural, pre-Synod faith that went down stairs on the Longest Night. A star-faith can be one of those "faiths", or its night-side twin: eight gods listened from below; a star-faith looks up. It must not claim the gods are in the sky (the books put them under eight cities).
2. **The Longest Night, 30 Frostmonth.** The only fixed festival, and B4P1:2077 lets characters guess it was chosen by "some reckoning of the stars". A star-faith can own the real "reckoning" (stars returning on the longest night) as folk knowledge that the Synod borrowed and the Cantors kept as the Root rite.
3. **Vesper's eyes and creature "lit from inside with a night sky"** (B2b:1063; B2c:106; B3P2:1793; B4P1:824): the books already paint a night sky with "very small, very cold points of light". A star-faith's central symbol can be an eye or a held small night, without any new fact.
4. **The starless Rift and the wrong end of the sky by the Decay Spire** (B3P1:983, B3P2:143, B1:5848): a star-faith can say the stars go out toward the eighth Gate, and use the Rift as its place of absence/dread.
5. **The eight-day week (Dawnday, Rootday, Memoryday, Hearthday, Nullday, Driftday, Massday, Stasisday)**: names with no stated meaning. They echo the laws; a star-faith can give eight "houses" or "watches" of the sky the same names, or read the week as the eight gods' turns, without altering any date.
6. **Standing stones as a blank.** Nothing is there, so nothing can be contradicted. The only stone-lore to hook onto: First Compact stone ("the stone agreed to be the floor", B4P1:198), flat stones on tongues (B1:308), name-stones (B1:1951), pilgrim stairs and the Pilgrim Chain's anchor-houses. Plan: stones that "hold still" and frame the sky should read as Compact-age markers (stone agreed to hold), and avoid chimes or hum, since bells and ringing glass are what hounds find (B1:410).
7. **The Eye on Lumina and the mirror ban** (B2c:471, 2399): the Church already treats a second light as a sin ("the Light is jealous"), and the Eye is the one permitted mirror. A star-faith naturally collides with this: stars are "second lights", which gives it a persecuted-folk-religion edge, and "Sun-Altar" faith has an obvious counter-sky faith. Keep consistent: the Church's own symbol is the sun-disc and flame.
8. **Dawn office "every eighth day"** (B4P2:715): the Church's day is Dawnday; a star-faith's worship can sit on the opposite end of the week (Nullday, the dead hour) and the dead hour is already named ("the dead hour", used widely).
9. **Dissonance as the heresy word** (B1:6198, B3P2:929 "Sin is the note going flat"): a star-faith would be judged by pitch; it can sing by interval and not by unison, the very thing the Hymn 9 chord and the "living chord" (B2c:707) suggest.
10. **The Silent Veil's eleven hundred years** (B4P1:1238) and **the Tomb of the Astrolabe** (B4P1:514): an astrolabe is a sky instrument; the Veil's books of "the ninth will be born" are the one text already placed in a star-flavoured archive. A star-faith's scripture can live in the Veil's stacks without changing a line.
