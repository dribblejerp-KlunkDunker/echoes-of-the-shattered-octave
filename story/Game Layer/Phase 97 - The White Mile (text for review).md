## What the ruling settled

Design (a) of the findings record: the book's mile, with a keeper who owes nothing to mirrors. No Mirror Walks, no great mirror, no beam, no Polisher. Accepted with it: the scavengers' shelf and its lamp, the two house names (Sancterre, Valmont-Lune), the Tallyman, the drop-line, a gear set.

## What was built

- **Two maps.** `p97_gutted` (the gutted house, 9 by 9, black, roofed) and `p97_mile` (48 by 112, open, lit by its own light). The Spire is to the south, so the way up the Mile is south and every shadow lies north.
- **The way in** (:941). A doorway in the top wall of the Umbral Tiers (cell 76,5) opens on "a dark room with nothing in it, black walls, a black-painted window", and a ladder goes up through its roof onto a walkway of black planks. The trapdoor takes you back down.
- **The walkway and the scavengers' shelf.** All of it in shade. Bedrolls, a bolt of black cloth, one shade-lamp (the Souls rules, group k: a place to rest and wake, with no keeper to put down). A scavenger of the line stands there by night.
- **The line and its offerings** (:955-957). At the end-ladder the shade stops "straight as a ruled line". On the stone lie a loaf and a tin cup, a candle-end and three pears, a child's drawing (a black shape with wings, a sun crossed out), a folded black blanket. Each can be looked at. Using one says "They are not for you." Nothing is ever taken.
- **Four tiers of white street**, each a little higher and joined by a roofed stair:
  1. A street of broken things: a broken wall, a fallen column in two pieces, a dry basin, a doorway deep enough to stand in.
  2. Two great houses, House Sancterre and House Valmont-Lune, with blind windows and domes gone white; a carriage with its shafts down; a balcony. East of them, Valmont-Lune's garden: white gravel, five white statues, a parapet over the drop.
  3. The Unison's street and plaza: a white house with its door open on a stair, a statue on a plinth, a dry fountain.
  4. The old Glassmarket (:1013): an arcaded square under the shoulder of a dome, five stalls ("White baskets of white fruit on white trestles under white awnings", :1019), a dry fountain. The street on toward the rim is shut by a fall of white stone.
- **Crossing** (:985). You go "from shadow to shadow, the way you cross a river on stones". The test measures the longest white stretch you cannot avoid: 4.4 strides up the first tier, 4.8 past the houses, 4.8 down the Unison's street (the book's "two to five strides"), and 6.0 across the market to the Tallyman, the widest on the Mile. Every shadow lies north of the thing that casts it (:947).
- **The light.** Out of shade it takes health: 1.6 a second by night, 0.8 in the dead hour before dawn, 6.0 by day ("the two of them together were more than anything could stand in", :1905), and 15 parts in a hundred more for each tier nearer the Spire. The first time: "The light comes onto you like hands. It does not hurt." (:975-977). Black cloth round the face and black gloves (:953) each slow it by a fifth. Staying in it is the game's own death. A small card at the top of the view tells the hour and whether you are in shade; with the scavenger's cloth-pole in hand it also tells what the cloth says.
- **The Unison** (:1103). Forty, "in four ranks of ten, coming slowly down the white street toward the plaza": they stand at the west end, walk to the plaza, stand, and walk back, on their own clock (103 seconds the round). They are not foes and cannot be fought.
  - They hear noise and nothing else (:965, "Everything up there can hear it"). Walk past them in plain sight and silent, and they do not find you.
  - Loud within fifteen strides (running, striking, singing): they stop, and forty heads turn. Go quiet and they go back.
  - Stay loud and they come, slowly. When they reach you they strike the note: it takes 14 health but never the last of it, empties the voice, deafens for twenty seconds and throws you down. Then they go back. "It is not you they want."
  - A blow or a song near them only says that nothing changed. Within three strides the note presses the voice out of your chest.
  - They are never stopped, broken, scattered or killed, and they never leave their tier.
- **The Glassmarket's people** (:1019-1027). Eight figures, nine people (two old men share a bench): a woman with her hand out for a coin, a man with a basket mid-stride, the two old men, a woman by the fountain facing the Spire, a porter under a sack, a woman holding a jug to be filled, a man looking up, a stall-keeper on his stool. White, see-through, casting no shadow, "seven years thin". Using one says "Don't touch them." (:1015). The woman at the stall: her outstretched fingers tremble (:1025, "still reaching for the coin"). No child is drawn or told.
- **The Tallyman.** By night only, at a pillar of the south arcade that he has tallied in black chalk from the floor up. A scavenger wound in black to the eyes; his left hand is bare and white, and you can see the pillar through two of its fingers. He counts the market's people every night: nine now, fourteen the first winter he came up. Reach him and he gives his blacks and his second hook, once (200 experience, a journal line). He is a side-quest marker on the map and the compass until then. Five subjects: the count, the Glassmarket, the Unison, crossing, further up.
- **The drop-line.** A scavenger's line lies coiled on the parapet of Valmont-Lune's garden. Until somebody lets it down from above, only an iron ring shows in the wall by the shelf. Let down, it is one climb between the shelf and the second tier, both ways, for good.
- **The Scavenger's Blacks** (set 18 of the gear sets): Scavenger's Wrap (head), Scavenger's Gloves (hands), Scavenger's Coat (body), the Tallyman's Hook (a two-handed pole-hook). Two pieces: quieter on white stone. All four: the Unison must be a third nearer to hear you. Each has its own picture, worn look and first-person look.
- **What you hear.** One note at 528 under everything, louder the nearer the Unison is. No music of its own.
- **The look.** White dressed stone with only the joints left to see; shadows drawn as black planks; no colour anywhere but you, the scavengers' black and the copper in forty throats. In the 3D view the place has its own colourless grade and fades to white with distance, not to dark.

## For the owner

1. **The count: nine, where there were fourteen.** *Recommend: keep. It gives the book's thinning a number a player can carry away.*
2. **The white house on the Unison's street is a doorway to stand in, not a house to climb.** The findings record listed "the white house you climb to hide". In the book the Unison climbs after the sound and gives up; here they come, listen and go back on the street. *Recommend: leave it; a climbable house is a second small map and can be added later if you want the scene.*
3. **By day the Tallyman and the shelf's scavenger are not there** ("The Tiers slept in the morning; they lived at night", :2911). *Recommend: keep.*
4. **The Unison's blow never kills, but the light can.** Struck down in the white, you can still die of the light afterwards. *Recommend: keep; it is the book's rule that the Mile is survived in shadow.*
5. **Phase 91, question 4, is now due.** The Heights' fourth keeper is built (the Tallyman, who is met and not fought). The question was: the Reach's two keepers (the Flint-Back, the Standing Note) do not yet drop a trophy and a piece of realm gear; shall they, now, all at once? *Recommend: yes, as a small phase of its own, with the Heights' three fought keepers checked at the same time.*
