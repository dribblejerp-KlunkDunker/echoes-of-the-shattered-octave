# Phase 124: Faces of their own. The trial, and why nothing was built (findings, for ruling)

24 November 2026. Step 2 of the roadmap from the audit. Seventy-nine people borrow one of two portraits in the talk window (forty the young townswoman's, thirty-nine the townsman's). The plan was a portrait made in code for each. Before making seventy-nine, four ways were tried on six people and put on one sheet beside the painted originals: `screenshots/p124-faces-trial.jpg`.

**Nothing was wired into the game. No way tried is good enough to sit beside the paintings.**

## The six people

Old Marrable (the Fold's lamp-teller); the trooper at the tip (Null-Watch); the Tallyman of the White Mile; Perpetua Lisle (the Pilgrim Chain); the packman on the Greenroot Road; Oona Salt of Port Cantabile. What each look was taken from is in the builder's notes; the books give two of them: the Watch wear "long coats with lead plates sewn into them that made them walk like people wading" (Book Three, Part One, 783), and Oona Salt has "her admiral's coat with a burn across the back of one hand and a face like a closed fist" and "flat sea-grey eyes" (Book Four, Part One, 658, 666).

## The four ways, and how each failed

| Way | What it is | The fault |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | A bust painted from nothing, in code. | Flat doll faces with faint features. Each is its own person, and none is a painted person. The lead plates read as bricks. |
| **B** | The two borrowed paintings, warped, aged, re-toned and re-dressed in code. | Still plainly the same townswoman and townsman with parts laid over them. |
| **C** | The nearest of all twenty-eight paintings, changed the same way. | The only way with real painted faces. But the strangers are recognisably Hetty, Rooke, the Tongueless and Mags in other clothes, which is worse than a borrowed face: it says they are kin. |
| **D** | Each person's portrait cut from their own figure in the world, enlarged and finished like a painting. | It showed the deeper fault. The later people's figures have too little in them to make a face from. |

## What way D found about the figures themselves

The Book One people are painted figures and painted portraits. The people added since are of two kinds, and the sheet's last column shows both:

1. **Figures drawn in code from flat blocks of colour** (the trooper, the Tallyman, Perpetua Lisle, most of the continent's people). Right in their dress, and nothing like a painting.
2. **The townsman's or townswoman's painted figure, recoloured** (the packman, Oona Salt, the roads' walkers of Phase 60). Oona Salt, whom Book Four gives an admiral's coat, stands in the world as the townswoman with her jar. The packman's blue recolour turns his **skin blue with orange patches**: a fault a player can see today.

So the borrowed portraits are the smaller half of it. A player who walks from Oakhaven to Sol-Luminary goes from painted people to block figures.

## Why code has not reached it

A painted face at 96 by 96 holds thousands of small decisions of light on skin. The four ways either invent them (A: it cannot), move someone else's (B, C: the someone else shows through) or enlarge what is not there (D). More rounds of the same would not change the kind of result.

## The choice (the owner's)

It turns on his standing rule that all art is made in code, so it is his.

1. **Keep the rule and keep the borrowed faces.** Nothing gets worse. Phase 124 closes with this record. The blue-skinned recolours are still put right (they can be: tint the cloth, spare the skin), and Old Marrable is still redrawn larger.
2. **Keep the rule, and give the later people one plain style of their own, on purpose.** Not imitation paintings: a style code can do well and that the world could own, such as ink and wash "as a Veil scribe would set a face down in a ledger". Every later person would get a face in it, and it would look meant instead of failed. It would be a second look beside the paintings, and it needs its own trial before anything is promised.
3. **Lift the rule for people only.** New painted portraits (and, later, figures) in the originals' style, made outside the game's code and added as picture files, as the first twenty-eight were. This is the only way the later people can stand level with Book One's. The paintings would have to come from the owner, or from a picture-making tool he allows.

**Recommended: 1 now, and 3 if he is willing.** Option 1 costs nothing and fixes the two faults a player can see. Option 3 is the only one that reaches the standard he set with Harrow and Orla. Option 2 is honest but adds a second look to a game whose rule so far has been one.
