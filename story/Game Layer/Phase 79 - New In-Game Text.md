# Phase 79: the long road (the Greenroot road, in three stretches, and Singers' Howe)

Owner's direction, 11 and 12 Nov: a world that takes a while to cross; on every stretch something to find, someone to meet with a small task, and something that only happens at night or in bad weather; "be sure to to not make the road between each area be a straight line and add lakes, streams, cliffs and on and on"; "make the road 3 times longer and add more to do and explore. add the occasional traveler or band of travelers, explorers. There should be old ruins that hide dangerous and deadly surprises and perhaps a full dungeon that can be explored and looted for special gear"; "Be sure not to put everything to close to each other so its claustrophobic"; "Take inspiration from real world areas and then add a fantasy element for that wow factor."

**Ruled by the owner, 12 Nov:** the names on the middle stretch are good (Oak Hill, the Shelf, the Leap, the Hang, the Little Hang, the Narrows, the eyot); the milestones are fine as they are (words, not numbers).

**Canon used:** the Greenroot road and the iron-bark oaks on either side of it, which ring; the two days' walk; the forty grain wagons of spring 413; the Grey Teeth; Greenroot found empty ("Tables laid, doors shut, kettles cold, and nobody", the boots on the step, the hush-lamp still burning); noise brings the hounds; hush-lamps and their reeds; hush-moths eat the light first; glass in the rock that is faintly warm and hums; the wind-reeds that went to dust the day the Spire broke, and the old reed-rows; Cantors sing the dead into the ground; the Dirge (funeral songs, eaten); the Quiet, and iron; a grave marked with a reed; a stone on the tongue and a stone on the stone.

**Where each stretch comes from, and its fantasy element [PROPOSED]:**
- *The Long Common* is limestone dale and moor country (a delph, becks, a clapper bridge, a tor). Its fantasy element: the glass that came up through the Delph's rock and lights the pit green and gold at night; and the Bell Oak, an iron-bark six times the height of a man, with the Ringing Stand round it tolling in a storm.
- *The middle stretch* is lake and fell country (a mere, a tarn, an eyot, a force coming off a scar). Its fantasy element is the Leap itself and the far skyline of the Grey Teeth.
- *Greenroot Wood* is a limestone gorge, a fen with an eel-stage, and a barrow under a ruin on a knoll. Its fantasy element: the old reed-row up to the lych-house, and what is under the hill.

**Everything below is [PROPOSED]** for the owner's ruling. New names: **the Long Common**, **the Delph**, **the Ringing Stand** and **the Bell Oak**, **Crow Tor**, **Long Water**; **Greenroot Wood**, **the Cleave**, **the lych-house**, **Singers' Howe** (the Howe). New people: a turf-cutter, a packman, a band of three who turned back from Greenroot, an antiquary and her digger. New gear: the **Old Cantor's Staff** (iron and blunt, reach 2.0, noise 0.6: the quietest long weapon in the game; damage 6 to 14) and the **Bearer's Gloves** (hands, armour 3, noise -0.2). New lore to rule on in particular: that glass has come up in a second place near Oakhaven (the Delph) which the Consortium does not work; that Greenroot's Cantors were buried apart from its other dead, under the Howe, for three hundred years; and the words over the inner door.

French place names used: la Longue Commune, la Carrière (the Delph), la Futaie sonnante, le Chêne-Cloche, le Tor aux Corbeaux, l'Eau longue, le bois de Greenroot, la Fente (the Cleave), la maison des morts (the lych-house), le Tertre des Chantres; and for the middle stretch la Corniche, le Saut, le Surplomb, le Défilé, l'îlot, le ru.

| 5 | The offering room. What Greenroot left at the door of its dead: a hundred years of it, and nobody has swept. | La salle des offrandes. Ce que Greenroot laissait à la porte de ses morts : cent ans d'offrandes, et personne n'a balayé. |
| 6 | The choir, cut round, with a step for the singer. The echo comes back a half-note flat. | Le chœur, taillé en rond, avec une marche pour le chantre. L'écho revient un demi-ton trop bas. |
| 7 | The lower vault. The water has come up into it and has not gone down. | Le caveau du bas. L'eau y est montée et n'est pas redescendue. |
| 8 | A grave set the Reach way: a stone on the tongue, a stone on the stone. The second stone is gone. | Une tombe faite à la manière des Confins : pierre sur la langue, pierre sur la pierre. La seconde pierre a disparu. |
| 9 | You set a stone on the stone. | Vous posez une pierre sur la pierre. |
| 10 | A grave set the Reach way. Both its stones are in their places. | Une tombe faite à la manière des Confins. Ses deux pierres sont à leur place. |
| 11 | The third stone goes down. Under the hill, something that has been waiting for it draws back a bar. | La troisième pierre se pose. Sous la colline, quelque chose qui l'attendait tire une barre. |
| 12 | The inner door | La porte intérieure |
| 13 | A door of one slab, with no handle and no keyhole. It is waiting for something, and it is not you. | Une porte d'une seule dalle, sans poignée ni serrure. Elle attend quelque chose, et ce n'est pas vous. |
| 14 | Cut over the inner door, in letters older than the Hedge: WE SANG THEM DOWN. LET NO ONE SING THEM UP. | Taillé au-dessus de la porte intérieure, en lettres plus vieilles que la Haie : NOUS LES AVONS DESCENDUS EN CHANTANT. QUE NUL NE LES REMONTE EN CHANTANT. |
| 15 | You say the words over until you have them by heart. | Vous répétez les mots jusqu'à les savoir par cœur. |
| 16 | The singers' own things, laid on a shelf of rock and left. | Les affaires des chantres eux-mêmes, posées sur une tablette de roc et laissées là. |
| 17 | Old Cantor's Staff | Bâton de vieux Chantre |
| 18 | Ash, shod with iron at both ends, the grip worn to a hand that is not yours. Iron bites the Quiet, and this makes very little noise about it. | Du frêne, ferré aux deux bouts, la prise usée à la forme d'une main qui n'est pas la vôtre. Le fer mord le Calme, et celui-ci le fait sans grand bruit. |
| 19 | Bearer's Gloves | Gants de porteur |
| 20 | Grey kid lined with hush-cloth. The ones who carried the dead down wore them, so that nothing they touched would ring. | Chevreau gris doublé d'étoffe sourde. Ceux qui descendaient les morts les portaient, pour que rien de ce qu'ils touchaient ne sonne. |
