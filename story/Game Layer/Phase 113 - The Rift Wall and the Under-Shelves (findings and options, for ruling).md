# Phase 113: the Rift Wall and the Under-Shelves (findings and options, for ruling)

22 Nov 2026. **The build is on hold until you rule.** The roadmap's rule for this phase: "If the book overturns the plan, hold and give the owner two or three designs." The book overturns it. Nothing was built and no line was written for the game.

I read the books myself for this one (no lore readers). Every quote has its file and line. Short names: **B3P1** = Book Three, Part One Ch1-4; **B3P2** = Book Three, Part Two Ch5-8; **B3P3** = Book Three, Part Three Ch9-12; **V2, V3, V6, V7** = World Bibles Vol II, III, VI, VII. The manuscript outranks every volume (V6:4), and Vol VI is the manuscript's for the Rift.

## 1. What the plan proposed

Phase 89 (line 829) and Phase 90 (line 568), written before the books were re-read place by place:

- A descent "by the rope (with the Quartermaster's leave), then by ledge and crack and the iron rungs of some earlier work (older than the Watch)".
- **The Shelf** as a haven where the player rests at the fire.
- **The Under-Shelves** [PROPOSED then]: "ledges, caves and old stairs on the Rift wall, where things and people fell and stayed. Gravewells drift on them."
- A keeper, **the Unfinished** [PROPOSED then]: a Throat-Wraith "made of many unfinished sentences", because "the Rift wall is where troopers fall, and troopers fall talking".
- Lamps at Tower Nine's buttress, the Shelf's fire and the last rung. The rope as the shortcut.

## 2. What the books say

**When.** The game shows Harvestmonth 419. Harvestmonth is the tenth month and Ashfall the eleventh (V7:37-38). The book's company reaches Null-Watch in the middle of Ashfall; the Quartermaster is killed and the rope cut on 24 Ashfall (V6:27). So the game stands about a month before the book.

**The rope and the basket.**
- From Tower Nine's foot "a great buttress of rock ran out ... like a pier into a sea. And on the end of it, a timber frame. A crane." (B3P1:889)
- What goes down is "a big round wicker basket with a lid, like a fisherman's creel, packed full" (B3P1:891). Nobody climbs the rope.
- "One for every time the basket went down. For years." (B3P1:905). Vane counts 2,011 knots (B3P1:923). "He's been going down since the summer of '13" (B3P1:927).
- It is the Shelf's only supply line, "lowered nightly from the buttress below Tower Nine" (V6:162).
- The Quartermaster, Halvard Greer: "Could not read; could write his name and two words more" (V6:147). Every note says KEEP TALKING (V6:167).
- He can hear glass. Of Vane's thirty-six crates of cores: "He said he could hear them from the next table." (B3P1:931)

**The way down is glass.** "Eighty feet. That's the black rock starting now. Don't touch it. It's glass. It'll have your hand off." (B3P1:1219)

**The Shelf.**
- "A ledge of black rock running along the Rift wall like a shelf in a cupboard. Three hundred feet below the lip. A quarter of a mile long, perhaps forty yards deep at its widest, narrowing at either end to nothing ... in the middle of it, in the angle where the ledge met the wall, there was a fire." (B3P1:1243)
- People come "out of holes and lean-tos and shacks along the back of the ledge, out of the black glass itself, where there were openings cut into it, dark doorways" (B3P1:1247).
- It lies under the wall, in the lee of the guns: the dawn roar "went out over their heads and away, the way the wind goes over a man lying in a ditch" (B3P1:1509).
- How they came: "I'd come over the lip that morning with forty others in a cart. They tipped the cart ... Everybody else did [die] ... They didn't rot, you know. Nothing does, here." (B3P1:1319). Each survivor has a docket pinned to the coat: "Schedule F. Residual. Disposal at the lip." (B3P1:1343)
- Customs: you read things aloud; you say your name and what was taken; nobody is touched (V6:172-174).
- The two tins: Orrin Hale's tin box of dockets "in my hole in the wall" (B3P1:1343), and Fen's box of nearly two thousand notes "in my hole in the wall. Next to Orrin's tin" (B3P2:365).

**Under the Shelf there is no second ledge.** There is the dark, and a thing in it: "A long slow heave, from very deep, far down under the Shelf in the dark of the Rift. As if something down there had rolled over in its sleep." (B3P1:1529). When it moves it goes "up from below the Shelf, past the Shelf, through the Shelf ... as if something on the wall had called it" (B3P1:1537). The bibles do say "the upper shelves" (V3:73, V2:505), but Vol VI says of Vol II that its "Rift entry predates the Shelf entirely" (V6:4).

**The Rift is six years old.** "The Rift opened in 413, not earlier" (V6:164, a ruling).

**Who falls.** Within thirty yards of the lip "anyone who goes silent begins to go. Not pulled; relieved." (V6:118). The ones who go over are the ones who stopped talking.

**The glass.** "Neither of them could go down. That was the arithmetic too. Kaelen, because of the glass." (B3P1:1806). "Wherever he stood, he was the leak." (B3P1:967). The old man hears through it (V3:143).

**What the book's people do there, later.**
- "On the morning of Stasisday they began going down in the Quartermaster's basket one and two at a time." (B3P1:957)
- Orrin of Sable: "The scribe was down today ... She walked the Shelf with me. She wrote down every name and every missing thing." (B3P1:1343)
- Orrin, of the index he has kept three years: "I didn't know what to do with it. There was nobody to give it to." (B3P1:1934)

## 4. The Shelf in Harvestmonth 419, if it is shown at all

- **There:** Orrin Hale (they took his ability to be afraid; he walks the edge at dusk), Fen (pressed at twelve in summer 413, the first survivor, looks twelve and is eighteen), Tessa Wray, Bridie Cole (the Malting, '17; about eight months with child), Maud Teller, Hob.
- **Not there yet:** the twenty-two Hollows. They "came over three weeks ago" as of the middle of Ashfall (B3P1:1353), so at the very end of Harvestmonth or just after.
- **The ledge is whole:** the hundred and fifty yards that are taken in the book (B3P1:1553) are still there, with Bridie's hollow three yards from the edge.
- **The knots:** fewer than 2,011. About 1,975 to 2,000, one for each night.
- **Nobody of the book's company** is named or known.

## 5. Three whole designs

### Design A: the wall keeps its secret (nothing below the lip)

The player never goes down and never learns what is down there by being told. The place is the Watch's side of it.

- **What is built:** the Quartermaster's stores at Tower Nine's foot (the book: he "went to the stores, and found a basket", V6:147), and a night on the wall-walk between Towers Five and Six.
- **What the player does:** errands for an old man who will not say what they are for: flour, lamp-oil, a blanket, a flask. He cannot read; the player may read a thing aloud for him. If he comes to trust the player, he lets them stand on the wall one night. The basket goes down as a gold point; three hundred feet down a second light answers. Nobody explains it. Two-Eleven talks about the dog, louder.
- **The glass arm:** he hears it (B3P1:931), and will not have the player near his stores at night. A glassed player gets the errands and never the wall.
- **Keeper, lamps, shortcut:** none new. This part of the Rift has no keeper; say so in the record.
- **For it:** breaks nothing. It makes the book's first sight of the basket land harder for a player who then reads Book Three.
- **Against it:** it is small, and it is not an underground. The Rift's "biggest underground in the world" goes unbuilt.

### Design B: the Shelf, as the book has it, a month early (your exception to your own rule)

Only you can allow this one, because it does what the book's company does later.

- **The way down:** in the basket, never by the rope. The Quartermaster talks the player down by the foot: fifty feet, eighty where the black glass starts, two hundred where you see the Shelf (B3P1:1215-1219). The player is a knot.
- **The glass arm:** refused, flatly. He hears it. A glassed player gets Design A's night on the wall and no more.
- **The place:** the ledge as written. A quarter of a mile, forty yards at its widest, the fire in the angle, holes and lean-tos and the doorways cut in the glass, the narrow ends, the drop. Whole, with no Hollows.
- **What the player does:** says their name and what was taken (the player chooses, or says "nothing", and they are kind about it). Reads aloud what is handed to them. Touches nobody. Sits at the fire. Sees the two tins and is not given them. Hears, at the dead hour, the thing turn over under the rock.
- **What is never done:** no index, no drawing of the Hall, no count taken, no promise made. Orrin tells a traveller nothing a traveller could carry. That keeps "nobody to give it to" true: a traveller is not somebody who can do anything about it.
- **Keeper:** none. The Shelf is a haven only. The fire is its lamp. No shortcut but the basket.
- **For it:** the Shelf is the best place in the Rift and the books give enough to build it truly.
- **Against it:** it is the book's own scene, shown first. A reader who plays before reading has met Fen and Orrin before Lyra does.

### Design C: the tip (another stretch of the lip, and the Unfinished) [PROPOSED]

The Shelf stays unseen and unnamed. The underground is not under the Shelf; it is where the carts are emptied.

- **From the books:** "Disposal at the lip" (B3P1:1343); "put in carts and sent east under escort and tipped over the lip at the Watch" (B3P1:1353); "They tipped the cart" (B3P1:1319). So there is a place on the lip where the Office's carts are tipped. The book never shows it.
- **Invented [PROPOSED]:** the tipping stage itself, north along the wall from Tower Nine: a ramp of sleepers, a gate the Watch does not look at, the ruts of six years of carts, and at its foot a cut in the rock (a sally-way under the wall for the carts, six years old, the Office's own work) that is the phase's underground.
- **The keeper, the Unfinished:** a Throat-Wraith as Vol II has it (V2:482), made by the right people this time. The ones tipped from the carts were talking when they went. It has many sentences. On that rock silence is what kills (V6:118), and a man with a wraith on his jaw cannot stop talking: so the Watch has left it alone for years, because it has never lost anybody to the edge. To resolve it the player finds how each sentence was going to end and says it. "The sentence is tender." (V2:483)
- **Where the endings are found [PROPOSED]:** on what the carts left behind at the stage (a shoe, a letter, a ration card), never on the dockets, which are on the Shelf.
- **Lamps and shortcut:** a talking-post at the gate; the cart-road back to the west gate.
- **For it:** an underground and a keeper that are the Rift's own; the Shelf's secret is kept; nothing the book does is done.
- **Against it:** nearly all of it is invented. It puts a named horror at the top of the fall the Rendered survived, and you may not want that place shown.

## 6. What I would choose, and why

**Design A now, and Design C if you want an underground here.** A breaks nothing and can be built in one phase. C is the only way I can see to keep "the underground" and "the keeper" without going below the lip, and it needs your yes to its inventions first. B is the richest, and it is yours alone to allow.
