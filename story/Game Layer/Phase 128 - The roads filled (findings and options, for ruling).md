# Phase 128: the roads filled (findings and options, for ruling)

26 Nov 2026. The plan (the roadmap from the audit, 23 Nov) asked for "something to meet, read or decide every few hundred paces, each from the books' own country", the emptiest places raised, an errand for Abel Sedge, and the road-walkers, grass tufts and Old Marrable redrawn. Two readers went through the four books and the bibles for the four roads and the four places before anything was designed. **Nothing is built yet.** The books changed the plan, so this is the findings, what can be built without a ruling, and the five decisions that are the owner's.

The readers' full briefs, with every quotation by file and line, are kept with the session (`LORE-128-roads.md`, 323 lines; `LORE-128-places.md`, 260 lines). B1 is the Book One manuscript; V2 is World Bible Vol II.

A shrine, a toll, a gibbet, a wayside grave: no book has one in Harvestmonth. The graves along the Greenroot road are Book Four's (after the Greening, in the snow) and must not be there yet.

## 2. Where the game and the books disagree (found by the readers)

1. **Greenroot is empty in the game; the books never say so.** The game: "Greenroot. Tables laid, doors shut, kettles cold, and nobody." (`js/parish.js`; also `js/eaves78.js`, `js/bearings82b.js`). The gazetteer: "GREENROOT HAMLET: Four hundred souls, a mill, a chapel with a silent reed. Primary site of the overgrowth cases." (V2:34). In Book One people leave Greenroot only when the Greening comes down the road, after the Rite (B1:3792, 3970). The audit said the hamlet is empty "as the story has it"; no line in the books supports that.
2. **The toll board on the Greenroot road** has no book behind it. The nearest line is the Consortium coming "up the Greenroot road with forty wagons of grain and a paper" in the spring of 413 (B1:849).
3. **"The road to the Redoubt with its ninety posts"** (the wall's west gate, `js/rift100.js`): no book has ninety posts. V2 has "ninety-day rotations" only.
4. **"The Hundred Hollows"** as a place name sits beside the books' own Hollows, who are people ("She came without any trouble at all, because Hollows do.", B1:5034). The pits are not people.
5. **The returners on the drove road** (a handcart, a fire, people coming back): the books have nobody coming back from anywhere in Harvestmonth 419; the first to leave go after the Greening.
6. **Abel Sedge** is in no book. Dorran Sedge is (Book Four), and the game has Dunna Sedge too. Nothing relates them; that is fine, but a link would be an invention.

## 3. What can be built without a ruling (Phase 128a)

Each from a book line, none pre-empting what the books do later.

- **The mail-cart on the Long Common** (the emptiest stretch of any road). A cart, a horse and a carter [name PROPOSED] who has a letter for a Captain Vane and has been "chasing" him a fortnight. Until Driftday he asks whether you have seen a skyship; from Driftday night the letter is with the Office's clerk at Oakhaven, as the book has it, and he says so. The traveller never carries the letter: the clerk hands it up the ladder in the book.
- **The brazier at the foot of Tower Six**, lit, with one of the battery crew warming their hands (the game's own trooper, moved a few steps; no new person).
- **An errand for Abel Sedge**, the turf-cutter: square turves for a charcoal clamp. The book: "A clamp was only a heap of cordwood built into a careful dome, packed round with turf and earth" (B1:604), and Kaelen "packed turf over two of its vents" (B1:608). Abel cuts the turf; the clearing's four clamps are where it goes. The traveller carries a load to the clearing's woodpile and comes back with Abel's pay [PROPOSED]. Nothing of Kaelen's scene is touched.
- **Old Marrable redrawn.** She is 26 by 30 pixels, the smallest person in the game. Drawn again in code at twice the size: the drover's coat, the shawl, the stick across her knees, the white hair, a face. She stays seated.
- **The grass tufts.** The 3D view already sharpens every close sprite (Phase 107, built after the visual sweep that found them blocky), and the road-walkers are the moving paintings already. The tufts' own pictures are 40 by 34 and still read as blocks at arm's length; drawn again at twice the size.

## 4. The owner's five decisions

1. **Greenroot: empty, or four hundred souls?** (a) Keep it empty as the game's own, and say so in Vol VIII as a departure from the gazetteer. (b) People it: a mill, a chapel with a silent reed, the overgrowth cases, folk on the road to Oakhaven. That is a place-sized phase of its own, and the three lines that say "nobody" are reworded. (c) Between: the hamlet is shut up and frightened, not empty; a few faces at windows, one door that opens. I recommend (c) now and (b) as its own phase, because the gazetteer is the weakest tier of canon but nothing in the books empties the hamlet before the Greening.
2. **The toll board**: keep as the game's own (it does no harm), or take it down. I recommend keeping it.
3. **"Ninety posts"**: reword the gate's line to say only "the road to the Redoubt". I recommend the reword; it is one line and its French.
4. **"The Hundred Hollows"**: keep the name, or rename the place so that "Hollows" stays the books' word for the memory-lost (the Hundred Pits, the Delves). I recommend asking nothing of the player and keeping it unless it bothers you: it is on signs, the map and in voiced lines.
5. **The drove road's emptiness**: the books give it nothing, so anything added there is invention. Leave it as it is (the Fold's stones were added in Phase 113), or shorten the 85-cell stretch. I recommend leaving it.

## 5. What is left out, and why

- No wayside graves, no refugees, no snow, no Church column on the road, no hounds at the tree-line: all later in the books.
- Davin Hale the carter must not be the mail-cart's carter: he is in the Boar on Hearthday and dead by the clerk's ledger (B1:1496, 1622).
- The Chalk Cliffs, the Deep Grove and the Anchorage: no book names them as places; the Deep Grove is empty by the plan's own design ("the one place on any road with no one in it"). The Anchorage has a real question (Book Four's nine chains against the game's capstan) that belongs to its own look. They wait for a part two.
- The rumours and notice boards: a count of what is hidden against what the boards point at is in the readers' brief; it becomes part two with the places.
