# Phase 22 — New In-Game Text: The Sheep-Walk (the Watch Downs region)

*Written for review, Oct 2026. Every line below is new and **[PROPOSED]**. It follows the Phase 20–21 Author Rulings: the Watch Downs become a region, nothing new joins Oakhaven, and the sheep went into the Hedge's wool and were eaten in the two shut years. The code is `js/sheepwalk22.js`. All text comes from its `TX` table, in the order below.*

## What is new, in short [PROPOSED]

- **Wethercombe**, a chalk combe south of the Watch Downs and west of Oakhaven, reached only by a new stile in the Downs' south hedge. It holds the farmsteads, barns and orchards painted on Oakhaven's skyline. On Pylon Hill, at the bearing the clearing's panorama paints it (2.07), stands the snapped iron pylon.
- **The pylon** was a Consortium of Bores tally-line station (WESTERN TALLY LINE — STATION 6). Its singing wire carried the bore's pitch; the line went dead at both ends in 417 ("LINE DEAD BOTH ENDS. HEDGE."). The wire still hums, and the hum draws hounds.
- **The sheep.** As ruled, the Downs flocks were shorn for the Hedge and eaten inside it. New here: Tamar Farrant, seventh of the Farrants who had the sheep-walk, has brought eleven Rust Coast ewes and a tup up the hill. Captain Vane carried them in the Drift-Kestrel's hold from Cantabile "and charged me for the smell". The player learns the truth about the Downs flocks from Tamar (the ruling's suggested shepherd's family), and it goes in the logbook.
- **The Bellwether**, the drovers' inn on the sheep-walk, shut the night the Hedge closed and reopened by Gideon Thrale the week Tamar came back.
- **The Chalk Ram**, a hill figure cut in Wethercombe's west scarp, grown over for ten years. Folklore (Kit's gran): scour it every seven years or it walks off the hill and takes the flock with it.
- **The fold-fires**: the Long Down beacon (the Downs' existing cairn), Hob's Knap and Ewe Barrow. They were shepherds' fires, not war fires: lit at the back-end of the year so every flock came down to the light. Absalom Rudd lit them forty years running.
- **Ewe Barrow**: a long barrow with a sarsen door, "not meant to move". Its inside is Phase 22b (below).
- **Mutton**: salt mutton (the Reach's staple), broth, chops, pie, hotpot and a roast leg. They are sold at the Boar & Dulcimer (Hetty has a supper menu now), Brenna Loft's, the market provisioner's board, Captain Vane's stores, Fenwick Sloe's chandlery, the Bellwether and the Farrants.
- **Changed line**: once the flock is home, the Downs finger-post's "There are no sheep" becomes "There are Farrant ewes on it again, and the turf is short because of them." Until then the original line stands, as the ruling asks.

## Questions for sleeves

1. Vane carrying ewes in the Kestrel's hold: does that fit his runs (garrison crates to the quarry)?
2. The pylon as a Consortium tally-line: keep it, or should it be something else?
3. The Chalk Ram's seven-year scouring: is that the right kind of folklore for the Reach?
4. Hetty's menu line "supper is always mutton": it agrees with salt mutton as the staple. Is fresh mutton at the Boar plausible before Tamar's flock? (The dishes don't say where the meat came from.)

# Phase 22b — What Bleats in the Wood

*Written for review, Oct 2026. Every line below is new and **[PROPOSED]**. The code is `js/hanger22.js`; all text comes from its `TX` table. French is in `tools/i18n/p22-sheepwalk.tsv`.*

## What is new, in short [PROPOSED]

- **The Hanger**, a beech wood on the steep ground behind Wethercombe, reached by a gap in the combe's south hedge. Fog, the charcoal-burners' ring, a still pool, the wool-tree hung with fleece, and the Woolwight's nest.
- **The Woolwight**: stolen fleeces stitched over something that is not a sheep. It bleats in the woods at night, comes only after dark on the region's open maps (Wethercombe, the Downs, the Hanger), keeps out of firelight, and is gone by dawn. When it attacks, the wool splits and a skinless bull's skull bursts out on a neck of raw meat, jaw wide open (owner's request). It is never saved. **Lambkins** are its small ones. **The Old Fleece** is the oldest, and waits in Ewe Barrow.
- **Ewe Barrow** opens at last: the sarsen moves when the Chalk Ram is white again and the three fold-fires burn. Inside: traps, lantern-bells for the only light, grave goods (a torc, amber, a gold lunula), the barrow helm, the leaf-blade and the barrow shears, and in the last chamber the Old Fleece and Kit Farrant.
- **Bleating in the Wood** (Gideon Thrale → Absalom Rudd → the nest → Tamar): find what is taking the hedge-wool. Tamar pays with the camp kit and a fishing line.
- **The Lambing at Night** (Tamar): Kit goes missing; his cap is in the nest; his tracks go to the barrow. Bring him home; Tamar gives you her grandfather's crook.
- **Camp anywhere**: the camp kit pitches a fire and bedroll on any open outdoor map, never in town. Sleep eight hours, roast what you caught, cook, fish, or strike camp. A second camp moves the first.
- **Fishing**: with a line, fish any water within a cast. Keep quiet; strike when the line jumps. Trout and perch by day, a glass eel at night, and sometimes a boot.

## Questions for sleeves

1. The Woolwight: is "stolen fleeces over something that is not a sheep" the right amount of explanation, or should it stay a pure mystery?
2. The Old Fleece's shroud as armour: is wearing it too grim for the player character?
3. Camping is refused only in towns and a few set places. Should dungeons and the Hedge refuse it too? (Indoor maps already do.)
4. The glass eel (night only): keep it as a fantasy fish, or a plain eel?
