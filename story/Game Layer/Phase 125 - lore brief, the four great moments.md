## Read this first (what will trip a designer)

1. **The "ten heartbeats" are at the rope in ch 12, not under the cutter in ch 21.** The words "about ten
   heartbeats" and the numerals One to Ten are at MS 3724-3774. Chapter 21 is *titled* "Ten Heartbeats" but the text
   of chapter 21 never counts ten heartbeats and never asks anyone to keep silent for ten. What chapter 21 does give
   Vane: the wheel (5576), "Don't cut it" (5582-5586) and "in four seconds" (5586). The game's silent ten-heartbeat
   count under the cutter is the game's own addition (`js/cutter68.js` line 15 says "[PROPOSED: the count is the
   chapter's title, played]"). See Scene 3B.
2. **The needle never goes through her voice.** In the book Kaelen fakes it: the needle goes through a pinch of
   skin under the jaw, with the glass thumb (MS 3086), and the Inquisitor never sees (MS 3098, 3110). Lyra's
   own plan said "DON'T THREAD IT" (MS 2497). So "the Inquisitor sees" is not a thing the book has; it is the
   game's grey Echo.
3. **The Hold in the book is not four hours.** Four hours is what Kaelen and Lyra expect (MS 3126, 2453). The
   Hold in the text lasts roughly one engineer's walk and fifty-one seconds of bolts, then the lock throws.
   The four-hour Hold only happens in the *untaken* thread, Echo I (MS 6459).
4. **The coda's words are not in the book.** The book gives three lyric lines, the naming, and the fact that the
   close is a two-voice part. It gives no words or notes for the coda itself (MS 6092 is the whole of it).
5. **Sable reads Vane three names in ch 16 (MS 4552-4572), and she reads them from the hold of the Kestrel with
   the Ledger open and unlooked-at.** There is no "three names" scene anywhere else in Book One. The phrase
   "three names" only turns up in Book Three (Part Three Ch9-12, line 491), which is a different matter.

## SCENE 1A. Kaelen at the needle (the Rite at the quarry)

**Where it is.** Ends ch 9 (`09 Beneath.md`) at MS 3034-3046 and runs through the first third of ch 10
(`10 The Siphon Casing.md`) MS 3062-3110. The plan for it is earlier: ch 7 (`07 Flambeau.md`) MS 2483-2523;
the slate talk is ch 9 MS 2874-2906. Setting: "The Root-vault, four hundred feet beneath the Oakhaven quarry.
Hearthday, noon." (MS 3058).

**The book's own words.**
- The case: "the brass collar, and the four tuning pegs, and the ivory spool of copper wire, and the long silver needle curved like a new moon, lying in its own fitted groove." (3040)
- "'Engineer,' said Ignatius Crane. And Kaelen Vance came across the platform and knelt down on the boards in front of her stool and picked up the needle, and Lyra looked at his hands. They were shaking." (3042-3046)
- "He knelt on the boards with the needle in his fingers and looked at them as though they belonged to someone else ... They had never once, in all of that, shaken." (3064)
- Lyra: "she turned her head a little to the side, and lifted her chin, and offered him her throat. *Get on with it.* He got on with it." (3068-3072)
- "'Closer, Engineer,' said Ignatius Crane, from the catwalk. 'I must be able to see.'" (3074)
- "Kaelen shifted a half-pace on his knees, angling his shoulder so that the Inquisitor had a clear view of the collar, and the needle, and the ivory spool — and not, if the light stayed where it was, of the place beneath the jaw where his left hand would be. The glass hand. He held it flat against the side of her neck, thumb along the line of the muscle, the way the Tuning Men had done it. The glass was cold. She flinched and made herself still." (3076)
- "He set the collar around her throat first, as the rubric required ... He drew it to the second notch, which was one notch looser than it should have been, and buckled it there." (3078)
- "The Rite called for the needle to go in above the collar, at the notch of the throat, angled up and back into the fold of the voice itself ..." (3082)
- "Kaelen put the needle in a fold of skin. He did it fast, because slow would look wrong: caught a pinch of skin along the side of her throat under the shadow of her jaw with the glass thumb, drove the needle through it, brought it out, and drew the copper wire after it with a little jerk exactly like the one a Tuning Man would use to seat the wire on the fold." (3084-3086)
- "Blood came at once ... *Good*, Kaelen thought, with the sick clarity of a man doing arithmetic. *It has to bleed.*" (3088-3090)
- "He knotted the wire at the left eyelet, and carried it across, and knotted it at the right, so that it lay in a shallow bight against her throat ... He took the tuning key from the case and set the pegs — one, two, three, four — turning each a half-turn, and with every turn the wire tightened against the skin, and Lyra's eyes watered and did not close." (3092)
- "The Inquisitor came down the ladder from the catwalk ... crouched, and looked at the collar from a foot away with his hands on his knees and the censer's chain wrapped around his fist. Kaelen did not move. He could hear his own arm ringing under the felt, high and fast." (3094-3096)
- "'Blood,' said Ignatius. He sounded satisfied. 'Good. It should bleed a little at the seating. The Foundry girls used to bleed for days.' ... 'Speak, daughter.'" (3098)
- Lyra, "every syllable ironed onto one pitch ... 'I. Am. Fortunate.' One hundred and seventy-four, all the way through. Not a hair off." (3102-3106)
- "Kaelen had to put the heel of his right hand against the boards for a moment." (3108)
- "'She is,' said Ignatius Crane, pleased, and blessed her with two fingers, and went back up the ladder." (3110)

**The beats, in order.**
1. Crane calls "Engineer"; Kaelen crosses the platform and kneels in front of Lyra's stool, takes the needle. His hands shake (3042-3046, 3062-3064).
2. Lyra offers her throat (3068). He starts (3072).
3. Crane orders him closer to see; Kaelen turns his shoulder so the collar, needle and spool are in Crane's view and the glass hand, under her jaw, is not (3074-3076).
4. The glass hand lies flat on the side of her neck, thumb along the muscle, as the Tuning Men do it; it is cold (3076).
5. He buckles the collar at the second notch, one looser than it should be (3078).
6. He puts the needle through a pinch of skin, with the glass thumb holding the pinch, fast, and pulls the wire with the Tuning Men's little jerk (3084-3086). Lyra goes rigid, knuckles white on the stool, makes no sound (3086).
7. Blood runs. He tells himself it has to bleed (3088-3090).
8. He knots the wire at the left eyelet, carries it across, knots at the right, so it lies against the skin looking seated (3092).
9. He tunes the four pegs, one to four, half a turn each; the wire tightens on the skin (3092).
10. Crane comes down and looks from a foot away (3094-3096). Kaelen does not move.
11. Crane: "Speak, daughter." Lyra's eyes go to Kaelen for one heartbeat, then she speaks the flat 174: "I. Am. Fortunate." (3098-3104)
12. Kaelen steadies himself with the heel of his right hand on the boards (3108). Crane blesses her and leaves (3110).

**What Kaelen perceives that others cannot.** His arm: "He could hear his own arm ringing under the felt, high and fast" (3096). The tuning of the wire is judged by the Inquisitor's eye; Kaelen has "the finest ear for failure in forty years" (Lyra's words, 2503). Lyra reads the Rite's anatomy: "you learned the anatomy in the second year at the Conservatory, in a room with a sheep's larynx on a tray" (3082).

**What can go wrong, in the book's own terms.**
- Slow would look wrong (3086): that is why he is fast.
- Crane "will be watching the needle" (2501) and "would not miss a needle" (2511). The book never says what Crane does if he sees.
- If the needle went through the fold of her voice: "they'll cut the collar away and draw the wire back out ... It doesn't come out, Lyra. It tears." (1169; also 795, the girl whose wire tore).
- Kaelen's own fear: "he did not know whether his hands would be steady, or whether, for the first time in his life, they would not" (2511). They are not steady (3046).
- The one wrong-thread version the book prints: in Echo I the same step is done "and his hands do not shake at all, because the woman on the stool is perfectly calm and that steadies him" (6453). That thread is the Wearer on the stool.

**Who else is there, and where they stand.**
- Lyra on a stool, "ten feet from the bore's collar" (2998), white shift, hands gripping the stool edge (3086).
- Ignatius Crane: on the catwalk above (3034, 3074); comes down the ladder to the platform and crouches a foot away (3094-3096); goes back up (3110).
- Factor Vosk: at the platform rail, green lenses (2992, 3186). Her lenses show pitch as colour (712).
- Tobiah Marsh: gauge platform (3116).
- The Church sisters; two bell-helmed Legionnaires; the pump-men at the channels (2924, 2972).
- The bore ten feet away, grinding so loud "men had to shout into each other's ears" (2998). Lyra cannot be heard anyway (3068).

**The hero's hands in the scene.**
- Left: glass from fingertips to shoulder (482), "no feeling except the one, and that one never switched off" and "no good for fine work anymore. It could still hold, and it could still hit" (608). In the Rite it is the hand flat on her neck (3076) and its thumb pinches the skin (3086). Under the felt sleeve (3096; the sleeve is laced at the shoulder, 646).
- Right: not described holding the needle. The book says "the needle in his fingers" (3064) and later "the heel of his right hand against the boards" (3108). **Which hand holds the needle is not in the book.**
- Tuning key from the case (3092).

**What the game plays there now.** `js/rite43.js` (header lines 1-31; needle 471-508). The code says plainly that "the needle, the coil and the speaking-tube are Kaelen's hands" (line 5): Kaelen is an NPC at the stool. The player, a stranger, stands in the line between Crane's eye and the glass hand for 12 seconds (`NEEDLE_SECS`, line 280; `blocked()` 478-482; tick 486-508). If Crane sees for 4 seconds (`NEEDLE_SEEN`), the game shows a grey Echo and resets (lines 495-498, text 144-146).

**Not in the book (a designer will be tempted).**
- Crane seeing the trick, or what he does about it (lines 144, 495 invent it; the book has no such beat).
- A timer on the needle, or a count of seconds. (Only the *bolts* have a number, 40/51/180.)
- Which hand holds the needle; how many rotations of the tuning key beyond "a half-turn" per peg.
- Any dialogue between Kaelen and Lyra at the stool (she cannot speak; he says nothing; only looks, 3100).
- Lyra "drawing breath" or humming during the wire. She makes no sound (3086).

## SCENE 1B. Kaelen at the eight bolts

**Where it is.** Ch 10 `10 The Siphon Casing.md`, MS 3142-3204 (the walk to the drum, the plate, the coil). The plan
and the numbers are set up earlier: ch 5 MS 1817; ch 9 MS 2882, 2888. "The bolts later": the press bolts, ch 19
`19 The Tuning Room.md`, MS 5102-5116. The only place the book shows what happens when the bolts go wrong is Echo I, MS 6459.

**The book's own words.**
- Ch 5, the port: "in the drum's flank, sealed by a plate and eight bolts, was the phase port: the place where the Sequence's timing entered the machine, and the place where, if you knew how — if you had written the book — it could be made to fail." (1817) "A reverse-phase coil, seated in that port at the wrong moment, would throw the lock." (1819)
- Ch 9, the slate: "*8 BOLTS ON THE PORT PLATE. 40 SECONDS IF THEY DON'T WATCH ME. 3 MINUTES IF THEY DO.*" (2882) Lyra: "*THEN DON'T LET THEM WATCH YOU.*" (2884) Kaelen: "*THE HOLD STARTS WHEN THE HEAD IS ONE SPAN OFF THE CASING. I'LL BE AT THE DRUM ON THE COLLAR WALK. WHEN I MOVE, SING.*" (2888)
- Kaelen reaches the plan: "Kaelen picked up his toolbag and walked along the collar walk to the drum." (3172)
- His cover: "'Slippage on the lock,' said Kaelen. 'I can hear it.' He could not, and the boy would know that he could not; the lock was singing like a bell in tune." (3178-3180) "'Spanner. Sit on the rail there and call the pitch to me every ten seconds.'" (3184)
- The bolts: "The plate over the phase port had eight bolts. He had promised himself forty seconds and he took fifty-one, kneeling on the collar walk with the drum's warm brass against his shoulder and the bore grinding a yard from his ear, and the whole time the boy sat on the rail calling numbers and Lyra sang and the Inquisitor's chain creaked and the Factor watched from the platform rail with her green lenses, and nobody stopped him, because he was the supervising engineer, and he was doing what supervising engineers do." (3186)
- "The plate came off. He laid it down quietly." (3188) "Inside: the timing gear of the Vance Sequence, brass and glass and hair-fine wire ... It had been built from his drawings. He knew every tooth of it the way you know a word you have said ten thousand times." (3190)
- The coil: "He put his hand into his toolbag and took out the reverse-phase coil." (3192) Tobiah: "'Sir, what's that?'" (3194) "He had perhaps two seconds and one sentence." (3200) "He said, 'You'll want to hold onto something.' And he seated the coil in the port." (3202-3204)
- The lock: "The lock threw. ... the Carrow Bore's harmonic lock ... went suddenly and violently out of phase with itself. The drum screamed. The chains came up taut and rang like a struck anvil, and the whole bore-head, thirty tons of iron with its teeth buried in living wood, tried to turn and could not ... and the crown of glass teeth snapped off inside the Root with a crack that went through the chamber like a bell breaking." (3208-3210)
- Then: "He got up off the collar walk, and put his glass hand on the drum, and shouted ... 'LYRA! *SING IT DOWN!*'" (3226-3228)
- The later bolts (ch 19): "The press itself was four hundred pounds of frame and fork and ampoule socket, held down by eight coach-bolts through the stone bed. Kaelen had the plate off the first bolt-box in ninety seconds. He had cracked ribs, an arm that had rung flat since the choir-room, and a spanner, and he worked the way he always worked, which was the only thing he had that still functioned properly." (5102) Then the archive door: "'Bolts,' said Kaelen. ... 'Bolts, Marsh.' But his hands had already stopped." (5110-5116)

**The beats, in order (Rite).**
1. At the moment the Hold begins he stands at the calibration rail with his toolbag at his feet, listening to Lyra (3126). He feels the Root hear her "in the arm before the gauges saw it" (3130).
2. He understands from the margin of Revision Seven that "She was the fuse" (3144-3160). He decides to move (3172).
3. He walks along the collar walk to the drum with the toolbag (3172).
4. He tells Tobiah "Slippage on the lock. I can hear it" and sends him to sit on the rail and call the pitch every ten seconds (3178-3184). This keeps Tobiah's eyes on the gauge.
5. He kneels on the collar walk, drum brass at his shoulder, and takes the eight bolts off in fifty-one seconds (3186).
6. He lays the plate down quietly (3188).
7. He takes the coil from the toolbag (3192), answers Tobiah in one sentence (3202), seats the coil in the port (3204).
8. The lock throws; the teeth snap; Crane shouts for the hold (3208-3218).
9. He stands, puts the glass hand on the drum, and shouts the cue (3226-3228).

**What Kaelen perceives that others cannot.** The "slippage" is a lie (3180). The lock "singing like a bell in tune" is what he actually hears. "Through the glass in his arm, Kaelen heard the lullaby start up again" (3242). Before that: "He felt it in the arm before the gauges saw it" (3130) and "felt the thing in the stone stop singing ... and turn, and listen" (3138).

**What can go wrong, in the book's own terms.**
- "3 MINUTES IF THEY DO [watch me]" (2882). The book does not show them watching.
- The Factor's lenses on the plate: the book says only that she "watched from the platform rail with her green lenses, and nobody stopped him" (3186). What she would have done is not said. (After the coil goes in she "turned them all at once", 3248; "which meant she had made up her mind about him somewhere between the coil going in and the drum screaming".)
- If the Hold fails: "On failure of the hold, for any cause, the Sequence is not to be halted. Slippage is to be absorbed by the source" (3054, 3150) ... "If the hold failed, the Sequence would not stop. The slippage ... would go into the source. Into her." (3156)
- The book's own wrong thread is Echo I: Kaelen waits "for a signal from an ally who is not there, and when he finally understands — at four hours and eleven minutes ... he goes for the drum anyway, and gets five bolts out of eight before Factor Vosk's Legionnaires put him on the boards." (6459) That is the one line in the whole of Book One that tells you what the wrong bolts look like: five of eight, then the Legionnaires.
- If the coil goes in and the Root is not sung down: "*Now*, thought Kaelen. *Now, or it does what the Light did.*" (3224)

**Who else is there, and where they stand.**
- Tobiah Marsh on the rail by the drum, calling pitch every ten seconds (3184, 3186).
- Lyra on her stool singing 174 (3186), ten feet from the collar (2998).
- Crane on the catwalk, chain creaking (3186, 3216).
- Vosk on the platform rail (3186).
- The Legionnaires at the rail; the bore "grinding a yard from his ear" (3186).

**The hero's hands.**
- Left glass hand: not mentioned during the bolts. It is set on the drum at 3226 afterwards, and later fists the Golem knee (3254) and drinks the second Golem (3264).
- Right hand with a spanner (3182-3184: "Spanner"). The book says "put his hand into his toolbag" without saying which (3192).
- The glass hand is "no good for fine work" (608), so the book does not say it turns bolts. **Which hand works the spanner is not in the book**, but the glass arm's own description makes the right hand the likely one; do not state it as canon.

**What the game plays there now.** `js/rite43.js` header lines 16-20, stage 4 at 510-535 (`tickHold`), constants line 280 (`BOLT_SECS = 51`, `WATCH_LIMIT = 18`). Kaelen is the NPC at the drum (`R.kaelenAt = 'drum'`, line 500). The player hums beside the stool, near Vosk, so her lenses stay on the player not the drum. The code names "forty seconds" as the book's, which is right; "3 minutes if they do" is from the slate (MS 2882) and the game does not use it.

**Not in the book.**
- A timer the player sees counting down the fifty-one seconds, or Kaelen's count of bolts as he turns each (the book gives the total and the duration only).
- The Factor looking at the plate and what follows (the code's 164 "step away from the drum" before the coil is invented; in the book she says it only after the coil is in, 3250).

## SCENE 2A. Lyra holding the note (the Hold) and singing it down

**Where it is.** Ch 10 `10 The Siphon Casing.md`, MS 3114-3140 (the Hold), 3216-3244 (the fifth). Earlier: ch 9 MS 2998-3030 (what she hears under the bore), ch 9 MS 2892-2898 (what she was told to sing).

**The book's own words.**
- Where she sits: "They set a stool for her on the casing platform, ten feet from the bore's collar, where the chain-drums came down and the noise was so great that men had to shout into each other's ears." (2998)
- What she hears: "Five notes. Down, and down, and up, and a little down again, and back to the beginning." (3008-3010) "It was a lullaby." (3012) "And it kept losing the tune." (3016) "Every twelfth or thirteenth time around, one of the five notes would come late, or flat, or not at all ... a terrible small pause, like somebody catching themselves at the top of a stair" (3018)
- Her understanding, "in the space of about four heartbeats" (3024): that it was not a spirit, law or engine (3026); that it "had been singing a lullaby to something, or someone, or everything, for a very long time, and it was so tired it could not hold the tune" (3028); and that "in a little while, they were going to ask it — in her voice — whether it could stop." (3030)
- The cue: "THE HOLD STARTS WHEN THE HEAD IS ONE SPAN OFF THE CASING" (2888). "The Hold began at noon. Tobiah Marsh called the mark from the gauge platform — 'Head at one span! One span and holding!' — and the bore-master brought the chains down another notch, and the grinding changed its note, and up on the catwalk the Inquisitor lifted the censer. 'One note,' he said. 'One law. One hand.'" (3114-3118)
- "And Lyra Ashgrove, sitting on a stool on a platform four hundred feet under Oakhaven in a white shift with blood on the collar, began to sing. One hundred and seventy-four. Clear, and hoarse, and absolutely steady." (3122-3124)
- Kaelen's reading of her: "she was very good — better than any of the Foundry girls had been, because she was not a wired instrument but a Cantor, and a Cantor can hold a note the way a mason holds a plumb-line — and ... she had four hours of it ahead of her with a torn throat and a wire through her skin, and that she would not stop, and that this was somehow worse." (3126)
- The Root answers: "Pitch coming up,' said Tobiah ... 'It's coming up to her, sir ... it's matching.'" (3132) "The lullaby stopped." (3136)
- The coil goes in, and Lyra is still singing: "Because the note *was* still going ... one hundred and seventy-four, hoarse and thin, and unbroken, because she was a Cantor and the last thing a Cantor does is stop." (3220)
- The cue is called: "LYRA! *SING IT DOWN!*" (3228). "She let it fall. He heard the moment the note bent: the Root note sliding down a whole fifth, slow as a leaf coming off a branch, in a torn hoarse voice with blood on the collar of her shift — and then climbing, and falling again, the small patient village trick out of the margin of a dead man's notebook. *Down. Go back where you came from.*" (3232-3234)
- "The Root's pitch, which had come up to her and locked there like a hand closing, *slipped*." (3238) "And then, very slowly, like a man who has stood up too fast and sits down again, it let go of the question." (3240)

**The beats, in order.**
1. She sits on the stool, hands in her lap (3000), and hears the five-note lullaby under the bore (3006-3020).
2. She understands what the Rite is for (3024-3030).
3. Crane is on the catwalk; the collar goes on and the wire is seated (see Scene 1A). She speaks "I. Am. Fortunate." at 174 (3104).
4. At "Head at one span" she begins to sing 174, "clear, and hoarse, and absolutely steady" (3122-3124). She is "a Cantor ... a mason holds a plumb-line" (3126).
5. The Root hears her within the first minute and rises to her pitch (3128-3132). The lullaby stops (3136).
6. She keeps the note while the lock throws, the drum screams, chains ring, scaffold falls, and glass runs across the platform (3208-3214). She does not stop (3220).
7. She hears Kaelen's shout (3228). She lets the note fall a whole fifth and climbs and falls again (3234).
8. The Root lets go; the lullaby starts again, badly (3240-3242).

**What Lyra perceives that others cannot.** Her pitch and her Cantor's ear: she alone hears the five notes as a lullaby "her father's hands had made in the air when he taught it to her in the reed-rows when she was four years old" (3010) and "knew that sound" from her own kitchen, humming five notes over and over (3020). Kaelen hears it through the glass (3002 "I can hear it through the glass. It breathes."). Tobiah reads it as a gauge needle (3132). Vosk sees pitch as colour (712).

**What can go wrong, in the book's own terms.**
- If the Hold fails the slippage goes into her: "She was the fuse." (3156-3160)
- If she holds *too well* the Root is relieved and lets go of its law, as the Light did: "Growth following Light" in the thread of Echo I (6459-6467): "it holds it beautifully ... for four hours and nineteen minutes ... The Root is relieved. ... It lets go." (6459-6465) This is exactly why the cue "sing it down" exists. The book's wrong move for the Hold is therefore *holding it for four hours and nobody calling the fifth.*
- Her voice: she has "not spoken in five days" (3068), "a torn throat" (3126). The hoarseness is in the book on purpose.

**Who else is there, and where they stand.** As Scene 1B: Crane on the catwalk, chain creaking (3116, 3120); Tobiah at the gauge platform / on the rail by the drum (3116, 3184); Vosk at the platform rail (3186); Kaelen at the calibration rail (3126), then the drum on the collar walk (3172); sisters and Legionnaires; twelve pump-men at the channels who "were part of the machinery" (2972-2976).

**The hero's hands.**
- On the stool: "Her hands closed on the edges of the stool until the knuckles went white" during the needle (3086). While singing: not described.
- In the pump scene earlier she lays a hand on a pump-man's wrist and hums the Root note into it: "her hands knew a body's business the way a carpenter's know wood" (2978-2984). Cupped hands for the falling fifth over Pim: "In the Narrow. Into the hollow of her own cupped hands, so the sound would go into his." (366)
- The slate and chalk: she has no slate on the platform. The slate is at the kitchen table (2876-2906); she also wrote on her own palm in ink for Rooke (2858-2864). She wipes it "before the sisters could come and see" (2906).
- A rag round her *right* hand appears in ch 4 (1396) and the "wrapped hand" is on her chest on the night of 1273. By Hearthday no wrapping is mentioned. **Whether the wrap is still on at the Rite is not in the book.**
- Her reed pipes are on pegs at home (1111, 2213: "her father's seven reed-pipes on their pegs"). No reed is at the Rite.

## Discrepancies inside Book One (quote both)

- **Days without speech:** "She had not spoken in four days." (2950, procession) vs "she had not spoken in five days" (3068, the needle) vs "the first sound she had made in six days" (5970, the coda).
- **Plan vs act, Rite:** Lyra's plan: "Leave the wire out, or thread it wrong ... so that I can still sing my own note. And when the bore comes to the casing, you seat your coil in its port, and I sing against it. Both of us. From the inside." (2503). Slate: "WHEN I MOVE, SING ... THE FALLING FIFTH." (2888, 2892). Act: Kaelen shouts "LYRA! SING IT DOWN!" (3228). All three are in the book; the act is the one that happens.
- **Third in the line:** Annet "was third in the line. She would not let go of the hand of the boy in front of her" (4564) vs the Aegis (the boy) "third in the line, or near it. The girl behind you would not let go of your hand." (5854). Both say "third". One is probably "near third".
- **Entry numbers:** the Aegis is "forty-one thousand and five" (5854); Annet is 41,006 (4564, 4630); Ketter Dell is 41,009 (4510). Consistent.
- **Which of Corwin Aye's:** "He was in your hold for eleven days" (4556) -- in the past tense; he is not aboard now. Mirren Salk "is in the stack behind you" (4560). The code's `manifest53.js` line 42 keeps this.
- **Hold length:** the slate and Kaelen both believe "four hours" (2453, 3126); the Hold in the text is minutes.
