## What was built

1. **The road between the lane and the Landing.** The lane cut down through the hedge on the Flume Road used to open straight onto Stillwater Landing. It now opens on the road itself, in two stretches, and the Landing's way up comes out at the foot of the second. Nothing else about either place changes.
2. **The Stride** (120 by 180 cells). The road winds down from the Wash into Sallow Bottom, a wet valley of alder and willow, and crosses the brook twice (a ford and a two-plank footbridge each time). The Flume crosses the whole valley in one straight line on grown trunks as tall as a church (the channel runs at 5.6, the height of a church roof in this game). After dusk the sap-light runs in the channel: in the 3D view the trough glows green, and along the ground under it a line of faint green light. In rain the brook comes up over its fords and only the footbridges cross. Ennis Crowle's far skeps stand in three bee-boles in a low stone wall; a gauging stone stands at the foot of the trunk nearest the middle of the bottom.
3. **The Pound Stair** (96 by 190 cells). Sixteen grown locks step down the hill, the channel down the middle, each lock with its square side-pound to the east. The road zigzags down the west side and crosses on the gates' top beams. In rain the Stair walks: the low road beside the channel goes under, and the way down is the high path along the pound-banks. When the rain stops the bottom side-pound stands empty for six hours and shows a grating, bolted from below (the Dry Flume is Phase 85's; nothing opens yet).
4. **The people,** on their own hours: a broadsheet-runner coming up from the Landing with Cantabile papers under her coat; a Synod gauger with his chain, by day on the Stride; the lengthsman on the Stair all day; two raft-hands walking up the Stair and away home in the evening.
5. **The lengthsman's task.** The top three gates have stuck since the lock was chained. Hum (sing) the Root within three or four steps of the top gate, then the second, then the third. Out of order, the gates go back and a stake rings (noise, which brings hounds). Done, the raft in the top lock goes down to the foot of the Stair, the gates stand open, and the lengthsman pays 8 sovereigns (and experience, and a journal line), once. Knocking against a mooring stake as you walk rings it, too.
6. **The raft** wedged in the sixth side-pound for two years, Compact chalk on it, with a small cache lashed by it (a tier-2 find, as caches go).
7. **Danger 1,** as the plan gives it: three knots of briar-sprites in the verges of the Stride; two mire-drones lying on the banks of two pounds (they cannot stand in the water); a pair of hounds on the east shoulder above the Stair, within hearing of a rung stake.
8. **Finger-posts at the four new gates,** in the Phase 82b pattern; the lane's post on the Flume Road and the Landing's post now stand by the doors onto this road, and the travel chart lays both stretches south of the Wash, where the road runs (the Reach chart is now about 977 by 567 cells).

# Road 3: the drove road (the Grey Wethers and the Hundred Hollows)

From the world plan, "Road 3. The drove road" (approved by the owner, 13 Nov 2026). Code: `js/drove83.js`; test: `test/drove83.test.js`; French: `tools/i18n/p83-drove.tsv`. ## What was built

1. **The road between the stile and Wethercombe.** The stile in the Watch Downs' south hedge used to drop straight into Wethercombe. It now opens on the drove road, in two stretches, and Wethercombe's way up comes out at the foot of the second. Nothing else about the Downs or the combe changes; the Downs stay danger 0, "the quiet way in".
2. **The Grey Wethers** (120 by 160 cells, danger 0). The drove road, six cells wide and green with the cart-ruts down its middle, swings round the head of a dry valley and runs through a flock of about a hundred grey sarsens lying in the grass like sheep lying down, past a dew-pond (a chalk dish, full) to **the Three Pines**, the drovers' halt, where the returners have stopped with their handcart. A **pinfold** of flint and chalk stands near the halt with a **tally-stick** in its wall; a **drover's cache** is under the middle pine. Every stone gives a note when you knock it; **in a high wind the flock sounds by itself**, faintly, one stone after another.
3. **The Hundred Hollows** (110 by 170 cells, danger 1). The road narrows into **a holloway**, sunk between its banks with hawthorn on both sides meeting overhead (in the 3D view a green roof with the light coming through in pieces), and comes out on a down that is all grassed-over pits, a hundred of them; then down by **terraces** into Wethercombe. **One hollow is not grassed over**: white chalk and a dark mouth going down (the Antler Galleries, Phase 85; nothing opens yet). **Hounds den in three of the hollows after dark** and are gone by day.
4. **The old woman's task.** She is a drover's daughter among the returners and cannot climb to the stones. Five Wethers carry her father's red raddle, one stroke to five; the drovers' call goes **three, one, four, two, five**, and she hums it. Struck in that order (use a stone to knock it) the call goes out over the down and she pays 6 sovereigns, with experience and a journal line, once. Out of order the stones only clang: the call starts again, and it is noise.
5. **The people:** the returners (a man pulling the cart, his wife, their boy, and his mother, the old woman), camped at the Three Pines, with a small fire there at night; a furze-cutter by day among the furze on the east side, cutting faggots for the beacons.
6. **At night** the three fold-fires (`js/sheepwalk22.js`), once lit, show from the road as fires on three hills, each in the direction of its own hill. **At dawn after a wet night** (rain between eight at night and five in the morning) the combe end fills with mist until half past eight: in the 3D view a white sea at one level over the low terraces, the higher ground standing out of it.
7. **Finger-posts at the four new gates;** the Downs' stile post and the travel chart moved to the new road, as for road 1.
