# Phase 100: the Continental Rift, part one. Null-Watch: the Wall and the Talking Halls (text for review)

18 November 2026. Branch `p100-rift`. Built by the Commander alone (no subagents). The owner, 18 Nov: "commit to github and merge then proceed." By the building order approved with Phase 90 (the Heights, then the Rift and the Middle Country), this is the Rift's first part.

Sources: Book Three ch. 2; World Bible Vol VI §1; Phase 89 "VIII. The Continental Rift"; the Phase 90 layout. Places are shown as of Harvestmonth 419, a season before the book: the Quartermaster is alive, Ninety-Four is the General's voice, and nobody here has heard of the Drift-Kestrel's company. What the books do later is not done by the player: nobody goes out by the postern, the crane and its rope are seen and not used, and nobody of the book's company is named.

## What was built

**Two places.**
- **Null-Watch: the Wall** (`p100_wall`, 372 by 133 cells), cut by `tools/maps/wall100.py` from the Cymatic City Engine's own 852 Hz plan of Null-Watch. East is the Rift. The mooring-yard and its mast; the wall stair between Towers Five and Six; the wall-walk past five dampening batteries (Five to Nine); the door with STANDING ORDER NINE over it; Tower Nine's foot with its barred postern, the buttress and the crane beyond; the square before the Talking Halls; the magazine lane; the west gate and the gate lamp; the battery rail.
- **Null-Watch: the Talking Halls** (`p100_halls`): the hall, the talk-roster, the issue-hatch, a squad's barrack behind a hung blanket, the powder passage and Magazine Four.

**From the books, kept as written.** Obsidian in blocks the size of wagons, mortared with lead and netted with tungsten cable. A squat round tower every two hundred yards with a brass mouth nine feet across under a lead hood. Thirty yards of bare grey rock, and then the land stops. Standing Order Nine: keep talking; "they don't look first". Grey long coats with lead plates sewn in and a rota-number in place of a name. General Theresa Ward, mute since the first night, speaking in iron-cant on her breastplate, and her voice saying it after her. Two-Eleven and the dog that could open a gate; Nineteen; Eighty-Eight learning iron-cant; the Quartermaster at Tower Nine's foot. The Lead Brothers along the lip, who do not fetch back anyone who is not theirs. Paired watchers who talk to the sleepers.

**The danger: quiet.** On the wall-walk a traveller who stops talking is warned after 4.5 seconds and, after 8, is Relieved: the controls are taken, and they walk to the coping and over it. Talking is the Murmur (N), which here says the next line of the subject you were given. Any sound of yours counts as talk. By a manned battery the bell is rung and you are fetched back. Tower Eight is hooded and unmanned, so from Seven to Nine nobody rings. Looking out at the not-colour for more than a few seconds turns your eyes down to your boots.

**The keeper: the Stray in Magazine Four.** A Carillon Legionnaire left with the Watch to guard the powder, whose core has begun remembering (Vol II: the Legion-Stray). It stopped mid-patrol nine days ago and says a cradle-song, three lines of four, on a 16-second round, and waits where the fourth should be. Blows ring on it and do not put it down. The four lines are in Fifty-One's footlocker in the barrack. Say the fourth line in the gap and it stands down, stays at ease by the kegs, and its squad leave the Magazine Mallet and Nine Days of Brass Tape. A wrong line costs 8 health and it begins again. No shot can be fired in the magazine or the powder passage.

**The Souls rules.** Three lamps, here called talking-posts (a lantern, and a tin with a note you read aloud): in the Halls, at Tower Nine's foot, and the gate lamp. Group `m`, note 852. The shortcut is the battery rail: an ammunition trolley chocked at Tower Nine's end; knock the chock out and it runs between Tower Nine's foot, the mooring-yard and the west gate.

**The Watch's Issue (the nineteenth set).** Watch Coat, Wall Mitts, Powder-Felts (free, one pair, from the magazines' rack), the Cable-Spike and the Magazine Mallet. Three pieces: on the wall, quiet takes half as long again to come on you. All five: a manned battery's bell reaches you from twice as far. Each piece has its own look, icon and first-person painting.

**Getting there.** The Kestrel flies east to Null-Watch (48 hours) and moors at the Watch's mast in the mooring-yard.

## Moved for the book
- Phase 90 drew the way in at the west gate. Book Three has the Watch's mooring-yard under the wall, with a stair from it to the wall-walk between Towers Five and Six. So the Kestrel moors there, and the west gate is the far end of the place (shut: the plain is a later phase).

## [PROPOSED] (not in the books; for the owner)
**Ruled, 18 Nov: the owner, "I approve of the proposals."** All thirteen below stand as built. (They keep the [PROPOSED] mark in the code only to show they are the game's and not the books'.)

1. The flight takes 48 hours.
2. The battery rail has a third stop, at the mooring-yard.
3. A visitor needs a subject from the talk-roster before Two-Eleven lets them past the chain at the wall stair. The four visitors' subjects: the days of the week, counting cable-eyes, onions, the road you came by.
4. "Keep talking" is played with the Murmur (N). The numbers: warned at 4.5 s, Relieved at 8 s, a bell's reach 7 cells.
5. Tower Eight is hooded and unmanned this week.
6. The Rift side of the wall-walk has a shin-high lead coping, not a parapet, so the rock and the edge can be seen over it. Nobody can step onto it.
7. The not-colour is drawn as the same grey as the air: the land stops and there is no horizon.
8. The Stray, its nine days, and that it is stood down and not killed. Its squad have not reported it ("We will see about the report").
9. The cradle-song: "The lamp is low, the bread is put away, / The hens are shut, the dog is on the stair, / Your father's boots are drying by the grate, / And nothing's wanted of you anywhere."
10. Troopers named only by number: One-Oh-Six at the issue-hatch, Corporal One-Forty-Two, Fifty-One, Two-Oh-Seven. Fifty-One wrote the song out and will not say where she is from.
11. The talking-posts and their notes. The Talking Halls being so loud that nothing you do there is heard.
12. The names of the set and its pieces, and of the Stray's leavings.
13. Prices at the issue-hatch: coat 54, mitts 16, spike 18.
