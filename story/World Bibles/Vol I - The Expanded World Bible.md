# PART I — THE DEEP COSMOLOGY

## 1.1 What the Octave Actually Is

Aethelgard is not a world that makes sound. It is a sound that makes a world.

The planet does not rotate. It **sustains**. Every solid thing — stone, bone, iron, the water in a man's eye — is a standing wave that has been held long enough to become stubborn. Physical law is not a rule the world obeys; it is a *note the world is currently holding*. Mass exists because something, somewhere, has not stopped singing 396 Hz. When that voice tires, iron forgets its weight.

The old scholars called this the **Doctrine of the Sounding Body**: *nothing is, everything is being played.* Drop a stone and it does not fall because of gravity. It falls because the Adamantine Keystone in Fortis is still insisting, at this exact instant, that downward is a direction worth having.

This is why the horror of Aethelgard is not that monsters come. It is that **the floor is an opinion**, and the thing holding that opinion has a drill in its throat.

### The Three Properties of a Resonant World
1. **Sustain.** A law persists only while its Anchor sounds. There is no inertia of law. There is only the note and the silence after.
2. **Sympathy.** Two bodies tuned alike will move alike, at any distance. This is the basis of all magic, all communication, and all torture.
3. **Interference.** Where two frequencies meet out of phase, reality thins. Thin reality is where the Cacophony gets in.

## 1.2 Why Eight — And Why Eight Is Wrong

An octave is eight notes. It is also, famously, **seven notes and a return**: the eighth is the first, doubled, one turn higher up the spiral. Do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-ti-*do*.

The Architects pinned eight Anchors. Seven laws — Growth, Heat, Mass, Time, Light, Velocity, Memory — and then, at 852 Hz, the eighth: **Decay & Silence**, the Maw of the Octave.

Here is the thing the Choral Orthodoxy spent ten thousand years refusing to say out loud:

> **Decay is not a law. Decay is the rest at the end of the measure.** The eighth Anchor does not sustain anything. It *releases*. It is the world exhaling. It is the pause that makes the next bar possible — and the Architects built it so the song could begin again.

Which means the Octave as built is **open**. It climbs seven steps, reaches the silence, and stops. There is no return to the root. There is no ninth.

### The Doctrine of the Ninth Anchor *(the central secret of the setting)*

Fragments recovered from the Tomb of the Astrolabe and the lower Mausoleum of Chords agree on one impossible line, rendered in three dead scripts:

> *"We left the chord open. A closed thing cannot be joined. The ninth is not stone. The ninth will be born."*

The Architects did not forget the ninth note. They **declined to build it**. A world anchored entirely in stone would be perfect, eternal, and dead — a music box, repeating. So they left the final interval unsounded and handed the resolution to whatever eventually woke up inside their instrument.

The Ninth Anchor is not a spire. It is a **person**: a living thing that has been tuned by suffering until it can hold a frequency without a body of stone to brace it. The Octave was always waiting for a volunteer.

**This is why Kaelen can do what he does.** The Aether-Glass in his arm is not an infection — it is *lathe-work*. The world has been slowly turning him into an instrument since the day the Foundry ruptured, and it did not ask.

**[CANON — Resonant Accord ending]** When Kaelen drives his crystalline fist into the open core socket, he is not sacrificing himself to patch a hole. He is **closing the octave**: becoming the return to the root, the living *do* at the top of the spiral, the new voice of Light. The eight spires reignite not because they were repaired but because for the first time in ten thousand years the chord *resolves*, and a resolved chord sustains itself.

The cost: a resolution cannot leave the chord. He is not dead. He is **held**.

## 1.3 The Interval Map — Which Spires Love Each Other

The Anchors are not politically neutral. They are in *musical relationship*, and those relationships are physical facts.

| Pair | Interval | Relationship | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth (174) & Mass (396) | Perfect Fifth | **Consonant — the Bedrock Fifth** | Oakhaven and Fortis have never gone to war. Crops grow heavy near lodestone. The oldest alliance on the continent is an acoustic accident. |
| Heat (285) & Velocity (639) | Consonant | **The Forge Pair** | Cinderhold's steam only holds pressure because the Archipelago is still moving. When Velocity stutters, boilers across the Caldera detonate simultaneously. |
| Time (417) & Memory (741) | Consonant | **The Keeping Pair** | The Dunes and the Woods share a dead language. When Memory was silenced, Sandglass clocks began running *backwards in sealed rooms.* |
| **Light (528) & Decay (852)** | **Tritone — *Diabolus in Octava*** | **Violently dissonant** | The single most dangerous relationship in the world. Light and Silence cannot be in the same place without reality shrieking. This is why the Maw sits at the continent's far edge from Sol-Luminary, and why the Null-Watch garrison's dampening cannons must fire *at dawn, every dawn,* forever. |

The Tritone Pair is the engine of the whole cosmology. **The Maw is hungry specifically for Light.** It has been crawling toward Sol-Luminary at roughly four meters a year for a hundred years, and the Synod has classified every survey that says so.

## 1.4 The Celestial Architects — Three Accounts, No Truth

Nobody knows what built the spires. There are three traditions, and the horror of the setting is sharpened by never confirming which is right.

**The Orthodox Account (the Choir Before).** They were a civilization of pure voice that sang themselves into the stone to give the next thing a place to stand. Benevolent. Sacrificial. Gone by choice. *Comforting. Almost certainly propaganda.*

**The Synod Account (the Engineers' Reading).** They were mortal, technological, and failed. The spires are infrastructure, not scripture, and infrastructure may be upgraded by whoever currently holds the deed. *Self-serving. Partially correct, which is worse.*

**The Veil Account (Sable's heresy).** They were not the first tenants. They were the *second*. The spires were driven into Aethelgard as **restraints** — eight pins hammered through something that was already here, lying under the crust, dreaming at 852 Hz. The Octave is not a song. It is a **gag**.

> Sable's marginal note, Great Tome, folio 1,118: *"Every account agrees the Architects left. None agrees they left willingly. I have catalogued the deaths of nine civilizations. Not one of them went quietly, and not one of them wrote down the reason."*

## 1.5 Resonance Kinetic Charge — The Economy of a Broken World

When a law vibrates out of true, reality tries to correct it. That correction is energy with nowhere to go. **Resonance Kinetic Charge (RKC)** is the froth on a world's panic.

- **Where it pools:** fracture seams, silenced provinces, the lee side of a working siphon rig, the inside of a grieving person.
- **How it's taken:** living things "tune in" to a leak and let the correction pass *through* them. It hurts the way a struck bell hurts, if the bell could feel.
- **What it costs:** every charge drawn leaves a *residue* — a small permanent detuning of the user's own signature pitch. Enough residue and you stop matching yourself. (See **Vitrification**, 1.7, and **Dissonance Sickness**, 1.8.)
- **Why everyone does it anyway:** because the alternative is fighting a steam golem with a hammer.

**Charge is why the world is getting worse.** Every mage, every skyship, every lamp in Cinderhold is drinking from the same wound and calling it a well. The more Aethelgard struggles to correct itself, the more valuable the struggle becomes, and the harder everyone pulls. The energy economy of this world is a crowd drinking from a cut artery and arguing about distribution.

## 1.6 Aether-Glass

Condensed celestial resonance. Cyan, faintly warm, faintly *wet-looking* even when dry. It hums at the pitch of whatever Anchor it was bled from, which means **no two tankers of glass are musically compatible**, and mixing grades in a single reservoir has killed more Synod workers than any battle.

### Grades
| Grade | Name | Source | Use | Hazard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Weep** | Surface seep from fracture seams | Lamp oil, cheap wards | Low. Tastes of iron. Slightly addictive. |
| II | **Draw** | Shallow bore, under 200m | Steam-arcana, munitions | Fumes cause tinnitus and short-term memory loss |
| III | **Marrow** | Deep bore, into the harmonic matrix | Skyship cores, automata | Handling without a dampened glove begins vitrification |
| IV | **Living Glass** | Taken from a *sounding* anchor, still in phase with it | Resurrection engines, the Patriarch's reliquaries | Actively grows. Seeks a host. Prefers bone. |
| V | **Signature Glass** | Rendered from a *person* | Synthetic legionnaire neural engines | Illegal in every province including the Synod's own. Produced in volume at the Null-Watch rendering yards. |

**Grade V is the moral floor of the setting.** A legionnaire's "mind" is a dead person's identity, crushed to cyan paste, poured into a brass skull, and asked to obey. They sometimes say the names of children they never had. The Synod calls this *acoustic echo artifacting* and issues a form.

## 1.7 Vitrification — "Glassing"

The slow conversion of living tissue into Aether-Glass. It is not a curse. It is **tuning**, and the subject is the instrument.

### Stages
1. **The Ring.** A persistent tone in one ear, usually a fifth above the subject's own pitch. Six weeks to a year. Treatable if caught — nobody catches it.
2. **The Sheen.** Skin at the contact site becomes faintly translucent in low light. Nerves begin reporting *sound* as touch. Patients describe "feeling a chord go past."
3. **The Set.** Tissue crystallizes. Joints lock to fixed angles and must be *broken and re-set* by a glass-smith to restore motion — a procedure done without anaesthetic, because anaesthetic dampens the resonance and the limb shatters instead of bending. The sound is described, universally, as *a chandelier being murdered.*
4. **The Spread.** The glass propagates along nerve paths, not blood vessels. It follows pain. Old injuries glass first.
5. **The Chord.** When the glass reaches the heart, the subject stops being a person and becomes a *sounding object*: a human-shaped bell that resonates with whatever Anchor claimed it. They remain conscious. This is the part the Synod's medical texts omit. **They remain conscious.**

There are nine documented Chord-cases standing upright in the Null-Watch rendering yards, and all nine have been there for over forty years, and Sable has recorded that three of them still track her with their eyes when she walks the row.

**Kaelen is in late Stage 4.** His arm reached The Set years ago. The Spread has crossed his shoulder. He measures his remaining life in the distance from his collarbone to his sternum and does not tell anyone the number.

## 1.8 Dissonance Sickness & the Tinnitus Plague

The civilian disease. You do not have to touch glass to be ruined by this world — you only have to live downwind of a province that stopped singing.

**Early:** ringing, nausea, a sense that a word you use daily is "the wrong shape." Sufferers mispronounce their own surnames.
**Middle:** **phase drift.** The subject's personal pitch wanders. Mirrors begin to feel subtly wrong. Dogs stop recognising them. Locks keyed to their chord refuse to open — this is how most people find out.
**Late:** **unmooring.** The subject no longer matches their own body's standing wave. They become *partially transparent to physical law*: falling through a floor that holds everyone else, aging four years in a night, catching fire in a room with no fire in it.
**Terminal:** the body releases its note. There is no corpse. There is a sound, and a smell like hot stone, and an outline of clean floor where the dust didn't settle.

Entire districts of Whisper-Grove are marked with chalk circles around clean floor.

## 1.9 Death, Echo, and the Release

*Revision note (2026-10-20): dates aligned with the game — the Silencing is 416.*

A soul, in Aethelgard's physics, is a **sustained note with no instrument** — a pattern that has learned to hold itself. Death is not the end of the note. Death is the end of the *resonator*.

- **The Sustain.** Life. The body holds the note.
- **The Decay.** Dying. The note persists after the body fails, fading on a curve that depends on how fiercely the person lived. Violent deaths decay slowest. The loved decay slower than the forgotten.
- **The Echo.** Where the Anchor of Memory still functions, decaying notes are *archived* — caught in the Weeping Canopy of Whisper-Grove and held. This is what the old faith called heaven and what Sable's order calls *the stacks*.
- **The Release.** Full dissolution into the 852 Hz null-chord. Peace, or annihilation, depending on whom you ask. The Orthodoxy says peace. The Veil says there is no difference and that the distinction is a courtesy extended to the bereaved.

**Since the Silencing of Memory, the Echo no longer catches.** Nobody who has died in the last three years has been archived. They decay in the open air, slowly, audibly, in the places where they fell — which is why battlefields in the present day **hum**, and why the hum gets quieter each year, and why veterans return to old fields specifically to listen to friends go faint.

> *This is the most important piece of horror in the setting and should be used sparingly and devastatingly. A character revisiting a hum that has become too faint to identify is a scene that should be earned, not spent early.*

## 2.1 The Calendar

Time is counted in **Cycles** (a full turn of all eight Anchors through their tuning drift — nominally 1,000 years) and **Measures** (a year). A year is eight months of 45 days, named for the Anchors: *Root, Hearth, Keystone, Dial, Empyrean, Drift, Canopy, Maw*. The month of Maw has no festivals and no weddings; it is the rest at the end of the bar.

The day is divided into **eight bells**, struck by the nearest carillon. In silenced provinces the bells still ring because the Synod pays men to ring them, and this is officially described as *continuity of service*.

**Present day of Book One: Harvestmonth, 419.**

## 2.3 **[CONTINUITY]** The Relay Network — fixing the Oakhaven/Light problem

*The Master Narrative places "the Anchor of Light vault" beneath Oakhaven, which is the Growth realm. Proposed canonical reconciliation, which adds rather than patches:*

The Synod does not only drill Anchors. It builds **Relays** — vaulted sub-chambers that capture a *sibling* Anchor's frequency and re-transmit it locally through a lattice of tuned quartz. A Relay lets the Synod draw Light-grade glass a thousand kilometers from Sol-Luminary, in a province whose governor they already own.

The Oakhaven Sub-Vault is **Relay VII-Light**, and it is worse than an Anchor site, because:
- a Relay has no self-correcting harmonic matrix; it fails *catastrophically* rather than gradually;
- it steals Oakhaven's Growth resonance as carrier signal, which is why the iron-oaks ring like bells and the sap won't rise;
- when Kaelen breaks the drill head in Act I Scene 2, the inverted sphere of black-white light is **a Relay collapse**, not an Anchor death — Oakhaven falls into shadowless twilight while the Light Anchor itself survives, fractured past forty percent, for Act III.

This keeps every line of existing dialogue intact and explains why the party can leave Oakhaven ruined and still have a Light Anchor to fight over later.

# PART III — THE EIGHT REALMS, DEEPENED

## III.1 — THE RESONANT REACH *(Growth & Root • 174 Hz • Capital: Oakhaven)*

**The land.** Temperate river country gone subtly wrong with abundance. Iron-Bough oaks grow in groves that are acoustically tuned — walk the wrong path and the trees fall silent in a widening circle around you, which is how they identify strangers. The **Chlorophyll Flume** is a living aqueduct: a river carried in a channel of grown wood, green-glowing, pumped by nothing but the Anchor insisting that water should want to climb.

**Government.** A **Reeve-Moot** of landholders, nominally elected, actually hereditary. The Synod does not occupy the Reach; it *leases* it, and the Moot sold the quarry rights in 404 for a price that seemed excellent.

**Economy.** Timber that rings. Grain that regrows within a day of cutting (and tastes of less each time). Resonant Moss, harvested for poultices. The Reach feeds four provinces and resents all four.

**Culture.** Agricultural, stubborn, sentimental, musically literate in a folk way — everyone can carry a tune, nobody can read one. Births are announced by hanging a tuned reed above the door, pitched to the child's Signature. Funerals are held in silence, because here the dead are supposed to be *heard* and are no longer being heard.

**Food.** Cider, hearth bread, river eel, a sharp white cheese called *moot-curd* that is aged beside a humming stone and tastes faintly of static.

**The horror here.** The Reach is not dying. It is *overgrowing*. Since the Relay began bleeding Growth as a carrier wave, cellular regeneration has lost its stop condition. Wounds close over splinters and keep closing. A man in Greenroot Hamlet has been regrowing the same finger for eleven months and has nine of it now, stacked, hinged in the wrong places. The Reach's hospitals are full of people who cannot stop healing.

**The secret.** The iron-oaks are not trees. The oldest grove was planted by the Orthodoxy over a **buried relay from the First Cycle** — not Synod-built. Something was transmitting here long before the Synod learned how.

## III.2 — THE DAWNING HEIGHTS *(Light & Clarity • 528 Hz • Capital: Sol-Luminary)*

**The land.** Glaciated alabaster peaks above a permanent cloud deck. Sol-Luminary hangs off the mountain face on kilometer-long adamantine sky-chains, a city of white stone and mirrored brass suspended over four thousand meters of nothing. Sunrise here lasts two hours and is audible.

**Government.** Theocratic. Seat of the **High Patriarchy** and the Synod's spiritual authority. The current High Patriarch is a **revived monarch** — Oren Calloway, dead since 397, walking since 398, maintained on Grade IV Living Glass. He is spoken of as though he never died. His hands are glass to the elbow and he wears gloves at all audiences.

**Economy.** Pilgrimage, refined light-glass, optics, and the manufacture of **solar mirrors** — vast arrays that focus the Anchor's output into coherent beams for industry and, lately, for war.

**Culture.** Ritualized, beautiful, airless. Status is pitch: high-born families claim higher Signatures and modify their children's voices to match. Dawn service is compulsory. Shadows are considered indecent — Sol-Luminary architecture is designed to eliminate them, and the city's residents find the shadowed lowlands physically nauseating.

**Food.** Thin fare. Preserved fruit, altitude-bread, and *sun-tea* steeped in focused light rather than heat. The nobility eat gold leaf for the look of it.

**The horror here.** The Rite of Pure Unison was not stopped. It was **scaled**. In the under-choirs beneath the Sun-Altar there are now four hundred bound singers, rotated in shifts, held at one pitch to keep the mirrors coherent. They are fed through tubes. They are replaced when their throats fail. The Synod recruits from orphanages in the silenced provinces, because the Unstrung do not have names to put on a complaint.

**The secret.** The sun's chord is down to five harmonics from seven. Two are simply gone. The Patriarchy's own astronomers have calculated the date at which coherent light becomes impossible and have been forbidden to publish the figure. **426.** Seven years.

## III.3 — FORGE-SPIRE CINDERHOLD *(Heat & Purge • 285 Hz • Capital: Cinderhold)*

**The land.** Basalt terraces stacked eleven tiers deep into a live caldera, roofed in soot. Reverberation steam-hammers the size of cathedrals beat continuously; the entire province operates on a **four-beat industrial pulse** audible at sixty kilometers. People here speak between hammer-falls and go silent in rhythm without noticing.

**Government.** **Guild-Oligarchy** — eleven Furnace Houses, one per tier, feuding constantly and presenting a unified face to the Synod. Tier Eleven, lowest and hottest, is run by indentured labour and has no House.

**Economy.** Everything metal. Munitions, boilers, rail, armor plate, and the refining of Grade II–III glass. Cinderhold makes the Synod's war and knows it.

**Culture.** Loud, profane, fiercely proud, scarred as a mark of rank. Oaths are sworn on a glowing ingot held bare-handed. The Houses maintain feud-ledgers going back two hundred years. Nobody sings here recreationally — the air is too bad — so music is *struck*, on pipes and plate, and Cinderhold percussion is the most sophisticated on the continent.

**Food.** Ash-cured meat, hard flatbread, a vicious spirit called *slag-wine* distilled in the heat exchangers. Everything is salted heavily; everyone sweats constantly.

**The horror here.** Heat has lost its *proportion*. Thermal dynamics no longer scale predictably: a candle can boil a room, or a blast furnace can run cold for an hour and then release the stored heat in one instant. The Tier Three disaster of 417 killed nine hundred in under four seconds when a pipe that had been cool for a week remembered. Workers listen for the **F-sharp whistle** — the sound of thermal law re-asserting — and have a drill: *dive behind basalt, mouth open, eyes shut.* Children practice it the way other children practice fire drills.

**The secret.** Siphon Harvester's dream engine — the one that converts dissonance into clean steam — **already exists**, in prototype, in a sealed gallery on Tier Eleven. It works. It was sealed because it works without glass, and three Furnace Houses would collapse within a year of its publication.

## III.4 — THE IRON PEAKS / BASTION FORTIS *(Mass & Inertia • 396 Hz • Capital: Fortis)*

**The land.** The Titan's Spine — a range of crags veined with anti-gravitational lodestone, wreathed in **magnetic mist** that drags metal sideways. Fortis is a fortress-city carved *into* the mountain, its battlements rain-swept, its lower levels descending into the Lodestone Vaults.

**Government.** **Martial Compact.** A standing army that elects its own command and acknowledges no civilian authority. Fortis has never been conquered and has also never won anything.

**Economy.** Ore, armor, mercenary contracts, and the **graviton trade** — lodestone that makes skyships possible. Every airborne vessel on Aethelgard, Synod or corsair, has a Fortis-cut core.

**Culture.** Dour, literal, deeply decent, obsessed with weight as a moral category. "A heavy man" is the highest compliment in the local dialect — it means someone whose word has mass. Fortis holds the longest unbroken watch-rota in recorded history: forty-two waves at the Bastion wall in Book One, and the garrison counts them because counting is what they have instead of hope.

**Food.** Root stew, salt mutton, black bread. Heavy, in the compliment sense.

**The horror here.** Mass is going soft in patches. The ground "forgets how to be solid" — Kaelen's phrase, and it is literal. The dense graviton field protects Fortis from knockback but it also means that when a patch of floor forgets, you do not stumble; you go through, at eighty pounds of effective weight, into a shaft that was not there a moment ago and will not be there to retrieve you from. The Bastion has a standing unit — **the Sounders** — who walk ahead of every patrol tapping the stone with tuned rods, listening for the dead note.

**The secret.** The Crystalline Golem is not the only one. There are **eleven more** in the deep vaults, dormant, part of the First Compact: living quartz that agreed, ten thousand years ago, to be the floor. Three have cracked past function. One has begun moving on its own and is going *down*.

## III.5 — THE SHIFTING DUNES *(Time & Stasis • 417 Hz • Capital: Sandglass Bazaar)*

**The land.** An Erg of Singing Silica — sand that holds pitch, so that dunes *chord* as they move and a storm is a scream in a key. Sandglass Bazaar shelters beneath an acoustic glass hemisphere two kilometers across, built to break the sand's voice before it reaches the city.

**Government.** **Mercantile Houses under the Chrono-Dial custodianship.** Nobody rules Sandglass; everyone has a contract with it.

**Economy.** The greatest market on the continent, because Sandglass can *hold* goods out of time. A **stasis crate** keeps cargo at the instant it was sealed. Medicine, bodies, evidence, wine, hostages.

**Culture.** Contractual to the bone. A Sandglass oath specifies its own duration. Marriage here has a term. So does grief: the Dunes practice **scheduled mourning**, a fixed period after which the mourner is socially obligated to stop, and considers the open-ended grief of other provinces indecent and self-indulgent.

**Food.** Spiced flatbread, dried fruit, strong coffee, and *dial-wine* — aged fifty years in a stasis crate in an afternoon, which connoisseurs insist tastes hollow and buy anyway.

**The horror here.** Causal momentum is slipping. Effects arriving before causes. The documented cases are the worst thing in the setting: a woman in Mirage Rest who began bleeding from a wound she would receive nine days later, and who spent those nine days trying to work out what she must avoid, and who died exactly as scheduled from an accident she could not have predicted because the universe was not offering her a warning. It was offering her a *receipt*.

In sealed rooms, clocks run backwards. The Bazaar's lower vaults are full of sealed rooms.

**The secret.** The Chrono-Dial is not drifting. It is being **read** — something is drawing causal data out of it, not energy. Tide-Weaver knows. He has found the signature in four hundred discarded timelines and in every one of them it is the same signature, and in every one of them it is **coming from the Maw**.

## III.6 — THE TEMPEST ARCHIPELAGO *(Velocity & Drift • 639 Hz • Capital: Port Cantabile)*

**The land.** Hundreds of islands on colossal singing coral reefs. The reefs are alive and tonal; the sea above them changes *key* with the tide. Port Cantabile is built across six islands, strung together with rope bridges and **acoustic carillon rigging** — vast harp-strung masts that convert wind into usable charge.

**Government.** **The Corsair Compacts.** Forty-one captains, one vote each, one annual gathering, and a standing agreement that disputes are settled by race rather than battle because battle is expensive.

**Economy.** Shipping, smuggling, salvage, and the only functioning free press on the continent — Cantabile's broadsheets are the reason anyone outside the Synod knows what happened at Whisper-Grove.

**Culture.** Loud, polyglot, superstitious, generous, and absolutely merciless about debt. Shanties are not entertainment; they are *navigation*, encoding reef pitches and safe passages in verse. A captain who forgets a shanty loses a ship.

**Food.** Fish, lime, rum, hardtack, and *reef-pearl* — a tonal shellfish that must be eaten within a minute of opening or it goes atonal and poisonous.

**The horror here.** Kinetic law is probabilistic now. A raindrop takes three days to fall; a sword stroke goes supersonic without warning and takes the swordsman's arm off at the shoulder from the recoil. The sea has begun **forgetting surface tension in patches** — Vane's frigate dissolved into blue jelly in 418 and forty-eight hands survived only because he cut a towing hawser and left his first mate to sink. Children in Cantabile are taught never to run on open deck, because a running stride is where velocity most likes to misbehave.

**The secret.** **The tonal sea-trenches are not natural.** They are *bore holes* — the Synod has been siphoning the Velocity Anchor from beneath the seabed for eleven years, and the trench that swallowed Vesper was Siphon Site 9. Vane has never known this. The day he learns it is a scene that should be written with care.

## III.7 — THE SPECTRAL WOODS *(Memory & Entropy • 741 Hz • Capital: Whisper-Grove)*

*Revision note (2026-10-20): dates aligned with the game — the Silencing is 416.*

**The land.** A twilight forest of silver weeping willows standing in phosphorescent reflecting pools. The Weeping Canopy — the Anchor itself — is grown into the forest rather than built on it, a spire of fused wood and crystal a kilometer high. Lilac mist at ground level, always.

**Government.** None. There has been no functioning government in Whisper-Grove since 416 because there is no one who remembers being appointed.

**Economy.** None. Salvage, by outsiders. The marble plazas are looted monthly by crews who have learned not to speak near the residents.

**Culture.** *Gone.* This is the setting's central atrocity and should be written as such.

**The horror here.** Seventy-four thousand people are still alive in Whisper-Grove. They do not know their names, their kin, their trades, or what happened. They **loop** — a woman repeats the last four words she said before the Silencing, forever, to a husband she does not recognise who is repeating his own four words back. They eat when food is put in front of them. They do not age correctly. They have been doing this for three years.

Relief convoys ran to the Grove from 416 until the workers could not continue; the stores were reassigned to the Synod's restorative administration. The present policy is **containment**: the Synod maintains a cordon and describes the province as "under restorative administration."

Worse, quietly: the loops are **shortening**. A phrase that was nine words in 416 is four words now. Sable has transcribed the decay curve. At the current rate the last Unstrung will fall silent in 421, and nobody knows whether silence means death or something worse, and Sable has written in the margin of folio 2,207: *"I will be here to find out. That is the entire point of me."*

**The secret.** The Echo stacks are still full. Every soul archived before 416 is still *in* the Canopy, held, unreleased, and — because the Anchor is silenced rather than destroyed — **aware**. Ten thousand years of the dead, conscious, in the dark, unable to decay. The Mausoleum of Chords is not a tomb. It is a *holding cell*, and the labyrinth's silencing effect is the only thing keeping the stacks from being audible to the living.

## III.8 — THE CONTINENTAL RIFT & THE MAW *(Decay & Silence • 852 Hz • Capital: Null-Watch)*

**The land.** An abyssal divide splitting the continent's eastern edge, widening by four meters a year. At its head is **the Maw of the Octave** — not a hole, exactly; a place where the world's note has finished and the next note has not begun. Looking at it is described as "forgetting that you were looking."

Null-Watch Garrison sits on lead ramparts above the lip, armed with dampening cannons that fire at every dawn without fail, forever, in a ritual the garrison calls **the Morning Refusal**.

**Government.** **Military-penal.** Null-Watch is a posting, a prison, and a monastery depending on which gate you came through. Command rotates every ninety days because longer postings produce the Quiet.

**Economy.** None, officially. Unofficially: the **rendering yards**, where Grade V Signature Glass is produced from people the Synod has decided are no longer people.

**Culture.** The garrison speaks constantly. Silence is a disciplinary offence. Soldiers are paired and required to keep up continuous conversation on patrol — "line-talk" — and a trooper whose partner goes quiet is required by standing order to sound an alarm *immediately*, without checking.

**The horror here.** **The Quiet.** Prolonged proximity to the Maw causes a subject to progressively stop. Not die — *stop*. They speak less, then move less, then breathe at longer intervals, and at no point do they experience distress, which is the terrible part: every recovered sufferer reports the approach of the Quiet as *the most profound relief they have ever felt*. Null-Watch's suicide rate is not high. Its **desertion-toward-the-Maw** rate is, and the garrison does not use the word desertion. They say a man has *gone to listen*.

**The secret.** Vaelen's Hollow Chorus is not a cult that worships the Maw. It is a **congregation that has heard it**, and what they heard was not a void. It was an *invitation*, in a voice, using names. The Supreme Arbiter's philosophy — *why do you fight for pain, when I offer you peace* — is not a tyrant's rationalization. **He is quoting.**

# PART IV — PEOPLES, CULTURE & DAILY LIFE

## 4.1 The Signature — Everyone Has a Pitch

Every living thing has a **Signature**: a fundamental frequency unique to it, audible to a trained ear, measurable with a tuning rod. It is not a soul; it is the frequency at which the soul is currently being held.

This single fact reorganizes the entire society:

- **Identity is acoustic.** Locks, wards, contracts, and inheritances key to Signature. A forged document is detectable because it doesn't ring right.
- **Names are pitched.** A true name is a *pitch plus a phoneme*. Written Cant notates both. Calling someone by name in the old way is to strike their note, which is why it is intimate, and why shouting a true name across a battlefield can stagger the person it belongs to.
- **Signature can be stolen.** This is the horror underneath Grade V glass, the synthetic legionnaires, and the Silencing of Memory. The Synod does not kill people for fuel. **It takes who they are and leaves the rest alive.** The Unstrung of Whisper-Grove are not dead. They are *spent.*
- **Signature drifts.** Illness, grief, guilt, and dissonance exposure all detune a person. Long-married couples drift *toward* each other, measurably. This is the most romantic fact in the setting and should be used at least once to break someone's heart.

## 4.2 Language

- **Cant (High Cant):** the pitched liturgical and legal tongue. Meaning depends on interval as much as word — the same syllable at a fifth means its opposite. Impossible to lie in, badly. Spoken fluently by perhaps four thousand people.
- **Low Common:** the trade tongue, unpitched, practical, what everyone in the books actually speaks.
- **Synod Standard:** a deliberately flattened dialect imposed by the Iron Synod, stripped of tonal inflection "for clarity." Its real purpose is to make Signature-reading harder and to make everyone sound the same. Children in Synod schools are corrected for singing.
- **Strike-speech:** Cinderhold's percussive dialect, hammered on plate between furnace beats.
- **Line-talk:** Null-Watch's compulsory patrol conversation, which has evolved its own grammar — endless, looping, meaningless by design, because meaning invites pauses.

## 4.3 Rites of the Life Cycle

- **The First Reed.** At birth, a reed is cut to the infant's Signature and hung above the door. If it ever stops sounding, the child is in danger. In the silenced provinces, every door still has a reed and none of them sound.
- **The Binding Interval.** Marriage. Two Signatures are sounded together and the officiant names the interval they make — a fifth is fortunate, a third is passionate, a second is a warning the couple is permitted to ignore, and a tritone is grounds for the ceremony to be stopped. Couples sometimes conceal their true pitches. It never works.
- **The Last Note.** Death. The family holds the dying person's pitch until the body releases it, so the note is never alone in the air. In the Reach this is done in silence now, because the note no longer carries, and holding a pitch that nothing answers is more than most people can do.

## 4.4 Money, Trade & Goods

- **The Sovereign** — Synod-minted, stamped with the Patriarch's profile, rings at 528 Hz when dropped. Counterfeits are caught by ear.
- **The Chime** — pre-Synod provincial coinage, varied, still accepted in the Archipelago and the Dunes out of spite.
- **Tuning-weights** — the real currency between serious people. Calibrated brass masses used to set rods and scales. Non-forgeable, universally useful, and in a world where mass itself is unreliable, a *verified weight* is the most trustworthy object there is.

**Notable goods:** wind-reed (Reach), iron-bough timber (Reach), lodestone cores (Fortis), singing silica (Dunes), stasis crates (Dunes), reef-pearl (Archipelago), bone-chime (Woods, illegal), dampening felt (Null-Watch, controlled), Grade I–III glass (everywhere, regulated badly).

## 4.5 The Eight Feasts

*Revision note (2026-10-20): dates aligned with the game — the Silencing is 416.*

One per month. Root (planting, loud), Hearth (forge-lighting), Keystone (the Weighing, Fortis's oath-renewal), Dial (contract day — every term in the province expires and is renegotiated at once), Empyrean (dawn service, compulsory), Drift (the Corsair gathering and the Great Race), Canopy (the remembering of the dead — *not celebrated since 416*), and Maw, which has no feast.

# PART V — FACTIONS & POWERS

## 5.1 The Iron Synod

Not an empire. A **holding company with a theology.**

**Structure:**
- **The High Patriarchy** (Sol-Luminary) — spiritual authority, legitimacy, the revived Oren Calloway. Produces doctrine.
- **The Arbiter's Bench** (mobile, currently the Planetary Engine) — legal and executive authority. **Supreme Arbiter Vaelen** sits at its head. Produces law.
- **The Siphon Corps** (Cinderhold/Fortis) — engineering and extraction. Kaelen's former service. Produces glass.
- **The Flambeau** (itinerant) — the Inquisition. Ignatius's former order. Produces silence. Motto: *Progress is noisy.*
- **The Legion** — synthetic legionnaires, Grade V-cored, brass-skulled, tireless, and occasionally, horribly, *reminiscent*.

**Their actual belief:** that suffering is an engineering problem, that the Octave is infrastructure rather than scripture, and that whoever maintains infrastructure owns it. Every individual Synod official in the books should be allowed to be *right about something*. The Synod is not evil because it is stupid. It is evil because it is competent and will not stop.

**The schism nobody outside the Bench knows:** the Patriarchy wants the Octave *repaired and owned*. Vaelen wants it **ended**. They are not allies. They are a man holding a door for someone carrying a bomb.

## 5.2 The Choral Orthodoxy

The old faith. Maintainers turned worshippers turned irrelevant. Broken at the Rite of Pure Unison, when the faithful watched the Patriarchy torture the Sky Choirs and did nothing. Survives as scattered rural clergy, a few honest ones, mostly tending reeds above doors in provinces where nothing sounds.

**Useful for the books:** the Orthodoxy is the faction that *knew better and failed to act*, which makes it the most human institution in the setting and an excellent source of broken, decent, ashamed old people.

## 5.3 The Silent Veil

*Revision note (2026-10-20): dates aligned with the game — the Silencing is 416.*

Sable's order. Archivists sworn to record the death of civilizations so that history cannot be rewritten. They do not intervene. **That is the oath, and the oath is the horror.** A Veil scribe stood in Whisper-Grove in 416 and wrote down what was happening while it happened.

Sable broke with them — stole the Great Tome, severed her access key, walked into the mist. She is the last, and the Veil is hunting the Tome, not her.

## 5.4 The Sky Choirs & What a Seraph Is

Seraphim are not angels. They are **humans grown into instruments**, the Orthodoxy's oldest and most beautiful atrocity: children with perfect Signatures, surgically fitted at seven years old with resonance-wings — vaned structures of tuned bone and glass that let them hold a pitch at altitude for hours. It is voluntary. It is voluntary the way a seven-year-old volunteers.

Seraphina is one. The Corrupted Seraph is one. The difference between them is which institution broke faith first.

## 5.5 The Hollow Chorus

Vaelen's congregation. Not a cult of nihilists — a cult of the **relieved**. They are overwhelmingly composed of people who have lost something unbearable: the bereaved of Whisper-Grove, Null-Watch veterans who have gone to listen, parents of rendered children. Their recruitment pitch is one sentence and it is devastating: *"Wouldn't you rather it had never had to hurt?"*

They are not monsters and should never be written as monsters. They are the setting's argument, and if the reader does not feel the pull of it at least once, the ending means nothing.

## 5.6 Minor Powers

- **The Tuners' Collegium** (Sandglass) — neutral technical guild, certifies rods and Signatures, sells to everyone, is quietly the most powerful institution nobody fears.
- **The Deep Consortium** (Fortis) — lodestone cartel. Controls skyship supply. Could end the war in a year by refusing to sell and will not, because contracts.
- **The Free Rigging** (Archipelago) — the Compacts' informal navy. Forty-one captains who hate each other and will not be conquered.
- **The Unstrung** — not a faction, a population: refugees from silenced provinces, nameless, undocumented, unprotected. The Synod's labour supply and the Hollow Chorus's congregation. Six hundred thousand people with no Signature on file.

# PART VI — THE DISCIPLINES OF RESONANCE

## 6.1 How Magic Actually Works

Four steps, always, regardless of tradition:

1. **Perceive** — hear the target's pitch. Hardest step. Most practitioners never get past rough approximation.
2. **Match** — produce that pitch yourself, with voice, rod, or machine.
3. **Drive** — add energy. Sympathy does the rest: the target moves because you are moving, at a distance, for free.
4. **Break** — or, instead of matching, deliberately miss. Dissonance shatters what sympathy moves.

Everything in the system is a variation of *make a thing agree with you, or make it disagree with itself.*

## 6.2 The Four Disciplines

| Discipline | Instrument | Strength | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cantor** (Lyra) | The voice | Precision, healing, no equipment to lose | Throat damage is permanent; mana is literally breath; a Cantor who loses her voice loses everything |
| **Tuner** (most field mages) | Rod, fork, bell, lyre | Reliable, learnable, repairable | Slow. Instrument-dependent. A snapped rod mid-combat is a death sentence |
| **Engineer** (Kaelen, Siphon Harvester) | Machinery, acoustic logic gates | Enormous scale, repeatable, no talent required | Equipment is heavy, loud, and announces you to everything nearby |
| **Siphon** (Synod) | Theft of ambient charge | Cheap, fast, powerful | Leaves residue. Vitrifies the user. Devours the surroundings |

## 6.3 The Cost: the Debt of Overtone

Every working leaves a small detuning in the practitioner. It is cumulative and it does not heal. Working magic is spending your own Signature, a fraction at a time, and the ledger is kept by physics.

A career mage at sixty is perceptibly *off* — their reflection feels wrong, their dog is nervous around them, locks hesitate. A great mage at the end is a person their own family can no longer quite recognise and cannot say why.

**Lyra knows her remaining working hours to within a hundred.** She has not told anyone. She heals Kaelen's arm anyway, every night, at 174 Hz, and each time it costs her an amount she has already calculated and has already decided is acceptable.

## 6.4 Forbidden Workings

- **Unison** — forcing many voices to one pitch. Produces coherent, weaponizable resonance. Also produces the Rite of Pure Unison, cannibal light, and four hundred bound throats under the Sun-Altar.
- **Inversion** — driving a frequency 180° out of phase with itself to cancel it. Creates localized *absence of law*. Banned by every tradition, used routinely by the Flambeau.
- **The Null Chord** — sounding 852 Hz deliberately, with intent. Not forbidden. **Impossible to forbid.** Anyone can do it. The problem is that it works.

## 6.5 Resonant Materials

Wind-reed (holds any pitch, dies in a day), iron-bough (rings like bronze, grows), lodestone (ignores mass, repels), singing silica (holds pitch indefinitely — the basis of all recording), aether-glass (holds *law*), bone-chime (holds a dead person's Signature — outlawed everywhere, manufactured in Whisper-Grove by looters, and the most reliable way to speak to the dead that currently exists).

# PART VII — THE CACOPHONY: A BESTIARY OF THE DISSONANT

Cacophony entities are not animals. They are **errors with appetite** — structures of static and disjointed note that form where reality thins, and which hunt voice, music, and name.

**General rules:**
- They cannot perceive silent, still things. They perceive *pitch*. A person holding their breath is invisible. A person holding their breath while terrified is not, because fear has a pitch.
- They are drawn to *meaning* over volume. A scream attracts them. A spoken name attracts them more.
- They cannot be killed, only **resolved** — forced into a consonant interval until they stop being an error.

---

**DISSONANCE HOUNDS** — *Common. Pack. The infantry of the Cacophony.*
Long-bodied, approximately canine, made of overlapping wrong notes that the eye keeps trying to resolve into fur. They do not bite. They **harmonize with you**, matching your Signature and then dragging it sharp until your body can't hold it. Death by hound looks like a seizure and sounds like a violin being tuned by someone furious.

---

**THROAT-WRAITHS** — *Uncommon. Solitary. Ambush.*
Formed where a person died mid-sentence. They are the unfinished half of the sentence, looking for a mouth. A wraith attaches to the jaw and completes itself — the victim speaks words they never knew, in a voice that is nearly theirs, and cannot stop until the sentence ends. Some sentences are long. The record is eleven days.

---

**THE UNSTRUNG** *(not Cacophony, but shelved here because soldiers shelve them here)*
The living amnesiacs of Whisper-Grove. Not hostile. Not dangerous. Listed in Synod field manuals as a hazard because troops who encounter them stop functioning. The manual's recommended procedure is *"do not establish eye contact; do not respond to looping speech; withdraw."*

---

**FEEDBACK ANGELS** — *Rare. Sol-Luminary and the high cliffs.*
What happens when a Sky Choir singer dies in Unison. A standing wave in the shape of a person with too many wings, all of them sounding, none of them matching. They are drawn to choral music and to the sound of children. They do not attack. They **join in**, and everything that sings with them vitrifies from the throat outward over approximately four minutes.

---

**THE CHOIR-THAT-EATS** — *Very rare. Migratory. Catastrophic.*
A collective: between forty and two thousand voices that was once a congregation, a crew, or a town. It arrives as beautiful distant singing. It recruits. Anyone who hears it for longer than a minute begins to sing along and walks toward it, and the Choir grows. It has consumed four settlements. It is currently somewhere in the Spectral Woods, and it is getting better at harmony.

---

**GRAVEWELLS** — *Uncommon. Rift and Caldera.*
Not creatures — *places* that have become predatory. A patch of ground where decay runs fast. Anything crossing it ages at a thousandfold: boots rot, flesh desiccates, a man crosses four meters and arrives as a dry outline. They drift, slowly, and they are invisible, and the only warning is that insects avoid the air above them.

---

**STATIC REMNANTS** *(the species, not the champion)* — *Common near fracture seams.*
Human-shaped voltage, the discharge-ghost of someone who died in a resonance surge. Most are mindless and dissipate within hours. A rare few **cohere**. The party's Static Remnant is one of maybe nine in history, and every other cohered Remnant on record has eventually stopped moving and ceased to exist. He knows this. It is why he never stops moving.

---

**NULL-AMALGAM** — *Singular. The Maw. Apex.*
Nine and a half thousand kilos of absence wearing the geometry of things it has eaten, including tiles, cannon, and two hundred and six members of the Null-Watch garrison. It consumes arena tiles — meaning it eats *the floor's agreement to exist* — and releases white-noise bursts that strip Signatures from everything within forty meters.

It is not the final threat. **It is the Maw's immune response**, and the fact that the Maw has one implies the Maw is alive, and nobody in the Synod will put that sentence in a report.

---

# PART VIII — TECHNOLOGY & INDUSTRY

**Skyships.** Lodestone core (Fortis-cut) cancels mass; carillon rigging or steam-arcana provides thrust. Hulls are brass and iron-bough. They are slow, beautiful, expensive, and the single most important strategic asset on the continent — a six-day road march is an eight-hour flight. The *Drift-Kestrel* is Vane's; it is older than him, under-gunned, and held together by a winch he does not trust.

**Acoustic logic gates.** Computation by resonance. A tuned chamber either sounds or doesn't; chain ten thousand and you have a mind that runs on chimes. This is what the Aegis Sentinel is. It is why he thinks slowly, why he cannot lie, and why his emotions — which are real — arrive as *chords he cannot name*.

**Siphon rigs & boring engines.** Thirty-story runic drills belching black steam. The defining image of Synod industry. Each one requires a crew of six hundred and a water supply and will, with absolute certainty, eventually kill the province it stands in.

**Dampening cannons.** Null-Watch's ramparts. They fire *destructive interference* — a shaped anti-wave that cancels incoming dissonance. Expensive, slow to reload, and the only reason the Rift has not already eaten Fortis.

**The Chime Network.** Continental communication by paired singing-silica resonators. Instantaneous, unencrypted, and entirely Synod-controlled. The Archipelago maintains a pirate network of stolen pairs and broadcasts **numbers stations** — tuned frequency bands carrying coded intelligence, which must be physically *dialed in* against drift and noise. (This is the diegetic justification for the Numbers Station Tuner minigame: those dials are real objects, and the friction in them is the hand of a frightened operator in a Cantabile attic.)

**Medicine.** Resonant setting of bones (fast, agonising), moss poultice, and glass-grafting — the Synod's miracle, which saves your life and begins your vitrification. Every grafted survivor is a slow death with a grateful family.

# PART IX — THE ENGINE OF THE PLOT

## 9.1 What Vaelen Actually Wants

Not power. Not even oblivion, exactly. **Vaelen wants to finish the piece.**

He has worked out — correctly — that the Octave is an open chord, that an open chord is an unbearable state, and that the Architects left it open on purpose. His conclusion differs from theirs by one assumption: he believes the resolution the Architects intended is **downward, into silence**, not upward into a ninth. He is not wrong that the chord must resolve. He is wrong about the direction, and he is wrong for a reason that is purely personal and which the books should withhold until very late.

*(Suggested, usable, and devastating: Vaelen was Null-Watch command. Ninety-day rotation. He stayed longer. He has heard the Maw use a name, and the name was his daughter's, and she has been in the rendering yards' Chord-row for forty-one years, standing, conscious, tracking people with her eyes.)*

## 9.2 The Three Endings, Reframed

- **The Resonant Accord *(canonical)*.** Kaelen becomes the Ninth. The chord resolves upward. The world sings. He is held — present, aware, and no longer able to be a person. *Lyra can still hear him.* That is the ending's wound and it should be the last line of the series.
- **The Shattered Crown.** Kaelen breaks all eight. No celestial law. Mortals must *engineer* physics, locally and badly, forever. Freedom at the cost of every guarantee. The most hopeful ending and the one with the highest body count.
- **The Sovereign Void.** Kaelen absorbs all eight and stands as a statue holding reality together while Sable writes the final page. The Veil's ending: perfectly recorded, perfectly still, perfectly alone.

## 9.3 Threads Left Open for Later Books

1. The sun's chord is down to five harmonics. **426.** Seven years. The Patriarchy knows.
2. The Maw advances four meters a year and is moving *toward Light.*
3. The Echo stacks are full, conscious, and shortening.
4. Something buried under the Reach's oldest grove was transmitting before the Synod existed.
5. The twelfth Crystalline Golem is awake and going down.
6. The Choir-That-Eats is learning harmony.
7. The Tier Eleven engine works without glass, and three Furnace Houses would die if that were known.
8. Tide-Weaver's signature from the Maw appears in every discarded timeline. Including the ones where the Synod never formed.

# APPENDIX B — TONE GUIDE

- **Show the horror.** The Unstrung loop on the page. The Chord-row tracks with its eyes on the page. The glass-smith breaks and re-sets the joint on the page, and the reader hears it.
- **Let the Synod be right sometimes.** The medicine saved people. The skyships fed provinces. The Hollow Chorus's question is a real question.
- **Deaths are expensive.** Only when the bench is deep enough that the note stays covered, and only when it costs the reader something they were enjoying.
- **Action is earned, never filler** — and when it happens, it is visceral, specific, and slow enough to be imagined.
- **The world's grief is audible.** Battlefields hum. Doors have silent reeds. Couples drift into tune. Whenever a scene needs weight, ask what it *sounds* like, and the setting will answer.
