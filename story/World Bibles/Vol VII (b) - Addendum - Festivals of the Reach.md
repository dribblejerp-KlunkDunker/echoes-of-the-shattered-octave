# World Bible Vol VII Addendum: Festivals of the Reach
### A second layer of holy days under the Eight Feasts. Approved by the author, 19 October 2026.

> Vol VII pegs the **Eight Feasts** to the calendar and puts none of them in Harvestmonth. This addendum does not move a single date. It adds what Vol VII §8 already implies: *the Reach dates by what is growing, not by the month.* The Feasts belong to the Anchors and the calendar. **The festivals belong to the fields**, and they fall when the crop says so. That is why there is room for them in Harvestmonth, and why the Synod has never managed to put them on a docket.

---

## 1. RULING — two kinds of holy day

| | **Feasts** (Vol VII §4) | **Festivals** (this addendum) |
|---|---|---|
| Pegged to | the calendar (an Anchor's day in a set month) | the crop, the river, the weather: *when the last field is cut* |
| Kept by | the province, in front of witnesses | a village, a street, a household |
| Synod's view | absorbed, made compulsory, or suppressed | tolerated as "agricultural custom", taxed when noticed |
| Sound | the Anchor's note | **folk tunes**: "everyone can carry a tune, nobody can read one" |

A festival never has a fixed date, so it never appears on an Office docket. Its date is announced the old way: **someone hangs a sheaf on the Hedge-gate**, and by evening everyone knows.

---

## 2. THE FESTIVALS OF THE RESONANT REACH

### The Last Sheaf (Harvest Home) — *Harvestmonth, when the last field is cut*
The Reach's harvest festival, and the one Book One stands in the middle of.
- The last stand of grain is never cut by one person. The whole village cuts it together, singing **the Sheaf Song** in unison, a cut on every beat. The grain regrows within a day and tastes of less each time (Expanded Bible III.1); the Last Sheaf is the one sheaf the Reach lets **stay cut** for the winter, as a promise that things are allowed to end.
- The sheaf is plaited into a **sheaf-figure** and hung over the Hedge-gate until spring.
- Food: hearth bread from the new flour, cider, river eel, moot-curd.
- **In 419:** the Church set the Rite for Hearthday the sixteenth of Harvestmonth, and the Last Sheaf fell due the same week. Oakhaven was told to postpone it. **It was never held in the open.** The sheaf stood uncut in the north field when the Greening came, and it is still standing. In the game the festival is prepared and then forbidden, and the player can help the village hold it in secret at the dead hour, or not.

### Reed-Hanging — *Reedmonth, when the wind-reeds come up*
Every household takes down last year's First Reeds and hangs new ones tuned to each child's Signature. The street is noisy with tuning for a day. In silenced places, every door has a reed and none sound, and the hanging is done anyway.

### The Eel-Run — *late Firstfruit, the night the eels go down the Flume*
Lanterns along the Chlorophyll Flume, eel-traps, and a long night of quiet fishing. It is the one festival kept in near silence, because the eels can hear. In 419 the seasons ran wrong and the eels went down late, inside Harvestmonth.

### Ringing-Wood Day — *the first hard frost*
Iron-bough timber is felled for the year's building, and each trunk rings as it falls. People gather to hear the wood ring and to name the beams. **Since 18 Frostmonth 419 wood no longer rings in the Reach (Vol V).** After that, Ringing-Wood Day is the festival that stopped.

## 3. THE FEASTS AS THE GAME MEETS THEM

- **Keystone (the Weighing), first Massday of Sheafmonth:** just past when Book One opens. Townsfolk talk about it; Fortis kit and names are in the rumours.
- **Canopy, first Memoryday of Ashfall:** not held since 416. Candles in windows facing in (in the game since Phase 11).
- **The Rite of Pure Unison:** not a feast and not a festival, but the Church dresses it as one. White banners, censers, a compulsory gathering, a sermon. **A festival that is a trap.**

---

## 4. WHISPER-GROVE

### The Drowned Lanterns — *the night the Grove's water is highest*
Paper lanterns are set on the lit water of the drowned capital for the names of the dead, one name per lantern, read aloud by whoever sets it. It is the Spectral Woods' version of Canopy, and the Synod never thought to ban it, because nobody from the Synod goes there.
