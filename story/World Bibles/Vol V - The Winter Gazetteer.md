## 0. The State of the World, Frostmonth 419

- **The year.** 419 SR. The Fracture (the Dissonant Cascade) was 412. The Silencing of Whisper-Grove was 416. The Oakhaven quarry bore, the planting of the Hedge, and the taking of Tobin Ashgrove were 417.
- **The clock.** The Supreme Arbiter will attempt the **Grand Unison** under the Organum on **the Longest Night, 30 Frostmonth (a Stasisday)**. Book Four runs against that date.
- **The calendar — RULING, corrected.** The week is eight days, named for the Anchors: **Rootday · Hearthday · Massday · Stasisday · Dawnday · Driftday · Memoryday · Nullday.** Derived from Book Three's chapter headers and consistent with 19 Frostmonth being a Rootday. On that cycle **the Longest Night falls on a Stasisday**, not a Rootday as this volume first had it — which is the better reading anyway: Stasis is the law of stopping, and the gods, not Vaelen, chose the night. See Vol VI §0.
- **The season.** Winter has reached every realm, including some that have never had one. The Anchors are tiring all at once. Their hold on their laws is slackening, and the cold is coming in through the gaps.
- **The Asking.** Since the night of 18 Frostmonth, every Anchor a party has reached has been heard asking the same question (§6).

## 1. BASTION FORTIS: The Weight of the Floor
### *Mass & Stasis · Stone · Capital of the Compact of Stone*

### Sensory Palette (winter)
**Sound:** stone answering stone. Every footstep on a terrace rings faintly back from the mountain. Deep down, the Sounders' rods go *tock, tock*, six clocks out of time with each other.
**Light:** grey, from above only. Sleet on the shutters. Oil lamps in the vaults.
**Smell:** wet stone, lamp-oil, iron filings, wool coats that never dry.
**Texture:** everything is heavy and cold, and everything is exactly where it was left.

### Status
**Fortis is falling from the bottom up.** The Ninth Stratum golem is descending the root-shaft toward the Keystone, and as it goes down the floor goes *soft* (the quiet comes up from below). Compact-Marshal Hadda Steyn has moved **9,000 of the city's 12,000** people up above the second terrace. The lower terraces are empty and dark.

### The Culture of Weight
- **Greeting.** *"You're heavy?" / "Heavy enough."* Meaning *are you well / I am myself.*
- **Light.** The worst thing you can say of a person: they will go where they are put, and nothing holds them down. Kaelen, without fear, is called *light*.
- **Heavy.** Steadfast, reliable, oneself. To die *heavy* is to die without going soft.
- **Hands.** You do not offer your hand to a stranger. It is presumptuous, a claim on their weight.
- **The commander's weight.** The Compact-Marshal wears the city's **tuning-weight**, a calibrated brass mass on a chain at the throat. Every scale in Fortis is checked against it.
- **Running is heavy, and heavy goes through.** On soft ground you step backward, slowly, in the footsteps of the person ahead.

### The Sounders
Fortis's deep-patrol corps. Each carries a **tuned rod** of black iron as tall as a man, and they walk in a wedge, tapping the floor ahead at every step. Good stone rings. The *dead note* (no ring at all) means the floor ahead has gone quiet.
- Six Sounders under **Sounder-Captain Ulf Garrow** went into the soft floor of the root-shaft on 18 Frostmonth. Seventeen Sounders have been lost in nine years.
- **Ulf Garrow** gave his rod to Kaelen ("Tap. Every step. All the way up. Don't run.") and went down without going soft. His last words: *"Heavy enough."*
- **Heir: Dalla**, the last Sounder on the ninth level, received the rod from Hadda.

### The Weighing Hall
A long vault on the third terrace. When a soldier of the Compact dies, his kit is weighed against calibrated masses, and the weight is cut in the wall beside his name. Tens of thousands of names cover the walls.
- The floor kept Ulf Garrow's kit, so there was nothing to weigh. Hadda put **the Marshal's weight** in the pan instead. The wall now reads: *Ulf Garrow, 419, the Marshal's weight.*
- **RPG:** a fallen PC's player chooses one item of kit for the pan. The Hall records it, and it becomes a relic other characters can invoke.

### The Strata
Twelve living-quartz golems, grown from the mountain at the First Compact, one to each sealed chamber below the ninth level. Each **Stratum** holds part of the Compact of Stone's tally.
- **Orm**, the old **Vault-Warden**, keeps the iron-bound **Register of the Strata**. Each page is headed with a Stratum's name in the angular cant of the Compact, and the column under it reads *Dormant* in a hundred hands.
- **The Ninth Stratum.** Five years ago Orm wrote *Ambulatory. Descending.* He reported it four times, and the Synod's surveyor wrote *settlement* twice. In its empty chamber the Ninth left **10,006 rows of tally-marks**, floor to ceiling, and one inscription: *IT IS THE TEN THOUSAND AND SEVENTH YEAR. THE TERM IS SERVED.*
- **RULING: the Compact of Stone's term was served in 412**, the year of the Fracture. The Rite of Pure Unison broke the Light, but the term had already run out underneath. The other Compacts may have terms too.
- **The Fourteenth Stratum.** The first Warden left the register's last page blank under the heading *the Fourteenth Stratum*. There is no thirteenth and no chamber for a fourteenth. **Open mystery, reserved for Book Five.**

### The Keystone
The Anchor of Mass. It hangs an inch off the floor of a pearl-dark chamber, reached by the **First Compact's spiral stair**, which is older, narrower and steeper than the root-shaft. On 18 Frostmonth it *leaned* toward Kaelen's arm and asked. He walked three steps toward it before Tobiah, Hadda and Dalla stopped him.

## 2. THE RUST COAST: The Sea That Asks
### *Velocity · 639 Hz · Free Captains · Capital: Port Cantabile*

### Status
- **The Burning of Port Cantabile.** On 15 Frostmonth (five days before Vane arrived), six Church cutters landed **about 200 Flambeaux of the Basilica's second chapter** at the dead hour on the slack tide. They burned the **Lantern Yard** and **landings 3 to 6** of the Stair as a *correction*. They arrested no one and read no warrants. **The Mole-bell rang to 71.** Oona Salt's hand was burned.
- **Driftwood Cay.** A second Flambeaux party came for the orphan-house as *"the house that sheltered the murderess of the counting-house."* Their leader, with the burned face, said *"The Pure Note does not distinguish."* Vesper killed eight; a glass-fire shell burned **Bettany** in the doorway with 26 children behind her. Her last words were *"Count them every night."* **Heir: Nell Carder.**

### Customs
- **The Mole-bell.** One stroke for each soul that goes into the water. The Coast does not ring it for traitors, and does not waste stones on them.
- **Children's graves are hung with bells.** Each has a flat stone with a name cut in it and a small bell on an iron hook, so the wind can ring for them. Adults do not get a bell, but **Bettany got one**: the orphan-house supper bell, hung beside Coll Madder's grave.
- **The Twelfth Article.** The Harbour-Queen's law of summary execution (Ormond Dace, Ashfall).

### Reef-Readers
The reefs off the Coast sing and change key with the tide, so the pitch of the ocean tells you the hour, the depth and the set of the current. **Reef-Readers** steer by ear through channels no chart shows.
- **Auld Bessa**, 84, is the last. Since the Fracture the sea has sung in **the winter key**: *"Seven years of winter."*
- Under the winter key, *"she's asking. The whole sea. Every reef from here to the Siren Shallows… It goes up at the end. Like a child at a door."*
- **RPG:** a Reef-Reader in the party grants safe passage through reef hexes and can read the hour without instruments.

### Site Nine
A **Synod siphon platform** on the lip of the trench off **Cape Dissonance**, forty miles south of Port Cantabile: four iron legs, a derrick, and a black pipe drawing water down in a slow swirl.
- **RULING: the Synod bored the trench in '13**, a year before the sea opened under the *Kestrel*. The sea did not open on its own. The Synod opened it to draw on the Velocity root. Oona found the survey under the Prior's receiving-book.
- **38 starving Hollows** worked the pumps, crewed and fed by a quarterly Synod tender for five years; the tender stopped after Harvestmonth, when the Synod split. When Mote hummed 639 they sang the god's question in unison, rising at the end of every stroke. They have been taken off. **The pipe keeps drawing on its own.**
- Ending state: *"Somebody's going to have to cut that."* (This foreshadows Vane's death in Part Three.)

### The *Merret*
Oona Salt's fast cutter-rigged ketch, named for her brother.

## 3. WHISPER-GROVE IN WINTER: The Shortening
### *Memory · 741 Hz · The Spectral Woods · Seat of the Weeping Canopy*

### Sensory Palette (winter)
**Sound:** none. Sound does not carry in the frozen mist. Then, from somewhere, a voice saying two words.
**Light:** white. The lilac mist has frozen into rime. Under the ice of the pools the phosphorescence still burns, lilac, deep down, like a lamp through frosted glass.
**Smell:** snow, wet stone, rot.
**Texture:** the willows are stiff white fountains, and the plazas are sheets of ice with people frozen into them to the shin.

### The Shortening of the Loops
The loops of the Unstrung (the Hollows of the Grove) are decaying, and the decay has accelerated sharply.
- The woman in the doorway, Plaza of the Willows: **nine words in spring 416, four in Harvestmonth 419, two on 19 Frostmonth.** *"— and I'll fetch the other one."* The first three words were struck out in 416 and are lost.
- **Plaza of the Willows census, 19 Frostmonth:** 204 loops. **11** still speaking (one or two words), **171** decayed to gesture, **22** decayed to null.
- **Loop-shade** is the Office's term for a Hollow whose loop has decayed to gesture. Its registers have a column for *Loop decayed to gesture* and another, never filled until now, for *Loop decayed to null*.
- **Sable's curve.** In Harvestmonth she projected zero in 421. In Frostmonth the curve is falling like a stone, and **the last loop in Whisper-Grove falls silent on 30 Frostmonth, the Longest Night.**

### The Chorister's List
Since the Silencing, the Memory Anchor (the Chorister of Memory, under the **Weeping Canopy**) has been reciting **the names of all 74,000 Unstrung**, in order, beginning with *Halder Wick*, so that someone would still know them. It is losing its place, and each pass reaches fewer names (on 19 Frostmonth it stopped at the 143rd). **When the god can no longer reach a name, that Hollow's loop loses a word.**

### The House of the Silent Veil (the "Mausoleum of Chords")
The Veil kept its house in the deep Woods for **eleven hundred years** before the Synod absorbed the order. The Mausoleum of Chords is the Synod's survey name. The scribes called it **the House**.
- A long low building of grey stone on a rise above a frozen pool, now roofless where the *Kestrel* came down through it in Harvestmonth. Over the round arch is carved the motto in the Veil's pitched notation: ***What is written is not lost.***
- **The scriptorium** is on the first floor at the east end, with two rows of sloped desks and a stove at each end. The duty-scribe lit the east stove at the second bell. **The third desk from the east end on the north side** was Sable's.
- The Office took the vellum to the Organum archive. Sable lit the first fire in the east stove in three years.

### The Stacks
Beneath the House, through a door behind the kitchens, a stair leads down through **the labyrinth**: passages cut to the pattern of a *silencing chord in stone* so that nothing below can be heard above. Beneath the labyrinth are **the stacks**, a great hall where the roots of the Weeping Canopy (a kilometre long and thick as towers) come down through the ceiling.
- **The archived dead.** For ten thousand years every soul that died within the Memory Anchor's reach was caught as it decayed and held in the Canopy's roots. They are aware, and they murmur their names. Since the Silencing nothing new has been archived (the Echo no longer catches), but the old dead are still held.
- **The labyrinth is broken.** The dead can be heard from above.
- **RULING: the fourth Hush mouth lies under the Woods.** At the far end of the stacks a line moves across the floor, one root at a time. On one side the roots murmur; on the other they are silent and unchanged. The dead are going down into the Hush. They do not cry out. They say their names until they are not there.

### The Choir-That-Eats (update)
It passed the House at dawn on 20 Frostmonth and **sang Hymn 9 in full harmony**: soprano, alto, tenor, bass and voices between, each a little off from its neighbour so the chord shimmers. It is now beautiful, and **it took the entire Plaza of the Willows**, all 204. Their footprints go east in the snow. It hunts the people with nothing left to say. Sable survived by **reading the Ledger aloud in the voice for entries**, which it did not know how to want.
- **RPG:** reading names aloud (a recitation check each round) is a counter-song against the Choir.

### Hennet Moor
The Veil scribe who stood beside Sable in spring 416 and spoke the woman's nine words aloud. *"Never mind, girl, we'll have it right tomorrow."* She was pressed at the Malting in the harvest of 416, aged seventy-one, for refusing to sign.

### Sable's Answer
The Chorister asked Sable *"Will you take it?"*, meaning become the list. She said ***"Not yet. I have to go and write the end."*** Her last fragment (the smell of the scriptorium on a winter morning) cannot be written down. It now exists nowhere in the world but in her.

## 4. THE REACH AFTER THE GREENING: The Root
### *Growth · 174 Hz · Orchard Country · Capital: Oakhaven*

### Sensory Palette (winter)
**Sound:** none where there should be bells. **RULING: since 18 Frostmonth wood no longer rings in the Reach.** A falling oak-limb lands with a dull wet crack.
**Light:** grey snow under bare grey trees. Houses hang in the crowns.
**Smell:** wet leaves, frost, cut stalks deep underground.
**Texture:** a foot of dead leaves under the snow that does not rustle.

### Status
- **The Leaf-Fall.** At the dead hour of Nullday 18 Frostmonth, every leaf in Oakhaven fell at once, *"a sound like the whole world sighing."* Nothing has grown since. **Growth is stopping, and the Reach is getting its first winter in living memory.** The Root kept the cold out for ten thousand years.
- **Oakhaven is a wood.** The Greening carried the houses up into the oaks' crowns, forty to eighty feet up, and they are still there: a cordwainer's shop-front (*HALE & DAUGHTER*), a bed with frozen blankets, a cart.
- **The Hedge is grey** but still holds every sound Oakhaven made inside it. **RULING: the Hedge, not the Wearer, speaks in the voices of the dead.** It still calls Lyra in Tobin Ashgrove's voice (*"Lyra-love"*, with a fall at the end like a question).
- **The road graves.** The Greenroot road is lined with the graves of refugees who walked toward the Malting.

### The Grove of Oakhaven
In the second week of Ashfall, when the Grafters never came, **Governor Aldric Vance** stood on the Root-shaft head with his feet already rooting and said ***"If we're to be a wood, we'll be one wood."*** Two hundred and twelve people stood with him in a closed ring around the shaft-head, holding hands, so that anyone wanting the Root again would have to cut through all of them. They sang for nine days while they rooted. They became slender trees with grey-white bark like birch, each with a face at head height and hands grown into its neighbours'.
- **They were awake in the wood.** With the Root letting go, the wood was dying around them.
- **The Asking (20 Frostmonth).** Lyra went round the ring and asked each one, the way a Cantor asks a tree with her hand: *Do you want to go?* **187 said yes. 25 said no.** She marked the 25 with red chalk.
- **The Felling.** That night the 187 were sung down. Seraphina took the falling fifth, Wren (in the Aegis) the alto, Dorran Sedge the bass, and Solenne in her void voice took *the fall below the fall*. Ilse Brennan breathed the tending breath, and Lyra conducted. At every chalk-mark the voices dropped to silence and went past.
- **Aldric Vance** was held by the Root until Lyra whistled the lullaby all through, at the cost of blood. Then it let him go. **Heir: Kaelen**, who inherits a message Lyra carries on her slate: *TELL HIM HE WAS AFRAID EVERY DAY. TELL HIM HE WAVED.*
- **The 25 remain**, faces in dying wood, holding hands with the dead.
- **Lyra's slate for the Organum:** *212 ASKED. 187 YES. 25 NO. NOT ALL OF THEM.* This is the seed of the Resonant Accord.

### The Felling-Song (orchard craft)
The last song an orchard Cantor learns, at sixteen. It was sung to an old tree before the axe, and to the burl-sick before the Grafters' saws: a falling fifth, held, let go, and then *the fall below the fall*, a note under the tending note that you feel in the floor. It tells the sap to go down and the tree to sleep. **Lyra sang it four hundred times in the Hollow.**

### The Cantors' Secret (Ilse Brennan's letter)
**RULING, major canon.** The orchard Cantors of the Reach were not founded to tend trees. **They were founded to sing to the Root.**
- At the First Compact a nameless woman went down to the bound god of Growth and sang it **a five-note lullaby** (five notes, a turn, the five again lower, a fall) so it would sleep, hold, and not be afraid of being bound. This is *the oldest song in the world*, passed from teacher to student for ten thousand years. The Cantors were *"the god's nurses."*
- **The Long Tending.** Every Longest Night, every Cantor in the Reach went down **the old stair** into the Root-vault and sang the lullaby all through, because a god holding a law for ten thousand years gets tired and forgets the tune. **The Root waited all year for the stair. It was the only night it was not alone.**
- The Synod's predecessors sealed the vault for a survey **three hundred years ago**. Humming into the hollow of the oldest orchard tree on the Longest Night is the remnant of that custom.
- **Implication:** every Anchor may once have had keepers who came down on the Longest Night. On 30 Frostmonth, after centuries of no one, someone will come down the stair to all of them at once, and it will be Vaelen. **He knows.** *"Then he has been listening longer than any of us."*
- **Implication, followed — see Vol VI §0.1, *The Rota* [PROPOSED].** If every Anchor had keepers, the eight-day week is their duty roster: one order to one god, one day each, and the Longest Night is the night all eight came down at once. That is why the Cantors' night is **Stasisday** and not Rootday — stasis is the only law that can hold the other seven still long enough to be handed over. It also means the Organum, built on the Keynote where all eight roots meet, stands on the old rendezvous, and that Vaelen is performing the Long Tending correctly, alone, with half the words.

### The Old Stair and the Gallery
On the east side of town, where the Hedge meets the Conservatory orchard wall, a slab with an iron ring opens onto **the old stair** (the Synod filled it at the bottom, not the top). It comes out at **a gallery** above the vault. From there the whole Root is visible, a hundred thousand roots twisted into one like a rope. It is grey now, with green only in the deepest places, and the Harvestmonth wound around the rusted bore-bit is still open. The vault is the coldest place in the Reach.

### Ilse Brennan (78)
Cantor-Master of the Reedside Conservatory for forty years, and Lyra's teacher. She lost her voice in 408 after singing nine nights in the Hollow during the throat-fever, and taught for eleven years by slate, hands and breath. *"A Cantor's instrument is the breath. The voice is only the most common way of spending it."*
- On 21 Frostmonth she **stayed in the gallery** to breathe the lullaby to the Root until the Longest Night, *"so that it is not alone when he comes… so that it knows it has been asked by someone who did not want anything from it."* The Root sang the lullaby to the end for the first time in three hundred years. She is not expected to live to the 30th.
- **Heir: Lyra**, who receives **the pitch-reed**, the last sugar-glass wind-reed in the Reach. Blown across, it gives 174 true. *"The breath is the instrument. The reed is only so you know where to start."* Lyra's charge: on the Longest Night, find whatever god she can reach and give it the note.

### Other Reach Figures
- **Dorran Sedge.** He kept the river gate and the reed-beds, sings bass like a bullfrog, and is married to Mabbie. He leads the 41 at Reedside.
- **Ennis Crowle** (beekeeper, Flume road), the first sung down. **Fennick Dray** (15). **Garra Thorne** (orchard-school teacher), one of the 25.

### Reach Customs (expanded)
- **"Does it ring?" / "True enough."** Since the Leaf-Fall *nobody says "true enough"*. Lyra answered NO on her slate.
- **Stone on the tongue, stone on the stone.** The dead are buried with bound jaws and a flat river-stone on the tongue so they cannot ring. A second stone on the grave-marker shows that this was done, so nobody digs. A grave without the second stone was dug in haste. **The Aegis set stones on every bare grave on the Greenroot road**, and the others followed.
- **Asking with the hand.** A Cantor can ask a rooted person or a tree one yes-or-no question through the bark by laying a palm flat and being still.
- **The tending breath.** Four counts in, a long fall out. It is the first thing a Cantor learns at eight. *Trees hear breath before voice.*

## 6. THE ASKING: A Unified Rule
Each Anchor asks the same thing, in the same shape, through whatever it has: glass, deck, bark, stone, or a Hollow's mouth.

> *Is it you? Have you come? Can I put it down now?*

**RULING, the inflection.** It goes up at the end, *"like a child at a door."* Auld Bessa heard it, then Mags (through the deck), then Lyra (through Aldric's roots). Ilse's letter supplies the meaning: **"Have you come?" means "Have you come down the stair? Is it the Night?"** The gods are asking for their keepers. They are asking whether this is the Longest Night, and whether anyone has finally come. Vaelen hears the words. In Book Four Part Three, Kaelen will make him hear the question mark.

**What answers it:**
- *Yes* (Vaelen's Grand Unison): the god puts the weight down.
- *Not yet* (Sable to the Chorister): the god turns away and resumes.
- *Company* (Ilse's breath, Lyra's lullaby): the god remembers the whole song and stops asking for a while. **This is the first evidence that the Anchors can be kept, not only relieved or replaced.** It is the mechanical root of the Resonant Accord.
