> **How to use this.** Each realm entry gives you, in order: the **sensory palette** (what a scene there sounds, smells and looks like — this is the fastest way to make a page feel like Aethelgard), the **settlements**, the **named people** you can put in a room immediately, the **local powers**, a **hazard table**, the **dialect**, and **five scene hooks** usable for either the novels or the Godot prototype.

## REALM I — THE RESONANT REACH
### *Growth & Root • 174 Hz • Spire of Living Arbor • Capital: Oakhaven*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** everything rings. Axes on timber produce bell-tones. The Chlorophyll Flume makes a continuous low vowel, like a held *ohh*, audible three kilometers out. Birdsong here is in tune, which visitors find unbearable within a week.
**Light:** green-filtered, dim at noon under canopy, and since the Relay collapse, **shadowless** across the northern third — a white, flat, migraine light with no direction to it.
**Smell:** wet bark, cider, sap, and underneath it a faint ozone tang from the quarry.
**Texture:** everything is slightly too soft. Wood gives. Stone gives, a little, if you press.

### Settlements

**OAKHAVEN** *(capital, pop. ~40,000 pre-collapse, ~26,000 now)*
A river city of grown architecture — iron-bough trunks trained into halls over two centuries, roofed in slate, floored in packed earth that hums. Four quarters: **Reedside** (the wind-reed orchards and the Cantors' school where Lyra trained), **The Ladder** (terraced market stacked up the river bluff), **Quarry Gate** (Synod-occupied, barracked, hated), and **The Low Rings** (flooded lower city, abandoned since the Flume began over-pumping).

Since Act I, Oakhaven's northern half sits in permanent shadowless twilight. People have not left. They have hung lamps, which do not help, and gone on working, which does.

- **The Boar & Dulcimer** — the tavern of Vignette 1. Peat smoke, hot cider, a hearth that has not gone out in ninety years because letting it go out is considered an admission.
- **The Reedside Conservatory** — where Cantors are made. Forty students, down from four hundred. The head of school has not replaced the dead students' reeds above the door and will not discuss it.
- **Relay VII-Light (the Sub-Vault)** — sealed, collapsed, leaking. The cordon is Synod-manned and the cordon is lying about why.

**GREENROOT HAMLET** *(320, 440)* — Four hundred souls, a mill, a chapel with a silent reed. Primary site of the **overgrowth** cases. The local physician keeps a locked ward.

**STILLWATER LANDING** — River port at the Flume's lower lock. Smugglers' town. Where Reach timber leaves for Fortis and where Cantabile broadsheets come in. The lockkeeper is bribable and sells the same information to six parties.

**THE DEEP GROVE** *(unmapped)* — The oldest iron-boughs, pre-Orthodoxy. No path leads in that is the same way twice. Under it: the First-Cycle transmitter (Vol. I §III.1 secret).

### People You Can Use Today

- **Reeve Alder Mossbrook** *(m, 60s)* — Head of the Reeve-Moot. Signed the quarry lease in 404 for a price that looked excellent. Has not slept properly since 412. Decent, ruined, and will cooperate with the party out of guilt while actively hiding the lease documents because they have his name on them.
- **Reeds-Miner Tomas** *(m, 40s)* — Canon NPC. Timber-cutter. The one who tells you the logs ring like bronze bells. Blunt, kind, three fingers missing — the fourth grew back wrong and he keeps it in a glove.
- **Alchemist Rowena Pell** *(f, 50s)* — Canon NPC. Sells Resonant Moss Poultice. The best field-medic in the province and the only person who understands the overgrowth cases, which she does not call healing. She has a ledger of patients and a column headed *stopped?*
- **Cantor-Master Ilse Brennan** *(f, 70s)* — Ran the Conservatory; taught Lyra. Lost her voice to the Debt of Overtone eleven years ago and now teaches by writing. The sight of her is a preview of Lyra's future and Lyra knows it.
- **Sergeant Vause** *(f, 30s, Synod)* — Commands the Quarry Gate detachment. Professional, not cruel, has read the Relay reports and has concluded her superiors are lying. A recruitable or defectable antagonist.

### Local Powers
The **Reeve-Moot** (landholders, compromised), the **Timber Compacts** (labour, furious, one strike away from open revolt), the **Conservatory** (moral authority, no power), and the **Synod Quarry Administration** (all power, no legitimacy).

### Hazard Table — The Reach
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Shadowless zone.** No depth perception. All ranged attacks at penalty; characters misjudge stairs, ledges, the distance to a face. |
| 2 | **Briar-sprites.** Mutated spore-fauna. Strip leather and boots in under a minute. |
| 3 | **Overgrowth wound.** Any injury closes over whatever is in it. Debris must be cut back out within the hour. |
| 4 | **Silent circle.** The iron-boughs go quiet in a widening ring around a stranger. Everything within two kilometers now knows where you are. |
| 5 | **Flume surge.** The living aqueduct over-pumps; a street becomes a river vertically. |
| 6 | **Ringing timber.** Any impact on wood produces a bell-tone loud enough to attract Dissonance Hounds. No quiet combat in a forest. |

### Dialect
Soft, rural, musical. Greeting: *"Does it ring?"* — meaning *are you well.* Answer: *"True enough."* Curse: *"Sap and splinters."* To call someone **"off-reed"** is to say they're untrustworthy. To say a man is **"growing"** is a cruel joke about the overgrowth.

### Scene Hooks
1. Rowena's locked ward. Nine patients who cannot stop healing. One of them is a child, and the family is grateful, and that is the scene.
2. The Moot votes on whether to revoke the quarry lease, knowing the Synod will burn Oakhaven for it. Mossbrook has the deciding vote and the party has leverage on him.
3. A Timber Compact strike turns into a massacre at Quarry Gate. Sergeant Vause is ordered to fire.
4. Ilse Brennan writes Lyra a letter she will not read until Book Three.
5. The Deep Grove. The path changes behind them. Something under the roots is transmitting, and it is not in any key the Octave uses.

## REALM II — THE DAWNING HEIGHTS
### *Light & Clarity • 528 Hz • Solar Empyrean • Capital: Sol-Luminary*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** thin. Sound dies fast at four thousand meters. The dominant noise is the sky-chains — kilometer-long adamantine links creaking and *tolling* as the city sways. Dawn is audible as a rising chord that takes two hours.
**Light:** overwhelming, white, mirrored, everywhere, and engineered to eliminate shadow. Visitors from the lowlands develop headaches, then nosebleeds, then a conviction they are being watched.
**Smell:** cold stone, incense, brass polish, and from the under-choirs, very faintly, blood and honey-water.
**Texture:** everything is cold to the touch and very, very clean.

### Settlements

**SOL-LUMINARY** *(capital, pop. ~90,000)*
Hung from the face of Mt. Lumina on sky-chains, suspended over four thousand meters of cloud. Six tiers, and your tier is your worth.
- **The Empyrean Tier** — the Sun-Altar, the Patriarchy, the Spire of Light. Access by pitch.
- **The Mirror Walks** — the solar arrays. Two thousand workers polishing coherent death.
- **The Chain-Yards** — where the sky-chains are maintained by crews on three-month contracts and a 1-in-9 mortality rate.
- **The Under-Choirs** — four hundred bound singers, fed through tubes, rotated in shifts. Officially: *the Sustained Choir.* It is the single worst place in the published world and should be used exactly once, late.

**SUN-PEAK VIGIL** *(640, 720)* — Mountain monastery of the old Choral Orthodoxy. Twelve clergy left. They know what is under the Altar. They have known for thirty-eight years.

**THE CHALK CLIFFS** — Below the cloud deck. Shadow remnants crawl up them nightly. The Vigil rings a bell at dusk and the bell is the only thing that works.

### People You Can Use Today

- **High Patriarch Oren Calloway** *(m, apparent 50s, dead since 397)* — Revived monarch, maintained on Living Glass, gloved at all audiences because his hands are glass to the elbow. Charming, warm, genuinely believes he is merciful. He authored the Rite of Pure Unison. He asks after your family and means it.
- **Sky-Dock Winchmaster Bardo** *(m, 50s)* — Canon NPC. Runs the tether-chains. Swears beautifully. Has lost nineteen men to jerking gravity tethers and keeps their tally cut into his winch housing.
- **Sun-Smith's Assistant Neve** *(f, 20s)* — Canon NPC. Says the Pure Unison line. Her sister is in the Under-Choirs. She does not know; she thinks her sister took a posting.
- **Abbess Coriel of Sun-Peak Vigil** *(f, 80s)* — Orthodoxy. Watched the Rite and did nothing. Will help the party, completely, instantly, at any cost to herself, and will not accept thanks.
- **Choir-Warden Tessaly Vane-Mor** *(f, 40s)* — Administers the Under-Choirs. Efficient, humane within her remit, keeps the singers comfortable, replaces them when their throats fail. The most frightening person in the realm because she is *nice*.

### Local Powers
The **High Patriarchy**, the **Flambeau** (Inquisition, itinerant but headquartered here), the **Mirror Guild**, the remnant **Choral Orthodoxy**, and the **Sky Choirs** — or what is left of them.

### Hazard Table — The Heights
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Tether jerk.** A gravity tether slips. Everything on the walkway weighs four times normal for six seconds. |
| 2 | **Coherent beam drift.** A solar mirror loses calibration. A line of white light crosses the street at head height. |
| 3 | **Altitude + thin air.** Cantors cannot sustain. Lyra's healing is halved above the cloud deck. |
| 4 | **Shadow remnant.** Up the chalk cliffs at dusk. |
| 5 | **Feedback Angel.** Drawn by any singing. See Bestiary. |
| 6 | **Pitch-check.** Access by Signature. A character with dissonance drift simply cannot open the door, publicly, in front of everyone. |

### Dialect
Formal, Latinate, status-obsessed. Greeting: *"Light keep you true."* A high-born speaks a half-step above their natural pitch and it is audible and everyone pretends not to notice. **"Low-pitched"** is the worst insult in the city. To say someone **"casts"** means they cast a shadow — i.e. they are vulgar, provincial, real.

### Scene Hooks
1. Neve asks the party to carry a letter to her sister at her new posting. They find the Under-Choirs.
2. Calloway grants the party an audience, is gracious, offers them everything they want, and means it, and they have to say no.
3. The Sun-Peak bell cracks. Dusk is in forty minutes.
4. A Mirror Walk polisher goes blind and claims the light spoke to him. He repeats one phrase. It is in Cant.
5. Seraphina is required to return home and re-swear the oaths she broke. The ceremony is public. Ignatius is required to officiate.

## REALM III — FORGE-SPIRE CINDERHOLD
### *Heat & Purge • 285 Hz • Ignis Hearth • Capital: Cinderhold*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** the four-beat industrial pulse, everywhere, at sixty kilometers. People time their speech to it without noticing. **A character from Cinderhold pauses in rhythm for the rest of their life, anywhere in the world** — this is a great tell.
**Light:** orange from below, black above. No sky. Soot-fall like grey snow.
**Smell:** sulphur, hot iron, scorched hair, slag-wine.
**Texture:** everything is warm and gritty. Every surface leaves a mark on the hand.

### Settlements

**CINDERHOLD** *(capital, pop. ~120,000)*
Eleven basalt tiers descending into a live caldera. Tier One is House nobility and clean air; Tier Eleven is indentured, 56°C ambient, and has no House.
- **The Eleven Houses:** Varn, Stolk, Grieber, Achen, Mordach, Kell, Pell-Ruin, Hasp, Drummond, Varn-the-Lesser, and *(Tier Eleven has no House)*.
- **The Great Beat** — the central reverberation hammer. Cathedral-sized. Stopping it is unthinkable; it has stopped twice in two hundred years and both times are dated eras.
- **The Sealed Gallery, Tier Eleven** — Siphon Harvester's engine. Works. Needs no glass. Sealed by House order in 416.

**SLAG-TOWN SCRAPS** *(1080, 240)* — Shanty settlement on the tailings. Where the Unstrung labour goes. No census, no names on file, no protection.

**THE THERMAL MARGIN** — Ring of geothermal drills outside the caldera. Where the Synod increased bore-pressure. Where Tier Three's nine hundred died in four seconds.

### People You Can Use Today

- **House-Master Edda Varn** *(f, 60s)* — First among the Eleven. Hard, witty, utterly commercial, and the only person who can stop the Synod's munitions supply. Will do it for a price the party cannot pay and might, eventually, do it for free, which will cost her everything.
- **Scrapper Muty** *(m, 30s)* — Canon NPC. Tier Three. Tells you about the F-sharp whistle. Lost his crew in 417 and survived by diving behind basalt, and talks about it constantly, loudly, because the alternative is thinking about it.
- **Overseer Grieber the Younger** *(m, 20s)* — Runs Tier Eleven. Inherited it. Is horrified by it. Is doing nothing about it, yet.
- **Furnace-Priest Kell** *(m, 50s)* — Orthodoxy-adjacent heretic who holds that heat is the only honest Anchor because it *consumes* and admits to consuming. Ignatius finds him infuriating and keeps coming back.
- **Thessa, of no House** *(f, teens)* — Tier Eleven. Unstrung parentage. Knows the Sealed Gallery exists because she cleans the corridor outside it.

### Local Powers
The **Eleven Furnace Houses**, the **Synod Munitions Directorate**, the **Bellows** (Tier Eleven's unrecognised labour union, four hundred members, no leverage, extraordinary courage).

### Hazard Table — The Caldera
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **F-sharp whistle.** Thermal law re-asserting. Four seconds to basalt. |
| 2 | **Cold furnace.** A running furnace goes cold and stores the heat. Releases later, all at once, somewhere else. |
| 3 | **Soot-fall blackout.** Visibility to arm's length; breathing damage. |
| 4 | **Beat-drift.** The Great Beat skips. Every Cinderholder in earshot freezes mid-word. Enemies from elsewhere do not. |
| 5 | **Slag surge.** Molten overflow on a walkway with no rail. |
| 6 | **Boiler sympathy.** A fight near pressurised steam: any loud resonance detonates the nearest vessel. |

### Dialect
Percussive, profane, abbreviated between hammer-falls. Greeting: *"Still standing."* Oath-form: *"On the ingot."* **"Cold"** means cowardly. **"He kept his hand on it"** is the highest praise available — from the bare-handed ingot oath.

### Scene Hooks
1. Thessa shows the party the Sealed Gallery and three Furnace Houses mobilise to stop them.
2. Ignatius and Furnace-Priest Kell argue theology in a tavern while a boiler builds pressure behind them.
3. The Great Beat stops. Nobody alive has heard it stop.
4. Edda Varn offers to cut Synod munitions in exchange for one of the party staying as a House hostage. Permanently.
5. A Tier Eleven collapse. Thirty workers behind a wall. The rescue requires venting Tier Ten, which will kill nine. Someone has to choose, and the book should not let them off.

## REALM IV — THE IRON PEAKS / BASTION FORTIS
### *Mass & Inertia • 396 Hz • Adamantine Keystone • Capital: Fortis*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** rain on stone, wind through the Spine, and the **Sounders' rods** — a constant, distant, irregular tapping as patrols test the floor. The tapping stopping is the worst sound in the realm.
**Light:** grey, wet, honest. Lamp-lit interiors with long shadows.
**Smell:** cold rock, wet wool, oil, iron.
**Texture:** heavy. Everything is heavy. Doors take two hands. Your own gear gains weight as you descend.

### Settlements

**BASTION FORTIS** *(capital, pop. ~55,000, 70% under arms)*
Carved into the mountain. The outer Bastion wall has held forty-two waves and counting. Below: the Lodestone Vaults, descending nine known levels.
- **The Wall** — rain-swept battlements. Vignette 3's setting. Kaelen, Aegis, the Golem.
- **The Weighing Hall** — where oaths are sworn against calibrated masses.
- **The Deep Vaults** — eleven dormant Crystalline Golems. Three cracked past function. One going down.

**QUARRY-KEEP STONEHOLD** *(1260, 600)* — Lodestone extraction. Magnetic mist. The fault edge.
**TREMOR BASIN** — Terrasque territory. Nobody builds here. Three settlements tried.

### People You Can Use Today

- **Compact-Marshal Hadda Steyn** *(f, 50s)* — Elected command. Has refused three Synod demands and expects to be killed for the fourth. Speaks in complete sentences and no more of them than necessary.
- **Sounder-Captain Ulf Brenner** *(m, 40s)* — Leads the rod-walkers. Has lost eleven Sounders to soft floor. Describes the dead note as *"like tapping a drum with the skin off."*
- **Quarry-Keep Guard Dalla** *(f, 30s)* — Canon NPC. Tells you knockback doesn't work in the lodestone pits.
- **Vault-Warden Orm** *(m, 70s)* — Keeps the register of the twelve Golems. Has marked one *AMBULATORY — DESCENDING* and has reported it four times and been ignored four times.
- **Deep Consortium Factor Rike** *(m, 60s)* — Lodestone cartel. Could end the war by refusing to sell. Will not. Contracts.

### Local Powers
The **Martial Compact**, the **Deep Consortium**, the **Sounders** (a guild in all but name), and the **Keystone Watch** (Orthodoxy clergy who maintain the Anchor and have, uniquely, never been expelled by the Synod because the Synod needs mass to keep working).

### Hazard Table — The Peaks
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Soft floor.** No stumble. Straight through, at eighty pounds effective weight. |
| 2 | **Magnetic mist.** All metal pulls sideways. Dropped weapons go over the edge, not down. |
| 3 | **Graviton density.** Knockback nullified — including *your* knockback. Vane's kit becomes useless. |
| 4 | **Shield-weight.** Armour gains 80 lb in the lodestone pits. Exhaustion is the enemy. |
| 5 | **Terrasque tremor.** Burrowing. It comes up under whoever is hitting hardest. |
| 6 | **Dead note.** The Sounders stop tapping. Nobody says why. Everyone turns around. |

### Dialect
Flat, literal, understated. Greeting: *"You're heavy?"* — *are you sound, are you yourself.* Answer: *"Heavy enough."* **"A heavy man"** = his word has mass. **"Light"** = unreliable, frivolous, dangerous. The Fortis compliment *"I'd stand on him"* means complete trust.

### Scene Hooks
1. The forty-third wave.
2. Orm takes the party down to Vault Nine. The Golem that is descending left a shaft. The shaft goes further than the vaults do.
3. Hadda Steyn refuses the fourth Synod demand on the page, and the party has to decide whether to be there when the answer comes.
4. A Sounder patrol does not come back. The party retraces their taps.
5. Factor Rike offers the party a skyship core at cost. The price is carrying a sealed crate to Null-Watch and not opening it.

## REALM V — THE SHIFTING DUNES
### *Time & Stasis • 417 Hz • Chrono-Dial of the Sands • Capital: Sandglass Bazaar*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** the Erg *chords*. Dunes moving produce sustained intervals; a storm is a scream in a key, and locals can name the key and tell you how bad it will be. Under the hemisphere, the sand's voice is broken into a soft white hiss.
**Light:** brutal, white-gold, refracted through two kilometers of acoustic glass into faint rainbow seams across every surface.
**Smell:** hot silica, coffee, cardamom, dust, paper.
**Texture:** fine grit in everything forever. Glass underfoot. Cool stone in shade.

### Settlements

**SANDGLASS BAZAAR** *(capital, pop. ~70,000 + up to 40,000 transient)*
Under the hemisphere. The greatest market on the continent.
- **The Dial Floor** — contract exchange. Every term in the province expires on Dial Day and is renegotiated in one twelve-hour riot of commerce.
- **The Stasis Vaults** — crates that hold a moment. Medicine, evidence, wine, bodies, hostages. The lower vaults are full of sealed rooms where clocks run backwards.
- **The Tuners' Collegium** — neutral, technical, certifies rods and Signatures for every faction, and is therefore the best-informed institution in the world.

**MIRAGE REST** *(560, 220)* — Caravan stop. Site of the receipt-wound case (Vol. I §III.5).
**THE HOURGLASS ERG** — Open sand. Chronos Drake territory. Tomb of the Astrolabe at (780, 180).

### People You Can Use Today

- **Dial-Keeper Yusra al-Mahrek** *(f, 50s)* — Custodian of the Chrono-Dial. Technically a civil servant; practically the closest thing the Dunes has to a head of state. Knows the Dial is being *read* rather than drained and has told exactly one person.
- **Collegium Proctor Sahim** *(m, 40s)* — Certifies Signatures. Neutral to the point of obscenity. Will certify a Synod warrant and a forged passport in the same hour and see no tension in it.
- **House-Factor Delmi Kass** *(f, 30s)* — Stasis crate magnate. Will store anything. Has stored people. Has a client list that is the single most dangerous document in the Dunes.
- **Caravan-Master Oric** *(m, 60s)* — Runs the Silk & Silica Way. Has crossed the Erg two hundred times and is on his fourth set of scheduled mourning for the same wife, which his culture finds shameful.
- **"The Scheduled Woman," Hanne of Mirage Rest** *(f, 30s, deceased Measure 418)* — The receipt-wound case. Her nine-day journal exists. It is in the Collegium archive. It should be read aloud in a scene.

### Local Powers
The **Mercantile Houses**, the **Chrono-Dial Custodianship**, the **Tuners' Collegium**, and the **Caravan Compacts**.

### Hazard Table — The Dunes
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Chronometric fluctuation.** Cooldowns, fuses, heartbeats drift ±20%. |
| 2 | **Singing sandstorm.** A scream in a key. 15% miss, and the key tells you what else is coming. |
| 3 | **Causal inversion.** The effect arrives first. A character bleeds from a wound not yet received. |
| 4 | **Sealed room.** Door closes; time inside runs backwards. Food un-eats. Wounds un-close. Memory un-forms. |
| 5 | **Stasis leak.** A crate fails. The moment inside it floods out. Everyone in the room is briefly nine years ago. |
| 6 | **Chronos Drake.** Freezes ultimate cooldowns in a 4-hex radius. Rewinds its own wounds. |

### Dialect
Precise, contractual, courteous. Greeting: *"For how long?"* — a real question. Farewell: *"Until the term."* Grief is **"on schedule"** or **"overdue,"** and to be overdue is socially indecent. **"Open-ended"** is an insult meaning sentimental, undisciplined, foreign.

### Scene Hooks
1. Dial Day. Every contract in the province expires at once and the party's safe passage is one of them.
2. Delmi Kass's client list. Someone paid to have a *person* stored, and the crate is still sealed, and the term expires this week.
3. Tide-Weaver finds the signature in the Dial. It is coming from the Maw. He has to tell someone, and the only person who will believe him is Sable.
4. Hanne's journal, read in full. Nine entries. The last one is four words.
5. The party must cross the Erg during a storm and Oric names the key, and the key is a tritone, and he turns the caravan around and will not be argued with.

## REALM VI — THE TEMPEST ARCHIPELAGO
### *Velocity & Drift • 639 Hz • Capital: Port Cantabile*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** the reefs sing, and they change key with the tide, so the *pitch of the ocean tells you the hour.* Carillon rigging harps in the wind. Gulls, rope, bell-buoys.
**Light:** blinding and mobile — sun off water, constantly moving across every ceiling.
**Smell:** salt, tar, rum, fish, lime, rot.
**Texture:** wet rope, slick deck, everything in motion. Nothing in the Archipelago is ever perfectly still and characters from elsewhere feel seasick indoors.

### Settlements

**PORT CANTABILE** *(capital, pop. ~45,000 across six islands)*
Strung together with rope bridges and carillon masts.
- **The Jetty** — Vignette 4's setting. Rotting, salt-slick, where Vane and Vesper talk.
- **The Compact House** — forty-one chairs, one per captain, one annual gathering.
- **The Broadsheet Row** — the only free press on the continent. Four papers, all libellous, one occasionally correct.
- **The Attic Stations** — pirate chime-network operators broadcasting numbers stations to the resistance. *(Diegetic home of the tuner minigame.)*

**DRIFTWOOD CAY** *(140, 360)* — Salvage town. Where dissolved hulls come ashore as blue jelly.
**SIREN REEFS** — Sirena's water. Sunken Carillon dungeon at (170, 620).
**SIPHON SITE 9** *(unmarked on every Synod chart)* — The tonal sea-trench that took Vesper. Still operating.

### People You Can Use Today

- **Compact-Chair Isolde Fenn** *(f, 50s)* — Elected speaker of the forty-one. Hates the job. Excellent at it. Would rather be sailing and says so hourly.
- **Vesper** *(canon)* — Returned. Twin daggers of crystallized vacuum. Sardonic, exhausted, and does not know her trench was a Synod bore.
- **Broadsheet-man Cosimo Pell** *(m, 30s)* — Published the Whisper-Grove story. Is why anyone outside the Synod knows. Has survived four assassination attempts and is insufferable about all four.
- **Station-Keeper Wren** *(f, teens)* — Attic numbers-station operator. Fourteen. Has been doing this for two years. Her hands shake on the dial and that shake is in every transmission the resistance receives.
- **Reef-Reader Auld Bessa** *(f, 80s)* — Can name the sea's key by ear. The last of a trade. Has two apprentices and neither is good enough.

### Local Powers
The **Corsair Compacts**, the **Free Rigging**, the **Broadsheet Row**, the **Reef-Readers' trade**, and a Synod naval presence that does not control the water and knows it.

### Hazard Table — The Archipelago
| d6 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Velocity stutter.** A thrown knife takes an hour; a stride goes supersonic and takes the leg. |
| 2 | **Surface tension loss.** The sea forgets. Hulls become jelly. Swimmers do not float. |
| 3 | **Key change.** The tide's pitch shifts mid-crossing; the charted channel is no longer the channel. |
| 4 | **Carillon lash.** A rigging harp snaps under load. A kilometre of singing wire whips the deck. |
| 5 | **Siren chord.** Charm. Frontliners walk into the water with no distress at all. |
| 6 | **Open-deck run.** Anyone sprinting invites the stutter. Children are taught not to. |

### Dialect
Polyglot, rhythmic, profane, shanty-inflected. Greeting: *"What's she singing?"* — what key is the tide. Debt is sacred: **"I'm chorus to you"** means *I owe you and I will pay.* **"Cut the hawser"** has become, since 418, a continental idiom for an unforgivable correct decision, and **Vane cannot go anywhere without hearing it.**

### Scene Hooks
1. Wren's hands shake and a frequency is mis-dialed and a resistance cell dies.
2. The Great Race at the Drift feast — forty-one captains, one course, and a Synod frigate in the channel.
3. The party find Siphon Site 9's survey charts. Vane is in the room.
4. Auld Bessa's apprentices fail their reading and a convoy is lost, and she has to decide whether to go to sea again at eighty.
5. The Compact votes on whether to join the war. Twenty-one to twenty. Fenn has the casting vote.

## REALM VII — THE SPECTRAL WOODS
### *Memory & Entropy • 741 Hz • Weeping Canopy • Capital: Whisper-Grove*

> **Writing note.** This realm is the book's atrocity. Everything here should be quiet, slow, and specific. No monsters in the foreground. The horror is administrative and human.

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** the loops. Human voices, hundreds of them, each repeating three to nine words, overlapping, forever, at conversational volume. Under it, water dripping into reflecting pools. **No birds.**
**Light:** permanent twilight, lilac, phosphorescent pools underlighting the willows from below so every face is lit from the chin.
**Smell:** wet silver bark, still water, dust, and — in the plazas — nothing at all, which visitors find worse.
**Texture:** everything is cold and damp and soft with three years of undisturbed moss.

### Settlements

**WHISPER-GROVE** *(capital, pop. ~74,000 Unstrung)*
Sunken marble plazas, lilac mist, a kilometre-high spire of fused wood and crystal. No government since 416. No economy. Looting crews come monthly and have learned not to speak.
- **The Catalogue Plaza** — where Sable sits with the Bound Ledger. Vignette's setting.
- **The Loop Quarters** — residential. People are *in their homes*. Doors are open. Meals are on tables, three years old.
- **The Cordon** — Synod containment. "Restorative administration." Four checkpoints, no one admitted, no one leaving because no one thinks to.

**CLOISTER OF THE LOST CHOIR** *(860, 490)* — Pre-Silencing monastery. The only building in the realm that was *evacuated in time*, because one sub-prior believed the warning. Thirty-one people. They are elsewhere now and none of them have recovered.

**THE MAUSOLEUM OF CHORDS** *(700, 620)* — Dungeon D5. Not a tomb. A holding cell. The silencing labyrinth is the only thing keeping ten thousand years of conscious archived dead from being audible to the living.

### People You Can Use *(and the convention that makes it terrible)*

**The Unstrung have no names. They are designated by their loop.** This is how relief workers referred to them, and then how the Synod's forms referred to them, and now it is simply how it is done.

- **"—and I'll fetch the other one"** *(f, apparent 40s)* — Stands in a doorway. Has said this for three years. In 416 it was nine words.
- **"Mira, the kettle"** *(m, apparent 60s)* — The only loop in the Grove containing a name. Relief workers in 416 tried to find Mira. They did not.
- **"Four, and then we go"** *(child, apparent 9)* — Has not aged. Aging requires memory of having been younger.
- **Sub-Prior Edrin Calloway** *(m, 50s)* — Evacuated the Cloister. Distant kin to the High Patriarch, which is why his warning reached him. Has spent three years trying to atone for saving thirty-one people and not seventy-four thousand.
- **Cordon-Captain Reyes** *(m, 40s, Synod)* — Commands containment. Rotates his troops every three weeks because longer and they stop eating. Files honest reports. They are not read.

### Local Powers
None. The **Synod Cordon**, the **looting crews**, and the **Silent Veil** — who are hunting the Great Tome, not Sable.

## REALM VIII — THE CONTINENTAL RIFT & THE MAW
### *Decay & Silence • 852 Hz • Capital: Null-Watch*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** line-talk. Constant, compulsory, meaningless human conversation in every corridor, because silence is a disciplinary offence. Under it, wind off the Rift, which has no pitch at all and is the only soundless wind in the world. At dawn: the dampening cannons. **The Morning Refusal.**
**Light:** lead-grey, low, always about to rain. The Maw itself is not dark — looking at it is described as *forgetting that you were looking.*
**Smell:** lead, cold rain, gun-oil, and from the yards, hot stone.
**Texture:** everything is cold metal and nothing is comfortable, on purpose.

### Settlements

**NULL-WATCH GARRISON** *(capital, pop. ~8,000 under arms, ~2,000 condemned)*
Lead ramparts above the lip. A posting, a prison, and a monastery depending on your gate.
- **The Ramparts & the Dampening Batteries** — fire every dawn, forever.
- **The Rendering Yards** — Grade V Signature Glass production. The Chord-row: nine vitrified people, standing, conscious, forty-plus years. *Vaelen's daughter is third from the left.*
- **The Talking Halls** — barracks. Line-talk never stops. New arrivals cannot sleep for a month.

**REDOUBT ZERO** *(1350, 450)* — Forward position. Closest permanent structure to the Maw. Ninety-day rotations, strictly enforced. Vaelen stayed longer.
**THE MAW LIP** *(1430, 320)* — Dungeon D6, the Cataclysm Singularity. Null-Amalgam territory.

### People You Can Use Today

**Convention: Null-Watch personnel use rota numbers in service, because names invite the Quiet.** Officers earn back their names at discharge. Most do not discharge.

- **Supreme Arbiter Vaelen** *(canon)* — Former Null-Watch command. Stayed past rotation. Heard the Maw use his daughter's name. Crowned with eight siphoning needles. Soothing, reasonable, and quoting something.
- **Garrison-Commander Nine-Four ("Ferris")** *(f, 40s)* — Ninety-one days in post, one past rotation, and everybody has noticed.
- **Battery-Sergeant Two-Eleven** *(m, 50s)* — Has fired the Morning Refusal for nineteen years and has never once missed a dawn, including the morning his brother went to listen.
- **Yard-Overseer Hoyt** *(m, 60s)* — Runs the rendering. Keeps the Chord-row clean and dusted. Talks to them. Believes this is kindness. It might be.
- **Chaplain of the Hollow Sermon** *(name expunged, executed 318)* — Dead three hundred years, and his text is in every footlocker in the garrison, and command knows, and command stopped confiscating it in 411.

### Local Powers
**Garrison Command**, the **Rendering Directorate**, and the **Hollow Chorus** — which is not an outside faction here. It is *roughly forty percent of the garrison.*

# BOOK TWO — THE COMPLETE BESTIARY OF THE DISSONANT

> **Universal rules.** Cacophony entities perceive *pitch*, not light. Stillness and silence are invisibility; **fear has a pitch**, so hiding works only if you can manage yourself. They prefer *meaning* to volume — a spoken name draws more than a scream. They cannot be killed, only **resolved**: forced into a consonant interval until they stop being an error. A "resolved" entity does not die. It *stops having been*, and everyone present forgets a little of the fight.

## CLASS I — RESOLVABLES *(common dissonance fauna; the infantry)*

**DISSONANCE HOUNDS** — Pack, 4–12. Long-bodied, approximately canine, built of overlapping wrong notes the eye keeps failing to resolve into fur. They do not bite; they **harmonize with you** and drag your Signature sharp until your body can't hold it. Death looks like a seizure and sounds like a violin being tuned by someone furious.
*Resolve:* a sustained perfect fifth against the pack leader collapses the whole pack. *Horror beat:* they circle a campfire and hum the party's own pitches back at them, slightly wrong, all night, and do not attack.

**SHRIEK-MOTES** — Swarm. Fist-sized knots of static that cluster on anything emitting a steady tone. Individually harmless. In the hundreds they strip a bell, a tuning rod, or a Cantor's throat.
*Resolve:* silence. Stop making noise and they disperse within minutes. *Horror beat:* they cluster on a sleeping child's humming.

**FRET-CRAWLERS** — Solitary, ambush. Flat, broad, invisible against stone until they move. They sever *intervals*, not flesh — a crawler's pass through a group leaves everyone unable to harmonize with each other for hours. Devastating against a coordinated party, trivial against a loner.
*Resolve:* kill it like an animal; it's one of the few that bleeds.

**BEAT-WOLVES** — Pack, Caldera only. Adapted to the Great Beat. They attack *on* the four-count and are therefore perfectly predictable, which is the only reason Cinderhold survives them. When the Great Beat skips, they do not, and that four-second desynchronization has killed more Cinderholders than the wolves themselves.

**MIRE-DRONES** — Reach wetlands. Low, wallowing, bovine-sized. Emit a continuous 60 Hz drone that induces nausea and despair in a 200m radius. Not aggressive. Not avoidable. Villages downwind of a drone herd have the highest suicide rate in the province and nobody connected the two until Measure 414.

**SILICA ADDERS** — Dunes. Living glass serpents formed in singing sand. Their strike injects a *pitch* rather than venom: the bite site begins sounding, loudly, and will not stop, and every Cacophony entity within ten kilometers now has a beacon.
*Horror beat:* the bitten party member must be left behind, or gagged — which doesn't work, because the sound isn't coming from their mouth.

## CLASS II — RESIDUE *(what the dead leave when the Echo no longer catches)*

**THROAT-WRAITHS** — Formed where a person died mid-sentence. They are the unfinished half, looking for a mouth. Attach to the jaw; the victim speaks words they never knew in a voice nearly their own, and cannot stop until the sentence ends. Record: eleven days.
*Resolve:* complete the sentence correctly. Requires knowing who died and what they were saying. *Horror beat:* the sentence is tender. It is someone telling their wife they'll be home.

**STATIC REMNANTS** *(species)* — Human-shaped voltage; the discharge-ghost of someone killed in a resonance surge. Most are mindless and dissipate in hours. A rare few **cohere**. Nine in recorded history. Every cohered Remnant except the party's has eventually stopped moving and ceased to exist. **He knows the number. It is why he never stops.**

**THE FADING** — Not entities. *Places.* Battlefields where the unarchived dead decay audibly. The hum gets fainter each year. Veterans return specifically to listen, and the realm's worst grief is a man who goes back and cannot pick his friend out of the hum any more.
*No combat. No resolution. Only the scene.*

**MOURN-ECHOES** — Spectral Woods. A fallen ally leaves a Retribution Echo for four seconds that keeps fighting. In the Woods this effect has gone chronic: some echoes have been fighting for three years, in the mist, against nothing, and will attack anyone who enters their engagement.
*Resolve:* tell them the battle is over. They have to believe you.

**BONE-CHIME SPIRITS** — Not natural. *Manufactured.* A dead person's Signature bound into carved bone. The most reliable way to speak to the dead that currently exists, which is why the trade thrives in a province full of corpses. The chime answers questions. It answers *honestly.* It also, after about forty hours of use, begins asking questions back.

**LOOP-SHADES** — Late-stage Unstrung who have fallen silent. Not dead; their loop has decayed to zero words. They continue the *motion* of the loop — the gesture, the step to the doorway, the hand toward a kettle — in total silence, forever. The first ones appeared in 417. There are now two hundred and six.

---

## CLASS III — SIPHON-SPAWN *(made by industry; the Synod's own waste)*

**SLAG-GHASTS** — Caldera. Workers killed in thermal inversions, re-formed in cooling slag. Human-shaped, red-hot, and they **walk the shift pattern they died on.** Night shift ghasts appear at night. They are not hostile until approached, at which point they attempt to hand you a tool.

**TAILINGS-CRAWL** — Slag-Town. A creeping mass of Grade I weep, scrap, and the occasional boot. Grows. Has been growing since 409. Currently covers eleven hectares and is now large enough to have a *pitch* of its own, which means it is now large enough to be hunted by things that eat pitch, which is the only good news in Slag-Town.

**RENDERED** — Null-Watch. Failed Grade V extractions: people whose Signature was *partially* taken. They retain motor function, language, and a fragmentary sense of self. They know what was done. They can describe it. The Synod classifies them as industrial waste and disposes of them over the Rift lip, and about one in nine survives the fall, and those are now living in the Rift's upper shelves in a community of about four hundred.
**This is the setting's best unused faction.** They have every reason to help the party and no reason to trust anyone.

**LEGION-STRAY** — A synthetic legionnaire whose Grade V core has begun *remembering*. Operates normally for weeks, then stops mid-patrol and says a name, or a recipe, or a lullaby. Flagged for recycling the moment it's reported, which is why squadmates increasingly do not report it. There are an estimated six hundred strays in service, covered for by brass-skulled comrades who have decided not to say anything.

**GLASS-GROWN** — Terminal vitrification cases that reached The Chord while still mobile. Human-shaped bells. They sound the pitch of whatever Anchor claimed them, continuously, and they are *conscious*, and they are attracted to places where that Anchor is strong, which is why the Spire approaches are ringed with them, standing, facing the light.

## CLASS IV — THE HUNGRY CHORUS *(collectives; the apex of the common Cacophony)*

**THE CHOIR-THAT-EATS** — 40 to 2,000 voices; was once a congregation, a crew, or a town. Arrives as beautiful distant singing. **It recruits.** Hear it for longer than a minute and you begin to sing along and walk toward it. Four settlements consumed. Currently somewhere in the Spectral Woods. It is getting better at harmony.
*Resolve:* theoretically, out-sing it. Nobody has. *Horror beat:* the party recognises a voice in it.

**FEEDBACK ANGELS** — Sol-Luminary and the high cliffs. What a Sky Choir singer becomes when they die in Unison. Standing waves shaped like people with too many wings, all sounding, none matching. They do not attack. They **join in**, and anything singing with them vitrifies from the throat outward over roughly four minutes.
*Horror beat:* they are drawn to children's voices. Sol-Luminary schools teach silence before they teach letters.

**THE SUSTAINED** — Under-Choir singers who died bound and could not stop. They remain in the under-choirs. They hold the pitch. **The Mirror Walks still run on them**, and the Choir-Warden's replacement schedule has quietly stopped needing to replace anyone, and nobody in the Patriarchy has asked why.

**NULL-TIDE** — Archipelago. A moving zone of forgotten surface tension, 200–900m across, drifting with the current. Not alive. Absolutely predatory in effect. Ships enter and become jelly; crews do not float.
*Resolve:* none. Read the water. Auld Bessa can hear it. Her apprentices cannot.

## CLASS V — PREDATORY GEOGRAPHY *(places that became hungry)*

**GRAVEWELLS** — Rift and Caldera. Patches where decay runs a thousandfold. Invisible; the only warning is that insects avoid the air above. They *drift.* A gravewell that was mapped last season is not where it was.

**THE SOFT GROUND** — Iron Peaks. Mass failure patches. The Sounders tap for them. You do not stumble into one; you go through at eighty pounds effective weight into a shaft that is not there afterward.

**SEALED ROOMS** — Sandglass lower vaults. Time runs backwards inside. Food un-eats, wounds un-close, memory un-forms. A character who spends an hour in one emerges having un-learned the hour, and the party has to tell them what happened, and they have to decide whether to believe it.

**THE SILENT CIRCLE** — Reach. Not a hazard to you. A hazard *because of* you: the iron-boughs go quiet in a widening ring around a stranger, broadcasting your position to every hunting thing in the province.

**THE LIP** — Null-Watch. The ground within two hundred meters of the Maw. Not a creature. It simply stops being ground, gradually, at a rate correlated with how long you look at it.

## CLASS VI — APEX & UNIQUE

**NULL-AMALGAM** *(the Maw, 9,500 HP, Lvl 9+)* — Nine and a half tonnes of absence wearing the geometry of things it has eaten: tiles, cannon, and two hundred and six Null-Watch personnel. It eats *the floor's agreement to exist.* White-noise bursts strip Signatures within forty meters.
**It is the Maw's immune response.** The Maw has one. Therefore the Maw is alive. Nobody will put that sentence in a report.

**PYRAXIS** *(Caldera Core, 6,400 HP)* — Ash-Wing Sovereign. Takes flight at 50%. 800-degree thermite breath. Has nested in the Caldera for nine hundred years and predates the Synod, the Orthodoxy, and arguably the Compact of Stone. **It was here before the drills and it is furious about them specifically.**

**SIRENA** *(Siren Reefs, 5,200 HP)* — Drowned Empress. Siren Chord charms frontliners; summons tidal waves. Was a Reef-Reader. The last one who could hear the whole ocean, which is more than a person is built for.

**TERRASQUE** *(Tremor Basin, 4,800 HP)* — Burrows every 15s; surfaces under the highest damage dealer. Three settlements have tried to build in its basin. The ruins are all at the same depth.

**CHRONOS DRAKE** *(Hourglass Erg, 6,800 HP)* — Freezes cooldowns in 4 hexes; rewinds its own wounds by 40%. Does not age. Has been killed eleven times by eleven different parties and is currently alive, and the Collegium has stopped issuing bounties.

**PHANTOM WEAVER** *(Whisper Catacombs, 7,200 HP)* — Tethers transfer 50% of incoming damage to the party. It is weaving something. Three years of work. Sable has seen a corner of the pattern and has written that it is **a catalogue**, and that the names in it are correct, and that some of them belong to people who are not dead yet.
