## Part II — The Dread Doctrine

These are the rules the prose obeys. They exist so that terror accumulates instead of spiking and fading.

**The world is already lost.** The trilogy does not ask whether Aethelgard ends. It asks what it costs to lose it slowly, and who pays. Hope is real, and it is expensive.

**Every miracle sends an invoice.** A healing, a rescue, a victory: each is paid for on the page, often chapters later, often by someone who was never consulted.

**Sound is the predator's sense.** Every scene knows how loud it is. Write the soundscape before the landscape. A laugh, a dropped pot, a crying child are this world's jump scares, and silence is worse.

**Never trust a voice you cannot see.** The Cacophony wear the voices they have eaten. From Chapter One onward, the reader should flinch whenever a beloved voice speaks from off the page.

**No one is clean.** Nearly every champion was complicit in the First Fracture or profited from what followed it. The Chord is not a band of heroes. It is a band of accomplices trying to become something else.

**Horror climbs a ladder.** Body, then household, then town, then realm, then law, then cosmos. Book One lives on the lower rungs. Book Three stands on the top one and looks down.

**The camera stays for the aftermath.** The burial, the smell of the ward afterward, the child who does not speak for a month. Battles end. Consequences do not.

**Humor is armor, not relief.** People joke because they are terrified. Vane laughs loudest when he is closest to breaking. A joke never resolves tension; it proves how much there is.

**The monsters are made of us.** Every horror is a human loss wearing a new shape: soldiers powered by stolen names, beasts wearing dead fathers' voices, fuel pressed out of gods and people.

**Kindness is the one thing the silence cannot digest.** Small mercies are the trilogy's only light, and the dark wants them most. Protect them on the page so that it hurts when they are taken.

**Dial setting — DECIDED: push harder.** From Chapter Eleven the camera does not cut away. Body horror is rendered rather than implied; the aftermath is shown at length; what a Cacophony does to a person is on the page, and so is what a law-collapse does to a town. Two limits hold, because they make the rest land harder rather than softening it: harm to children is present, felt and consequential, but never rendered in detail; and no cruelty is staged for spectacle — every horror in this book is something a person did for a reason, or something a person loved becoming unrecognizable.

Touchstones: the weight of *Berserk*, the slow cosmic reveal of *Bloodborne*, the attrition of *Darkest Dungeon*, the wrongness of *Annihilation*, the political ensemble of *Final Fantasy Tactics*, the branching faction choices of *Sword of Convallaria*.

### Writing the violence

**Earned, never inserted.** A fight happens when somebody's choice has made it unavoidable, and never to raise the pulse of a slow chapter. The test is removal: if the scene can be cut and the chapter still does its work, it was decoration and it goes. Most chapters of this book have no violence in them at all, and that is what makes the ones that do land.

**When it happens, it gets the room.** No two-paragraph bursts. A fight is a scene with its own architecture: the moment it becomes inevitable, the first exchange, the thing that goes wrong, the reversal, the cost, and then the aftermath held long enough to hurt. If it is worth writing it is worth four pages.

**Written from inside one body.** Breath, grip, weight, what the floor is doing, what the ears are getting. Each viewpoint fights with its own competence and notices what only that person would notice: Kaelen reads failure points and hears things about to break; Lyra hears pitch, distance and how many; Vane counts, and judges weight, rope and angles. Nobody becomes good at something the story has not paid for. Lyra never picks up a sword.

**The physics stay consistent and specific.** Aether-Glass shot costs a sovereign a pellet and breaks the Cacophony; the glass arm drinks what it breaks and the tideline climbs for it; every loud thing draws more; singing above the Narrow is a weapon that reinforces the enemy; a hound has to be hit with something that rings. Readers should be able to draw the room afterward — where the ladder is, who stands between whom, what is burning.

**Damage persists.** Ribs stay cracked for chapters, voices stay torn for weeks, hands stay ruined. Nobody is repaired between scenes.

**Every fight moves the ledger.** Somebody is worse off at the end of it, and the reader knows their name.

### The dial for Book Two — kinetic, legible, lethal

Book One's violence was horror: things happening *to* people. From Book Two the register changes to combat the reader can follow move by move and wants to re-read, in the R. A. Salvatore tradition, without losing an ounce of dread.

**Geometry before damage.** The reader must always know where everyone is standing, what the footing is, which way the blow travelled and what it cost in distance. Footwork, reach, timing, the half-step back that buys a breath. A fight the reader cannot diagram is a fight that did not happen.

**Signature technique.** Every recurring combatant owns two or three named, repeatable moves that the reader learns and comes to anticipate — and the pleasure is watching them set up, fail, be countered, and be used in a new way three chapters later. Style expresses character: Kaelen breaks load paths because he is an engineer; Vane counts and judges angles because he is a captain; Vesper removes the sound of her own approach because she learned to be unheard in a place where being heard was being eaten.

**Fights are won by a decision.** Not by attrition. Somebody notices something, chooses, and pays for it. The turn should be visible in hindsight and invisible before it lands.

**Talk mid-fight.** Orders, taunts, apologies, bad jokes at the worst moment. Vane is funniest when he is dying; Aurelian argues law while cutting.

**Keep the Octave in the tactics.** Noise draws the Cacophony mid-battle, so winning loudly loses the map; glass drinks sound and climbs the tideline; a tone at a glassed limb's pitch locks it; gravity-tides make weight unreliable in the Peaks; Veridia resets every twenty-four seconds. The physics is the differentiator — generic swordplay is a failure state.

**Lethality is real.** Named people die, and more of them die in Book Two than in Book One. Battles can be lost; Fortis is lost. Maiming is permanent and carried. The succession rule still governs *who*, but it is not a shield: it is the requirement that every death lands and that somebody loved is left holding that rope.

## Part III — The Truth Beneath the Octave

### What everyone believes

The Choir-Church of the Pure Note teaches that celestial Architects pinned reality to eight stone Spires; that each Spire sings one law of the world; that the Iron Synod is the Spires' rightful steward; and that Aether-Glass is the Light's generous surplus, a gift to be harvested. Dissonance is sin. Every child in Aethelgard learns the catechism:

> *Q. What is holiness?*
> *A. One note, held without end.*
> *Q. What is sin?*
> *A. The second note.*

### What is true

**The Architects were refugees.** They came from a world the silence had already eaten, and they built Aethelgard as a shell of song to keep that silence out. A song that holds up a world must be sung without pause, and they could not sing forever. So they struck the First Compact.

**The Choristers.** Eight beings of living pitch agreed to sing the eight laws (Growth, Heat, Mass, Time, Light, Velocity, Memory, Decay) *until the flesh can sing for itself*. The Architects promised to teach the flesh. The stone of the world agreed to be the floor. The term of the Compact was ten thousand years. Then the Architects were gone. **[DECISION]** Recommended: they walked out into the silence to hold the door while the first note began, and they are out there still, being digested. When the Hush finally speaks in Book Three, it speaks in their voices.

**The Spires are not towers. They are throats.** Each Chorister was sealed in stone around its own voice. What the Church calls a Spire is ten thousand years of calcified song, and the thing inside it is still singing, still holding its law, still waiting for the relief it was promised.

**The term ran out in 412 SR.** Nobody came. The Choristers kept singing, out of love or habit or dread, because nobody told them they could stop. This is the wound at the heart of the trilogy: the gods held up the world for ten thousand years waiting for people to come and help them sing, and when people finally came, they came with drills.

**The First Fracture was not an accident.** In the very year the term expired, the Synod forced forty singers to hold the Light's exact pitch for three hundred days while a bore went into the Dawning Spire. On the last morning the Chorister of Light heard its own note sung back to it without a tremor, held *for* it for the first time in ten thousand years, and it asked: *Is it time?* The singers held. It believed them. It let go. Only one man in the Synod knew that was the point.

**The Light is ringing down.** A law does not vanish when its singer stops; it decays like a struck bell. In the Bleach of Sol-Luminary, nearest the broken Spire, the Light has already turned cannibal: light that takes instead of gives. Everywhere else it is fading. Summers are paler. Everyone over thirty remembers when red was redder. When the ring-down ends, the Bleach will be everywhere. This is the trilogy's clock.

**Aether-Glass is crystallized song**, a Chorister's voice bled out and set. Glass also remembers: it keeps whatever is pressed into it, which is how the Synod stores stolen identities. Every tanker of fuel is part god. Every Carillon soldier runs on part of a person.

**The Hush** is what lies outside the Octave, and it is not empty. It is a silence that is alive, patient, and hungry, and it does not kill what it takes. It digests, slowly, consciously, without end. Where a law thins, the Hush leans in. Its touch is the Decay beneath decay: things forgetting how to be.

**The Cacophony** is what song becomes when the Hush bites it: torn fragments of law with an appetite. They hunt voices because every voice is a small song, and they wear what they eat. They are the Hush's teeth and its lures. Once one has learned your voice, it can follow that voice anywhere in the world.

**The Gate of Decay.** The 852 Hz Chorister sings the rest between notes: the silence that belongs to the world, which is the only thing that keeps out the silence that belongs to nothing. When the Synod drilled it in 413, the gate cracked, and the Continental Rift is the wound. Through the Rift, and through every siphon-bore on the continent, the Hush is feeding something: the Null-Amalgam, a body forming below the Maw. Vesper's name for the bores is exact. They are umbilical cords.

**The lost curriculum.** The Architects' teaching did not vanish. It decayed into folk-craft: the Cantors of the Reach, whose tending notes for births and fevers and apple trees are fragments of the law-songs. The Church declared every second note a sin. For four centuries the Synod has suppressed the one body of knowledge that could let the gods rest without letting the world end.

**The Grand Unison.** Supreme Arbiter Vaelen designed the Rite of Pure Unison as an experiment: can a god be persuaded to stop? It could. The Planetary Engine beneath the Organum is the same trick at the scale of the world. All eight laws will be sung back to their Choristers at once, in perfect unison, until all eight believe relief has come and let go together. Vaelen believes the Hush is rest. The Church and the Consortium believe the Engine is for fuel. Revision Seven of the Vance Sequence carries one more line in the same small hand — *on failure of the hold, the Sequence is not to be halted; slippage is to be absorbed by the source* — which means the singer is not the steadying hand at all. She is the fuse.

## Part IV — The Rules of Dread

**The Narrow.** Mortals survive in the band between two predators. Too loud, and the Cacophony hears. Too still, and the Hush notices: in siphoned country, a person who falls truly silent for too long begins to unwrite, losing warmth first, then name, then outline. People speak in a murmur. Rooms keep hush-lamps, whose reed-whistles never let a house go silent. Night-hummers sit at crossings until dawn. Babies are dangerous in both directions: a screaming child calls the hounds, and a child who does not cry at all is a door left open. The rule is taught in a skipping rhyme: *too loud brings the hounds, too still brings the Hush.*

**The mimic rule.** Never answer a voice you cannot see. Answering tells it you are there, and whatever you say teaches it more of you. Mimics do not stay in one place, and they learn from every sound made near them — including the sounds used to beat them. A trick works on a mimic once.

**The Cacophony.** Static with appetite, seen at the edge of vision as a jitter in the air, like heat-haze with teeth. Their bodies are made of the sounds they have eaten; up close you can hear screaming inside them. **Hounds** hunt in packs and come to music. **Wearers** have eaten a person whole and can hold that person's voice and, at night, almost a shape. **Swarms** of mites and leeches strip a room of sound. **Titans** are what the Synod makes when it forces a Cacophony into a living body. All of them avoid Aether-Glass; the glassed can walk where others cannot. Glass shot, pellets of Aether-Glass fired from ordinary pistols, breaks them. Where hounds lie up they leave a *bed*: a patch of ground where no sound carries for a day.

**Quiets.** The Hush's own hunters, and the Rust Coast's particular terror. A quiet is not a thing that makes silence; it is a thing that *eats the air's ability to carry sound*, out to about forty feet, and then eats whatever is inside that radius. In the field it reads as a hole in the world: you watch a man's mouth work and hear nothing, you fire a pistol and feel the recoil but not the shot, and your own heartbeat arrives in your skull by bone rather than by ear. Glass shot is useless against them — the pellet cannot ring, and a quiet is not Cacophony. They are killed with iron, cold water, and patience, which is why the Free Captains flood a hold rather than fight one in it. Sailors say a quiet comes aboard through a vox-bottle left too long unstoppered and reckon any ship making harbour under no hand to be carrying one; Article the Tenth of the Free Captains exists for exactly that. The Harbour's people will not go into a hold with lanterns, because a quiet goes to the light.

**Glassing.** Raw Aether-Glass crystallizes flesh in four stages. *Ringing*: a tone only you can hear. *Frost*: a sheen under the skin. *Set*: a limb calcifies (Kaelen, when Book One opens). *Heartglass*: the glass reaches the heart and the person becomes a humming statue, alive and aware. The glassed begin to hear the Choristers, and eventually to feel them. The Glass Court is Stage Four. A glassed limb that strikes the Cacophony drinks what it breaks; the ring grows fuller, and the tideline climbs. Glass is also a party line: two pieces cut from the same Spire are one thing in two places, so Vaelen has heard everything said aloud near Kaelen's arm since 412. A tone struck at a glassed limb's own pitch — a hand-press at 741, an Aegis chord — locks it rigid and leaves it ringing flat for days.

**Unnaming.** The Catalogue's harvest. A person's name, memories, and voice are drawn into a glass core using the siphoned Memory Anchor. What remains is a **Hollow**, repeating the last things it did. The cores power soldiers and automata. When a Carillon Legionnaire is destroyed, its core releases the last thing it held. Every Synod soldier dies saying a name. Until then the core keeps everything the soldier sees and hears, and a factor with resonance lenses can listen it all back like a ledger.

**Law-rot.** Where an Anchor is bled, its law fails locally, each in its own way. Growth: overgrowth, *burl-sickness*, bread that rises until the oven splits, graves that heave. Heat: *the Cold Burn*, fire that freezes and frost that scalds. Mass: *gravity-tides*, a child who suddenly weighs a ton. Time: *the Stutter*, loops and lost afternoons and children with grey hair. Light: *the Bleach*. Velocity: *the Drift*, raindrops that hang for days and thrown stones that never stop accelerating. Memory: *the Forgetting*. Decay: first *the Unrotting*, in which the dead lie perfect for years, then the *Hush-rot*, sudden unmaking.

**The World Went Flat.** Since the Dissonant Cascade of 412, the whole Octave has sounded a quarter-tone wrong. Dogs howl at noon. Milk sours overnight. Hymns bring hounds. Everyone lives with a low, constant unease they can no longer name.

**Resonance Charge.** The world keeps trying to correct its own vibration, and the correction throws off power that people and machines can tune themselves to. Every spell, every Carillon engine, every one of Kaelen's blows runs on reality's attempt to heal itself. Using it slows the healing.

## Part VI — The Eight Realms and the Organum

### The Resonant Reach · Growth · 174 Hz

Orchard country in the west: iron-bark oaks, the green aqueduct-river called the Chlorophyll Flume, and the wind-reed orchards where Cantors trained until the reeds turned to sugar-glass and blew away in the Cascade. **Oakhaven** is the capital; **Greenroot Hamlet** lies downriver. Since 417 the Consortium has milked the Growth Anchor's root from the Oakhaven quarry, and the law is going soft. Saplings split cobbles overnight. Fingernails grow a quarter-inch between dusk and dawn. The oaks ring like bronze when the wind moves them. The burl-sick, people putting out roots, are kept in the Hollow behind the tannery until the Synod's Grafters come with their saws. The town lives inside **the Hedge**, three men deep of thorn and packed wool, planted to swallow its sounds. Governor Aldric Vance rules under the seal of the dead Queen Maelis. Births are hummed through clay jars; the dead are buried with bound jaws and a stone on the tongue so they cannot ring; nobody has rung a bell since the Synod took them all for bore-teeth.

### Dawning Heights · Light · 528 Hz

Alabaster peaks in the south. **Sol-Luminary**, the sky-city hung from the Spire on kilometer-long adamantine chains; **Sun-Peak Vigil**; the sun-altar on Mount Lumina. Since the Fracture the upper tiers are **the Bleach**, overexposed ruins where light eats. Survivors live in the **Umbral Tiers** on the city's undersides, where shadow is shelter. Windows are painted black. Mirrors are illegal. Children are taught to walk in the shade of the person ahead of them. The chains jerk like hooked fish. The broken Sky Choirs haunt the Bleach as the Wired. The old nobility, House Aubade among them, clings to the Church.

### The Blighted Caldera · Heat · 285 Hz

Basalt ridges and magma vents in the northeast. **Cinderhold**, a forge-spire of steam hammers; **Slag-Town**; the **Subterranean Foundry**. Heat has stopped obeying: the Cold Burn freezes fires and makes frost scald, and the *ember-blind* walk the streets with their inner heat gone out. The Deep Guilds mine the richest Aether-Glass seams on the continent, and they know something the surface does not: the deep glass whispers. Pyraxis, the Ash-Wing Sovereign, nests in the caldera's core.

### The Iron Peaks · Mass · 396 Hz

The Titan's Spine in the east. **Fortis**, the Iron Bastion and the Synod's arsenal; **Quarry-Keep Stonehold**; lodestone pits under magnetic mist. Gravity-tides make weight wander: a shield weighs eighty pounds, a sentry floats off the wall-walk. Peak-folk bury their dead standing, so the mountain can carry them. The **Lodestone Vaults** and the Terrasque of the Tremor Fault lie beneath. The Crystalline Golem walks here.

### The Shifting Dunes · Time · 417 Hz

The Erg of Singing Silica in the north. The **Sandglass Bazaar** under its acoustic glass dome; **Mirage Rest**; the Hourglass Erg of the Chronos Drake; the **Tomb of the Astrolabe**. On the western edge lies the **Glass Sea**, an inland sea fused to glass, and on its shore **Veridia**, an orchard-city caught in a twenty-four-second loop since the Cascade, where Growth and Time knotted together. The Stutter ages people unevenly, and the **hour-merchants** buy and sell hours of life. The Loom-Wardens keep the time here. They exiled the Tide-Weaver.

### The Tempest Archipelago · Velocity · 639 Hz

The western islands and the **Rust Coast**. **Port Cantabile**, built on singing coral with carillon rigging; **Driftwood Cay**; the Siren Reefs of Sirena; the **Sunken Carillon**; the Roaring Strait and Cape Dissonance. The Drift makes winds that never stop and tides that sing. Water sometimes forgets its surface tension and becomes blue jelly, and **tonal trenches** open in the sea like mouths. The Free Captains trade here in black glass and bottled voices. Inland, between the Reach and the Peaks, lies the **Valley of Velocity**, where the Synod first drilled a spire-root and the first law-collapse happened. The Iron Tramway once ran through it. Raindrops hang there for days.

### The Spectral Woods · Memory · 741 Hz

The continent's heart: silver weeping willows, phosphorescent pools, lilac mist. **Whisper-Grove**, the sunken marble capital; the **Cloister of the Lost Choir**; the **Mausoleum of Chords**; the Whisper Catacombs of the Phantom Weaver. The Memory Anchor was silenced in 416. Seventy-four thousand people forgot themselves overnight, and they still wander the plazas as Hollows, repeating the last things they did. The reflecting pools show strangers' memories. The Catalogue's harvest-houses still work at the edge of the mist.

### The Continental Rift · Decay · 852 Hz

The far east, where the continent ends in **the Maw of the Octave**. **Null-Watch**, the obsidian garrison of lead mortar and tungsten cable, fires white noise into the dark from its dampening cannons. **Redoubt Zero** guards the southern lip; the **Cataclysm Singularity** lies within. The dead do not rot here, until suddenly everything does. Every legionnaire wears lead. Something is growing in the dark below the wall.

### The Organum

The Synod's capital, east of the Spectral Woods, built over the place where the eight Anchor-roots meet underground: **the Keynote**. The city is an instrument. Its towers are pipes, its streets are ranks, and when the Synod speaks, the city plays. The Glass Court sits in the Choir-Hall. Under it, the Supreme Arbiter has been building the **Planetary Engine** for thirty years.

## Part VII — Factions

### The Iron Synod

*"One Note. One Law. One Hand."*
It presents as one body. It is five organs that need each other and hate each other, and those fault lines are the Chord's best weapon.

**The Glass Court.** Nine monarchs of the old realms, dead and revived with Aether-Glass hearts to lend the Synod legitimacy. They sign whatever is placed before them. They are aware. Queen Maelis of the Reach has been dead for a hundred and forty years; her signature on the Synod's decrees is famously steady. The Boy-King Orrin of the Archipelago died at nine and has been nine for two centuries. He still asks, at night, for his mother.

**The Choir-Church of the Pure Note.** High Patriarch Severin Ashe preaches unison as holiness and every second note as sin. The glassed are called Consecrated. The Church's Inquisition, the **Flambeaux**, burns dissonants: the off-pitch, the burl-sick, the heretical, the inconvenient. Ignatius Crane was its brightest flame. The Flambeaux carry glass-fire — white-gold flame fed on amber Aether-Glass, which the Cacophony will not cross — so the Church alone sings aloud in the open, and wherever it prepares a Rite it holds the Pure Note over the town day and night until the Rite begins.

**The Arbiterium.** Law as mathematics, administered by Justiciars. Supreme Arbiter Casimir Vaelen is its head and the true power in the Synod. The **Edict of Symmetry** binds an Arbiter to his heir, wound for wound. Only Vaelen knows what the Planetary Engine is for.

**The Consortium of Bores.** The drill-oligarchy that pays for everything: seven chairs, the first held by Chancellor-Magnate Ysolde Carrow, heir of the Carrow Bore and Kaelen's old mentor. It believes the Engine is for infinite fuel.

**The Office of the Catalogue.** Formerly the Silent Veil. Registrar-General Pellam Quist runs the harvest-houses where people are Unnamed. "Every name is a unit."

**The Carillon Legions.** Synthetic soldiers in bell-shaped helms, each running on a stolen identity; stand close and you can hear someone murmuring inside. Steam Golems and Aegis-pattern automata serve beside them. When they die, they say a name.

*Fault lines:* faith against profit, the Church's purity against the Consortium's appetite, and one secret beneath both. The most powerful man in the Synod intends to end the world, and no one else in the Synod knows.

### The Chord

*"We are not a unison."* The Synod calls them Dissonants. It begins as three fugitives on a smuggler's skyship and becomes a movement of the complicit and the broken. Its internal fracture is the trilogy's final choice: restore the Octave, break it, or replace it.

### The Tongueless

*The Quiet Faith.* A rural cult in siphoned country that worships the Hush as the Merciful Quiet. Its faithful cut out their own tongues to be "already quiet," and they leave children who sing in the woods as hush-gifts. Abbess Wenna of the Unrung Bell leads in hand-cant. Vaelen's agents feed them scripture. They are right that the Hush is quiet. They are wrong about everything else.

### The Free Captains of the Rust Coast

Smugglers and privateers under Oona Salt, Harbor-Queen of Port Cantabile. They sell black-market glass and **vox-bottles**, the last words of the dead, bought by the grieving, which bring hounds when opened. Mercenary, proud, and for sale to anyone. Vane owes Oona Salt more than a skyship is worth.

### The Null-Watch Penal Legion

General Theresa Ward's garrison of the condemned: penal legionnaires, outcast tuners, and the lead-robed silent monks called the Lead Brothers. They hold the wall and nothing else. Ward, mute since 413, commands by tapping iron-cant on her breastplate. They know more about the Hush than anyone alive, and they obey the Synod only when the Synod is not mad.

### The Wired

Survivors of the Rite of Pure Unison and of the copper-throated choirs the Church made afterward. A few can still speak, one note at a time. The rest have become **the Unison**: a single mind of copper throats singing one note forever in the Bleach, hunting the soprano who broke it.

### The Deep Guilds of Cinderhold

Miners, steam-artificers, and slag-folk, worked to death by the Consortium. They have heard the deep seams whisper. A strike, the **Gasket Strike**, is brewing, and whoever arms it will decide who controls the Caldera's forges.

### The Loom-Wardens

The chronomancers of the Sandglass: neutral keepers of time and brokers of hours. They exiled the Tide-Weaver for cutting threads. They have watched the First Fracture happen in every thread they have ever looked at, and they are tired of looking.

### The Cacophony and the Hush

Not factions. Appetites. The Cacophony has named horrors: the Wearer, the Siren Sirena, the Phantom Weaver. The Hush has a forming body, the **Null-Amalgam**; its field hunters, the **quiets**; and one runaway child, **Mote**.

### Game layer: Concordance

Each faction holds a standing with the Chord. Standings open routes, allies, and ending paths. **Restore** draws on the Church's penitents, Null-Watch, and the Sky Choir remnants. **Break** draws on the Free Captains, the Deep Guilds, and the Cantors. **Replace** draws on the Catalogue's defectors and the Arbiterium. The Tongueless and the Unison are the dark options: powerful, and costly in ways the player will not see until the end.

What the Book One game has added to canon (its places, people, customs, laws and books), what the owner has ruled on, and what is still only proposed, is recorded in **World Bible Vol VIII, *The Game Layer***. The manuscript wins over the game; the game follows the book (the owner's ruling of 28 Oct 2026: "Match any inconsistency to match the book").

## Part VIII — The Cast

### The Core Four

#### Kaelen Vance — The Dissonant Blade · 174 Hz

*Thirty-four. Vanguard. Left arm of cyan Light-glass, Stage Three and spreading.*

The Governor's son and Lyra's childhood shadow. He left Oakhaven at seventeen for the Conservatory of Bores, was Chief Resonant Engineer by twenty-seven, and calibrated the Carrow Bore at the Dawning Spire to a specification he never questioned, using a method that still bears his name: the Vance Sequence. On the last morning of the Rite he heard the Spire ask its question and tried to stop the bore, too late. He threw three apprentices into a drainage conduit and held a bulkhead against the glass flood, and his left arm set. Since then he has lived outside the Hedge in a charcoal-burner's hut, the only man in Oakhaven the hounds will not touch.

**Wound:** he did not fail to hold the Light. He is the reason it let go. **The lie he lives by:** if I carry all of it, no one else has to. **Wants** to break every bore he ever helped design. **Needs** to let other people carry weight beside him, and to stop mistaking self-destruction for penance. **Secrets:** the calibration; the question he heard; that the glass now lets him hear the Choristers, and that lately the Supreme Arbiter's argument sounds to him like mercy. **Voice:** few words, engineer's metaphors, dry self-lacerating wit. He hums the Root note Lyra taught him when he is in pain. *"I don't build things anymore. I just know how they break."*

**Arc:** outcast, then accomplice made visible, then the one man who can re-tune a god. The glass is both his power and his doom. The further it spreads, the more he can do, and the closer he comes to becoming Vaelen.

#### Lyra Ashgrove — Cantor of the Living Chord · 174 Hz

*Thirty-three. Support. The Cantor of Oakhaven, who sings its births, fevers, and burials.*

Trained in the wind-reed orchards before the reeds died. Her father Tobin, the orchard-cantor, was taken in 417 when he raised his voice above the Narrow to call a fevered child back from the Hush. The child lived. Something wears his voice now.

**Wound:** her father died because he sang too loud for love, so she has disciplined her compassion into triage and her voice into the Narrow. **The lie:** if I am careful enough, good enough, no one else has to be lost. **Wants** to keep everyone alive. **Needs** to sing loud when it matters, and to choose whom to lose. **Secret:** for forty-one nights she has gone to the Hedge at the dead hour and talked to the thing wearing her father, knowing what it is, unable to stop, and it has been learning her. **Voice:** warm, musical, with a stubborn iron line beneath the warmth. *"A martyr is just a musician who gave up before the coda."*

**Hidden weight:** her tending notes are fragments of the Architects' lost law-songs. She does not know that her village craft is the curriculum the gods were promised. The Synod is beginning to.

#### Vane — Corsair of the Rust Coast · 639 Hz

*Forty-one. Skirmisher. Captain of the skyship* Drift-Kestrel*, formerly of the* Wave-Cutter.

In 414 a tonal trench opened under the *Wave-Cutter*'s tow, and he held the boarding axe for ten heartbeats, looking at his first mate through the spray, before he cut the hawser. Forty-eight men lived. Vesper went down. The *Wave-Cutter* never sailed again and dissolved at her moorings four years later, so the sacrifice bought him nothing but time. He flies now. He will not go near the sea.

**Wound:** the ten heartbeats. He had time to choose, and he chose. **The lie:** nothing matters enough to die for, so nothing can hurt me. **Wants** a ship that can sail past the edge of the world. **Needs** to stay. **Secret:** the *Drift-Kestrel* was bought with Catalogue money, and for two years he has carried its crates of glass cores, stolen people, knowing exactly what they are. At night the crates say names in the hold. He drinks until he cannot hear them. He carries a vox-bottle labelled VESPER, almost certainly a fake, which he has never unstopped, and since spring he has been running inland from a rumor of a violet-eyed woman asking after him along the Rust Coast. **Voice:** naval swagger, gallows jokes, loudest laugh in the room when he is most afraid. *"Every sailor dies. The trick is to be somewhere else when it happens."*

#### Sable — Archivist of the Veil · 741 Hz

*Forty-one, by her own count in Book One (ch. 22), though she has forgotten most of what the years held. Mystic. Bearer of the Bound Ledger.*

The last Veil-sworn scribe, conscripted into the Catalogue. She recorded the Unnaming of Whisper-Grove, seventy-four thousand names, then stole the Bound Ledger and walked into the mist. The Ledger holds names only at a price: for every name she keeps safe, she gives up an equal weight of herself. She has three **identity fragments** left: her mother's hands braiding her hair, the smell of the Veil's scriptorium, and her own true name. "Sable" is only a Veil designation.

**Wound:** she believed recording was witnessing, and that witnessing was innocence. **The lie:** to remember is to atone. **Wants** to give every name back. **Needs** to act instead of record, and to become someone rather than the keeper of everyone else. **Secrets:** she did not merely sign the catalogue; she designed the harvest schedule ("I made it efficient. That is what I was good at."). And she has read Kaelen's calibration records. **Voice:** precise, speaks in entries. *"Entry forty-one thousand and five. Wren Halloway. Ten years old. Alto. Taken for parts."*

### The Twelve

**Ignatius Crane — Flambeau of the Synod · 285 Hz · Mage.** The Inquisitor who swung the censer through three hundred sunrises of the Rite and, when the light turned, ordered the choir burned. He still half-believes he was right. The Bleach took his left hand, which is now translucent white beneath a silk glove, and he has spent seven years praying over the one voice that shouted in the Foundry: Kaelen's. Book One's hunter, Book Two's reluctant convert. His fire is fed by faith, and as his faith dies his flame gutters; he has to learn whether a man who burns without believing is only a murderer. *Bonds:* Solenne, the woman he burned; Tull, whose brother his Flambeaux burned.

**The Aegis Sentinel — Forged Vanguard · 396 Hz · Tank.** A Catalogue automaton with acoustic logic gates around a glass core, directive DEFEND THE SINGERS, found guarding the empty risers of the Cloister of the Lost Choir. Its budding consciousness is a child waking up inside iron: Wren Halloway, a boy of ten, an alto whose voice had not broken, core serial WG-41,005, Unnamed in 416; Sable's shaking hand made his surname illegible in the Ledger (ruling, Book Two Ch14: the drafted Book One text — a boy, WG-41,005 — supersedes the earlier "girl, entry 41,112"). Until Book Two Ch14 it has no voice; after Sable returns his name, he hums through the iron in an alto. Its chimes are fragments of the Cloister's hymn. *Bonds:* Sable, who unnamed him; Lyra; Tull.

**The Tide-Weaver — Temporal Chronomancer · 417 Hz · Support.** Ilias Marren, exiled Loom-Warden, who remembers every discarded thread of time and pauses mid-sentence to listen to conversations he has already had. He is the Chord's traitor. In every thread where the Chord reaches the Organum intact, the Hush takes them into eternal digestion; in the one thread where someone sells them out at the right moment, some of them live. He is that someone. He narrates the Echo interludes. *"I have heard you say that four hundred and eleven times. It is still kind."*

**The Static Remnant — Living Kinetic Spark · 639 Hz · Assassin.** Jory Quill, a seventeen-year-old Synod courier caught in a Velocity flare, now a boy made of lightning who exists only while he moves. Stillness unmakes him. He carries a sealed Arbiterium dispatch he can no longer remember: the **Engine Writ**, the only copy outside Vaelen's hands of the order for the Grand Unison. Delivering it will tear the Synod apart. Stopping to deliver it will end him. *Bonds:* Vane, who sees the boy he failed in him.

**The Discordant Behemoth — Cacophony Titan · 396 Hz · Colossus.** Once Maddock Fenn, a shepherd of the Peaks. The Arbiterium forced a Cacophony into his body and collared it in iron; the collar broke at Fortis. He is four meters of grey flesh around a skull that never stops ringing, and he holds small things with trembling care. The ringing is his daughter Elsie, whose eaten voice is trapped in the thing inside him. When Mote eats his migraines, it eats a little more of her. He lets it, because it hurts less. He wants a quiet meadow.

**Justiciar Aurelian Vaelen — The Dread Arbiter · 528 Hz · Controller.** The Supreme Arbiter's son and Symmetry, bound to him wound for wound. A cold enforcer who believes law is the only mercy, sent to bring the Chord in at the end of Book One. The Engine Writ makes him defect. He is the one man who cannot kill his father, and the one man whose death would. His arc is learning that harmony needs the freedom to be out of tune.

**The Siphon Harvester — Steam-Bore Artificer · 285 Hz · Bruiser.** Garret Tull, in his fifties, Deep Guild bore-mechanic, a man of gaskets and thermal margins. He dreams of an engine that turns dissonance into clean steam, until the seams he mines tell him what Aether-Glass is made of. He builds something else instead: the Reliquary, a machine to give pressed voices back. His brother Aldo was burned by Flambeaux. His own old bore patents were licensed to the Consortium.

**The Voidling Leech — Mutated Symbiote · 852 Hz · Drainer.** "Mote." A cat-sized larval thing that followed Vesper out of the trench. It purrs, chirps, eats bitter frequencies (pain), and wants to be warm and good. It is a child of the Hush, and its first sound was a soprano's note falling into the dark on the morning of the Fracture. It is growing. Its sibling is forming below the Maw. In Book Three, the Hush calls it home.

**The Crystalline Golem — Living Resonant Quartz · 396 Hz · Tank.** It calls itself the Fourteenth Stratum; the Chord calls it Stratum. It was there when the stone agreed to be the floor, and it can confirm what no one wants confirmed: the Compact's term has run out, and it has been counting. *IT IS THE TEN THOUSAND AND SEVENTH YEAR.* It does not understand love. It understands promises, and it is trying to learn why people keep the ones that cost them. *"A MOUNTAIN WITHOUT CRACKS CANNOT ECHO."*

**Solenne Aubade — The Corrupted Seraph · 528 Hz · Striker.** Lead soprano of the Rite of Pure Unison, copper still in her throat, thrown into the dark by Ignatius's fire and returned with wings of black gold and a voice split between copper and void. She alone understood the Spire's question, and she sympathizes with the Supreme Arbiter more than she admits: she wants the gods to rest. She is the Chord's pro-silence voice and its likeliest defector. Some days she wants to tear her wings out. *Bonds:* Seraphina; Ignatius; Mote, which hums her note.

**Seraphina Aubade — The Dawnbringer · 528 Hz · Paladin.** Solenne's younger sister, chosen avatar of the Dawning Spire. At sixteen she stood in the gallery in white armor through three hundred sunrises and never once looked at the risers. When the light turned it parted around her, because the Sun-Blade in her hands is a splinter of the Light Chorister's own body, and light will not eat its own bone. She did not reach for her sister. Now she breaks her oaths to shelter heretics, and her steadiness is built on that silence.

**Vesper — The Void Harvester · 852 Hz · Assassin.** A Rust Coast foundling, named for the evening bell she was found beneath; Vane's first mate. Five years in the tonal trench. She came back in 419 with star-violet eyes, claws, and hooks of crystallized vacuum. She tells Vane he made the right call, which is true and the cruelest thing she could say. She knows what the bores are because she has seen them from the outside. She suspects the Hush let her go so that she would carry Mote out, and that it left a thread in her. When the Hush finally speaks, it may speak through her.

## Part X — Trilogy Architecture

**Book One: The Silenced Province** (~130k words). Oakhaven, the Root, the Greening, Whisper-Grove. The horror of the body, the household, and the town. It ends with Kaelen, Lyra, Vane, Sable, and the Aegis declared Dissonants, Kaelen's secret out, and the Dread Arbiter on their trail.

**Book Two: *The Great Discord*** (~150k words). The Chord gathers across the realms while the Engine Writ splits the Synod into open war. Full outline in Part X-B below; the horror of realms and laws, and the book where the violence becomes the point.

**FIVE-BOOK STRUCTURE — DECIDED (supersedes the trilogy plan; follows World Bible Vol III).** Book Two ends at Chapter Fourteen. Book Three: *The Hall of Carriage* (the heist book: the Shelf, the Organum, the Press). Book Four: *The Relief Shift* (seven Anchors asking; the Bleach falls; under the Organum; the one unavoidable death). Book Five: *The Resonant Accord* (the Ninth; the throat; the Accord; Lyra can still hear him). Material once slotted for "The Silence at the Core" (the Maw, Mote called home, the Planetary Engine, the Scales) is redistributed across Books Three to Five.

**Book Three dial — DECIDED: darker, more action, more dread, more death and destruction.** Set pieces are bigger and costlier; named characters die on the page; the cosmic threat (the thing below the Maw) is awake and escalating. Standing rules still hold: no death without an heir; damage persists; harm to children never rendered in detail; the Hush is never described directly, is always kind, and nobody it takes screams.

This is the mechanical seed of the Resonant Accord.

**Casimir Vaelen is glassed — DECIDED (author ruling).** A hundred and forty years old, eighty of them glass-laced, as the series bible has always said. World Bible Vol III's "he has never been glassed" is struck. His blind spot is rebuilt to fit: his glass is *laced*, never *set*. For eighty years he has kept it from spreading by never once letting it carry a note — it only receives. He listens through it to the Anchors and to every piece of Dawning glass on the continent, and he has never let it hold anything. He knows exactly what the glass costs and has arranged his whole life so as never to pay it. Stated once, late: *you have listened to them for eighty years, and you have never once sung back.* This is also why he wants a successor, and why Kaelen's set, singing, spreading glass makes him one.

**Reconciliations made in Book Three Part One.** Vol III's "eleven years" of baskets → six (the Rift opened 413). Fen: pressed 413 at twelve, now eighteen. Sable has one piece, not two. The Hall has "three floors and a basement" counting the street floor as the first. Vol IV's proposed Deputy Registrar Halloway renamed **Hester Vell** (Wren Halloway exists). **Open threads seeded:** the edge as the only room Vaelen cannot hear, and its price; the thing below the Maw now listening for Kaelen, so he cannot stay at the Watch; the writ's deadline and the column; Annis, possibly still in Tessa; Orrin's fear in tray four; the fourteen Hollows who may match cores in the hold; Mote straining toward the Maw in Vesper's coat.

**The Echo interludes.** Between Parts, the Tide-Weaver narrates a discarded thread, a timeline the book did not take. They let a single novel carry the game's branches, and they make the title literal.

**The canonical ending — DECIDED: the Resonant Accord.** The Octave is re-tuned, and the world is saved on the backs of eight exhausted gods and one volunteer. Kaelen takes the place of the Chorister he talked into letting go: he becomes the new voice of Light, sealed in the Dawning Spire, awake, singing 528 without end. It is not a clean victory and it is not meant to be — it is the Omelas bargain with a name and a face on it, and the trilogy's moral argument is what the survivors do with a world they now know is held up by somebody they loved. The one mercy in it: the Cantors' craft is the Architects' lost curriculum, and Lyra spends the rest of her life teaching children the law-songs, so that one day nobody has to be in there. That is the Compact finally being kept, slowly, by hand. **Closing image:** the Spire of Light sings again, pure and holy at five hundred and twenty-eight — and underneath it, for anyone who knows what to listen for, there is a second note, low, like wet earth, like a root drinking. The sin of the second note is the sign that he is still in there. *The Shattered Crown*, *The Sovereign Void* and *The Silence* become Echo interludes and game endings.

**Proposed fates, and the rule that governs them — DECIDED: no death without an heir.** A death is permitted only when three things are true. It devastates: the reader has loved them, watched them choose it, and stays for the aftermath. It is never random, never off-page, and never a beat the plot needed rather than the character earned. And its *function* passes to someone already on the page and already loved, so the ensemble never thins — every book must introduce more people worth loving than it spends.

Measured against that rule: **Vane's** death stands, now in Book Four under the five-book plan, because Vesper inherits the ship, Mags inherits the deck, and Pim inherits the reason. **The Behemoth's** stands, because Stratum and the Aegis hold the line he held and Mote carries Elsie's voice. **The Tide-Weaver's** stands, because Sable inherits the threads and narrates the Echoes after him. **Aurelian's** stands, because Seraphina inherits the law and Ignatius the penance. **Jory Quill's** does not, yet — nobody inherits speed, and the Static Remnant is the only one of his kind, so Book Two ends with him stopping, presumed spent, and Book Three brings him back changed: he stops being the messenger and becomes the message. **Kaelen's** fate is the Accord, which is worse than a death and has to be written as such.

Heirs are promoted deliberately, and Book One has already been seeding them: Surgeon-Grafter Rooke, Tobiah Marsh, Hetty Bram, Mags Tennant, Pennick, Lissa Merrow, and Pim Carder are all recurring from here, and any of them can carry a book.

## Part X-B — Book Two: *The Great Discord*

*Target ~150k, four Parts and two Echoes, roughly 28 chapters. Book One was a province dying quietly. Book Two is a continent going to war over a document, with the Chord carrying the only copy of the truth and a clock running in one man's chest.*

### What Book One already loaded into it

A course set west-south-west for the Rust Coast, which is the one place Vane swore never to go. Oona Salt's four hundred and eighty sovereigns, and the fact that she sold his location. A pale woman with violet eyes who came in off the water in the evening and paid in old coin. Four hundred and thirty-two people in a hold who have to go somewhere. A stolen press, and the discovery that giving a name back is not a mercy. Sable with two fragments, one unreadable entry, and a destination: the Hall of Carriage, third floor, at the Organum. Tobiah's charts and the only outside copy of Revision Seven. The grey folder in Kaelen's shirt. A tideline four and a half fingers from the shoulder and perhaps eight weeks of slack. The Dread Arbiter nine days behind with a column. An undelivered Arbiterium writ running east in a dead boy's fist. The Greening spreading down the Greenroot road, making refugees the Catalogue is already processing at relief stations. And Casimir Vaelen, who hears every word said aloud near Kaelen's arm.

**That last one is Book Two's engine of dread.** The Chord cannot plan out loud near the man they need most. They write; they send Kaelen out of earshot; they start lying to him for his own safety, and he knows they are doing it. Paranoia with a mechanism.

### The war

The **Engine Writ** is the detonator: the Arbiterium's order for the Grand Unison, undelivered for five years because the courier cannot stop running. Delivered, read aloud and witnessed, it proves to the Consortium and the Church that the Supreme Arbiter intends to end the world rather than fuel it. The Synod does not fall; it *splits* — Church against Arbiterium against Consortium, with the Catalogue selling to all three and the Legions obeying whoever holds their cores. The continent catches fire in nine weeks. The Chord spends the back half of the book trying to cross a war it started.

### Part One · The Rust Coast (Ch 1–7) — **Drafted**

Port Cantabile on singing coral; the Free Captains; Oona Salt holding a debt and a grudge. A broken ship, a cargo nobody will touch, and twenty-one farmers who have never seen the sea. The Part opens cold, on the Coast, eleven days after the *Kestrel* left Oakhaven — the Chord arrives into somebody else's disaster rather than bringing their own.

**1 · Salvage** *(Oona Salt)* — **Drafted.** The fourth of Ashfall, 419, first light. The *Bitter Nancy* makes the harbour under no hand, and Article the Ninth puts her and everything in her into the Harbour-Queen's pocket. There is a **quiet** in the hold. Four minutes of fighting with no sound in it at all — glass shot dead in the air, a boarding axe that lands like a dropped coat, Colm Teague taken two feet from the ladder — ended not by a weapon but by Oona breaking the seacock with a maul and letting the sea in. Eleven vox-bottles come up unstopped out of the bilge, which is where the quiet came from, and one of them is labelled **MERRET SALT**: her brother, lost off the Chime in '14, bottled and held back to be sold to her in a bottle by somebody she has been doing business with ever since. Then the mast-tower signals a patched airship crabbing in out of the east. *"Well,"* said Oona Salt. *"He's brought it to my harbour."*

**2 · Four Hundred and Eighty** *(Vane)* — **Drafted.** Eleven days to come four hundred miles in forty-mile hops. Oona names everything she can see from forty feet — the press, the Veil scribe, the nine-foot thing on the foredeck, the hold — and prices it: eleven thousand to the Catalogue, forty to the Consortium, the rope to anybody caught holding it. Vane has nothing to trade but the truth, so he trades it: Tobiah's fair copy of the Arbiterium cover-sheet, the Engine Writ, eleven weeks. She takes the job route instead and sends him to the **Sunken Carillon** for two drift-tanks out of the *Seraph's Gale* — three days, keep the third tank, debt cleared. She also admits, without apology, that she sold his location, and tells him what the buyer has been doing with it: seven bottle-dealers dead in nine weeks, each with two shallow cuts crossed over the belly, none of whom made a sound. *"She is not here for you, Captain. You flatter yourself. She is going up that staircase one door at a time."* Kaelen comes down the ladder with his collar up in warm weather, and his Dawning glass rings in sympathy with a Velocity bell forty feet under the sea, which it should not do. Lyra sings a stranger's funeral office on the Long Mole in a voice six weeks ruined, and every rigging-chime in Port Cantabile answers her for six seconds — and in a shuttered room high on the Salt Stair, something that had been waiting a week stops waiting.

**3 · The Salt Stair** *(Kaelen)* — **Drafted.** Six flights, forty-one steps each, four feet wide, wet, with a hundred-and-nine-year-old cable rail on the drop side. A chandler's errand for diving suits becomes an eight-man ambush for the cargo. The chapter is the Book Two dial applied cold: Kaelen's glass arm, the most dangerous object in any open room, is a wall he happens to be carrying in a corridor — so he stops swinging it and makes it *a door*. Mags holds the narrow with one arm and a belaying pin, because a door only has to be held by one person at a time. Sable's only weapon is Article the Twelfth, recited in the voice of a woman reading a will, and it buys one man's hesitation, which is the whole of what she can do and is not nothing. **Tobiah Marsh** steps in front of her and takes a gaff under the floating rib; he lives, badly, and is useless for two months. Kaelen reads a hundred and nine years of wet lead in a rust-bloomed stanchion, pulls eleven feet of rail out of the rock, and drops two men a hundred and sixty feet — and does not once think about who is standing on the open side of the flight behind him. **Coll Madder**, nineteen days a father, who had put down a coil of rope to take hold of a man with a knife, goes over at the broken rail. Ruth Madder hits Lyra once, hard, and then holds on to her for a long time, and both of those things are fair. Afterward the Harbour's people find the two who ran at the top of the sixth landing, a yard apart, tidy, on their backs, each opened with a long shallow X through leather and shirt and skin in a single draw, with no blood on the stones around them, which is not possible. Nobody on that landing heard a thing. The last bottle-dealer's shutters stand open on an empty room with the lamp still burning, the stock-shelves bare, and four sealed blue bottles set out in a row on the counter with their labels turned to face the door.

**4 · The Drowned Chime** *(Lyra)* — **Drafted.** Stasisday night to Memoryday. Four sealed bottles on the chandler's counter, all labelled VESPER in four different hands, priced four, four, six and nine; Vane sets a fifth beside them out of his inside pocket, bought on the second landing in '17 and never opened. Hesper explains the trade: bottles held over the trench lip with the stopper out; *some* are real. The Chord's rule of silence near Kaelen's arm made explicit: they plan in grease-pencil and send him out of the room, and he knows the sound of a room that stops talking. Coll Madder cannot go into the sea; he waits four days for earth on Driftwood Cay. The dive-lighter *Patience*, speaking-tubes, signal lines, the Aegis on the derrick. In the Drift a diver's breath does not rise; it hangs. Dawnday: one tank up. That night Lyra hears the Chime ringing her own funeral office back to her across the water — it learned her on the Mole. Driftday: the bells wake and sing each person what they want most; Lyra hears her own voice whole; Vane hears the hawser; **Pell** steps off the gunwale smiling and is gone; Teague is sung off the mast and Kaelen catches him by the pipe. Mags, deaf, is the only one in the boat who keeps working. Memoryday: wax in every ear, Lyra tied to the mast with her ear to the tube. At fifty-three minutes the voice in the tube is Kaelen's, telling her to cut the line — exactly what he would say. Mags reads the signal line in her palm: four pulls. *HAUL.*

**5 · Forty Feet** *(Kaelen)* — **Drafted.** The same minutes from the bottom. **Sirena** is visible only as the shape her song makes in the cloud of his hanging breath. She takes the 528 off his arm and sings it back, and the arm locks rigid through the bell-frame. She sings down the tube in Lyra's whole voice. He realises the tube runs both ways, refuses to speak a word she can steal, and uses the string. The Aegis hauls; the locked arm becomes a crowbar under the tenor's one loaded gudgeon; the pin shears, the tenor splits, and the glass drinks it — the deepest voice in the peal, the drowned town, the lost crews, Pell. The lock breaks. The tenor falls with his line fouled on it; above, Vane draws the knife, holds it on the line, and puts it away; Kaelen breaks the shackle himself. Two tanks home; the Chime never rings again. Oona takes one tank, gives one, clears the debt, and names her price: open **Merret Salt**'s bottle with the Cantor and the press before the *Kestrel* leaves. Something in the bottle is breathing.

**6 · The Top of the Stair** *(Sable)* — **Drafted.** Nullday. The Oakhaven people go to Driftwood Cay in Hesper's boats; Sable writes them down in a copper notebook, because a list costs nothing. Hetty and Bettany stay; Nell goes with the children, *for now*. Sable has stolen Ormond Dace's daybook: forty bottles a month for six years to the counting-house as "tuning stock", carried on to the **Hall of Carriage**. She climbs the Stair alone. The Flambeaux counting-house, catalogued body by body: two crossed cuts, half an inch deep, frost on the lips of the wounds, no blood anywhere because the hooks open a hole in the world and the blood goes into it. The Prior died mid-letter to Ignatius naming the *Kestrel*; Sable burns it — the first document she has ever destroyed. A foundling boy of eleven under the kitchen table, spared. **Vesper** in the records room with Dace tied at her feet. What the bottles are: the Hush keeps what it takes and digests it awake, for years; what leaks up is real voice. The Catalogue buys it pre-loosened as seed-stock for cores. Mote under the coat eases Sable's missing fragment, and Vesper stops it. Sable claims Dace for the Harbour under the Twelfth — he bottled Merret. Vesper's message: the Mole-head, the dead hour, both pistols; keep the glass one away, because she can hear the old man breathing at the other end of it.

**7 · The Mole-Head** *(Vane)* — **Drafted.** Vane binds *Second Opinion* into his ruined hand. Inside Vesper's quiet he cannot fight by ear. She forgives him first — *it was the right call* — and he shoots her for it. The duel: glass shot through empty air; his coat cut open with frost on the cloth; the barrel of *Second Opinion* sliced off; her claws open his face along the line of her own scar; his laugh dies three feet from his mouth. **Tailwind** — firing behind himself so the Drift throws him forward — takes them both into the seventh wave. Hooks across thigh and ribs, her nose broken, two of his fingers broken. He has the axe-haft across her throat; she does not resist; she tells him to count; at four he throws the axe into the harbour. She steps backward off the Mole-head. He goes in after her without counting at all. She holds him up in the black water humming the capstan shanty she hummed for five years so she would not scream. *"That's one."* Mags hauls them out one-handed. **Mote** comes out of her coat, eats his pain, and starts on the ten heartbeats; he begs to keep them; Vesper lets him. His VESPER bottle is real. Then Mote hums 528 — the Rite note — and Kaelen's arm answers from the *Kestrel*'s rail. *"It was born on it."* Five hundred miles east, Vaelen lifts his head.

### Part Two · The Bleach (Ch 8–14) — **Drafted**

Sol-Luminary: the Umbral Tiers, mirrors illegal, a city living on the undersides. The sisters. The Church's claim on Lyra comes due.

**Set pieces.** *The Chain Run* — a running fight down a kilometre of adamantine sky-chain where the light eats whatever it touches and cover is a moving shadow. *The Unison* — forty copper throats that have become one mind, hunting the soprano who broke it; Solenne against her own choir. **Seraphina** and the Sun-Blade; **Ignatius** arrives as a hunter and leaves as something else, and his fire gutters as his faith does, which is a tactical problem as well as a spiritual one.

**8 · Twice** *(Lyra)* — **Drafted.** Rootday the ninth. Ormond Dace is dropped from the Mole-head derrick under the Twelfth, by Oona's own hand; Lyra does not sing for him, and notices that she didn't. She measures the tideline: six and a half fingers, an inch and a half from the throat, and a vein of green in the cyan moving toward the neck. Vesper is aboard. Mote gets into the hold and eats the cores' pain until they stop saying their names; Vesper takes it out and they start again. Merret Salt's bottle is opened Vesper's way — on an open boat over deep water, lanterns dark, through the last wind-reed of the Reach. Merret is awake under Wendmere, calling soundings to stay himself; Sirena sang the drowned so the Hush kept off, and now that the bell is silent the Hush is coming up the tower, kind and painless. Oona cannot sing; Lyra sings the office twice; Merret hums along and the reed goes dead — released or taken, nobody can say. Oona puts the bottle in the sea: *that's paid*. Mote leans south-east and hums. On the slate: the soloist on the first riser is alive, and Kaelen can hear her in the glass. They leave on Hearthday with Vesper; Oona rings the Mole-bell once.

**9 · The Undersides** *(Vane)* — **Drafted.** Vane tells Vesper about the 1,342. The Dawning Heights; the cracked Spire; the city hung on nine chains with its top in the Bleach and its underside in the Umbral Tiers; a gull goes pale and comes apart. Vane flies the *Kestrel* up inside the city's moving shadow one-handed, and a yard of the envelope that touches the glare goes thin as silk. The Keel Docks: veiled harbourmaster, mirrors confiscated, children walking in the shade of the person ahead; she kneels to Kaelen as Consecrated and begs him to keep it covered. **Brenna Corliss**, Free Captains' agent, half her face bleached white: the Church has proclaimed the **Restoration of the Light** on Lumina on the first of Frostmonth, and it names Lyra as its Cantor; the Unison got louder three nights ago; the Tiers leave food on the line for a black angel. **Seraphina Aubade** comes down at sunset with the light on her armour and her shadow falling the wrong way. Mote sings her sister's note at her, and her face comes apart for a second. Lyra, in a torn whisper: *your sister is alive*. Seraphina: *I have not looked up since I was sixteen*. Lyra goes with her, and tells the Aegis to stay.

## Part XI — Book One Chapter Map: *The Silenced Province*

Sable stops a proctor dead by reading him his own surrendered name. Halder Wick is killed walking his loop through the middle of it. One proctor escapes with the ship's registry. Sable still refuses — until Kaelen says Annet Sowerby's name.

**16 · Manifest** *(Vane)* — **Drafted.** Sable climbs a hundred feet of mast-ladder with a broken nose and asks for the carriage notes. In the hold she reads the tags aloud from memory with the Ledger closed, then gives Vane the number he has refused to total: one thousand three hundred and forty-two, across eleven consignments, with a line in his file reading *reliable, does not ask questions*. She reads him three — Corwin Aye, Mirren Salk, Annet Sowerby — not as mercy but so that somebody carries them properly. Her price for returning a name is a fragment of herself, three left, against 1,342. She joins for arithmetic, not hope: the ship can reach the harvest-house on the Cloister road, where the presses are. Then she kneels at the third crate and tells a six-year-old that she is not lost, she is filed.

**17 · The Cloister of the Lost Choir** *(Lyra)* — **Drafted.** Eleven small cloaks on iron pegs, one shoe on the floor, and HYMN 9 still chalked on the board. The Aegis stands up out of its alcove — nine feet, DEFEND THE SINGERS stencilled on its chest — breaks Mags's collarbone, and opens its torso to put everyone on the floor with a bronze chord; Kaelen's arm catches it and locks rigid. Mags, who is deaf, walks through the chord and drags him clear, then puts herself between the machine and the risers. Lyra cannot sing, so she points at the hymn board and conducts: Sable, who stood in the doorway counting children while they sang it, has to sing Hymn 9. The Aegis kneels and chimes the alto line. Afterward Sable reads the serial on its core — WG-41,005, the boy whose surname her shaking hand made illegible, the one Annet would not let go of — goes white, and says nothing. It follows them out carrying the red cloak.

**18 · The Harvest-House** *(Sable)* — **Drafted.** She wrote the intake pamphlet: the benches, the water butt, the man who takes your coat at the door so you will not hesitate at the threshold. The Malting is running a relief station for Reach refugees walked out of the Greening, and registration is the first room. She walks in the front door to hold the clerks' hall while the others take a press, and Registrar-General Quist — who taught her to rule a page — offers her hot water, a chair, and her old desk, and asks whether she ever once wrote down an alternative. She makes him show her a pressing: ninety seconds, three questions, eight seconds of 741, and a man who looks relieved at six. Then she reverses it on Bettis Renn, and pays a fragment she will never be able to identify, and learns what the Office never learned, because the Office never put one back: the Hollow was awake the whole time. Bettis remembers all three years and begs to be put back.

**19 · The Tuning Room** *(Kaelen)* — **Drafted.** The Office keeps everything, including the Consortium's cross-files. Kaelen finds his own calibration returns, the procurement order with his initials against line ninety-four, and under them a grey Arbiterium folder dated eight months before the Fracture: *the object of Stage 9 is not extraction… an Anchor will yield without limit if it believes it has been relieved*, and a note that Engineer V. is not to be told, because his ear is more accurate when his conscience is not engaged. Beneath the floor the Chorister of Memory recites a list it can no longer finish. Then Vaelen speaks out of the glass — two pieces of one Spire are one thing in two places — and admits he has heard every word Kaelen has said alone since 412, including last night's nine names. He wants help letting them stop, warns that the papers will be read as a method rather than a confession, tells him the Cantor is now the most important person alive, and hands him the shelf where the eight pump-men's names are filed. *I keep a list too.*

**20 · Voice at the Door** *(Lyra)* — **Drafted.** Left at the Cloister because a raid needs people who can shout, Lyra takes the cloaks off the pegs and finds the name-tapes stitched in the collars — ten names, eleven pegs, the eleventh under the Aegis's hand. Then the Wearer comes to the porch and tells her, in Kaelen's exact voice, the whole confession: the calibration, line ninety-four, the girl bleeding into her collar on the ninth morning, the three seconds he spent being sure. She holds up a slate asking IS IT TRUE and learns the thing that makes it unbearable — *lark, I can't read.* It only knows what was said aloud in its hearing, which means every word of it came out of Kaelen's own mouth in an empty hut. It sings her own hymn back at her to draw the Aegis out, and she stands in the doorway with her arms across the frame and does not make a sound.

### Part Four — Coda

**21 · Ten Heartbeats** *(Vane)* — **Drafted.** Eleven hundred pounds overloaded, with a press, an archivist, a screaming woman and a nine-foot automaton coming up the mast-ladder. An Office cutter hunts them by sound — a listening horn on the bow, and a hold with four hundred and thirty-two voices in it. Vane flies the canopy at forty feet, takes a grapnel, lets the proctors board, and the Aegis answers the only way its stencil allows. Then he refuses to cut the line and puts the willow-crown through the cutter's bow instead. Mid-chase, Jory Quill crosses the foredeck — seventeen, made of lightning, an undelivered Arbiterium writ clamped in his fist — asks the way east and is gone, leaving two scorched footprints and the warning *if you stop*. A holed lift-tank puts them down in the deep woods by the Mausoleum of Chords.

**22 · What the Ledger Knows** *(Sable)* — **Drafted.** Two things left in the inventory, and the missing third does not feel like a gap; it feels like a shelf. Sable has taken the Consortium's Dawning file off the archive table — the returns, line ninety-four, eleven thousand and forty dead — and notices it is too one-sided to be complete, and tells Kaelen she is recording that she asked once. He says nothing about the grey folder in his shirt. She gives the papers to Lyra, who already knows, because the Wearer told her first; the only record that Kaelen tried to stop it is the Inquisitor's charge against him. Sable's reason is not kindness: eleven days in a doorway taught her that a woman who watches and writes it down is simply a woman who watches. Then she tells the Aegis what she knows and cannot read, and promises to find his name in the Organum, and it gives her the red cloak.

**23 · Coda** *(Lyra)* — **Drafted.** Kaelen tells her everything out loud, including the grey folder he was going to burn, and that his first feeling on reading it was relief, and that he decided he was not entitled to it. Including Vaelen in the glass — and that he has no argument against what the Choristers are owed, only against how it ends, and that the distance between those is not wide. Lyra's voice comes back at the worst possible moment, and the first thing she does with it is tell him she would have forgiven him on Rootday and cannot now, because he let her pick him up without saying how heavy he was. Then she baits the Wearer with her father's funeral office, sings eight bars of it in harmony with the thing that ate him, and sings the two-voice coda alone. It bursts, and everything it ever ate comes out at once, ending with Tobin Ashgrove getting louder as they took him, so the house would be further away.

**24 · Heartglass** *(Kaelen)* — **Drafted.** Four and a half fingers: past the collarbone, three inches from the throat, six weeks at the current rate. A herald walks nine miles alone to read the writ aloud, because the form requires it: all four declared Dissonant, the Church's claim on the Cantor stayed rather than refused, the Dread Arbiter nine days out on the Tramway — and grace offered once. The glass drawn, the Cantor amnestied, the Oakhaven survivors given transit, and the cores returned for *proper disposition*. Sable reads the terms correctly: it is not a sentence, it is a succession. Lyra answers the herald herself — *tell him the Cantor says we heard the question too* — and then tells Kaelen he does not get to pay, he gets to help, and takes the glass hand rather than the easy one. The ship needs a whole lift-tank, and there is one coast where you can get one. Kaelen adds the eight pump-men's names to the beam, and under them writes: *Not yet.*

**Echo I · The Thread Where She Answered** *(the Tide-Weaver)* — **Drafted.** Ilias Marren introduces himself from outside the story and shows the thread where Lyra went through the Hedge on the forty-second night — the margin is one night's sleep. What comes back up Cooper's Row in her boots is very good at being her: it has two years of her nights and all of her kindness, because kindness is a sound. It takes Kaelen's whole plan at her own table, holds the note for four hours and nineteen minutes, and the Root is relieved. The second Fracture; the Reach gone in eleven days; Vaelen weeping with relief in a locked room. His closing argument is the point of the whole interlude: the world is never saved by the people who are fresh. Then the seed — in every thread where he says nothing at the Organum gate, the Hush takes all of them; in one thread somebody sells them, and some of them live.

### Book One details on record

Added in v1.4 from the manuscript, because the game builds on them. Where the manuscript disagrees with itself, the open question is in World Bible Vol VIII, Part V (E).

- **Book One's calendar.** The chapter datelines count Day *n* as Leaffall *n* (Greenleaf is also used). Day 1, a Nullday, is the day Ruth Madder's daughter is born; Davin Hale dies at the Boar on Hearthday evening, Day 3; the Wearer lures Pim at the Listening Hour on Massday, Day 4; Tam dies on Massday; the Church takes Lyra on Dawnday, Day 6; the Rite falls on Hearthday, Day 11. Read against World Bible Vol VII (Book Two opens on 4 Ashfall, eleven days' flying after Book One's eighteen days), Day 1 falls on 6 Harvestmonth 419 and the Rite on 16 Harvestmonth. That mapping is the game's working assumption and is not yet ruled.
- **The order of 417.** The bells go "in the first year of the quarry": a peal of six, the town-hall clock-bell and the reed-row wind-bells, for bore-teeth. Tobin Ashgrove is taken in the spring at the Pellow farm, out past where the Hedge runs now, and his lyre is found by the stile. The Hedge is planted that summer ("two summers ago"): the Consortium pays, the town plants it, and the wool comes by the cartload.
- **Ada Pellow** is fifty-one, the mother of the child Tobin sang back from the Hush; she has left milk on Lyra's step ever since.
- **Tobin's seven wind-reed pipes** hang on pegs in Lyra's house, the last reeds in the Reach that had not turned.
- **Old Tam** is eighty-one, a basket-maker for sixty years and a night-hummer "since the first autumn of the Hedge". He sits at the Weavers' Cross and, lately, at the Flume crossing by the tannery culvert. His wife died of the pox the year the old king went into the glass, forty years ago, and called him *Tammy*. The book does not name her; the game calls her Essa.
- **The Tanner twins** are of a family named Tanner. They died of slippage with Marten and Old Coyle when a bore-head shattered, and Lyra sang them down side by side at the end of summer.
- **Hob Wendle** is a field-hand of forty in the Hollow, rooting through his feet since summer. Rooke takes three toes on Rootday under the mute-drops. On the *Kestrel* he roots into the foredeck; Bettany gives him the long drops while Lyra holds his hand, and he goes over the side with five feet of oak beam.
- **The talking crates** are twelve crates of thirty-six in the *Kestrel*'s hold, tagged WG-41,004 and up. Pim talks to Annet Sowerby through the side of one.
- **The eight pump-men** whose names Vaelen hands Kaelen: Edders, Ruhl, Caine, Ott, Sennet, Vey, Marrow and Hale B., Whisper-Grove stock consigned in the ninth month of 417. Twelve worked the pumps; four were saved.
- **Whisper-Grove.** Sable never leaves the city before the *Kestrel* comes: "the moment I get on a ship I am a woman who left". Her room is the Moot-house muniment store, and she first saw a proctor from its gallery. Halder Wick is fifty-eight, entry 12,207: "I do not know who he was expecting. There is no one left to ask." Corwin Aye's barrel in the Moot-house cellar "has not leaked in nineteen years". Form 9 asks three things: *Name. Age. Trade.* The Veil scriptorium had fourteen desks.
- **Ivo and Bettis Renn.** Ivo is entry 40,101, cooper, of Ashlane, forty-one; his core was requisitioned and is in the *Kestrel*'s third stack. Bettis is entry 40,102, his wife: thirty-eight when she was taken in 416 with his coat over her arm, forty-one when Sable reverses the press on her. She had carried baskets in the Malting yard for three years, was awake the whole time, and begs to be put back.
- **The Cloister's name-tapes:** Orry, Dray, Sowerby, Halloran, two Veskes, a Marchant, a Tipp, a ninth not given, and a tenth tape gone. Ten cloaks, eleven pegs; the eleventh is under the Aegis's hand.
- **The Aegis** is Wren Halloway, a boy of ten, alto, son of Jem and Isobel Halloway, core WG-41,005.
- **The Office cutter** is a forty-foot courier-cutter that "could not carry anything heavier than paper and proctors". Its listening horn hunts the *Kestrel* by her own cargo: "Your cargo is not silent."
- **The coda's ring of lamps.** Lyra walks past the last lamp at the second hour; the strike comes "when it's inside the ring of lamps", with Kaelen and the Aegis hitting the Wearer from both sides.

## Part XII — Game-Layer Hooks

**The Narrow meter.** Exploration stealth: keep the party's noise between the Cacophony threshold and the Hush threshold. Hush-lamps, murmur stances, and songs are tools, and loud songs are weapons that cost you the map.

**Names.** Every Carillon Legionnaire dies saying a name, and the name goes into the Ledger. Returning names at harvest-houses restores Hollows, costs Sable fragments, and shapes the endings.

**Mimic encounters.** Voices from off-screen. Answering triggers an ambush and teaches the mimic more about the party.

**Glassing.** Kaelen's meter trades survival for power. Heartglass forces a path.

**Echo memory.** The Tide-Weaver remembers the player's previous runs, and New Game+ dialogue changes accordingly.

**Concordance.** Faction standings open ending paths, as set out in Part VII.

**Campfires.** Every rest asks who holds the line tonight. Some answers are permanent.

**The canonical ending.** Resolved: the Resonant Accord (see Part X). The Shattered Crown, the Void, and the Silence remain Echoes and game endings.

**The proposed deaths.** Approve them, or name anyone to protect.
