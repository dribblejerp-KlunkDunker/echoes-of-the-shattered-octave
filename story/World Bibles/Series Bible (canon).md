## How to use this bible

This is the canon every chapter is written against. Nothing in the source material has been thrown away; most of it has been revealed to mean something worse than it first appeared. Where the source files disagreed, Part I records the ruling. Anything marked **[DECISION]** needs sign-off before the chapters that depend on it are drafted.

## Part II — The Dread Doctrine

These are the rules the prose obeys. They exist so that terror accumulates instead of spiking and fading.

**The world is already lost.** The trilogy does not ask whether Aethelgard ends. It asks what it costs to lose it slowly, and who pays. Hope is real, and it is expensive.

**Every miracle sends an invoice.** A healing, a rescue, a victory: each is paid for on the page, often chapters later, often by someone who was never consulted.

**Sound is the predator's sense.** Every scene knows how loud it is. Write the soundscape before the landscape. A laugh, a dropped pot, a crying child are this world's jump scares, and silence is worse.

**Never trust a voice you cannot see.** The Cacophony wear the voices they have eaten. From Chapter One onward, the reader should flinch whenever a beloved voice speaks from off the page.

**No one is clean.** Nearly every champion was complicit in the First Fracture or profited from what followed it. The Chord is not a band of heroes. It is a band of accomplices trying to become something else.

**Horror climbs a ladder.** Body, then household, then town, then realm, then law, then cosmos. Book One lives on the lower rungs. Book Three stands on the top one and looks down.

**The camera stays for the aftermath.** The burial, the smell of the ward afterward, the child who does not speak for a month. Battles end. Consequences do not.

**Humor is armor, not relief.** People joke because they are terrified. Vane laughs loudest when he is closest to breaking. A joke never resolves tension; it proves how much there is.

**The monsters are made of us.** Every horror is a human loss wearing a new shape: soldiers powered by stolen names, beasts wearing dead fathers' voices, fuel pressed out of gods and people.

**Kindness is the one thing the silence cannot digest.** Small mercies are the trilogy's only light, and the dark wants them most. Protect them on the page so that it hurts when they are taken.

**Dial setting — DECIDED: push harder.** From Chapter Eleven the camera does not cut away. Body horror is rendered rather than implied; the aftermath is shown at length; what a Cacophony does to a person is on the page, and so is what a law-collapse does to a town. Two limits hold, because they make the rest land harder rather than softening it: harm to children is present, felt and consequential, but never rendered in detail; and no cruelty is staged for spectacle — every horror in this book is something a person did for a reason, or something a person loved becoming unrecognizable.

Touchstones: the weight of _Berserk_ , the slow cosmic reveal of _Bloodborne_ , the attrition of _Darkest Dungeon_ , the wrongness of _Annihilation_ , the political ensemble of _Final Fantasy Tactics_ , the branching faction choices of _Sword of Convallaria_.

### Writing the violence

**Earned, never inserted.** A fight happens when somebody's choice has made it unavoidable, and never to raise the pulse of a slow chapter. The test is removal: if the scene can be cut and the chapter still does its work, it was decoration and it goes. Most chapters of this book have no violence in them at all, and that is what makes the ones that do land.

**When it happens, it gets the room.** No two-paragraph bursts. A fight is a scene with its own architecture: the moment it becomes inevitable, the first exchange, the thing that goes wrong, the reversal, the cost, and then the aftermath held long enough to hurt. If it is worth writing it is worth four pages.

**Written from inside one body.** Breath, grip, weight, what the floor is doing, what the ears are getting. Each viewpoint fights with its own competence and notices what only that person would notice: Kaelen reads failure points and hears things about to break; Lyra hears pitch, distance and how many; Vane counts, and judges weight, rope and angles. Nobody becomes good at something the story has not paid for. Lyra never picks up a sword.

**The physics stay consistent and specific.** Aether-Glass shot costs a sovereign a pellet and breaks the Cacophony; the glass arm drinks what it breaks and the tideline climbs for it; every loud thing draws more; singing above the Narrow is a weapon that reinforces the enemy; a hound has to be hit with something that rings. Readers should be able to draw the room afterward — where the ladder is, who stands between whom, what is burning.

**Damage persists.** Ribs stay cracked for chapters, voices stay torn for weeks, hands stay ruined. Nobody is repaired between scenes.

**Every fight moves the ledger.** Somebody is worse off at the end of it, and the reader knows their name.

### The dial for Book Two — kinetic, legible, lethal

Book One's violence was horror: things happening _to_ people. From Book Two the register changes to combat the reader can follow move by move and wants to re-read, in the R. A. Salvatore tradition, without losing an ounce of dread.

**Geometry before damage.** The reader must always know where everyone is standing, what the footing is, which way the blow travelled and what it cost in distance. Footwork, reach, timing, the half-step back that buys a breath. A fight the reader cannot diagram is a fight that did not happen.

**Signature technique.** Every recurring combatant owns two or three named, repeatable moves that the reader learns and comes to anticipate — and the pleasure is watching them set up, fail, be countered, and be used in a new way three chapters later. Style expresses character: Kaelen breaks load paths because he is an engineer; Vane counts and judges angles because he is a captain; Vesper removes the sound of her own approach because she learned to be unheard in a place where being heard was being eaten.

**Fights are won by a decision.** Not by attrition. Somebody notices something, chooses, and pays for it. The turn should be visible in hindsight and invisible before it lands.

**Talk mid-fight.** Orders, taunts, apologies, bad jokes at the worst moment. Vane is funniest when he is dying; Aurelian argues law while cutting.

**Keep the Octave in the tactics.** Noise draws the Cacophony mid-battle, so winning loudly loses the map; glass drinks sound and climbs the tideline; a tone at a glassed limb's pitch locks it; gravity-tides make weight unreliable in the Peaks; Veridia resets every twenty-four seconds. The physics is the differentiator — generic swordplay is a failure state.

**Lethality is real.** Named people die, and more of them die in Book Two than in Book One. Battles can be lost; Fortis is lost. Maiming is permanent and carried. The succession rule still governs _who_ , but it is not a shield: it is the requirement that every death lands and that somebody loved is left holding that rope.

## Part III — The Truth Beneath the Octave

### What everyone believes

The Choir-Church of the Pure Note teaches that celestial Architects pinned reality to eight stone Spires; that each Spire sings one law of the world; that the Iron Synod is the Spires' rightful steward; and that Aether-Glass is the Light's generous surplus, a gift to be harvested. Dissonance is sin. Every child in Aethelgard learns the catechism:

> _Q. What is holiness?_ _A. One note, held without end._ _Q. What is sin?_ _A. The second note._

### What is true

**The Architects were refugees.** They came from a world the silence had already eaten, and they built Aethelgard as a shell of song to keep that silence out. A song that holds up a world must be sung without pause, and they could not sing forever. So they struck the First Compact.

**The Choristers.** Eight beings of living pitch agreed to sing the eight laws (Growth, Heat, Mass, Time, Light, Velocity, Memory, Decay) _until the flesh can sing for itself_. The Architects promised to teach the flesh. The stone of the world agreed to be the floor. The term of the Compact was ten thousand years. Then the Architects were gone. **[DECISION]** Recommended: they walked out into the silence to hold the door while the first note began, and they are out there still, being digested. When the Hush finally speaks in Book Three, it speaks in their voices.

**The Spires are not towers. They are throats.** Each Chorister was sealed in stone around its own voice. What the Church calls a Spire is ten thousand years of calcified song, and the thing inside it is still singing, still holding its law, still waiting for the relief it was promised.

**The term ran out in 412 SR.** Nobody came. The Choristers kept singing, out of love or habit or dread, because nobody told them they could stop. This is the wound at the heart of the trilogy: the gods held up the world for ten thousand years waiting for people to come and help them sing, and when people finally came, they came with drills.

**The First Fracture was not an accident.** In the very year the term expired, the Synod forced forty singers to hold the Light's exact pitch for three hundred days while a bore went into the Dawning Spire. On the last morning the Chorister of Light heard its own note sung back to it without a tremor, held _for_ it for the first time in ten thousand years, and it asked: _Is it time?_ The singers held. It believed them. It let go. Only one man in the Synod knew that was the point.

**The Light is ringing down.** A law does not vanish when its singer stops; it decays like a struck bell. In the Bleach of Sol-Luminary, nearest the broken Spire, the Light has already turned cannibal: light that takes instead of gives. Everywhere else it is fading. Summers are paler. Everyone over thirty remembers when red was redder. When the ring-down ends, the Bleach will be everywhere. This is the trilogy's clock.

**Aether-Glass is crystallized song** , a Chorister's voice bled out and set. Glass also remembers: it keeps whatever is pressed into it, which is how the Synod stores stolen identities. Every tanker of fuel is part god. Every Carillon soldier runs on part of a person.

**The Hush** is what lies outside the Octave, and it is not empty. It is a silence that is alive, patient, and hungry, and it does not kill what it takes. It digests, slowly, consciously, without end. Where a law thins, the Hush leans in. Its touch is the Decay beneath decay: things forgetting how to be.

**The Cacophony** is what song becomes when the Hush bites it: torn fragments of law with an appetite. They hunt voices because every voice is a small song, and they wear what they eat. They are the Hush's teeth and its lures. Once one has learned your voice, it can follow that voice anywhere in the world.

**The Gate of Decay.** The 852 Hz Chorister sings the rest between notes: the silence that belongs to the world, which is the only thing that keeps out the silence that belongs to nothing. When the Synod drilled it in 413, the gate cracked, and the Continental Rift is the wound. Through the Rift, and through every siphon-bore on the continent, the Hush is feeding something: the Null-Amalgam, a body forming below the Maw. Vesper's name for the bores is exact. They are umbilical cords.

**The lost curriculum.** The Architects' teaching did not vanish. It decayed into folk-craft: the Cantors of the Reach, whose tending notes for births and fevers and apple trees are fragments of the law-songs. The Church declared every second note a sin. For four centuries the Synod has suppressed the one body of knowledge that could let the gods rest without letting the world end.

**The Grand Unison.** Supreme Arbiter Vaelen designed the Rite of Pure Unison as an experiment: can a god be persuaded to stop? It could. The Planetary Engine beneath the Organum is the same trick at the scale of the world. All eight laws will be sung back to their Choristers at once, in perfect unison, until all eight believe relief has come and let go together. Vaelen believes the Hush is rest. The Church and the Consortium believe the Engine is for fuel. Revision Seven of the Vance Sequence carries one more line in the same small hand — _on failure of the hold, the Sequence is not to be halted; slippage is to be absorbed by the source_ — which means the singer is not the steadying hand at all. She is the fuse.

## Part IV — The Rules of Dread

**The Narrow.** Mortals survive in the band between two predators. Too loud, and the Cacophony hears. Too still, and the Hush notices: in siphoned country, a person who falls truly silent for too long begins to unwrite, losing warmth first, then name, then outline. People speak in a murmur. Rooms keep hush-lamps, whose reed-whistles never let a house go silent. Night-hummers sit at crossings until dawn. Babies are dangerous in both directions: a screaming child calls the hounds, and a child who does not cry at all is a door left open. The rule is taught in a skipping rhyme: _too loud brings the hounds, too still brings the Hush._

**The mimic rule.** Never answer a voice you cannot see. Answering tells it you are there, and whatever you say teaches it more of you. Mimics do not stay in one place, and they learn from every sound made near them — including the sounds used to beat them. A trick works on a mimic once.

**The Cacophony.** Static with appetite, seen at the edge of vision as a jitter in the air, like heat-haze with teeth. Their bodies are made of the sounds they have eaten; up close you can hear screaming inside them. **Hounds** hunt in packs and come to music. **Wearers** have eaten a person whole and can hold that person's voice and, at night, almost a shape. **Swarms** of mites and leeches strip a room of sound. **Titans** are what the Synod makes when it forces a Cacophony into a living body. All of them avoid Aether-Glass; the glassed can walk where others cannot. Glass shot, pellets of Aether-Glass fired from ordinary pistols, breaks them. Where hounds lie up they leave a _bed_ : a patch of ground where no sound carries for a day.

**Quiets.** The Hush's own hunters, and the Rust Coast's particular terror. A quiet is not a thing that makes silence; it is a thing that _eats the air's ability to carry sound_ , out to about forty feet, and then eats whatever is inside that radius. In the field it reads as a hole in the world: you watch a man's mouth work and hear nothing, you fire a pistol and feel the recoil but not the shot, and your own heartbeat arrives in your skull by bone rather than by ear. Glass shot is useless against them — the pellet cannot ring, and a quiet is not Cacophony. They are killed with iron, cold water, and patience, which is why the Free Captains flood a hold rather than fight one in it. Sailors say a quiet comes aboard through a vox-bottle left too long unstoppered and reckon any ship making harbour under no hand to be carrying one; Article the Tenth of the Free Captains exists for exactly that. The Harbour's people will not go into a hold with lanterns, because a quiet goes to the light.

**Glassing.** Raw Aether-Glass crystallizes flesh in four stages. _Ringing_ : a tone only you can hear. _Frost_ : a sheen under the skin. _Set_ : a limb calcifies (Kaelen, when Book One opens). _Heartglass_ : the glass reaches the heart and the person becomes a humming statue, alive and aware. The glassed begin to hear the Choristers, and eventually to feel them. The Glass Court is Stage Four. A glassed limb that strikes the Cacophony drinks what it breaks; the ring grows fuller, and the tideline climbs. Glass is also a party line: two pieces cut from the same Spire are one thing in two places, so Vaelen has heard everything said aloud near Kaelen's arm since 412. A tone struck at a glassed limb's own pitch — a hand-press at 741, an Aegis chord — locks it rigid and leaves it ringing flat for days.

**Unnaming.** The Catalogue's harvest. A person's name, memories, and voice are drawn into a glass core using the siphoned Memory Anchor. What remains is a **Hollow** , repeating the last things it did. The cores power soldiers and automata. When a Carillon Legionnaire is destroyed, its core releases the last thing it held. Every Synod soldier dies saying a name. Until then the core keeps everything the soldier sees and hears, and a factor with resonance lenses can listen it all back like a ledger.

**Law-rot.** Where an Anchor is bled, its law fails locally, each in its own way. Growth: overgrowth, _burl-sickness_ , bread that rises until the oven splits, graves that heave. Heat: _the Cold Burn_ , fire that freezes and frost that scalds. Mass: _gravity-tides_ , a child who suddenly weighs a ton. Time: _the Stutter_ , loops and lost afternoons and children with grey hair. Light: _the Bleach_. Velocity: _the Drift_ , raindrops that hang for days and thrown stones that never stop accelerating. Memory: _the Forgetting_. Decay: first _the Unrotting_ , in which the dead lie perfect for years, then the _Hush-rot_ , sudden unmaking.

**The World Went Flat.** Since the Dissonant Cascade of 412, the whole Octave has sounded a quarter-tone wrong. Dogs howl at noon. Milk sours overnight. Hymns bring hounds. Everyone lives with a low, constant unease they can no longer name.

**Resonance Charge.** The world keeps trying to correct its own vibration, and the correction throws off power that people and machines can tune themselves to. Every spell, every Carillon engine, every one of Kaelen's blows runs on reality's attempt to heal itself. Using it slows the healing.

## Part VI — The Eight Realms and the Organum

### The Resonant Reach · Growth · 174 Hz

Orchard country in the west: iron-bark oaks, the green aqueduct-river called the Chlorophyll Flume, and the wind-reed orchards where Cantors trained until the reeds turned to sugar-glass and blew away in the Cascade. **Oakhaven** is the capital; **Greenroot Hamlet** lies downriver. Since 417 the Consortium has milked the Growth Anchor's root from the Oakhaven quarry, and the law is going soft. Saplings split cobbles overnight. Fingernails grow a quarter-inch between dusk and dawn. The oaks ring like bronze when the wind moves them. The burl-sick, people putting out roots, are kept in the Hollow behind the tannery until the Synod's Grafters come with their saws. The town lives inside **the Hedge** , three men deep of thorn and packed wool, planted to swallow its sounds. Governor Aldric Vance rules under the seal of the dead Queen Maelis. Births are hummed through clay jars; the dead are buried with bound jaws and a stone on the tongue so they cannot ring; nobody has rung a bell since the Synod took them all for bore-teeth.

### Dawning Heights · Light · 528 Hz

Alabaster peaks in the south. **Sol-Luminary** , the sky-city hung from the Spire on kilometer-long adamantine chains; **Sun-Peak Vigil** ; the sun-altar on Mount Lumina. Since the Fracture the upper tiers are **the Bleach** , overexposed ruins where light eats. Survivors live in the **Umbral Tiers** on the city's undersides, where shadow is shelter. Windows are painted black. Mirrors are illegal. Children are taught to walk in the shade of the person ahead of them. The chains jerk like hooked fish. The broken Sky Choirs haunt the Bleach as the Wired. The old nobility, House Aubade among them, clings to the Church.

### The Blighted Caldera · Heat · 285 Hz

Basalt ridges and magma vents in the northeast. **Cinderhold** , a forge-spire of steam hammers; **Slag-Town** ; the **Subterranean Foundry**. Heat has stopped obeying: the Cold Burn freezes fires and makes frost scald, and the _ember-blind_ walk the streets with their inner heat gone out. The Deep Guilds mine the richest Aether-Glass seams on the continent, and they know something the surface does not: the deep glass whispers. Pyraxis, the Ash-Wing Sovereign, nests in the caldera's core.

### The Iron Peaks · Mass · 396 Hz

The Titan's Spine in the east. **Fortis** , the Iron Bastion and the Synod's arsenal; **Quarry-Keep Stonehold** ; lodestone pits under magnetic mist. Gravity-tides make weight wander: a shield weighs eighty pounds, a sentry floats off the wall-walk. Peak-folk bury their dead standing, so the mountain can carry them. The **Lodestone Vaults** and the Terrasque of the Tremor Fault lie beneath. The Crystalline Golem walks here.

### The Shifting Dunes · Time · 417 Hz

The Erg of Singing Silica in the north. The **Sandglass Bazaar** under its acoustic glass dome; **Mirage Rest** ; the Hourglass Erg of the Chronos Drake; the **Tomb of the Astrolabe**. On the western edge lies the **Glass Sea** , an inland sea fused to glass, and on its shore **Veridia** , an orchard-city caught in a twenty-four-second loop since the Cascade, where Growth and Time knotted together. The Stutter ages people unevenly, and the **hour-merchants** buy and sell hours of life. The Loom-Wardens keep the time here. They exiled the Tide-Weaver.

### The Tempest Archipelago · Velocity · 639 Hz

The western islands and the **Rust Coast**. **Port Cantabile** , built on singing coral with carillon rigging; **Driftwood Cay** ; the Siren Reefs of Sirena; the **Sunken Carillon** ; the Roaring Strait and Cape Dissonance. The Drift makes winds that never stop and tides that sing. Water sometimes forgets its surface tension and becomes blue jelly, and **tonal trenches** open in the sea like mouths. The Free Captains trade here in black glass and bottled voices. Inland, between the Reach and the Peaks, lies the **Valley of Velocity** , where the Synod first drilled a spire-root and the first law-collapse happened. The Iron Tramway once ran through it. Raindrops hang there for days.

### The Spectral Woods · Memory · 741 Hz

The continent's heart: silver weeping willows, phosphorescent pools, lilac mist. **Whisper-Grove** , the sunken marble capital; the **Cloister of the Lost Choir** ; the **Mausoleum of Chords** ; the Whisper Catacombs of the Phantom Weaver. The Memory Anchor was silenced in 416. Seventy-four thousand people forgot themselves overnight, and they still wander the plazas as Hollows, repeating the last things they did. The reflecting pools show strangers' memories. The Catalogue's harvest-houses still work at the edge of the mist.

### The Continental Rift · Decay · 852 Hz

The far east, where the continent ends in **the Maw of the Octave**. **Null-Watch** , the obsidian garrison of lead mortar and tungsten cable, fires white noise into the dark from its dampening cannons. **Redoubt Zero** guards the southern lip; the **Cataclysm Singularity** lies within. The dead do not rot here, until suddenly everything does. Every legionnaire wears lead. Something is growing in the dark below the wall.

### The Organum

The Synod's capital, east of the Spectral Woods, built over the place where the eight Anchor-roots meet underground: **the Keynote**. The city is an instrument. Its towers are pipes, its streets are ranks, and when the Synod speaks, the city plays. The Glass Court sits in the Choir-Hall. Under it, the Supreme Arbiter has been building the **Planetary Engine** for thirty years.

## Part VII — Factions

### The Iron Synod

_" One Note. One Law. One Hand."_ It presents as one body. It is five organs that need each other and hate each other, and those fault lines are the Chord's best weapon.

**The Glass Court.** Nine monarchs of the old realms, dead and revived with Aether-Glass hearts to lend the Synod legitimacy. They sign whatever is placed before them. They are aware. Queen Maelis of the Reach has been dead for a hundred and forty years; her signature on the Synod's decrees is famously steady. The Boy-King Orrin of the Archipelago died at nine and has been nine for two centuries. He still asks, at night, for his mother.

**The Choir-Church of the Pure Note.** High Patriarch Severin Ashe preaches unison as holiness and every second note as sin. The glassed are called Consecrated. The Church's Inquisition, the **Flambeaux** , burns dissonants: the off-pitch, the burl-sick, the heretical, the inconvenient. Ignatius Crane was its brightest flame. The Flambeaux carry glass-fire — white-gold flame fed on amber Aether-Glass, which the Cacophony will not cross — so the Church alone sings aloud in the open, and wherever it prepares a Rite it holds the Pure Note over the town day and night until the Rite begins.

**The Arbiterium.** Law as mathematics, administered by Justiciars. Supreme Arbiter Casimir Vaelen is its head and the true power in the Synod. The **Edict of Symmetry** binds an Arbiter to his heir, wound for wound. Only Vaelen knows what the Planetary Engine is for.

**The Consortium of Bores.** The drill-oligarchy that pays for everything: seven chairs, the first held by Chancellor-Magnate Ysolde Carrow, heir of the Carrow Bore and Kaelen's old mentor. It believes the Engine is for infinite fuel.

**The Office of the Catalogue.** Formerly the Silent Veil. Registrar-General Pellam Quist runs the harvest-houses where people are Unnamed. "Every name is a unit."

**The Carillon Legions.** Synthetic soldiers in bell-shaped helms, each running on a stolen identity; stand close and you can hear someone murmuring inside. Steam Golems and Aegis-pattern automata serve beside them. When they die, they say a name.

_Fault lines:_ faith against profit, the Church's purity against the Consortium's appetite, and one secret beneath both. The most powerful man in the Synod intends to end the world, and no one else in the Synod knows.

### The Chord

_" We are not a unison."_ The Synod calls them Dissonants. It begins as three fugitives on a smuggler's skyship and becomes a movement of the complicit and the broken. Its internal fracture is the trilogy's final choice: restore the Octave, break it, or replace it.

### The Tongueless

_The Quiet Faith._ A rural cult in siphoned country that worships the Hush as the Merciful Quiet. Its faithful cut out their own tongues to be "already quiet," and they leave children who sing in the woods as hush-gifts. Abbess Wenna of the Unrung Bell leads in hand-cant. Vaelen's agents feed them scripture. They are right that the Hush is quiet. They are wrong about everything else.

### The Free Captains of the Rust Coast

Smugglers and privateers under Oona Salt, Harbor-Queen of Port Cantabile. They sell black-market glass and **vox-bottles** , the last words of the dead, bought by the grieving, which bring hounds when opened. Mercenary, proud, and for sale to anyone. Vane owes Oona Salt more than a skyship is worth.

### The Null-Watch Penal Legion

General Theresa Ward's garrison of the condemned: penal legionnaires, outcast tuners, and the lead-robed silent monks called the Lead Brothers. They hold the wall and nothing else. Ward, mute since 413, commands by tapping iron-cant on her breastplate. They know more about the Hush than anyone alive, and they obey the Synod only when the Synod is not mad.

### The Wired

Survivors of the Rite of Pure Unison and of the copper-throated choirs the Church made afterward. A few can still speak, one note at a time. The rest have become **the Unison** : a single mind of copper throats singing one note forever in the Bleach, hunting the soprano who broke it.

### The Deep Guilds of Cinderhold

Miners, steam-artificers, and slag-folk, worked to death by the Consortium. They have heard the deep seams whisper. A strike, the **Gasket Strike** , is brewing, and whoever arms it will decide who controls the Caldera's forges.

### The Loom-Wardens

The chronomancers of the Sandglass: neutral keepers of time and brokers of hours. They exiled the Tide-Weaver for cutting threads. They have watched the First Fracture happen in every thread they have ever looked at, and they are tired of looking.

### The Cacophony and the Hush

Not factions. Appetites. The Cacophony has named horrors: the Wearer, the Siren Sirena, the Phantom Weaver. The Hush has a forming body, the **Null-Amalgam** ; its field hunters, the **quiets** ; and one runaway child, **Mote**.

### Game layer: Concordance

Each faction holds a standing with the Chord. Standings open routes, allies, and ending paths. **Restore** draws on the Church's penitents, Null-Watch, and the Sky Choir remnants. **Break** draws on the Free Captains, the Deep Guilds, and the Cantors. **Replace** draws on the Catalogue's defectors and the Arbiterium. The Tongueless and the Unison are the dark options: powerful, and costly in ways the player will not see until the end.

## Part VIII — The Cast

### The Core Four

#### Kaelen Vance — The Dissonant Blade · 174 Hz

_Thirty-four. Vanguard. Left arm of cyan Light-glass, Stage Three and spreading._

The Governor's son and Lyra's childhood shadow. He left Oakhaven at seventeen for the Conservatory of Bores, was Chief Resonant Engineer by twenty-seven, and calibrated the Carrow Bore at the Dawning Spire to a specification he never questioned, using a method that still bears his name: the Vance Sequence. On the last morning of the Rite he heard the Spire ask its question and tried to stop the bore, too late. He threw three apprentices into a drainage conduit and held a bulkhead against the glass flood, and his left arm set. Since then he has lived outside the Hedge in a charcoal-burner's hut, the only man in Oakhaven the hounds will not touch.

**Wound:** he did not fail to hold the Light. He is the reason it let go. **The lie he lives by:** if I carry all of it, no one else has to. **Wants** to break every bore he ever helped design. **Needs** to let other people carry weight beside him, and to stop mistaking self-destruction for penance. **Secrets:** the calibration; the question he heard; that the glass now lets him hear the Choristers, and that lately the Supreme Arbiter's argument sounds to him like mercy. **Voice:** few words, engineer's metaphors, dry self-lacerating wit. He hums the Root note Lyra taught him when he is in pain. _" I don't build things anymore. I just know how they break."_

**Arc:** outcast, then accomplice made visible, then the one man who can re-tune a god. The glass is both his power and his doom. The further it spreads, the more he can do, and the closer he comes to becoming Vaelen.

#### Lyra Ashgrove — Cantor of the Living Chord · 174 Hz

_Thirty-three. Support. The Cantor of Oakhaven, who sings its births, fevers, and burials._

Trained in the wind-reed orchards before the reeds died. Her father Tobin, the orchard-cantor, was taken in 417 when he raised his voice above the Narrow to call a fevered child back from the Hush. The child lived. Something wears his voice now.

**Wound:** her father died because he sang too loud for love, so she has disciplined her compassion into triage and her voice into the Narrow. **The lie:** if I am careful enough, good enough, no one else has to be lost. **Wants** to keep everyone alive. **Needs** to sing loud when it matters, and to choose whom to lose. **Secret:** for forty-one nights she has gone to the Hedge at the dead hour and talked to the thing wearing her father, knowing what it is, unable to stop, and it has been learning her. **Voice:** warm, musical, with a stubborn iron line beneath the warmth. _" A martyr is just a musician who gave up before the coda."_

**Hidden weight:** her tending notes are fragments of the Architects' lost law-songs. She does not know that her village craft is the curriculum the gods were promised. The Synod is beginning to.

#### Vane — Corsair of the Rust Coast · 639 Hz

_Forty-one. Skirmisher. Captain of the skyship_ Drift-Kestrel*, formerly of the* Wave-Cutter.

In 414 a tonal trench opened under the _Wave-Cutter_ 's tow, and he held the boarding axe for ten heartbeats, looking at his first mate through the spray, before he cut the hawser. Forty-eight men lived. Vesper went down. The _Wave-Cutter_ never sailed again and dissolved at her moorings four years later, so the sacrifice bought him nothing but time. He flies now. He will not go near the sea.

**Wound:** the ten heartbeats. He had time to choose, and he chose. **The lie:** nothing matters enough to die for, so nothing can hurt me. **Wants** a ship that can sail past the edge of the world. **Needs** to stay. **Secret:** the _Drift-Kestrel_ was bought with Catalogue money, and for two years he has carried its crates of glass cores, stolen people, knowing exactly what they are. At night the crates say names in the hold. He drinks until he cannot hear them. He carries a vox-bottle labelled VESPER, almost certainly a fake, which he has never unstopped, and since spring he has been running inland from a rumor of a violet-eyed woman asking after him along the Rust Coast. **Voice:** naval swagger, gallows jokes, loudest laugh in the room when he is most afraid. _" Every sailor dies. The trick is to be somewhere else when it happens."_

#### Sable — Archivist of the Veil · 741 Hz

_Age unknown; she has forgotten. Mystic. Bearer of the Bound Ledger._

The last Veil-sworn scribe, conscripted into the Catalogue. She recorded the Unnaming of Whisper-Grove, seventy-four thousand names, then stole the Bound Ledger and walked into the mist. The Ledger holds names only at a price: for every name she keeps safe, she gives up an equal weight of herself. She has three **identity fragments** left: her mother's hands braiding her hair, the smell of the Veil's scriptorium, and her own true name. "Sable" is only a Veil designation.

**Wound:** she believed recording was witnessing, and that witnessing was innocence. **The lie:** to remember is to atone. **Wants** to give every name back. **Needs** to act instead of record, and to become someone rather than the keeper of everyone else. **Secrets:** she did not merely sign the catalogue; she designed the harvest schedule ("I made it efficient. That is what I was good at."). And she has read Kaelen's calibration records. **Voice:** precise, speaks in entries. _" Entry forty-one thousand, one hundred and twelve. Wren Halloway. Ten years old. Alto. Taken for parts."_

### The Twelve

**Ignatius Crane — Flambeau of the Synod · 285 Hz · Mage.** The Inquisitor who swung the censer through three hundred sunrises of the Rite and, when the light turned, ordered the choir burned. He still half-believes he was right. The Bleach took his left hand, which is now translucent white beneath a silk glove, and he has spent seven years praying over the one voice that shouted in the Foundry: Kaelen's. Book One's hunter, Book Two's reluctant convert. ## Part X — Trilogy Architecture

**Book One: The Silenced Province** (~130k words). Oakhaven, the Root, the Greening, Whisper-Grove. The horror of the body, the household, and the town. It ends with Kaelen, Lyra, Vane, Sable, and the Aegis declared Dissonants, Kaelen's secret out, and the Dread Arbiter on their trail.

**Book Two:_The Great Discord_** (~150k words). The Chord gathers across the realms while the Engine Writ splits the Synod into open war. Full outline in Part X-B below; the horror of realms and laws, and the book where the violence becomes the point.

**Book Three: The Silence at the Core** (~140k words). Null-Watch and the Polyphonic Eve; the Maw; Mote called home; the Glass Court; the Planetary Engine; the Scales; the Architects' voices in the Hush; the end. The horror of the cosmos.

**The Echo interludes.** Between Parts, the Tide-Weaver narrates a discarded thread, a timeline the book did not take. They let a single novel carry the game's branches, and they make the title literal.

**The canonical ending — DECIDED: the Resonant Accord.** The Octave is re-tuned, and the world is saved on the backs of eight exhausted gods and one volunteer. Kaelen takes the place of the Chorister he talked into letting go: he becomes the new voice of Light, sealed in the Dawning Spire, awake, singing 528 without end. It is not a clean victory and it is not meant to be — it is the Omelas bargain with a name and a face on it, and the trilogy's moral argument is what the survivors do with a world they now know is held up by somebody they loved. The one mercy in it: the Cantors' craft is the Architects' lost curriculum, and Lyra spends the rest of her life teaching children the law-songs, so that one day nobody has to be in there. That is the Compact finally being kept, slowly, by hand. **Closing image:** the Spire of Light sings again, pure and holy at five hundred and twenty-eight — and underneath it, for anyone who knows what to listen for, there is a second note, low, like wet earth, like a root drinking. The sin of the second note is the sign that he is still in there. _The Shattered Crown_ , _The Sovereign Void_ and _The Silence_ become Echo interludes and game endings.

**Proposed fates, and the rule that governs them — DECIDED: no death without an heir.** A death is permitted only when three things are true. It devastates: the reader has loved them, watched them choose it, and stays for the aftermath. It is never random, never off-page, and never a beat the plot needed rather than the character earned. And its _function_ passes to someone already on the page and already loved, so the ensemble never thins — every book must introduce more people worth loving than it spends.

Measured against that rule: **Vane's** death in Book Three stands, because Vesper inherits the ship, Mags inherits the deck, and Pim inherits the reason. **The Behemoth's** stands, because Stratum and the Aegis hold the line he held and Mote carries Elsie's voice. **The Tide-Weaver's** stands, because Sable inherits the threads and narrates the Echoes after him. **Aurelian's** stands, because Seraphina inherits the law and Ignatius the penance. **Jory Quill's** does not, yet — nobody inherits speed, and the Static Remnant is the only one of his kind, so Book Two ends with him stopping, presumed spent, and Book Three brings him back changed: he stops being the messenger and becomes the message. **Kaelen's** fate is the Accord, which is worse than a death and has to be written as such.

Heirs are promoted deliberately, and Book One has already been seeding them: Surgeon-Grafter Rooke, Tobiah Marsh, Hetty Bram, Mags Tennant, Pennick, Lissa Merrow, and Pim Carder are all recurring from here, and any of them can carry a book.

## Part X-B — Book Two: _The Great Discord_

_Target ~150k, four Parts and two Echoes, roughly 28 chapters. Book One was a province dying quietly. Book Two is a continent going to war over a document, with the Chord carrying the only copy of the truth and a clock running in one man's chest._

### What Book One already loaded into it

A course set west-south-west for the Rust Coast, which is the one place Vane swore never to go. Oona Salt's four hundred and eighty sovereigns, and the fact that she sold his location. A pale woman with violet eyes who came in off the water in the evening and paid in old coin. Four hundred and thirty-two people in a hold who have to go somewhere. A stolen press, and the discovery that giving a name back is not a mercy. Sable with two fragments, one unreadable entry, and a destination: the Hall of Carriage, third floor, at the Organum. Tobiah's charts and the only outside copy of Revision Seven. The grey folder in Kaelen's shirt. A tideline four and a half fingers from the shoulder and perhaps eight weeks of slack. The Dread Arbiter nine days behind with a column. An undelivered Arbiterium writ running east in a dead boy's fist. The Greening spreading down the Greenroot road, making refugees the Catalogue is already processing at relief stations. And Casimir Vaelen, who hears every word said aloud near Kaelen's arm.

**That last one is Book Two's engine of dread.** The Chord cannot plan out loud near the man they need most. They write; they send Kaelen out of earshot; they start lying to him for his own safety, and he knows they are doing it. Paranoia with a mechanism.

### The war

The **Engine Writ** is the detonator: the Arbiterium's order for the Grand Unison, undelivered for five years because the courier cannot stop running. Delivered, read aloud and witnessed, it proves to the Consortium and the Church that the Supreme Arbiter intends to end the world rather than fuel it. The Synod does not fall; it _splits_ — Church against Arbiterium against Consortium, with the Catalogue selling to all three and the Legions obeying whoever holds their cores. The continent catches fire in nine weeks. The Chord spends the back half of the book trying to cross a war it started.

### Part One · The Rust Coast (Ch 1–7) — **Ch 1–3 drafted**

Port Cantabile on singing coral; the Free Captains; Oona Salt holding a debt and a grudge. A broken ship, a cargo nobody will touch, and twenty-one farmers who have never seen the sea. The Part opens cold, on the Coast, eleven days after the _Kestrel_ left Oakhaven — the Chord arrives into somebody else's disaster rather than bringing their own.

**1 · Salvage** _(Oona Salt)_ — **Drafted.** The fourth of Ashfall, 419, first light. The _Bitter Nancy_ makes the harbour under no hand, and Article the Ninth puts her and everything in her into the Harbour-Queen's pocket. There is a **quiet** in the hold. Four minutes of fighting with no sound in it at all — glass shot dead in the air, a boarding axe that lands like a dropped coat, Colm Teague taken two feet from the ladder — ended not by a weapon but by Oona breaking the seacock with a maul and letting the sea in. Eleven vox-bottles come up unstopped out of the bilge, which is where the quiet came from, and one of them is labelled **MERRET SALT** : her brother, nine years gone, sold down this coast in a bottle by somebody she has been doing business with ever since. Then the mast-tower signals a patched airship crabbing in out of the east. _" Well,"_ said Oona Salt. _" He's brought it to my harbour."_

**2 · Four Hundred and Eighty** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** Eleven days to come four hundred miles in forty-mile hops. Oona names everything she can see from forty feet — the press, the Veil scribe, the nine-foot thing on the foredeck, the hold — and prices it: eleven thousand to the Catalogue, forty to the Consortium, the rope to anybody caught holding it. Vane has nothing to trade but the truth, so he trades it: Tobiah's fair copy of the Arbiterium cover-sheet, the Engine Writ, eleven weeks. She takes the job route instead and sends him to the **Sunken Carillon** for two drift-tanks out of the _Seraph's Gale_ — three days, keep the third tank, debt cleared. She also admits, without apology, that she sold his location, and tells him what the buyer has been doing with it: seven bottle-dealers dead in nine weeks, each with two shallow cuts crossed over the belly, none of whom made a sound. _" She is not here for you, Captain. You flatter yourself. She is going up that staircase one door at a time."_ Kaelen comes down the ladder with his collar up in warm weather, and his Dawning glass rings in sympathy with a Velocity bell forty feet under the sea, which it should not do. Lyra sings a stranger's funeral office on the Long Mole in a voice six weeks ruined, and every rigging-chime in Port Cantabile answers her for six seconds — and in a shuttered room high on the Salt Stair, something that had been waiting a week stops waiting.

**3 · The Salt Stair** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** Six flights, forty-one steps each, four feet wide, wet, with a hundred-and-nine-year-old cable rail on the drop side. A chandler's errand for diving suits becomes an eight-man ambush for the cargo. The chapter is the Book Two dial applied cold: Kaelen's glass arm, the most dangerous object in any open room, is a wall he happens to be carrying in a corridor — so he stops swinging it and makes it _a door_. Mags holds the narrow with one arm and a belaying pin, because a door only has to be held by one person at a time. Sable's only weapon is Article the Twelfth, recited in the voice of a woman reading a will, and it buys one man's hesitation, which is the whole of what she can do and is not nothing. **Tobiah Marsh** steps in front of her and takes a gaff under the floating rib; he lives, badly, and is useless for two months. Kaelen reads a hundred and nine years of wet lead in a rust-bloomed stanchion, pulls eleven feet of rail out of the rock, and drops two men a hundred and sixty feet — and does not once think about who is standing on the open side of the flight behind him. **Coll Madder** , nineteen days a father, who had put down a coil of rope to take hold of a man with a knife, goes over at the broken rail. Ruth Madder hits Lyra once, hard, and then holds on to her for a long time, and both of those things are fair. Afterward the Harbour's people find the two who ran at the top of the sixth landing, a yard apart, tidy, on their backs, each opened with a long shallow X through leather and shirt and skin in a single draw, with no blood on the stones around them, which is not possible. Nobody on that landing heard a thing. The last bottle-dealer's shutters stand open on an empty room with the lamp still burning, the stock-shelves bare, and four sealed blue bottles set out in a row on the counter with their labels turned to face the door.

**4–7 · to draft.** _The Drowned Chime_ — Sirena at the Sunken Carillon, three days, forty feet of green water, a flooded peal that rings with nothing in it, air-pipe and bellows crew and a boss that sings the divers off the rigging; the Chord's first fight where the medium itself is the enemy. Oakhaven's farmers put ashore on Driftwood Cay with money, which is a kindness nobody in the Part thinks is one. **Vesper** finishes her staircase and is introduced properly by body count; then the reunion, then the thing worse than hatred — forgiveness. Vane and Vesper's duel is not to the death and is the most violent scene in the Part. **Mote** comes out of her coat. Oona Salt's brother's bottle becomes the Harbour-Queen's price for everything that happens after.

### Part Two · The Bleach (Ch 8–14)

Sol-Luminary: the Umbral Tiers, mirrors illegal, a city living on the undersides. The sisters. The Church's claim on Lyra comes due.

**Set pieces.** _The Chain Run_ — a running fight down a kilometre of adamantine sky-chain where the light eats whatever it touches and cover is a moving shadow. _The Unison_ — forty copper throats that have become one mind, hunting the soprano who broke it; Solenne against her own choir. **Seraphina** and the Sun-Blade; **Ignatius** arrives as a hunter and leaves as something else, and his fire gutters as his faith does, which is a tactical problem as well as a spiritual one.

### Part Three · The Discord (Ch 15–22)

The war proper. Veridia's twenty-four-second loop and the **Tide-Weaver**. The Valley of Velocity, where **Jory Quill** finally stops. The Writ delivered, and the Synod tearing. Cinderhold's **Gasket Strike** , and **Garret Tull** , who builds the Reliquary. Fortis besieged, with the **Behemoth** and **Stratum** on the wall.

**Set pieces.** _Twenty-Four Seconds_ — a fight inside a time loop: the enemy forgets, the party does not, and the choreography is built across seven iterations. _The Tramway Raid_ — a moving tram through gravity-tides, where weight is unreliable and a dropped hammer can kill a man two carriages away. _The Breaking of Fortis_ — a siege the Chord loses.

### Part Four · The Gate (Ch 23–28)

Converging on the Organum. **Aurelian Vaelen** defects over the Writ and brings the Scales with him: a duel in which wounding him wounds his father, which neither man can use and both must. The Tide-Weaver sells them at the gate, because in every thread where he does not, all of them go into the Hush. Kaelen is taken. The book ends with the Chord broken in three places and the Engine eleven days from its first full test.

### Deaths, measured against the succession rule

**Jory Quill** stops in Part Three — the Writ delivered, read aloud, witnessed, and the boy who exists only while moving finally standing still. He returns changed in Book Three; nobody inherits speed. **Pennick** dies in the Tramway Raid, and Tobiah Marsh inherits the engine-room, which is why Book One spent a chapter making you like him. **One of the Oakhaven matriarchs** — Hetty or Bettany — dies between children and a door, and Nell Carder inherits the children. **Fortis falls** , which costs named defenders. Kaelen is maimed further; Lyra loses more of her voice permanently; Mags does not get her arm back. Nobody is repaired between Parts.

### Character clocks running through the book

Kaelen's tideline against eight weeks, Vaelen's cure offer standing open, and Tull's Reliquary as the only honest alternative. Lyra learning that a Cantor's craft can be made to break things, and what that does to a woman whose whole training is mending. Sable's two fragments against four hundred and thirty-two names and one she cannot read. Vane with a coast at his back and a first mate who has forgiven him. Solenne's sympathy for the Supreme Arbiter, which is not a secret for much longer.

### Canon added in Book Two, Part One

**Quiets** (Part IV): Hush hunters that eat the air's ability to carry sound; immune to glass shot; killed with iron and cold water; drawn to light; come aboard through unstoppered vox-bottles. **Port Cantabile:** built on singing coral whose note has wandered flat since the Fracture; carillon rigging at every shroud-head; the **Salt Stair** in six flights with the Lantern Yard halfway and a new Flambeau counting-house at the top, which pays for talk. **The Articles of the Free Captains** are live law and plot-bearing: the Ninth (a vessel under no hand belongs to the Harbour), the Tenth (unless what is aboard her is still hungry, at which point the Harbour disclaims all interest), the Twelfth (theft from the Harbour's own pocket carries the drop). **Merret Salt** , Oona's brother, nine years in a vox-bottle, recovered out of the _Bitter Nancy_ 's bilge — the Harbour-Queen now has a personal stake in the Catalogue's trade and a reason to need a press and a Cantor. **The Drowned Chime job:** two drift-tanks out of the _Seraph's Gale_ , sunk in the gale of '16; two crews lost; three days, because the tide is wrong before Dawnday and there is weather after Memoryday. **Kaelen's arm answers 639 Hz** — Dawning glass ringing to a Velocity bell, which by the party-line rule should be impossible and is the first crack in what the Chord thinks it understands about the Spires. **A Cantor's voice can be answered by tuned metal at scale:** forty sets of rigging-chimes gave Lyra's funeral note back across the water, which is the seed of what she learns to do with harbours, bells and tram-rails later. **Dead:** Colm Teague, nineteen, Harbour crew, taken by the quiet; **Coll Madder** , thirty-four, of Oakhaven, father of a nineteen-day-old daughter, killed on the Salt Stair by a rail Kaelen pulled. **Wounded:** Tobiah Marsh, gaff under the right floating rib, a month to his feet and two to usefulness — which means the _Kestrel_ dives the Chime without her second engineer.

## Part XI — Book One Chapter Map: _The Silenced Province_

**Prologue · The Cracked Bell** _(Solenne, 412 SR)_ — **Drafted.** The three hundredth sunrise of the Rite of Pure Unison. The Spire asks its question; the Light lets go; the engineer holds the bulkhead; Ignatius orders the fire; Seraphina does not reach; Solenne falls into a dark where something learns her note.

### Part One — The Narrow

**1 · The Tending Note** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** A birth hummed through a clay jar. Pim Carder's roots. The Listening Hour. Her father's voice at the Hedge, which she refuses in public and answers, alone, on the forty-first night.

**2 · Charcoal and Glass** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** A hound's bed twenty paces from his door; a Legionnaire murmuring about a blue jug. At the Greenhall, Factor Adela Vosk lays out the Vance Sequence, Revision 7 — his own method, improved in an Arbiterium hand — with a new Stage Nine: a four-hour unison hold at 174 Hz. _Source: Cantor, local (1). Collar: standard. Wire: standard._ Nine days to the casing. He takes the Consortium's purse and its pass. "You sold them." "I fed them." His father's hidden roots; his father's aired room.

**3 · Burl** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Lyra spends her whole voice before dawn to get Pim through inspection, and has nothing left for Orla Hale, rooted into her chair. Surgeon-Grafter Rooke chalks the sap-mark and admits the Inquisitor has asked for his list. Kaelen comes to her door for the first time in seven years; she mends his arm with breath alone. He tells her about Stage Nine, and about the girl whose collar bled on the ninth morning. They agree to break the bore together and lie to each other about the berths. At the door he hums his note, and she recognizes it from the Hedge. She does not go that night. It hums his note at her until dawn. It is practicing.

**4 · The Boar & Dulcimer** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** Twelve crates of replacement cores for the quarry garrison talk in their sleep while the paymaster stalls. Vane has run inland from a rumor of a violet-eyed woman asking after him on the Rust Coast. Kaelen buys three berths, held eight days, with Consortium scrip. Davin Hale sings his sap-marked mother's harvest song, and the hounds sing it back. Glass shot; a glass arm that drinks what it breaks; a laugh Vane keeps going on purpose to pull the hounds off the children. Afterward: a pair of boots, a rules-board wiped and rewritten, a child counting to ten inside a crate, and a vox-bottle labelled VESPER that he has never unstopped.

**5 · The Quarry** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** The quarry at night: a headframe over a green-lit shaft, and at the bottom a chamber grown out of the Root itself, veined with glass and breathing. Junior Engineer Tobiah Marsh asks Kaelen to sign his copy of the Vance Sequence, and shows him the Stage Nine kit — collar, wire, silver needle. Hollows work the pumps. Kaelen lays his glass hand on the Root; the humming stops, and something turns toward him. In Depot Three, a reverse-phase coil in his hand, he is found by the Legionnaire that walked him across town. He drinks its bell, breaks its core, and hears Tamsin Reed say her own name three times. At dawn the hound's bed is five paces from his door, and something in the clearing repeats her name in his voice.

**6 · The Listening Hour** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Davin's boots go to Orla, who heard her son's song and then heard it sung back by something with nobody inside it. Old Tam admits that he listens to his dead wife at the Flume crossing. The Wearer calls Pim out through the tannery culvert in Lyra's own voice — learned over forty-one nights — singing the falling fifth. Lyra sees it for the first time: her father's shape, her voice. A whistle-signal only she and Pim share works once, and the Wearer learns it while she watches. She sings the calling-song at full voice, and Pim runs to the torn voice, because it is the one that spent itself on his hands. Tam walks out into the meadow singing _The Miller's Daughter_ to pull the hounds away. At the dead hour the Hedge sings to her in four voices, and whistles the falling fifth exactly right.

**7 · Flambeau** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** The Church comes up the Greenroot road singing the Pure Note under glass-fire, and Kaelen's Light-glass arm sings with it. Ignatius Crane, his left hand bleached white, has spent seven years blaming the Fracture on the one voice that shouted — Kaelen's — and Kaelen tells him the truth: it let go because they held. The warrant orders the Pure Note held over Oakhaven day and night, the Hollow burned at the hour of the Rite, and Lyra taken into the Church's protection. The Factor offers to defer the fire if Kaelen signs as the hand at the wire, and mentions a Legionnaire lost in the slag-pit. Planning on a slate because the bells keep everything, Lyra chooses: he signs, the Carders board the _Kestrel_ first, the coil goes down in his toolbag, and at the casing he fakes the wire so she can sing against the bore from inside the Rite. He signs. His father weeps with relief. His arm keeps time with the censer.

### Part Two — The Root

**8 · Cargo** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** Nell and Pim are smuggled aboard in daylight, because nobody looks up. A paymaster's clerk counts the crates while the hold murmurs and does not break stride; delivery is set for Hearthday, after the Rite. Oona Salt's letter calls in the debt and tells Vane she sold his whereabouts to a pale woman with violet eyes who paid in old coin. At the dead hour he finds Pim with his ear to a crate, talking to a six-year-old called Annet Sowerby, and opens it at last: thirty-six lilac spheres, tagged WG-41,004 and up, numbered in the order they were taken. He tells Mags they are not delivering, throws his long gun over the side to make room for eighteen people, and burns the letter.

**9 · Beneath** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Four days of the Church's rubric: no speech, honey and raw egg, oiled throat, and two kind women who tell her she is fortunate. Rooke reads her palm and takes her instruction about Orla away with a thumb and a nod. Kaelen's one visit ends with _DON'T SHAKE_ on a slate. On Hearthday they dress her in white and walk her through Oakhaven, and the town hums the Root note under the Church's Pure Note, too quietly to punish. Down in the Root-vault she stops a Hollow's lips with a breath of the tending note; then she hears what is under the grinding — five notes, a Cantor's lullaby, held for ten thousand years by something so tired it keeps losing the tune. They set the collar case beside her, and Kaelen kneels with the needle, and his hands are shaking.

**10 · The Siphon Casing** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** He threads the wire through a fold of skin instead of the fold of her voice; it bleeds, which is what makes it convincing, and Lyra passes the Inquisitor's test by saying _I am fortunate_ flattened onto 174. During the Hold, Kaelen finally reads Revision Seven properly: slippage is to be absorbed by the source, and nobody brings a Church four hundred feet down a shaft to save a bore-head. The Rite is a second asking. He seats the coil, the lock throws, the bit snaps, and Lyra sings the falling fifth until the Root lets go of the question and starts its lullaby again from the beginning. Steam Golems; the glass arm drinks a boiler; the tideline climbs. Then Aldric's voice on the speaking-tube counts down the purge, the reservoir dumps forty thousand gallons into an open wound, and the Root drinks it, and screams, and cracks, and begins to grow.

**11 · The Greening** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Four hundred feet of service ladder with the shaft filling with leaves beneath them, and eight men left at the pumps. Oakhaven goes under in an hour: the oaks doubling and ringing, the churchyard heaving, the baker's wife saying _I punched it down_ , a carter rooting to the cobbles. Lyra sings a corridor open at full voice and walks sixty people to the square. Rooke could not cross Orla's floor; Lyra can, and sings the harvest home — all the lines, even the fourth — while the tree closes over her. At the sealed North Gate the Hedge speaks to the town in the voices of its dead, offering a way through, and Ada Pellow answers Tobin Ashgrove's voice and climbs into the thorn. Then the Chapel falls, the Pure Note stops for the first time in five days, and the _Kestrel_ comes down out of the green sky.

**12 · Grab the Line** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** The mooring mast is taken by a tree; Mags cuts them free. Vane turns toward the town instead of away. Children first, four to a line; a hound comes down the rope and a man's hands stay on the knot after the rest of him is gone. The ship settles as the oaks rise. On the last load, Aldric Vance is at the bottom, because he has been loading his town onto that rope for twenty minutes. The line snags in a crown, the winch drum starts out of the deck-beams, and Vane counts to ten. Aldric looks up and waves them on. "Do it," says Kaelen. The _Kestrel_ leaps a hundred and fifty feet, and three hundred and eighty people stand in the mud and wave the ship away, and six feet of cut rope hangs from the drum for four days.

**13 · Ash in the Hold** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** Twenty-one survivors and no room. Lyra has no voice at all and writes _YOU SAID DO IT_ on a slate. Through the glass Kaelen can still hear the Root singing with a hole in it — still holding — and files the thought. Vane gives his confession — thirteen hundred crates in two years — and sets the course for Whisper-Grove and the scribe. Then Hob Wendle's feet root into the foredeck, the growth goes into the beams, and Bettany hums the tending note for a Cantor who cannot; they put him over the side with five feet of oak beam. Tobiah Marsh has seen the coil go into the port, and hands over his charts and the only copy of Revision Seven with the margin on it. At dawn, from four miles back, Lyra's whole voice sings the calling-song after the ship. That night Kaelen starts a list of names on a deck-beam, and understands that everyone who ever kept one before him was the Catalogue.

### Part Three — The Forgetting

**14 · Entry** _(Sable)_ — **Drafted.** Whisper-Grove at dusk: a plaza under a foot of lit water, two hundred Hollows walking their loops, and Halder Wick setting a table for four. Sable reads twenty names a night — ten years and two hundred and twenty days to finish, and then you start again. The Bound Ledger floats at her elbow and pays for what it keeps, weight for weight; her three remaining fragments are an inventory she recites nightly. The Office is three days behind her, and it has pressed a man a second time to find out whether she spoke to him. Then a skyship crosses the plaza with registry OK-114 under two coats of paint — a mark she has initialled eleven times — and a hold that murmurs.

**15 · Statistical Anomaly** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** A dead city that never fell down: a lamplighter lighting dry lamps, a cat that doesn't care, and Lyra's slate asking whether the Hollows are hungry. Sable recites Kaelen's badge and his two-percent survival, and refuses him: she is a list, not a cure. Then the Office's proctors come out of the mist — grey oilcloth, silver mesh, felted rods, and hand-presses tuned to 741 that lock Kaelen's arm rigid when struck. The plaza fight: Lyra kills a man with a marble finial and is sick in the water, finds the second by his breathing and puts a tuning key through his knee, and whistles the falling fifth to aim Kaelen at the third. Sable stops a proctor dead by reading him his own surrendered name. Halder Wick is killed walking his loop through the middle of it. One proctor escapes with the ship's registry. Sable still refuses — until Kaelen says Annet Sowerby's name.

**16 · Manifest** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** Sable climbs a hundred feet of mast-ladder with a broken nose and asks for the carriage notes. In the hold she reads the tags aloud from memory with the Ledger closed, then gives Vane the number he has refused to total: one thousand three hundred and forty-two, across eleven consignments, with a line in his file reading _reliable, does not ask questions_. She reads him three — Corwin Aye, Mirren Salk, Annet Sowerby — not as mercy but so that somebody carries them properly. Her price for returning a name is a fragment of herself, three left, against 1,342. She joins for arithmetic, not hope: the ship can reach the harvest-house on the Cloister road, where the presses are. Then she kneels at the third crate and tells a six-year-old that she is not lost, she is filed.

**17 · The Cloister of the Lost Choir** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Eleven small cloaks on iron pegs, one shoe on the floor, and HYMN 9 still chalked on the board. The Aegis stands up out of its alcove — nine feet, DEFEND THE SINGERS stencilled on its chest — breaks Mags's collarbone, and opens its torso to put everyone on the floor with a bronze chord; Kaelen's arm catches it and locks rigid. Mags, who is deaf, walks through the chord and drags him clear, then puts herself between the machine and the risers. Lyra cannot sing, so she points at the hymn board and conducts: Sable, who stood in the doorway counting children while they sang it, has to sing Hymn 9. The Aegis kneels and chimes the alto line. Afterward Sable reads the serial on its core — WG-41,005, the boy whose surname her shaking hand made illegible, the one Annet would not let go of — goes white, and says nothing. It follows them out carrying the red cloak.

**18 · The Harvest-House** _(Sable)_ — **Drafted.** She wrote the intake pamphlet: the benches, the water butt, the man who takes your coat at the door so you will not hesitate at the threshold. The Malting is running a relief station for Reach refugees walked out of the Greening, and registration is the first room. She walks in the front door to hold the clerks' hall while the others take a press, and Registrar-General Quist — who taught her to rule a page — offers her hot water, a chair, and her old desk, and asks whether she ever once wrote down an alternative. She makes him show her a pressing: ninety seconds, three questions, eight seconds of 741, and a man who looks relieved at six. Then she reverses it on Bettis Renn, and pays a fragment she will never be able to identify, and learns what the Office never learned, because the Office never put one back: the Hollow was awake the whole time. Bettis remembers all three years and begs to be put back.

**19 · The Tuning Room** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** The Office keeps everything, including the Consortium's cross-files. Kaelen finds his own calibration returns, the procurement order with his initials against line ninety-four, and under them a grey Arbiterium folder dated eight months before the Fracture: _the object of Stage 9 is not extraction… an Anchor will yield without limit if it believes it has been relieved_ , and a note that Engineer V. is not to be told, because his ear is more accurate when his conscience is not engaged. Beneath the floor the Chorister of Memory recites a list it can no longer finish. Then Vaelen speaks out of the glass — two pieces of one Spire are one thing in two places — and admits he has heard every word Kaelen has said alone since 412, including last night's nine names. He wants help letting them stop, warns that the papers will be read as a method rather than a confession, tells him the Cantor is now the most important person alive, and hands him the shelf where the eight pump-men's names are filed. _I keep a list too._

**20 · Voice at the Door** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Left at the Cloister because a raid needs people who can shout, Lyra takes the cloaks off the pegs and finds the name-tapes stitched in the collars — ten names, eleven pegs, the eleventh under the Aegis's hand. Then the Wearer comes to the porch and tells her, in Kaelen's exact voice, the whole confession: the calibration, line ninety-four, the girl bleeding into her collar on the ninth morning, the three seconds he spent being sure. She holds up a slate asking IS IT TRUE and learns the thing that makes it unbearable — _lark, I can't read._ It only knows what was said aloud in its hearing, which means every word of it came out of Kaelen's own mouth in an empty hut. It sings her own hymn back at her to draw the Aegis out, and she stands in the doorway with her arms across the frame and does not make a sound.

### Part Four — Coda

**21 · Ten Heartbeats** _(Vane)_ — **Drafted.** Eleven hundred pounds overloaded, with a press, an archivist, a screaming woman and a nine-foot automaton coming up the mast-ladder. An Office cutter hunts them by sound — a listening horn on the bow, and a hold with four hundred and thirty-two voices in it. Vane flies the canopy at forty feet, takes a grapnel, lets the proctors board, and the Aegis answers the only way its stencil allows. Then he refuses to cut the line and puts the willow-crown through the cutter's bow instead. Mid-chase, Jory Quill crosses the foredeck — seventeen, made of lightning, an undelivered Arbiterium writ clamped in his fist — asks the way east and is gone, leaving two scorched footprints and the warning _if you stop_. A holed lift-tank puts them down in the deep woods by the Mausoleum of Chords.

**22 · What the Ledger Knows** _(Sable)_ — **Drafted.** Two things left in the inventory, and the missing third does not feel like a gap; it feels like a shelf. Sable has taken the Consortium's Dawning file off the archive table — the returns, line ninety-four, eleven thousand and forty dead — and notices it is too one-sided to be complete, and tells Kaelen she is recording that she asked once. He says nothing about the grey folder in his shirt. She gives the papers to Lyra, who already knows, because the Wearer told her first; the only record that Kaelen tried to stop it is the Inquisitor's charge against him. Sable's reason is not kindness: eleven days in a doorway taught her that a woman who watches and writes it down is simply a woman who watches. Then she tells the Aegis what she knows and cannot read, and promises to find his name in the Organum, and it gives her the red cloak.

**23 · Coda** _(Lyra)_ — **Drafted.** Kaelen tells her everything out loud, including the grey folder he was going to burn, and that his first feeling on reading it was relief, and that he decided he was not entitled to it. Including Vaelen in the glass — and that he has no argument against what the Choristers are owed, only against how it ends, and that the distance between those is not wide. Lyra's voice comes back at the worst possible moment, and the first thing she does with it is tell him she would have forgiven him on Rootday and cannot now, because he let her pick him up without saying how heavy he was. Then she baits the Wearer with her father's funeral office, sings eight bars of it in harmony with the thing that ate him, and sings the two-voice coda alone. It bursts, and everything it ever ate comes out at once, ending with Tobin Ashgrove getting louder as they took him, so the house would be further away.

**24 · Heartglass** _(Kaelen)_ — **Drafted.** Four and a half fingers: past the collarbone, three inches from the throat, six weeks at the current rate. A herald walks nine miles alone to read the writ aloud, because the form requires it: all four declared Dissonant, the Church's claim on the Cantor stayed rather than refused, the Dread Arbiter nine days out on the Tramway — and grace offered once. The glass drawn, the Cantor amnestied, the Oakhaven survivors given transit, and the cores returned for _proper disposition_. Sable reads the terms correctly: it is not a sentence, it is a succession. Lyra answers the herald herself — _tell him the Cantor says we heard the question too_ — and then tells Kaelen he does not get to pay, he gets to help, and takes the glass hand rather than the easy one. The ship needs a whole lift-tank, and there is one coast where you can get one. Kaelen adds the eight pump-men's names to the beam, and under them writes: _Not yet._

**Echo I · The Thread Where She Answered** _(the Tide-Weaver)_ — **Drafted.** Ilias Marren introduces himself from outside the story and shows the thread where Lyra went through the Hedge on the forty-second night — the margin is one night's sleep. What comes back up Cooper's Row in her boots is very good at being her: it has two years of her nights and all of her kindness, because kindness is a sound. It takes Kaelen's whole plan at her own table, holds the note for four hours and nineteen minutes, and the Root is relieved. The second Fracture; the Reach gone in eleven days; Vaelen weeping with relief in a locked room. His closing argument is the point of the whole interlude: the world is never saved by the people who are fresh. Then the seed — in every thread where he says nothing at the Organum gate, the Hush takes all of them; in one thread somebody sells them, and some of them live.

## Part XII — Game-Layer Hooks

**The Narrow meter.** Exploration stealth: keep the party's noise between the Cacophony threshold and the Hush threshold. Hush-lamps, murmur stances, and songs are tools, and loud songs are weapons that cost you the map.

**Names.** Every Carillon Legionnaire dies saying a name, and the name goes into the Ledger. Returning names at harvest-houses restores Hollows, costs Sable fragments, and shapes the endings.

**Mimic encounters.** Voices from off-screen. Answering triggers an ambush and teaches the mimic more about the party.

**Glassing.** Kaelen's meter trades survival for power. Heartglass forces a path.

**Echo memory.** The Tide-Weaver remembers the player's previous runs, and New Game+ dialogue changes accordingly.

**Concordance.** Faction standings open ending paths, as set out in Part VII.

**Campfires.** Every rest asks who holds the line tonight. Some answers are permanent.
