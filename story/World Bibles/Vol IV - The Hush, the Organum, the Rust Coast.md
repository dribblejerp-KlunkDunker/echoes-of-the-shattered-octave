# BOOK ONE — THE HUSH

## 1.1 The central fact

**The Hush does not kill what it takes. It keeps it.**

Everything else about the Hush follows from that one sentence, and every scene written about it should be built outward from it. It holds you in the dark, in the cold, and digests you slowly, the way the sea digests a wreck — a little at a time, for years. **You stay awake for all of it.**

It is not cruel. There is no evidence anywhere in the record that it intends suffering, and a good deal of evidence that it doesn't understand the concept. It is patient, attentive, and — this is the worst of it, and it must never be softened — **affectionate**. Vesper's own word for five years at the bottom of a trench is *being digested by something that loved me.*

## 1.2 What it is — three readings, none confirmed

**The Outside Reading.** The Hush is what is beyond the Octave: not the eighth Anchor's silence, which is a *rest in a measure*, but the condition outside any measure at all. Decay at 852 Hz is the world exhaling. The Hush is the room the breath goes into.

**The Underneath Reading** *(the Veil Account, from Volume I §1.4).* The eight spires were driven through something already here, lying under the crust, dreaming. The Octave is a gag. The Hush is what is under it, and the trench off Cape Dissonance and the Maw at Null-Watch are not two phenomena but **two places where the gag has worn through.**

**The Bereaved Reading** *(what the Rust Coast actually believes).* It is the sea's own appetite, older than the Octave, and it was always there, and the only thing that has changed is that something has stopped keeping it polite.

Leave all three standing. The books should never adjudicate. What should be absolutely firm is the **operational** behaviour below, because a monster whose rules are consistent is more frightening than one whose nature is explained.

## 1.3 The Keeping — how it takes a person

1. **The taking is gentle.** No violence, no pain, no struggle that anyone has ever reported. The Drift takes you: harbour water moving in long surges that *do not stop where water should stop*, a hand on the coat, drawing out and down toward the trench.
2. **You do not drown.** This is the part nobody above the water understands. People go down into the trench and do not die.
3. **You are held.** Cold, dark, airless, and conscious. Time does not work; Solenne's testimony is *there isn't time down there, you know that.*
4. **You are digested slowly.** Years. A little at a time. Vesper: five years. The four hundred of Wendmere: six.
5. **You do what you can to stay yourself.** Every single survivor and every single leaked voice does the same thing, independently: **they repeat.** Merret Salt calls soundings — *by the mark, five; and a quarter less six* — in the chains of a ship that is not there. Vesper hums the capstan shanty from rounding Cape Dissonance. Solenne kept singing because the wire would not let her stop.

> The repeating is the setting's deepest piece of design. Note that it is exactly what the Unstrung of Whisper-Grove do, and exactly what the Unison does, and exactly what the market-people of the Bleach are still doing with their outstretched hands. **Everything in Aethelgard that is being slowly taken holds onto one repeated thing.** Loop, note, sounding, shanty, gesture. Three different horrors, one grammar.

## 1.4 The Leak

While the Hush digests you, **some of you leaks upward**. You think about your mother, or your ship, or you hum to keep from screaming, and a little of that goes up through the dark toward the light the way a bubble goes up.

At the lip of the trench, on a calm day, a man holding a bottle upside-down over the water with the stopper out and counting to a hundred will sometimes catch some.

That is the entire vox-bottle trade. It began in the **Screaming Year**, when somebody did it once and a drowned man's widow heard her husband say her name in a kitchen in the Lantern Yard, and then she paid everything she had and **told people**.

Six years of trade. Four to nine sovereigns a bottle to widows; six a bottle, forty a month, to the Office of the Catalogue.

## 1.5 The three kinds of bottle

Nobody can tell which they have until the stopper comes out. This is the engine of the whole trade, and of its horror: *if none were real it would be a cheat and we'd have hanged them years back; if all were real it would be a mercy; it's* **some**.

**THE REAL.** A living person, still down there, still awake, still being eaten. Not a ghost — *their husband*. They can hear you. They can answer. They will ask whether it is the tide. They are calm, and the calm is the worst part.

**THE MIMIC.** Something that learned a name off the water. It will say the name correctly. It will be glad to hear from you. **[PROPOSED] tells:** a mimic never asks a question it does not already have the answer to; it does not know the smell of a house; and if you say a name that belongs to nobody, it will answer to that too.

**THE QUIET.** Nothing says anything at all — and then you cannot hear your own kitchen. A bit of the Hush has come up the neck instead of a man. The quiets began in the same year as the bottles and are the reason the Coast handles them the way it does.

### Handling protocol (as Vesper gives it, and it should be treated as sacred procedure in-world)
- **Never on a ship.** In an open boat, in deep water, with somebody at the seacock and the lanterns dark, so you can put it in the sea and sail away.
- **Never take the stopper out.** Bore the wax with something fine, leave a skin of wax across the glass mouth, and go through it with a **reed**.
- **Listen through the reed, ear first, never mouth first.**
- **You always hear your own breath coming back off the wax.** If the reed goes dead — if your breath stops coming back — that is a quiet, and the maul goes through the seacock and the bottle goes over the side and you do not look back.
- Afterward, re-seal with warmed candle-wax and a thumb.

## 1.6 The Returned

Three people have come back up out of the Hush. They are not the same kind of thing as each other, and the differences matter.

**VESPER — let go.** Five years. She believes she was released *to carry something out*, and that this is the only reason. What came up with her:
- **The quiet.** A field of thinned carrying that goes where she goes and takes the edge off the world. Inside it, nothing can be heard — which makes her the only shelter in the world from any sound-based attack, and the thing that broke seven Unison singers out of a shared mind by simply standing near them.
- **The eyes.** Not purple. The colour the sky goes on the far side of evening — violet shot through with very small, very cold points of light, as if looking out through two holes into a night sky a long way below the sea.
- **The claws.** Darker and longer than nails, curved, faintly translucent, like a cat's claws carved out of smoked glass.
- **The hooks.** Sickle-daggers that are not metal and are not anything. Looking at them is like looking at the place on a wall where a picture used to hang.
- **The voice.** She had a voice like a bell. She has a low dry broken hum now — five years in a place without air.

**SOLENNE — climbed.** She was not let go. She fell all the way down still singing, because the wire would not let her stop, and the dark came close and listened. She climbed back up the way she had fallen, with her hands, until her hands were raw to the bone, and then **something grew out of her back instead**, and she climbed with that. What came up:
- **The wings.** Black plated blades, twice her height, edged with moving gold. The light bends round them and goes gold where it bends.
- **The second voice.** The void under the copper: a dark low hollow thing with no pitch at all, that sounds like the far side of a door opening onto nothing. She sings over it constantly without meaning to.
- **Immunity.** The Bleach does not take her. Her own explanation: *because I'd been down there, and the light doesn't know what I am any more. Nothing does.*

**MOTE — born.** Not a survivor. The dark came close to listen to a soprano singing, and **something was born out of the listening.** It sings her note, 528, because that was the first thing it ever heard. It is the size of a cat, pale, soft, lit from inside with a night sky, warm as a bath, and it purrs.

### What Mote does, and why it is the most dangerous thing in the series
It finds whatever in you hurts worst, and it **eats that**. Gently. Believing it has been asked. It does not know the difference between a mercy and a theft, and neither, after a while, does the person it has helped. Sable's objection is the correct one and should never be resolved cheaply:

> *I have been reading them their names for three weeks so that they would not forget them, and your animal has spent one afternoon on top of the Sowerby crate, and now the six-year-old in it has stopped asking for her mother.*
>
> *They are quiet. They are* **comfortable**.

**Mote is the Hush's argument in miniature, and it is adorable.** That is the design. The reader must love it. The reader must also, by Book Four, be frightened of what the party has let it do.

## 1.7 Taxonomy correction — the Hush is not the Cacophony

Volume II filed the Hush's effects alongside dissonance fauna. **That was wrong.** They are opposite kinds of thing and the books should never confuse them:

| | **The Cacophony** | **The Hush** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Errors with appetite, formed where dissonance thins reality | Unknown; outside, underneath, or older than the Octave |
| Behaviour | Hunts voice and meaning. Shrieks. Attacks. | Does not hunt. Does not attack. **Keeps.** |
| Sound | Static, wrong notes, overlapping dissonance | None. It is the absence of carrying. |
| Effect on the taken | Kills, harmonizes to death, consumes | Holds them awake for years |
| Counter | Resolve into a consonant interval | **Nothing known.** Glass-fire does not hold it. A note does not hold it. |
| Feeling it produces | Terror | **Relief.** *It doesn't hurt. That's what you'd want to know. It doesn't hurt at all. It just goes on.* |

**The Quiet at Null-Watch and the Hush in the trench are the same phenomenon.** Garrison troopers who "go to listen" and Rust Coast men who go over the side are doing the same thing for the same reason. The Maw and the trench are two mouths. Nobody in-world has put this together except Vaelen, who has heard both, and Sable, who has written it down and told no one.

## 1.8 Rules for writing the Hush

1. **No description of the Hush itself. Ever.** It is only ever reported, remembered, or inferred. The moment it is on the page in the third person it becomes a monster, and it stops working.
2. **It is always kind.** Every testimony says so. Every one.
3. **Nobody screams.** Not the taken, not the kept, not the leaked. The absence of screaming is the horror.
4. **It is quiet when it arrives.** The warning sign in a room is never a noise — it is that you cannot hear something you should. Your own kitchen. Your own breath in a reed.
5. **It has never once been fought successfully**, and no scene should imply that it could be.
6. **It gives things back.** Vesper, Solenne, Mote, and the bottles. Everything it has returned has been a gift and a catastrophe at once.

## 1.9 Where the mouths are

- **The trench off Cape Dissonance** — forty miles south of Port Cantabile. The canonical mouth. Siphon Site 9 bored into it, which is its own problem (see Vol II).
- **The Maw at Null-Watch** — the eastern mouth. The Quiet, the Morning Refusal, the Null-Amalgam.
- **Wendmere** — *newly opened.* Four hundred drowned people under a sunken tower, held awake for six years by a monster's song, and the bell has stopped, and the Hush is coming up the tower one step at a time. **This is a mouth that the party opened**, and it is on Kaelen's ledger whether he says so or not.
- **[PROPOSED] a fourth** — under the Spectral Woods, which would explain why the Echo stacks cannot decay and why the loops are shortening rather than stopping. Hold this in reserve for Book Four.

# BOOK TWO — THE ORGANUM
### *The ninth city. Not a realm; a seat.*

### Sensory Palette
**Sound:** paper. Pens. Doors. The Organum is the quietest large city on the continent and the quiet is *administrative* — not the Narrow of the Umbral Tiers, not the Hush, just the hush of a building where everyone is working and nobody is talking. Under it, from the undercroft, something very low that the staff have stopped hearing.
**Light:** grey, even, northern. Clerestory windows. Lamps lit at three in the afternoon all winter.
**Smell:** ink, wet wool, coal smoke, floor polish, and in the Hall of Carriage a faint sweetness like warm honey that nobody is comfortable mentioning.
**Texture:** carpet in the corridors that matter. Stone in the ones that don't. You can navigate the Organum by your feet.

### The city
Built around **Registry Square** — a rectangle of grey stone buildings, four storeys, no ornament, each with a brass plate. The city grew as the paperwork grew. There is no palace. **That is the whole point of the Organum: the most powerful place in the world looks like a tax office, and it is one.**

**THE HALL OF CARRIAGE** *(east side of Registry Square)*
Long, grey, three floors and a basement. **Does not appear on public plans of the city.** Where the Catalogue sends what it takes.
- **Basement — Sorting.** Where pressings arrive from the harvest-houses and are graded. Write it once, in full, without flinching, and never return.
- **Ground — Receiving & Carriage.** Carrier's receipts stamped in red wax: *a key, crossed with a quill, inside a ring.* Tuning stock comes in here, forty bottles a month.
- **First & Second — Assignment.** Cores allocated to Legions, engines, and clients.
- **Third — the corridor with the carpet.** At the end of it, a room Sable has been inside exactly once. **The cabinet of staff trays**: very small cores in velvet, assigned to nothing, belonging to the Office's own staff, *kept against the day the staff might need reminding of their duty.* Sable's is in the third tray.

**THE ARBITERIUM** *(north side)* — Courts, writs, Justiciars, and the columns that enforce them. The Dread Arbiter answers here. Writs from this building stay or lift the Church's claims, which is why the Church hates it and obeys it.

**THE WHITE ROOM** — Casimir Vaelen's. He is a hundred and forty years old and sits in a chair and breathes slowly and deliberately, like a man who has to think about each breath. He has listened to every word spoken within thirty feet of any Dawning glass on the continent for seven years. **[PROPOSED]** the room is white because colour is a distraction from listening, and there is nothing in it but the chair, a window he does not look out of, and a table with nothing on it.

**THE UNDERCROFT** — Where Vaelen intends to do it. Beneath the Organum, the convergence: tuning spires and runic spheres where all eight Anchors can be reached at once. *"What I have been planning to do under the Organum."* The party does not see it until Book Four.

**REGISTRY SQUARE ITSELF** — Clerks crossing at the hour. Pigeons. A coffee stall that has been there sixty years. **The banality is the horror and should be played completely straight.**

### People
- **Casimir Vaelen** — see Vol III §A.3. Kind, honest, correct about the exhaustion, wrong about the question mark.
- **The Dread Arbiter** — the Arbiterium's enforcement hand; has a column in the field with a writ for Kaelen and everyone who has sailed with him.
- **The Prior** *(counting-house, Port Cantabile — Organum's man on the Coast)* — kept the receiving-book in his own hand. Dead or taken by Book Two's end; his book is Sable's.
- **[PROPOSED] Deputy Registrar Halloway** *(f, 40s)* — Runs third-floor Assignment. Knew Sable. Was fond of her. Will not help her, will not raise the alarm, and will stand in a corridor and let her pass and then go back to her desk and be unable to work for the rest of the day. The Organum is staffed entirely by people like this and that is why it functions.
- **[PROPOSED] the mole in Severin Ashe's household** — reads Vaelen's correspondence aloud to the High Patriarch as revelation. Vaelen *has never discouraged this.* Whoever it is should turn out to be someone who thought they were doing a kindness.

### Hazards
Not monsters. **Paperwork, recognition, and carpet.** A severed access key does not change a face. A clerk who knew you fifteen years ago. A receipt with your handwriting on it. A door that opens to a Signature you no longer match.

### Dialect
Flat, procedural, euphemistic. *Incomplete draw, Schedule F.* *Restorative administration.* *Acoustic echo artifacting.* **Nobody at the Organum ever uses a verb with a person as its object.** Things are *processed, assigned, graded, reconciled*. Sable's entire moral arc is the recovery of the active voice.

### Hooks
1. The coffee stall. Sixty years. The man who runs it has served the staff of the Hall of Carriage every morning and has never once asked what they do.
2. Halloway in the corridor.
3. The third tray, and the fact that 432 is more than 2.
4. Vaelen asks to meet, politely, by letter, delivered correctly, to a ship.
5. The undercroft heard through a floor by someone who does not know what they are hearing.

# BOOK THREE — THE RUST COAST
### *Canon-corrected. Supersedes Volume II §III.6 where they disagree.*

### Errata against Volume II
Volume II's Archipelago entry was written blind and is wrong in its particulars. **Port Cantabile is not a six-island rope-bridge town.** It is a cliff harbour. Corrections:

| Vol II said | Canon |
|---|---|
| Six islands strung with rope bridges | A **cliff town** above a harbour, climbed by the **Salt Stair** in six wet flights, with numbered **landings** (first through sixth) and shops on them |
| "Compact-Chair Isolde Fenn," forty-one captains | **Oona Salt, the Harbour-Queen**, in an admiral's coat; the **Free Captains**; the **Articles** |
| Generic corsair governance | Governance *is* the **Articles**, read aloud in a flat voice and enforced by **the drop** from the derrick on the Mole-head |
| "Cosimo Pell, broadsheet-man" | Keep as **[PROPOSED]**; not contradicted, not attested |
| Reef-readers as the central trade | The central trade is **salvage, pilotage, and vox-bottles** |

Keep from Vol II: the reefs sing and change key with the tide; shanties encode navigation; the dialect; hazards 1–3.

### The town as written
- **The Long Mole** — the harbour arm. At its head, **the derrick** and the **Mole-bell**, rung once for the dead when they go in the water.
- **The Salt Stair** — six wet flights up the cliff, lit at night as a zigzag of small gold points. Shops on the landings. At the top, the **counting-house** (the Organum's).
- **The Lantern Yard** — lower town. Hesper's four houses. Where the first vox-bottle was opened.
- **The Cay** — out past the harbour mouth. Burial ground; children are buried in earth there.
- **The mast-tower** — skyship mooring, sixty feet of iron rungs.
- **Wendmere** — the drowned town, with the sunken **Chime** that rang in threes for six years and has now stopped.
- **Cape Dissonance** — forty miles south. The trench. The lip.

### The Free Captains and the Articles
Law on the Rust Coast is a numbered list, read aloud, applied without discretion, in the voice you'd use for a tide-table.

- **The Twelfth** *(canon)* — theft from the Harbour's own pocket. A different crime, and it carries **the drop**.
- **The Sixteenth** *(canon)* — what lies below the tide-mark belongs to whoever brings it up. The Harbour takes a fifth. *The sea takes what it likes, and no court on this coast will hear a complaint against it.*
- **[PROPOSED] The First** — a captain's word at sea is absolute and ends at the tide-mark.
- **[PROPOSED] The Fourth** — no man is held to a debt his captain contracted without him.
- **[PROPOSED] The Ninth** — a ship that takes aboard a Rust Coast crew takes aboard their dead.
- **[PROPOSED] The Twenty-First** — no bottle may be opened within the harbour.

**Oona Salt.** Fifty-six, built like a capstan, admiral's coat, reads executions in the same voice as the tide-tables. Cannot sing — a voice like a cable running out over a fairlead — and has never tried in public in fifty-six years. Her brother Merret was a pilot, lost off Wendmere, and his bottle was held back by a dealer because *his sister would pay more.* She hanged the dealer under the Twelfth and did not nod to anybody; she put her hand flat on his chest, the way you fend a boat off a quay, and pushed.

### Wendmere and the Chime
A town drowned by the trench. Its bell — the **Chime** — rang in threes for six years, out past the Cay, and the whole harbour learned to sleep by it. What was ringing it was a monster that ate the town's four hundred and **sang to them every day so they would not have to hear the other thing.**

The party broke it to get a lift-tank.

The bell is silent. The Hush is coming up the tower. It is very quiet, and very kind, and it does not hurt, and it goes on. **This is the single heaviest unpaid debt in the series and the books should not let anyone off it.**

### Hooks
1. The Mole-bell rung once, for someone nobody can name.
2. Hesper's wreck-orphans on the Cay, and what they are told about the Chime.
3. A widow on the fourth landing with a bottle she has not opened in three years and will not sell.
4. The Twenty-First Article tested when someone opens one in the Lantern Yard.
5. The Free Captains vote on whether the Coast owes Wendmere anything, now that it is known what the bell was for.
