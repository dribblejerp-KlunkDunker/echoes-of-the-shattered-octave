# World Bible Vol VII: The Calendar of Aethelgard
### A complete, closed calendar — reconciled against every dated chapter header in Books Two, Three and Four

> Three choices were left open at the end of Vol VI §0: how many months, whether the solstice is pinned by construction or by luck, and what the Eight Feasts are pegged to. This volume settles all three and builds the rest out from them. **Everything here is checked: the system reproduces all seventeen dated chapter headers in the manuscript without a single exception** (§6). Where it extends canon rather than deriving from it, the extension is marked **[PROPOSED]** and can be cut without touching the arithmetic.

---

## 1. THE SHAPE OF THE YEAR — RULING

**The year is 360 days: twelve months of thirty, and forty-five weeks of eight, exactly.**

That single choice does all the work. It is the only year-length that lets an eight-day week and a thirty-day month both close cleanly, and it means:

- **The weekday of any date is the same every year, for ever.** 19 Frostmonth is always a Rootday. 30 Frostmonth is always the Longest Night and always a Stasisday. A rota that repeats annually is possible at all *because* the year divides by eight.
- **The week does not divide the month** (30 ÷ 8 = 3¾), so the weekday slides against the date within a year, which is why the manuscript's chapter headers look irregular. They are not. They are exact.
- **The solstice is pinned by construction, not by luck.** This was the open question and this is the answer. The Longest Night falls on Stasisday every year and always has.

> **[PROPOSED], optional, cut freely.** Vol III retired the old eight Anchor-named months of 45 days. Note what the numbers do: **8 × 45 became 45 × 8.** The reform swapped the two figures and kept the 360-day year. Somebody preserved the arithmetic and threw away the meaning — which is the Synod in one line. The old month-names stay retired and must never appear as a current date; a Veil scribe reading an eleven-hundred-year-old ledger would meet them, and nobody else ever would.

## 2. THE TWELVE MONTHS

Seasonal, plain, agricultural. The Reach learns the seasons *"as a list of things that grew,"* and the calendar is named the way farmers name things. Three are canon; nine are new.

| # | Month | Season | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stillmonth** | deep winter | Begins the morning after the Longest Night. The name is the world holding its breath. |
| 2 | **Thawmonth** | late winter | Ice off the Flume. Contract Day. |
| 3 | **Seedmonth** | early spring | Planting. The loudest month in the Reach. |
| 4 | **Lambmonth** | spring | |
| 5 | **Reedmonth** | late spring | The wind-reeds come up. A Cantor's year properly begins here. |
| 6 | **Longsun** | midsummer | Ends on the Longest Day. |
| 7 | **Dustmonth** | high summer | Dry. The Corsair gathering and the Great Race. |

**Two bare names (Ashfall, Longsun, Firstfruit) among the -months** is deliberate and matches the canon sample: *Ashfall* is already bare. Do not regularise them.

### The year turns on the Longest Night
**1 Stillmonth is the morning after.** The new year does not begin at a bureaucratic midnight; it begins when the keepers come back up the stair. This is why the hinge of the year and the hinge of the series are the same date.

## 3. THE THREE FIXED POINTS — and what they land on

The calendar was not designed to produce these. They fall out of it.

| Point | Date | Weekday | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Year** | 1 Stillmonth | **Dawnday** | The year begins on the day of **Light**. Morning, after the longest dark. |
| **The Longest Day** | 30 Longsun | **Nullday** | The brightest day of the year is the day of **Decay and Silence**. |
| **The Longest Night** | 30 Frostmonth | **Stasisday** | The longest dark is the day of **Stasis** — the law of stopping. |

**The inversion is the gift here and it costs nothing.** The year's brightest day belongs to the god of silence; its darkest night belongs to the god who holds things still; and the first light of the new year belongs to the Light. An Architect built that, or an Architect found it funny. Nobody in the text needs to say so.

> **Maw has no feast because its day is the Longest Day.** The old bible said *the month of Maw has no festivals and no weddings; it is the rest at the end of the bar.* Re-pegged: **nobody gathers on 30 Longsun.** It is the one attendance no one ever wanted to make public, and Null-Watch answers it by firing the Refusal into the edge of the world at dawn.

## 4. THE EIGHT FEASTS, RE-PEGGED
*Vol III kept the concept and left it unpegged. This pegs it.*

**[PROPOSED] The Feasts are the Rota made public.** Weekly attendance was one person, quietly, at a tree or a floor or a channel. Once a year the attendance was **open** — the whole province came, on that Anchor's own day, in the month where it mattered. The Feast is the keepers' duty performed in front of witnesses.

| Feast | Falls on | Season | What it is now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hearth** | first Hearthday of **Stillmonth** | deep winter | Forge-lighting at the coldest point of the year. Cinderhold's guild rite. |
| **Dial** (Contract Day) | first Stasisday of **Thawmonth** | late winter | Every term in the province expires and is renegotiated at once. The busiest day in the Organum. |
| **Root** | first Rootday of **Seedmonth** | spring | Planting. **Loud on purpose** — the one sanctioned day of noise. |
| **Empyrean** | first Dawnday of **Longsun** | midsummer | Dawn service. Compulsory. The Church kept the hour and lost the chord. |
| **Drift** | first Driftday of **Dustmonth** | high summer | The Corsair gathering and the Great Race. The Coast's only law-free day. |
| **Keystone** (the Weighing) | first Massday of **Sheafmonth** | early autumn | Fortis's oath-renewal. Kit weighed, names cut in the wall. |
| **Canopy** | first Memoryday of **Ashfall** | late autumn | The remembering of the dead. **Not celebrated since 416.** |
| **Maw** | 30 Longsun (always Nullday) | the Longest Day | **No feast. No gathering. Nothing.** |

**Longsun opens with the Light's compulsory dawn service and ends with the day that has no feast.** That is the whole theology of the Synod era in one month, and it was there before anyone designed it.

## 5. HOURS — RULING, and a register tell

**Strike the eight bells.** The old *day divided into eight bells struck by the nearest carillon* does not match a single usage in the manuscript and should go the way of the month-names.

What the manuscript actually does, consistently:
- **Hours of the morning** count from midnight. *The eighth hour of the morning* is eight o'clock.
- **Hours of the afternoon and evening** count from noon. *The fourth hour of the afternoon*; *the sixth hour*, which is dusk in Frostmonth.
- **Hours of the night** count from full dark, and are given bare. *The first hour of the night*, then *the third*, *the fourth*, *the fifth* — and then dawn.
- **The dead hour** is the hour before dawn. It is a name, not a number, and it is when things come.

### The tell — [PROPOSED], and free
**The Organum counts from the clock. The Watch counts from the dark.**

Clerks, Justiciars and the Office say *the eighth hour of the morning*. Null-Watch, the Shelf and ships' crews say *the third hour* and expect to be understood, because everyone they are talking to has also been awake since dusk. You can place a character's whole life by which system they reach for. A Justiciar at Null-Watch asking what hour it is, and being given an answer counted from a darkness he has not been awake for, is a one-line way to show he does not belong there.

### How to date any scene
1. **Day of year** = (month number − 1) × 30 + day.
2. **Weekday** = the (((day of year + 3) mod 8) + 1)-th name in **Rootday, Hearthday, Massday, Stasisday, Dawnday, Driftday, Memoryday, Nullday**.

Worked: 19 Frostmonth → (12−1)×30 + 19 = **349**. (349 + 3) mod 8 = 0, +1 = **1st** = **Rootday.** ✓

## 8. USING IT

- **In prose:** keep doing exactly what the chapter headers already do — *Rootday the nineteenth of Frostmonth*. Never *19/12/419*. Nobody in Aethelgard writes a date as numbers except the Office, and the Office writes *F/3* and a docket number instead.
- **Ordinary speech** drops the month when it's obvious: *"Nullday week"*, *"the Dawnday after the Weighing"*, *"three Rootdays back."*
- **The Reach** dates by what is growing, not by the month, and a Reach character giving a calendar date is a Reach character who has been away.
- **RPG:** a campaign clock of 45 weeks, with the Rota as a recurring eight-day beat — each day is somebody's duty day, and a party can be in the wrong province on the wrong day. Feast days are the eight natural act-breaks in a year-long campaign.
